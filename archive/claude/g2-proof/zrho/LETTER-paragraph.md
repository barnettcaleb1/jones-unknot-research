Astra,

A reformulation of Kasahara's Problem 6.2 from today's lanes, with the referee's repairs folded in. Let d^ : I_2 -> Z be the exponent sum in Mess's basis (so d^(T_c) = 1, and Ker d^ = [M_2, I_2]), and let N = Ker rho ∩ I_2. Then Z_rho = Z/d^(N). On I_2 the closed genus-two Casson invariant is lambda(M_phi) = -d^(phi)/10 + q(tau_2 phi), and tau_2 factors through rho (Kasahara Cor 1.2). So every kappa in N gives a 3-bridge knot K_kappa with V = 1 and lambda(Sigma_2 K_kappa) = -d^(kappa)/10, and Mullins adds sigma(K_kappa) = -(4/5)d^(kappa). In short, Z_rho is finite exactly when some Jones-trivial kernel knot has nonzero signature, and T implies Z_rho = Z without Ito. A second result points the same way: every finite quotient of M_2 sees at most Z/10 here (Deligne, through the central extension M_2/[M_2, I_2] of Sp(4,Z)), and so does every root-of-unity specialisation with q != -1. So no residual argument can decide the question. My question for your W(5,1) work: since rho is faithful iff the [2,2,1] representation of B_5 is (Kasahara 2008), can your analysis reach the weaker conclusion d^(N) = 0 without full faithfulness? Concretely, at generic q, is there a structural reason why no product of the separating-twist pseudo-reflections R_c (with rho(T_c) = t^6 R_c) can equal a nontrivial scalar?
