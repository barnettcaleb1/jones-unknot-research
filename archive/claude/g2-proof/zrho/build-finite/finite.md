## 0. Summary

1. **What the 10 is.** Kasahara's Z/10 splits as Z/5 x Z/2, and the two factors have different origins.
   - The Z/5 is the scalar part. It is the image of I_2 in H_1(M_2) = Z/10, which is only Z/5 because [T_c] = 12 = 2 mod 10. It is seen at q = 1, where rho restricted to I_2 is a scalar character. In the first truncation it appears as the 1/5 in Delta_1 = -30(P_c - I/5), which det = 1 forces in dimension 5.
   - The Z/2 is not in H_1(M_2), because the 2-part of H_1(M_2) dies on I_2. It is unipotent. It comes from a parity lemma: the identity is not an integral combination of the separating-curve projections P_c, because the even lattice Lambda^2_0 H_Z has B mod 2 nonzero. Invariantly, d^ mod 2 is the sum of the 10 even Birman-Craggs homomorphisms, which is a Rochlin-type invariant.
   - Neither factor is evidence about Ker rho. Given Morita's genus-2 constant, |Z_{phi(k)}| = 10 [Z : lambda(Ker phi(k))], where lambda is the Casson invariant. The 10 is the Casson denominator 2(2g+1).
2. **What a finite-order relation must look like.** Write R_c := t^-6 rho(T_c) = I + (t^-30 - 1) P_c and let Gamma_R be the group these generate. Then Z_rho = Z/m exactly when the centre of Gamma_R is generated by t^{-6m} I. So Z_rho is finite iff the separating-twist reflection group Gamma_R has nontrivial centre.
   - A certificate is a single identity: a product of R_c^{+-1} with exponent sum m equal to the scalar t^{-6m} I.
   - Rank-two relations cannot do this. Two distinct separating-twist images never commute, and never satisfy a dihedral (braid-type) relation of any length, because tr(P_c P_c') at t = -1 is an odd square (checked).
   - Relations with d^ = 0 can force a scalar only through Schur's lemma, and that needs a long word.
3. **Verdict.** There is no proof either way. Every reason found points to Z_rho = Z (infinite). The "evidence" for 10 is entirely classical, and every local mechanism for a centre is excluded.
   - The stakes are larger than a single counterexample to T. If |Z_rho| = m < infinity, then for any kernel element kappa with d^(kappa) = m, the knots K_{kappa^n} (n >= 1) are all Jones-trivial with signature -(4/5)mn. That is an infinite family of Jones-trivial knots of unbounded signature.

## 1. Normal form and reformulations

**1.1 The pseudo-reflection form** [LIT + PROVED]. Kasahara's (2.2): rho(psi_0) - t^6 I has rank one (read-kasahara). Its eigenvalues are t^6 (four times) and t^-24 (once).
- So rho(T_c) = t^6 (I + (t^-30 - 1) P_c), where P_c = E/(1 - t^15) is a rank-one idempotent. In Kasahara's basis, P_c at t = -1 is E44 - E42 - E45.
- Conjugates give the same form for every separating curve c, since all are of type (1,1).
- In braid terms: T_c lifts to Delta_3^4 on three of the six strands. P_c projects onto the [3]-isotypic line of that B_3 in W(6,0) = [3,3], which restricted to B_3 is [3] + 2[2,1].
- rho is unitary for the Jones Hermitian form h (bar t = t^-1). The eigenvalues t^-24 and t^6 satisfy t^-24 conj(t^6) = t^-30 != 1, so l_c := Im P_c is h-orthogonal to Ker P_c. So P_c is the h-orthogonal projection onto the root line l_c.

**1.2 Definitions.**
- R_c := t^-6 rho(T_c) = I + (t^-30 - 1) P_c, a pseudo-reflection with det R_c = t^-30.
- Gamma_R := <R_c : c separating> in GL_5(Z[t^+-1]).
- deg : Gamma_R -> Z by det g = t^{-30 deg g}, so deg R_c = 1.
- R : I_2 -> Gamma_R by R(phi) = t^{-6 d^(phi)} rho(phi). This is a homomorphism because d^ is one, with d^(T_c) = 1 (closed core, exponent sum in Mess's basis). R is M_2-equivariant under conjugation by rho(M_2), since d^ is invariant. deg R(phi) = d^(phi).
- N := Ker rho cap I_2.

**Lemma 1** [PROVED].
- (a) Ker R = N cap Ker d^.
- (b) Gamma_R cap {scalars} = { t^{-6 d^(kappa)} I : kappa in N }.
- (c) Z_rho = I_2 / (N [M_2,I_2]) = Z / d^(N) (Kasahara's Ker d^ = [M_2,I_2]). Equivalently, Z_rho is the M_2-coinvariants of H_1(rho(I_2); Z).

Proof. If R(phi) = lambda I with lambda in Z[t^+-1]^x = +-t^Z, then rho(phi) = t^{6d^} lambda I lies in SL_5. A scalar in SL_5 over this ring is I, because (-1)^5 = -1 rules out the sign. So rho(phi) = I and lambda = t^{-6 d^(phi)}. Part (c): I_2/[M_2,I_2] = Z via d^, and the image of N is d^(N). QED

**Lemma 2 (centre form)** [PROVED, uses Lemma C1 below]. Gamma_R acts absolutely irreducibly on V = C(t)^5, so its centre consists of scalars. Hence

> **Z_rho = Z/m <=> Z(Gamma_R) = < t^{-6m} I >, and Z_rho = Z <=> Gamma_R is centreless.** Also rho(I_2) = Gamma_R / Z(Gamma_R).

Proof of irreducibility. Let W be invariant. Then P_c W is in W, so for each c either l_c is in W or W is h-orthogonal to l_c. By Lemma C1, no two root lines are h-orthogonal. So if one l_c lies in W, all do. They span V, because their span is rho(M_2)-invariant and rho is irreducible. Otherwise W is orthogonal to V, so W = 0. QED

**Lemma 3 (prime-by-prime)** [PROVED].
- Hom(I_2, Z/n)^{M_2} = Z/n, generated by d^ mod n. Reason: H_1(I_2) is free abelian on the splittings (Mess), and M_2 acts transitively on them.
- Any invariant characteristic class of I_2 mod n is a unit times d^ mod n.
- |Z_rho| is divisible by n iff d^ mod n factors through rho.
- Since Z_rho is cyclic: **Z_rho = Z iff, for one prime p (any one), d^ mod p^k factors through rho for every k.** Finiteness means every prime tower stops.

**Lemma 4 (bounded defect)** [PROVED]. Z_rho = Z iff there are a function F on rho(I_2) and C < infinity with |d^(phi) - F(rho(phi))| <= C for all phi in I_2.
- Proof (<=): for kappa in N, |n d^(kappa) - F(I)| <= C for all n, so d^(kappa) = 0. (=>): F := d^ composed with rho^-1 is well defined.
- By the identity lane's (*), the same holds with d^ replaced by (5/4) sigma(K_phi). So **Z_rho = Z iff signature on I_2 is coarsely a function of the genus-2 Jones matrix.** That is the plat analogue of Gambaudo-Ghys, where closed-braid signature equals a Meyer quasimorphism of Burau up to O(1).

**Lemma 5 (h-adic discontinuity)** [PROVED from the addendum]. If Z_rho = Z, then d^ composed with rho^-1 is a homomorphism rho(I_2) -> Z that is discontinuous for the h-adic topology at t = -1 (and likewise at t = 1).
- A continuous one would factor through some truncation phi(k). That would make Z_{phi(k)} infinite, against the addendum.
- The same argument works at t = 1, where sl_5 has no S_6-invariants because [3,3] is absolutely irreducible.
- So no proof of infiniteness can come from finitely many Taylor coefficients of rho at t = +-1.
- I_2 acts trivially exactly when t^6 = 1 and (t^15+1)E = 0, i.e. at t in {1, -1, e^{+-i pi/3}}. The points e^{+-i pi/3} were not analysed.

## 2. Task (1): what Kasahara's Z/10 is

**2.1 The Z/5 is the scalar part, and it is H_1(M_2) restricted to I_2** [PROVED].
- H_1(M_2) = Z/10 is generated by a nonseparating twist. The chain relation gives T_c = (T_a T_b)^6, so [T_c] = 12 = 2. Hence **the image of I_2 in H_1(M_2) is Z/5, not Z/10**. The Z/2 quotient of H_1(M_2), the sign character, is trivial on I_2.
- At t = zeta_5 (q = 1), every off-diagonal term of (2.2) vanishes and rho(T_c) = t^6 I. Here rho is (S_6-rep) x (character of M_2), and I_2 dies in S_6 = Sp(4,F_2). The resulting I_2 -> mu_5, phi -> zeta^{6 d^(phi)}, is by Lemma 3 the class d^ mod 5. It is also the Meyer function mod Z: phi_Meyer = -(4/5) d^ on I_2, with denominator 2g+1 = 5.
- In the first truncation, Delta_1(c) = 6I - 30 P_c = -30 (P_c - I/5). The scalar 6 = 30/5 is forced by trace zero (det = 1) in dimension 5. Integrality of the P_c gives 5 | |Z_{phi(1)}| (2.3).
- Is this "an artefact of normalisation"? It is the normalisation, but it cannot be removed.
  - The unnormalised R is a representation of I_2 only. It does not extend to M_2 by any character twist: any character chi of M_2 has chi(T_c) = chi(T_a)^12 with chi(T_a)^10 = 1, so chi(T_c) can never equal t^-6. With R one has Z_R = Z trivially, since det detects deg.
  - Twisting rho by a character of M_2 does not change Z_rho. On I_2 the character has order dividing 5, so the new kernel is N cap Ker(d^ mod 5), whose d^-image is still d^(N) because 5 divides it.

**2.2 The Z/2 is unipotent (parity lemma)** [PROVED]. At t = -1, rho = sgn (x) Lambda^2_0 H (Kasahara Lemma 2.1; Lambda^2_0 H is iso to Lambda^2 H/omega over Q).
- Use the auxiliary lattice Lambda = {x in Lambda^2 H_Z : x ^ omega = 0}, with B(x,y) = x^y/vol. Lambda is even, has signature (2,3) and |disc| = 2, and B mod 2 != 0: for example B(e1^e2, f1^f2) = -1.
- A separating curve c gives a splitting H = H_1 + H_2 and a vector u_c = omega_1 - omega_2 in Lambda with B(u_c,u_c) = -2.
- By Stab(c)-invariance, P_c(-1) is the B-orthogonal projection onto Q u_c. The decomposition Lambda^2_0 H = Q u_c + H_1(x)H_2 is the unique invariant splitting.
- For x in Lambda, B(x,u_c) = -2 x_{e1 f1} is even. So lam_c(x) := -B(x,u_c)/2 is in Z, P_c x = lam_c(x) u_c, and **B(P_c x, y) = -2 lam_c(x) lam_c(y)**.

> **Parity lemma.** If kI = sum m_i P_{c_i} with m_i in Z, then k is even.
> Proof: k B(x,x)/2 = -sum m_i lam_i(x)^2, which is congruent to sum m_i lam_i(x) mod 2, a linear function of x. If k were odd, the quadratic form Q(x) = B(x,x)/2 mod 2 would be linear. Its polar form B mod 2 would then vanish, and it does not. QED

- The statement is basis-free, so the auxiliary lattice is legitimate.
- Invariant reading [PROVED, using Johnson 1980 [LIT]: BC_q(T_c) = Arf(q restricted to one side)]. **d^ mod 2 = sum of BC_q over the 10 even spin structures q.**
  - The sum is M_2-invariant.
  - Its value on T_c is #{even q : q odd on both sides} = 1 x 1 = 1.
  - Then apply Lemma 3.
- So the 2-part is a Rochlin/Arf-type class, matching "Casson = Rochlin mod 2".
- At q = i (t = zeta_20) one gets rho(T_c) = zeta_10^3 (I - 2P_c): an order-10 scalar times an honest reflection [PROVED from 1.1]. That fits Kasahara 2002 seeing "order >= 10" at the 4th root of unity [GUESS: that paper not read].

**2.3 The first truncation, exactly** [PROVED].
- Let L = Z-span{Delta_1(c)}, D = Z-span{P_c - P_c'} and Lambda_P = Z-span{P_c}. Then IL = 30 D.
- So |Z_{phi(1)}| = order of (P_c - I/5) mod D = min{N > 0 : (N/5) I in Lambda_P}. The trace matches automatically.
- Integrality (Lambda_P preserves the lattice Lambda) gives 5 | N. The parity lemma gives 2 | N/5. So **10 | |Z_{phi(1)}|** conceptually.
- Kasahara's computer value (reproduced in ../read-kasahara/check/zphi1.py) is exactly 10, which says 2I lies in Lambda_P. Cross-check: Lambda_P tensor Z_(5) contains I, so the 5-adic tower stops at 5 at first order (section 5).

**2.4 Casson reading** [DERIVED-SIB: Morita's genus-2 formula lambda = -d^/10 + q(tau_2) on I_2, constant derived by two sibling lanes].
- Ker phi(k) lies in Ker tau_2 (Kasahara Cor 1.2), so lambda = -d^/10 there. Hence

> |Z_{phi(k)}| = 10 [Z : lambda(Ker phi(k))] and |Z_rho| = 10 [Z : lambda(N)] (or infinity).

- Kasahara's Z_{phi(1)} = Z_{phi(2)} = Z/10 says exactly that some phi with rho(phi) = I mod h^3 has Casson invariant +-1. This is a classical-type fact and says nothing about N.
- The coincidence "Z/10 = H_1(M_2)" is half structural. The 5's are the same class (Meyer denominator 2g+1, which is the order of [T_c] in H_1(M_2)). The 2's are different: H_1(M_2)'s 2-part dies on I_2, while Z_{tau_2}'s 2-part is Rochlin.
- The addendum's finiteness of every Z_{phi(k)} is equivalent to: for each k there is phi, Jones-trivial to order h^k, with lambda(M_phi) != 0.

## 3. Task (2): shape of a finite-order relation

**3.1 The certificate.** If |Z_rho| = m, then rho(T_c)^m lies in rho([M_2,I_2]). In pseudo-reflection form this is equivalent to: there are separating curves c_1..c_k and signs e_j with

> **R_{c_1}^{e_1} ... R_{c_k}^{e_k} = t^{-6m} I_5 in GL_5(Z[t^+-1]), with sum e_j = m.**   (F)

This is the same as rho(T_{c_1}^{e_1}...T_{c_k}^{e_k}) = I_5 with d^ = m. Determinants match automatically, since both sides give t^{-30m}. For "exactly 10": m = 10 and the right side is t^-60 I.

**3.2 Necessary features** [PROVED].
- (i) Full span. The root lines l_{c_j} must span V; otherwise the product fixes the nonzero space orthogonal to their span. So at least 5 splittings with independent u's are needed, and the word has length at least m >= 10.
- (ii) First-order shadow. At t = -e^h, (F) gives sum e_j P_{c_j}(-1) = (m/5) I, which is 2I for m = 10. Equivalently, sum e_j u_j u_j^T = -(2m/5) B^-1, a signed "tight frame" of (-2)-vectors. This exists (Kasahara's index), and by the parity lemma it forces 10 | m. Each higher h-order adds a further lifting condition. Lemma 5 says no finite number of these can settle the question.
- (iii) It is a central element of an absolutely irreducible group (Lemma 2), i.e. a Garside-type element.

**3.3 Lemma C (no rank-two relations).** For separating c, c' with rho(T_c) != rho(T_c'):
- **C1** [PROVED + CHECK]. tr(P_c P_c') at t = -1 equals (2n-1)^2, where n = omega_1 ^ omega_1' is an integer.
  - Proof: u = 2 omega_1 - omega, so B(u,u') = 4 B(omega_1, omega_1') - 2 B(omega_1, omega) - 2 B(omega, omega_1') + B(omega, omega) = 4n - 2 - 2 + 2 = 4n - 2, and the trace is B(u,u')^2/4.
  - So the trace is an odd square, never 0, and B(u,u') is 2 mod 4. Checked on 4000 random conjugates using Jones's matrices: no violations, values 1, 9, 25, 49, ... (section 7).
- **C2** [PROVED]. rho(T_c) and rho(T_c') do not commute. Two rank-one idempotents with gamma := tr(P P') != 0 commute only if they are equal.
- **C3** [PROVED]. They satisfy no dihedral Artin relation of any length k >= 2.
  - Setup: on U = l_c + l_c', r_i = I + kappa P_i with lambda = 1 + kappa = t^-30.
  - Irreducible case: a length-k relation makes (r_1 r_2)^{k'} central (k' = k/2 or k), hence scalar on U. So the eigenvalue ratio of r_1 r_2 is a nontrivial root of unity, and F(t) := (2 lambda + kappa^2 gamma)^2/lambda^2 - 2 would equal a constant 2cos(2 pi j/k') != 2.
  - But F is rational and regular at t = -1, since gamma(-1) is finite by C1, and F(-1) = 2. Contradiction.
  - Reducible case (gamma = 1): the same centrality forces r_1 = r_2 on U, which is impossible.
- **C4** [PROVED modulo LIT-standard positivity of the TL form on W(6,0) for q = e^{i theta}, theta small].
  - On that arc h is definite, so gamma <= 1 with equality only for parallel lines. Hence gamma is not identically 1, U is nondegenerate, and the pair acts irreducibly on U.
  - (Remark: were some plane degenerate, the pair would act through a solvable group on the flag w < U < V. Then third-derived words in T_c, T_c' would lie in N, giving unfaithfulness with d^ = 0. C4 rules this out.)
- Freeness of <R_c, R_c'> is expected (ping-pong at a place where |t^-30| != 1) but not proved [HEUR].

**3.4 Why commutator relations cannot supply (F)** [PROVED].
- (a) The normal closure in M_2 of any set of relations with d^ = 0 lies in Ker d^ = [M_2,I_2]. So it has no d^-image, and Z_rho stays Z unless something else enters.
- (b) The "separating-curve criterion" for unfaithfulness reads: N != 1 iff rho(T_c) = rho(T_c') for some c != c', and by C2 that forces the same splitting. It produces T_c T_c'^-1 = [psi, T_c] with c' = psi(c) and psi in I_2. These lie in [I_2,I_2], so they are invisible even in H_1(I_2).
- (c) Commuting families give nothing. Pairwise commuting separating twists would need pairwise orthogonal root lines: at most 3 by the signature (2,3) at t = -1, and in fact none by C1. A product of commuting reflections is t^-30 on their span and 1 elsewhere, never scalar.
- (d) Rank-two relations of braid type are excluded by C3. So the Artin/Garside route, where pairwise braid relations plus irreducibility give a central Delta^2 (degree 30 for A_5, 40 for D_5, 50 for B_5), has no input.
- (e) The only remaining mechanism is Schur. If some x in Gamma_R with deg x != 0 commutes with a family of R_c acting irreducibly, then x is scalar and (F) holds. The relations [x, R_c] = 1 have d^ = 0 but force a d^ != 0 kernel element. This is equivalent to finiteness (Lemma 2), and x must be a long word: deg >= 10 and full span.
- (f) Finite symmetry cannot trigger Schur [PROVED, modulo the LIT list of genus-2 automorphism groups].
  - Finite subgroups of M_2 are automorphism groups of genus-2 curves (Nielsen realisation), of order at most 48.
  - None has a 5-dimensional irreducible representation. That needs 5 | |G| with |G| > 25, i.e. |G| in {30, 35, 40, 45}. Groups of those orders have irreps of degree at most 2, or a normal abelian subgroup of index prime to 5 (order 40, by Ito's degree theorem).
  - Infinite centralising subgroups in M_2 were not analysed. The commutation that (F) needs happens in the image, not in M_2.

## 4. Task (3): for or against finiteness

**4.1 Stakes** [DERIVED-SIB: identity (*) of build-identity on N, where V = 1, L = 0 and tau_2 = 0].
- For kappa in N: sigma(K_kappa) = -(4/5) d^(kappa) and lambda(Sigma_2 K_kappa) = -d^(kappa)/10.
- If |Z_rho| = m, pick kappa with d^ = m. Then **K_{kappa^n}, n = 1, 2, ..., are Jones-trivial 3-bridge knots with sigma = -(4/5) m n <= -8n**. Their unknotting number and 4-genus are at least 4n, and their double branched covers are homology spheres with Casson invariant -mn/10.
- This is far stronger than the negation of T, and needs neither Ito nor the normal-intersection lemma.
- Unfaithfulness alone (Ito) gives one Jones-trivial knot. Finiteness gives an infinite family of unbounded signature. If m = 10, the first member has sigma = -8 and lambda(Sigma_2) = -1, like Sigma(2,3,5).

**4.2 Reasons against finiteness (for Z_rho = Z), strongest first.**
1. [Conditional, LIT + DERIVED-SIB] T implies Z_rho = Z, and so does faithfulness of rho. Finiteness is strictly stronger than the negation of T (4.1). Every T-belief argument transfers.
2. [PROVED, structural] Every local mechanism for a centre of Gamma_R is excluded:
   - no commuting pairs (C2);
   - no dihedral relations (C3);
   - no degenerate planes (C4);
   - no commuting families (3.4c);
   - no finite symmetry (3.4f).
   A centre needs a global relation of rank 5 among pseudo-reflections that pairwise satisfy no Artin relation. [HEUR] In the RAAG-like model suggested by C2 and C3 (pairwise free generators, possibly with identifications R_c = R_c'), the group is centreless.
3. [PROVED] All the evidence for "10" is classical and would look identical if Z_rho = Z.
   - d^ mod 5 is H_1(M_2) restricted to I_2 (the q = 1 character).
   - d^ mod 2 is the sum of the even Birman-Craggs homomorphisms (Rochlin).
   - The truncation orders are 10 times Casson indices (2.4).
   - By (2.2), rho(T_c) = t^6 I + ((t^15+1)/t^24) E, which is scalar iff q = 1 or q^3 = -1. So at q in {1, -1, e^{+-i pi/3}}, I_2 acts by scalars and the coinvariants are at most Z/5. At q = i (finite image, r = 4), rho(T_c) is an order-10 scalar times a reflection, which gives Z/10. At q = e^{2 pi i/3}, (t^15+1)E is rank one with trace 0, so rho(T_c) is a scalar times a transvection; W(6,0) is non-semisimple there, and I did not analyse it.
   - By the addendum and Lemma 5, no rational expansion at t = +-1 can ever show more.

**4.3 Reasons for finiteness.**
- Only weak ones. Every computable quotient gives exactly 10 (Kasahara's "remains possible").
- The Schur mechanism exists in principle (3.4e).
- If rho is unfaithful at all, N is a large free normal subgroup, and nothing forces its image in H_1(I_2) to have augmentation zero.
- No structural reason was found.

**4.4 Verdict** [HEUR]. Open. The theoretical reasons favour Z_rho = Z. The finite side is a counterexample hunt that needs one exact global identity, (F). Truncations, specialisations and rank-two analysis cannot produce or rule it out.

**4.5 The single exact identity that would certify a finite order.**

> Separating curves c_j (as conjugates beta_j Delta_3^4 beta_j^-1 in B_6, or as words in Kasahara's zeta_1, xi) and signs e_j with sum e_j = 10, such that
> **prod_j (I_5 + (t^-30 - 1) P_{c_j}(t))^{e_j} = t^-60 I_5** over Z[t^+-1], i.e. rho(prod T_{c_j}^{e_j}) = I_5.

- Its first-order shadow sum e_j P_{c_j}(-1) = 2 I_5 is necessary and is known to be solvable.
- Knot form: the plat closure K of prod_j (beta_j Delta_3^{4 e_j} beta_j^-1) has V_K = 1 and sigma(K) = -8 (identity-lane sign conventions). That is a counterexample to T, checkable in minutes by Kauffman bracket plus Gordon-Litherland.
- For order 10g, replace 10 with 10g, t^-60 with t^{-60g} and -8 with -8g.

## 5. Routes to settle it (theory only, not pursued)

- **Infinite side via Lemma 4.** Find any function of rho(phi) within O(1) of d^, equivalently of signature, on I_2.
  - Candidates: a Guichardet-Wigner/rotation quasimorphism of rho_theta(I_2) in U(p,q) at an indefinite unitary point, or a Busemann function on a building at a non-archimedean place. This is the plat analogue of Gambaudo-Ghys.
  - Lemma 5 says it must be non-perturbative at t = +-1.
- **Infinite side via Lemma 3 at p = 5.** Over Z_5[[t-1]], I_2 lands in the first congruence subgroup (a pro-5 group). Mod 5, sl_5 contains the scalars (tr I = 5 = 0), so invariant classes can recur at every level. This is exactly where the rational addendum argument does not apply.
  - Showing that d^ mod 5^k factors through rho for all k (for example via t = zeta_{5^k}) would prove Z_rho = Z. At first order the tower stops at 5, because 2I is in Lambda_P (2.3), so a proof must use higher levels.
- **Finite side.** Only by exhibiting (F). No theory here produces it.
