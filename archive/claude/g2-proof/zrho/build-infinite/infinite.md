# build-infinite: the infinite side of Kasahara's Problem 6.2

Label: build-infinite. Date: 2026-11-03. Theory only; no sweeps were run.
Tags: [LIT] read in a source (here or by a sibling lane), [DER] derived here with a proof or proof sketch, [SKETCH] argument outlined but with a step not fully checked, [GUESS] heuristic.

## 0. Verdict at a glance

No proof of the infinite side (Z_rho = Z, i.e. d^ vanishes on N = Ker rho cap I_2). What this lane did establish:

1. **Theorem A (Deligne bound) [DER from LIT].** For every homomorphism pi from M_2 to a finite group, Z_pi := pi(I_2)/pi([M_2,I_2]) is cyclic of order dividing 10. Consequently every h-adic truncation has Z_{phi(k)} = Z/10 exactly, for all k >= 1. This reproves Kasahara's two computer results (k = 1, 2) without a computer and extends them to all k. It also explains his "10": the 5 is the index of the transgression of d^ in H^2(Sp(4,Z)), the 2 is Deligne's |mu(Q)| = 2.
2. **Lemma B [DER].** If a representation sigma of M_2 has sigma(M_2)/sigma(I_2) finite, then |Z_sigma| divides 10. This covers every root-of-unity specialisation of rho with q != -1, unitary or not, finite image or dense image.
3. **Reformulations [DER, elementary].** (a) rho(phi) = t^{6 d^(phi)} R(phi) on I_2, where R is the homomorphism generated by the spin-3/2 pseudo-reflections R_c = I + (t^{-30} - 1) P_c. Z_rho is finite iff the group R(I_2) contains a scalar other than I; the scalars it can contain are exactly t^{-6m} I with m in d^(N), so m is a multiple of 10. (b) Z_rho = Z iff the Hodge class lambda_1 vanishes in H^2(rho(M_2); Q); also H_2(rho(M_2); Q) = H_1(N; Q)_{M_2}, and lambda_1 pairs with it as a nonzero multiple of d^.
4. **Consequence for the infinite side.** Every residual or perturbative approach is closed: finite quotients, congruence quotients, h-adic truncations (mechanism (i)), roots of unity (mechanism (iii)), and any finite product of these see at most Z/10. If Z_rho = Z, the invariant functional f with f o rho = d^ is discontinuous for every congruence, h-adic and p-adic topology on rho(M_2). Z_rho = Z is then a non-commuting limit: d^(F_k) = 10Z for every k, yet d^(intersection of the F_k) = 0.
5. **Archimedean picture [SKETCH].** On I_2, d^ is (-5/4) times Meyer's function. That is the central coordinate of the unique lift of the t = -1 specialisation Lambda^2 H/omega to the universal cover of SO_0(3,2), the real form. Near t = -1 on the unit circle rho is only U(3,2)-Hermitian, and SO_0(3,2) -> SU(3,2) is zero on pi_1. So the natural Hermitian (Toledo) lift carries no trace of d^: on the signature arc through t = -1 it kills Ker rho for trivial reasons. The Meyer winding lives only on the real point t = -1, where rho(I_2) = 1.

**Named crux (what d needs that rho might not carry).** For kappa in N, d^(kappa) is the Meyer/Hodge winding of kappa: the class in pi_1(SO_0(3,2)) = Z traced by the unique lift of Lambda^2 H/omega. Equivalently it is the winding of det(a-periods of the holomorphic differentials) along the Torelli loop, corrected by half the braid exponent sum (Section 4). The infinite side is exactly the statement that this winding vanishes on every element that rho kills. Theorem A and Lemma B show that no finite, perturbative or root-of-unity shadow of rho can see it. A proof needs a genuinely non-residual input.

## 1. Setup and two exact reformulations

Notation as in the sibling lanes. d^ : I_2 -> Z is the exponent sum in Mess's basis, with d^(T_c) = 1 for every separating twist. It spans Hom(I_2, Q)^{M_2}, and Ker d^ = [M_2, I_2]. N = Ker rho cap I_2. Z_rho = Z / d^(N) [read-casson, build-identity]. T denotes the class of T_c in E := M_2/[M_2, I_2].

### 1.1 Pseudo-reflection form [DER]

Kasahara's (2.2) [LIT, via read-kasahara]: rho(T_c) = t^6 I + ((t^15 + 1)/t^24) E, where E has one nonzero row (row 4) with E_44 = 1 - t^15. So E^2 = (1 - t^15) E, and P_c := E/(1 - t^15) is a rank-one idempotent over Q(t). Then

    rho(T_c) = t^6 R_c,   R_c := I + (t^{-30} - 1) P_c,   det R_c = t^{-30}.

R_c is a pseudo-reflection. Its nontrivial eigenline is the spin-3/2 channel of the three branch points inside c: on W(6,0) restricted to B_3 x B_3 we get W(3,1)(x)W(3,1) + W(3,3)(x)W(3,3), and the full twist acts by a scalar on each summand. Since I_2 is free on separating twists and d^(T_c) = 1 for all of them, define R(x) := t^{-6 d^(x)} rho(x). This R is a homomorphism I_2 -> GL_5(Z[t^{+-1}]), equivariant for conjugation by rho(M_2) because d^ is M_2-invariant, and R(T_c) = R_c for every separating c.

**Proposition 1.** For x in I_2: R(x) is scalar iff x is in N, and then R(x) = t^{-6 d^(x)} I. Hence
- R(I_2) cap {scalars} = { t^{-6m} I : m in d^(N) };
- Z_rho = Z iff R(I_2) contains no scalar other than I, iff Ker R = Ker rho on I_2;
- if Z_rho is finite, |Z_rho| = min{ m > 0 : t^{-6m} I in R(I_2) }, and 10 divides it.

*Proof.* If R(x) = lambda I, then lambda is a unit, so lambda = +-t^k. Determinants give lambda^5 = t^{-30 d^(x)}, which forces the sign + and k = -6 d^(x). So rho(x) = t^{6d^(x)} lambda I = I. The converse is immediate. The last clause holds because d^(N) is contained in d^(Ker tau_2) = 10Z (Section 2.3). QED

Reading: rho(x) determines R(x) only up to the scalar t^{6 d^(x)}. The infinite side says this scalar can be read off rho(x), i.e. no product of spin-3/2 pseudo-reflections equals a nontrivial scalar. Two specialisations make the scalar separable, and they are exactly the two prime parts of Kasahara's 10 [DER]:
- t^{30} = 1 (q = 1): R_c = I, so rho = t^{6 d^} on I_2. This is d^ mod 5, matching read-kasahara's root5 check.
- t = i (q = i, Kasahara's 4th-root-of-unity paper): t^6 = -1 and t^{-30} = -1, so R_c = I - 2P_c is a genuine reflection and rho = (-1)^{d^} R. d^ mod 2 is a function of rho_i iff -I is not in the reflection group R_i(I_2). This is my reading of where his 2-part comes from; I did not read that paper.

### 1.2 Hodge-class form [DER, standard 5-term sequences]

Put Gamma = rho(M_2) and Delta = rho(I_2). Then Gamma/Delta = M_2/(I_2 <iota>) = PSp(4,Z), and this quotient map is the t = -1 specialisation (Kasahara Lemma 2.1). Inputs [LIT, standard]: H^1(M_2;Q) = H^2(M_2;Q) = 0; H^2(Sp(4,Z);Q) = Q lambda_1; H^1(I_2;Q)^{Sp} = Q d^.

**Proposition 2.** Z_rho = Z iff the inflation of lambda_1 to H^2(Gamma; Q) is zero. Moreover H_2(Gamma; Q) is isomorphic to H_1(N; Q)_{M_2}, and on it lambda_1 evaluates as a nonzero multiple of d^ restricted to N.

*Proof sketch.* Compare the 5-term sequences of 1 -> I_2 -> M_2 -> Sp -> 1 and 1 -> Delta -> Gamma -> PSp -> 1. For M_2, the transgression d_2 : Q d^ -> H^2(Sp;Q) is an isomorphism, because H^1(M_2;Q) = H^2(M_2;Q) = 0.
- If d^ = f o rho, naturality gives d_2^Gamma(f) = lambda_1 up to a nonzero scalar, and exactness puts lambda_1 in the kernel of inflation to Gamma.
- Conversely, if lambda_1 inflates to 0, exactness gives an invariant f on Delta with d_2(f) = lambda_1. Then f o rho is a nonzero multiple of d^, so d^ kills N.
- The homology statement is the 5-term sequence of 1 -> N -> M_2/<iota> -> Gamma -> 1, using H_1 = H_2 = 0 rationally for Mod(S_{0,6}). QED

This restates Kasahara's Prop 6.1 in a form that names the obstruction class: rho(M_2) must forget the relation "10 lambda_1 = 0 on M_2" in the way M_2 does, through the Torelli coinvariant d^.

## 2. Theorem A: the Deligne bound on every residual shadow of Z_rho

### 2.1 The extension E = M_2/[M_2, I_2] [DER]

E is a central extension 1 -> <T> = Z -> E -> Sp(4,Z) -> 1. It is central because [M_2,I_2] is killed, and the kernel is infinite cyclic because d^ : I_2/[M_2,I_2] -> Z is an isomorphism. Call its class eps in H^2(Sp(4,Z); Z).

- **eps has infinite order.** In the 5-term sequence with Z coefficients, 0 = H^1(M_2;Z) -> H^1(I_2;Z)^{Sp} = Z d^ -> H^2(Sp;Z) is injective, and eps = d_2(d^) up to sign.
- **Normalisation: eps = +-5 lambda_1 modulo torsion.** The next map in the 5-term sequence, H^2(Sp;Z) -> H^2(M_2;Z) = Z/10, is onto [LIT, standard: H_1(M_2) = Z/10 and H_2(M_2;Z) = Z/2; Pic of the moduli stack of genus-2 curves is Z/10 generated by lambda]. So the cokernel of Z eps -> H^2(Sp(4,Z);Z) is Z/10. Since lambda_1 generates the free part, eps = +-5 lambda_1 + (torsion).
- **Cross-check through Meyer [DER].** Meyer's function phi (delta phi = tau, the signature cocycle) restricts on I_2 to c_0 d^ with |c_0| = 4/5 (Endo, via read-casson). It is constant on cosets of [M_2,I_2], so s := phi/c_0 descends to E with s(T^n e) = n + s(e) and delta s = tau/c_0. Hence eps = -(1/c_0)[tau] = +-(5/4)[tau] rationally, and [tau] = +-4 lambda_1 (Meyer: the signature equals 4 lambda_1 on bases). This gives +-5 lambda_1 again.

### 2.2 Deligne's theorem transported to E [DER from LIT]

[LIT] (Deligne 1978, as quoted in the secondary sources found today): let Sp~ be the Z-extension of Sp(2g,Z) obtained by restricting the universal cover of Sp(2g,R), with g >= 2. Then every finite-index subgroup of Sp~ contains 2Z, a subgroup of the centre. Its class c is c_1 of det on the maximal compact U(2), pulled back, i.e. c = +-lambda_1 modulo torsion [LIT, standard; I did not re-derive the sign].

**Lemma 2.2.** Every finite-index subgroup V of E contains T^10.

*Proof.* Let o be the order of the torsion class theta = eps -+ 5c.
- Push E out along Z -> Z, x -> ox, to get E_o with central generator w and T = w^o. Its class is o eps = +-5o c, which is also the pushout of Sp~ along x -> 5o x. So E_o is isomorphic to that pushout, and Sp~ sits inside E_o with its central generator z = w^{5o} = T^5.
- V has finite index in E_o, so V cap Sp~ has finite index in Sp~ and contains z^2 = T^10. QED

### 2.3 Theorem A

**Theorem A.** (a) For every homomorphism pi from M_2 to a finite group, Z_pi = pi(I_2)/pi([M_2,I_2]) is cyclic of order dividing 10.
(b) For every k >= 1, Kasahara's truncation has Z_{phi(k)} = Z/10. Equivalently, d^(F_{k+1}) = 10Z.

*Proof of (a).* Let V be the image of Ker pi in E. It has finite index. The modular law gives I_2 cap (Ker pi)[M_2,I_2] = (I_2 cap Ker pi)[M_2,I_2], hence Z_pi = <T>/(<T> cap V). Lemma 2.2 finishes the proof.

*Proof of (b).* The bound 10 | m_k: on F_2 = Ker tau_2, Morita's formula reads lambda(M_phi) = -d^(phi)/10 [read-casson, build-identity], and lambda is an integer. So d^(F_{k+1}) is contained in d^(F_2), which is contained in 10Z. (This is Casson integrality; no computer is needed.)

The bound m_k | 10 takes three steps.
- The ring map Z[t^{+-1}]/(t+1)^{k+1} = Z[s]/(s^{k+1}) -> Q[h]/(h^{k+1}), s -> 1 - e^h, is injective. So on M_2, Ker phi(k) equals the kernel of rho mod (t+1)^{k+1}. Hence P_k := I_2/F_{k+1} is a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group of unipotent matrices in GL_{5(k+1)}(Z). It has finite index in U(Z), where U is its Zariski closure (Malcev: both are lattices in U(R)).
- Let C_k be the image of [M_2,I_2]. Then P_k/C_k = Z_{phi(k)} is finite. read-kasahara deduced this from the addendum. Independently: if P_k/C_k were Z, each index-n overgroup of C_k would be a congruence subgroup by the next step, giving a finite quotient with |Z_pi| >= n and contradicting (a).
- Chevalley's congruence subgroup property for unipotent arithmetic groups [LIT, standard]: C_k contains P_k cap Gamma(n) for some n. The finite quotient pi_n := rho mod (n, (t+1)^{k+1}) then satisfies I_2 cap Ker pi_n contained in F_{k+1}[M_2,I_2]. So Z_{pi_n} = Z_{phi(k)}, and (a) gives m_k | 10. QED

**Remarks.**
- (b) contains Kasahara's Remark 6.3(3), Z_{phi(1)} = Z_{phi(2)} = Z/10, and settles every higher truncation. read-kasahara's "m_k unbounded would prove Z_rho = Z" never happens.
- His "coincidence" with H_1(M_2) = Z/10 has a reason. Both 10s come from lambda_1 having order 10 on M_2: H^2(M_2;Z) = Ext(H_1 M_2, Z) = Z/10. The factor 2 in Lemma 2.2 is Deligne's |mu(Q)|.
- (a) is a statement about M_2 alone, for any finite quotient: the Torelli coinvariant Z is profinitely visible only mod 10. I have not seen it stated. It follows quickly from Deligne, so it may be known to experts.

### 2.4 Lemma B and a dichotomy [DER]

**Lemma B.** If sigma(M_2)/sigma(I_2) is finite, then |Z_sigma| divides 10.

*Proof.* Let V be the image of Ker sigma in E. It maps onto a finite-index subgroup Lambda of Sp(4,Z).
- If V cap <T> = 1, then V is a homomorphic section of E over Lambda, so eps restricted to Lambda is 0. That is impossible: restriction to finite index is injective on H^2( ; Q), and eps has infinite order.
- So V cap <T> = <T^m> with m >= 1. Then E/V is an extension of the finite group sigma(M_2)/sigma(I_2) by Z/m, hence finite. So V has finite index, and Lemma 2.2 applies. QED

**Application to roots of unity.** Let t = zeta be a root of unity with q != -1 (equivalently delta != 0). The image of a nonseparating twist (a half-twist in TL form, A + A^{-1} e_i) is diagonalisable with eigenvalues A and -A^{-3}, both roots of unity. So it has finite order n. The normal closure of the n-th power of a transvection has finite index in Sp(4,Z) (Bass-Milnor-Serre). So Lemma B applies: |Z_{rho_zeta}| divides 10. This holds in the unitary (TQFT) cases, the finite-image cases (Jones) and the dense-image cases (Freedman-Larsen-Wang) alike. At the five points with q = -1, I_2 acts by scalars in mu_5 (read-kasahara), so Z is 1 or Z/5.

**Dichotomy.** By the Margulis normal subgroup theorem for Sp(4,Z), the image of Ker sigma in Sp(4,Z) is central or of finite index. So for every representation sigma of M_2, either Ker sigma is contained in I_2 <iota>, or |Z_sigma| divides 10. Any witness to Z_rho = Z through a specialisation must therefore come from a point t_0 where rho_{t_0} remembers all of Sp(4,Z): t_0 not a root of unity. At a transcendental t_0 the specialisation is isomorphic to rho itself, and nothing is gained.

**Finite products.** For a finite list of roots of unity, the product of the rho_zeta still has twists of finite order, so Lemma B applies to it too. Laurent polynomials vanishing at infinitely many roots of unity are zero, so the intersection of all Ker rho_zeta equals Ker rho. Thus Z_rho is again a non-commuting limit: every finite stage gives at most Z/10, while the limit is Z_rho itself.

## 3. The four candidate mechanisms, weighed

### (i) h-adic logarithm and a Morita-style secondary construction: closed

*Why it looked plausible.* For x in I_2, phi(x) = I + O(h), so log phi(x) lies in h gl_5[[h]], and BCH makes phi(I_2) a subgroup of a pro-unipotent group. An Sp-invariant additive functional on its Lie algebra would be an invariant homomorphism. Morita's d is not a function of tau_2. It is a secondary class: the transgression of the relation delta d = c_k + 3 tau between two cocycles for the same class. The hope was that a "next-order" analogue built from the h-adic coefficients could do the same for rho.

*Why it fails.*
- **Continuous version.** The graded pieces G_kF_Q lie in Gamma_{0,2} + Gamma_{2,0} = sl_5 and never contain Gamma_{0,0} (Kasahara's addendum). So no h-adically continuous invariant character exists.
- **Discontinuous "limit" version.** This would need m_k unbounded, and Theorem A(b) gives m_k = 10 for every k.
- **Morita's actual mechanism.** Its analogue for rho is a transgression in H^2 of the image group, and that is exactly Proposition 2. It asks whether lambda_1 dies on rho(M_2); it does not supply a functional.

*Precise obstruction.* A functional of finitely many h-adic coefficients of rho(x) recovers d^ at most mod 10. Mod 10 it is already known: Casson integrality gives d^ = -10 lambda + 10 q(tau_2) with 10 q(tau_2) integer-valued, so d^ mod 10 = 10 q(tau_2) mod 10. **So the whole residual content of rho about d is Casson integrality.**

### (ii) Scalar data on separating twists: an exact equivalent, not a route

Proposition 1 shows that the scalar t^6 carried by each separating twist is exactly the data d^ needs. It can be read off rho(x) iff no product of spin-3/2 pseudo-reflections R_c is a nontrivial scalar.
- At t^30 = 1 and at t = i the scalar separates, giving d^ mod 5 and d^ mod 2.
- At generic t nothing forbids a scalar. Finite complex reflection groups often contain -I, and the Burau image contains the scalar image of the full twist.
- A scalar t^{-6m} I in R(I_2) would have 10 | m, and its element kappa would give a Jones-trivial 3-bridge knot with sigma = -4m/5, a multiple of -8.
- Tools that see the group but not the scalar are blind here. In the Bruhat-Tits building of PGL_5(Q((t))) scalars act trivially; the extended building records only v(det), which is -30 d^ on both sides of the equation.

So (ii) gives the cleanest formulation of the infinite side: **no spin-3/2 pseudo-reflection word equals t^{-60k} I with k != 0**. It does not give a proof.

### (iii) Roots of unity (unitary, finite image or dense): closed

By Lemma B every root-of-unity specialisation with q != -1 has |Z| dividing 10. At q = -1 one gets Z = 1 or Z/5. The same holds for any finite product of specialisations. The TQFT and Freedman-Larsen-Wang density cases fall under this too, because a nonseparating twist has finite order there, so the symplectic quotient is finite. The dichotomy of Section 2.4 says a witness must sit at a point that is not a root of unity.

### (iv) Four-dimensional and eta readings: they route back to t = -1

- **Signature.** By build-identity's identity (*), Z_rho = Z iff sigma(K_phi) is a function of rho(phi) on I_2. Wall non-additivity for the double branched cover of B^4 gives sigma(K_{phi psi}) - sigma(K_phi) - sigma(K_psi) = Maslov(L_0, phi L_0, phi psi L_0), a Gambaudo-Ghys-type formula for plats. This term is computed at t = -1 from the symplectic action, and it vanishes on I_2 [SKETCH; it agrees with read-casson's claim that sigma is a homomorphism on I_2]. The invariant part of the homomorphism sigma(K_.) is -(4/5) d^, which is Meyer's function. So the 4-dimensional reading returns to symplectic data at t = -1, where rho(I_2) = 1. It does not touch rho at generic t.
- **Eta invariants.** Meyer's function in genus 2 is an adiabatic eta invariant of the hyperelliptic family (Atiyah, Morifuji, Iida) [LIT, recalled, not re-read]. Again it is a function of x, not of rho(x).
- **Hermitian (Toledo) lifts at |t| = 1 [SKETCH].** For |t| = 1, rho_t preserves the Jones-Wenzl Hermitian form h_t.
  - Since H^1(M_2;Z) = 0 and H^2(M_2;Z) = Z/10, rho_t lifts uniquely to the universal cover of U(h_t), pushed out by (1/10)Z. On a kernel element kappa the lift is a central element w_t(kappa). It is locally constant in t on any arc where h_t stays nondegenerate: relation values are continuous and lie in a discrete fibre.
  - At t = -1 the image lies, up to finite index, in the real group SO_0(3,2) (Lambda^2 H/omega). The inclusion SO_0(3,2) -> SU(3,2) is zero on pi_1: the SO(3) and SO(2) blocks have complex determinant 1. So on a finite-index subgroup M'' the lift equals (a section) o rho_{-1} times a homomorphism M'' -> Q. On I_2 that homomorphism is c d^, and d^ does not extend to any finite-index subgroup, because its transgression restricts nontrivially. Hence c = 0.
  - Therefore w_t(kappa) = 0 on the whole signature arc through t = -1, whatever d^(kappa) is. **The Hermitian Toledo lift is blind to d^.** The nonzero Meyer winding lives in the real form SO(3,2), and that form exists only at t = -1, where rho(I_2) = 1. For real t != +-1 there is no invariant form, and SL_5(R) has finite pi_1.
  - On arcs away from q = +-1, w_t restricted to N is some invariant homomorphism with no known link to d^. At a degeneration endpoint whose nondegenerate quotient has finite image, the same continuity argument gives w = 0 on N.
  - Assumptions not checked: that the normalised invariant forms h_t converge to the (3,2) form at t = -1, and that they stay nondegenerate on an arc around it.

*Verdict on (iv).* Every archimedean invariant I can attach to rho is either anchored at t = -1 through the real form (Meyer, eta, signature), and so is a function of x rather than of rho(x), or is a Hermitian Toledo winding that provably kills N for trivial reasons. **I found no archimedean invariant that both sees d^ and factors through rho.**

## 4. The named crux: what d needs that rho might not carry

### 4.1 An archimedean formula for d^ [SKETCH; standard ingredients]

Mess: Torelli space T_2 is the Siegel space H_2 minus the reducible locus Z. Z is the Sp(4,Z)-orbit of H_1 x H_1, with one component D_s for each splitting s. I_2 = pi_1(T_2) is free on meridians of the D_s. Igusa's cusp form chi_10 = chi_5^2 vanishes to order exactly 2 along each D_s and nowhere else on H_2; here chi_5 is the product of the ten even theta constants. So

    d^(x) = (1/2 pi i) * (integral over the period loop of x of d log chi_5).

For the curve y^2 = f(x), deg f = 6, take a braid beta of the branch points over x-bar, and let A be the a-period matrix of (dx/y, x dx/y). Igusa's discriminant formula gives chi_10 = kappa Disc(f) (det A)^{10}. The exponent is forced by scale invariance: under x -> lambda x, Disc scales by lambda^30 and det A by lambda^{-3}. The winding of Disc along beta is the exponent sum w(beta). Hence

    d^(x) = w(beta)/2 + 5 * wind_beta(det A).

- Check: the full twist Delta^2 is trivial in Mod(S_{0,6}). It has w = 30, and det A winds by -3 under the 2 pi rotation, so the formula gives 15 - 15 = 0. ✓
- Forced value: T_c = (s1 s2)^6 has w = 12, so the formula forces wind(det A) = -1.

### 4.2 The crux

The two terms behave differently with respect to rho.
- **The w(beta)/2 term.** rho sees w(beta) only through Jones's scalar correction, so only mod 10, which is H_1(M_2).
- **The wind(det A) term.** det A is the period of the top Hodge line Lambda^2 H^{1,0}. That line sits inside the t = -1 specialisation of rho, which is Lambda^2 H/omega. wind(det A) is the winding of this holomorphic sub-line along the loop, measured against the flat trivialisation. On I_2 the t = -1 monodromy is the identity. So this is Hodge data (it is lambda_1) and not monodromy data. Equivalently, it is the pi_1(SO_0(3,2)) coordinate of the real canonical lift, which is Meyer's function.

**Crux.** For every kappa in Ker rho cap I_2, show that wind_kappa(det A) = -w(beta_kappa)/10, i.e. d^(kappa) = 0. Equivalent forms:
- no word in the spin-3/2 pseudo-reflections equals t^{-60k} I with k != 0 (Proposition 1);
- lambda_1 inflates to 0 in H^2(rho(M_2); Q) (Proposition 2);
- the Jones-trivial knots K_kappa all have signature 0 (build-identity (*)).

Constraints on any proof:
- It must be non-residual (Theorem A, Lemma B).
- It must work at a point that is not a root of unity.
- It cannot go through the Hermitian Toledo lift (Section 3(iv)).
- It has to relate flat data (the KZ/Jones monodromy at generic q) to non-flat data (the Hodge line at q = -1).

### 4.3 Non-residual inputs that would suffice (none available)

- Faithfulness of rho on I_2, equivalently of the [2,2,1] representation of B_5 (Kasahara 2008). Then N = 1.
- T itself (build-identity (d)).
- A computation of H^2(rho(M_2); Q). For example: rho(M_2) has the same rational H_2 as Mod(S_{0,6}), namely 0.
- A degeneration theorem carrying a Hodge-line winding from the Gauss-Manin system at q = -1 to the KZ monodromy at generic q, via Kohno-Drinfeld. I know of no mechanism of this kind.

**Status.** The infinite side is open. It follows from faithfulness of rho on I_2 and from T, and it is a necessary condition for T. It does not imply T.

## 6. Caveats and what was not done

- **Normalisations recalled, not re-read**: c = +-lambda_1 mod torsion, lambda_1 generating the free part, and H^2(Sp(4,Z);Q) of dimension 1. If instead c = 2 lambda_1 mod torsion, Lemma 2.2 gives T^20, (a) becomes "divides 20", and (b) gives m_k in {10, 20}. Kasahara's m_1 = m_2 = 10 stand, and the qualitative conclusion (bounded, so residual methods cannot decide) is unchanged. Lemma B's finiteness part needs only that eps has infinite order.
- Section 3(iv) and Section 4.1 are sketches. Unchecked: the limit of the normalised Hermitian forms at t = -1, the signature on the arc through t = -1, and Igusa's constants. None of these affects Theorem A or Lemma B.
- No computer check was run. The algebraic identities used (E^2 = (1 - t^15)E; R_c at t^30 = 1 and at t = i; the scaling exponents) are one-line hand checks.
- Kasahara's q = i paper was not read. The reading of his 2-part as "-I is not in the reflection group R_i(I_2)" is mine.
