# read-casson: Morita's core, Mullins, and the signature route to Z_rho

Label: read-casson. Date 2026-11-03. Sources were read in full text (pdftotext of the arXiv PDFs that WebFetch saved) unless marked "secondary".

Strength tags: [LIT] stated in the literature, with a pointer. [DERIVED] follows from [LIT] statements by a short argument written out here. [CHECK] verified numerically on explicit examples. [CLAIM] a new observation with a proof sketch that has not been audited.

---

## 0. Summary of corrections to the observation under test

1. In genus two the right object is not Morita's bounded-surface core d, normalised by d(T) = 4h(h-1), with weight 1/24. That d lives on K_{g,1}. For a separating curve of type (1,1) on the closed surface it is not even well defined, since 4h(h-1) is not symmetric under h <-> g-h. The closed-surface core is the Faes-Massuyeau / Morita combination d^ with d^(T_gamma) = h(g-h). In genus two d^(T_c) = 1 for every separating twist, and its Casson weight is 1/(2(2g+1)) = 1/10, not 1/24.
2. The tau_2 term in Morita's formula is a homomorphism of K_g, i.e. linear in tau_2(phi). It is "quadratic" only in the sense that it is built from products of linking numbers. It depends on the Heegaard embedding.
3. The Meyer cocycle vanishes on I_2 x I_2. The Meyer function phi_2 on M_2 (Endo) does NOT vanish on I_2: restricted to I_2 it is the invariant homomorphism -(4/5) d^.
4. The corrected identity is: 5 sigma(K_phi) + 4 eps d^(phi) is a function of rho(phi) on I_2, where eps = +-1 is a global gluing/orientation convention. It replaces "d - 3 sigma". The equivalences in the observation then hold as stated [DERIVED]. There is also a new by-product: T implies Z_rho = Z directly, with no appeal to Ito or to the normal-intersection lemma.

## 1. Morita's core of the Casson invariant

### 1.1 Bounded surface (Morita, Topology 28 (1989), Topology 29 (1990), Topology 30 (1991)) [LIT]
Source: Massuyeau-Meilhan, arXiv:1104.2763, Section 7.1, which reviews Morita's [51,53] and gives the sign conversions.

- Setup: j: Sigma_{g,1} -> S^3 is a Heegaard embedding. For phi in K_{g,1} = M_{g,1}[2], lambda_j(phi) is the Casson invariant of S^3 cut along j(Sigma) and reglued by phi. Morita proved that lambda_j restricted to K is a homomorphism.
- Morita's twist formula (7.1): lambda_j(T_gamma) = eps_j o theta(omega_gamma (x) omega_gamma). Here omega_gamma = sum u_i ^ v_i is the symplectic form of the genus-h subsurface bounded by gamma, and theta takes values in an algebra of symbols l(u,v), which eps_j evaluates as linking numbers lk(u, v^+) in S^3.
- The bounded core. Let k: M -> H be the Earle-Morita crossed homomorphism, c_k(phi,psi) = omega(k(phi^{-1}), k(psi)) the cocycle representing e_1, and tau the Meyer cocycle pulled back from Sp, so that [-3 tau] = e_1. Then d_k is the unique stable 1-cochain with delta d_k = c_k + 3 tau. Because k vanishes on K, d := d_k|_K is a homomorphism K -> Z that does not depend on k.
- Value (7.2): d(T_gamma) = 4h(h-1) for gamma bounding genus h, the side away from the boundary. So d takes values in 8Z.
- Decomposition (7.3), in Massuyeau-Meilhan's sign convention: -lambda_j = d/24 + q_j on K, with q_j = -(eps_j o theta + d''/3) o tau_2. Here d'' is the explicit omega-contraction on Lambda^2 H (x)_S Lambda^2 H, which kills (u^v)^{(x)2}. Their lambda_j equals -lambda*_j in Morita's papers, and their tau_k equals -tau_{k+1}^{Morita}. So in Morita's own convention: lambda* = d/24 + (tau_2-term). [LIT]
- On M(3) = Ker tau_2: lambda* = d/24. This is why Morita calls d the core. [LIT; restated in Morita-Sakasai-Suzuki arXiv:1711.07855, Thm 7.1]
- Example in Massuyeau-Meilhan (proof of Prop 6.3): psi = T_{gamma_0}^{-3} prod T_{gamma_i}^{-+1} in M(3) with d(psi) = -24, so lambda_j(psi) = 1.

### 1.2 Closed surface [LIT]
- Faes-Massuyeau, arXiv:2209.12740, Section 3.1. Morita has two invariant homomorphisms on K_{g,1}: the core d with d(T_gamma) = 4h(h-1), and d' with d'(T_gamma) = h(2h+1). d' is defined from tau_2.
- Morita [Topology 30 (1991) 603-621, Thm 5.7] proved that d^ := -((1+2g)/12) d + ((g-1)/3) d' kills Ker(K_{g,1} -> K_g). It therefore descends to an M_g-invariant homomorphism d^: K_g -> Z with **d^(T_gamma) = h(g-h)**.
  - Arithmetic check [CHECK by hand]: -((1+2g)/12)·4h(h-1) + ((g-1)/3)·h(2h+1) = (h/3)(3g-3h) = h(g-h).
- H^1(K_g; Z)^{M_g} = Z for g >= 2, rationally generated by the class called d_1. It is characterised by T_gamma -> h(g-h) up to a nonzero constant. [LIT: Morita-Sakasai-Suzuki 1711.07855, Intro, citing Morita Topology 30]
- Kasahara 2001 Section 6 writes the closed formula as lambda_f = -d_1/24 + q_f, with q_f factoring through tau_g(2). Uniqueness of an invariant Q-valued homomorphism up to scalar is [14, Thm 5.7]. The constant hidden in d_1 is not pinned down there.

### 1.3 Genus two, exact constants [DERIVED]
- Substitute d = -(12/(2g+1)) d^ + (4(g-1)/(2g+1)) d' into lambda* = d/24 + q (Morita convention). This gives
  **lambda*(phi) = -d^(phi)/(2(2g+1)) + B(phi)**, where B = (g-1)/(6(2g+1)) d' + q is a homomorphism factoring through tau_2. It descends to the closed surface because lambda* and d^ do. For B to factor through closed tau_2 one also needs M_{g,1}(3) -> M_g(3) to be onto, or equivalently MSS Thm 7.1 on M_g(3); MSS state this for closed surfaces.
- **g = 2: lambda(M_phi) = -eps d^(phi)/10 + q(tau_2(phi)) for phi in I_2 = K_2**, where eps = +1 in Morita's convention (lambda* = lambda(M_phi)). On Ker tau_2 this is lambda = -eps d^/10.
- Consistency check with Kasahara [DERIVED]. Casson integrality gives d^(Ker tau_2 ∩ I_2) ⊂ 10Z. Kasahara's computation Z_{tau_2} = Z/10 says exactly d^(Ker tau_2) = 10Z, since Z_sigma = I_2/([M_2,I_2]·Ker sigma) = Z/d^(Ker sigma). So the "10" Kasahara found by computer is the denominator 2(2g+1) of the Casson weight. It also matches H_1(M_2) = Z/(4g+2) = Z/10.
- Value on a separating twist: in genus two every separating curve has type (1,1), and M_2 acts transitively on them. So **d^(T_c) = 1** and Morita's bounded d gives 4·1·0 = 0 when the genus-1 side is away from the boundary. This is exactly why the bounded normalisation is the wrong one here.

### 1.4 d^ spans the invariant homomorphisms; Ker d^ = [M_2, I_2] [DERIVED, elementary]
- I_2 is generated by separating twists (Mess: free on a set of them), and these are all M_2-conjugate. An M_2-invariant homomorphism f: I_2 -> Q is therefore determined by f(T_c). So Hom(I_2,Q)^{M_2} = Q·d^.
- I_2/[M_2,I_2] is cyclic, generated by the class of T_c. d^ is onto Z with d^(T_c) = 1, so d^ induces I_2/[M_2,I_2] ≅ Z and Ker d^ = [M_2,I_2]. Kasahara says "easily see"; this is the reason.
- Concretely, **d^ = exponent sum in Mess's free basis**, because every basis element is a separating twist with d^ = 1.
- Hence **Z_rho ≅ Z / d^(Ker rho ∩ I_2)**. If finite, its order generates d^(Ker rho ∩ I_2), which lies in d^(Ker tau_2) = 10Z. So the order is a multiple of 10. This divisibility is already implicit in Kasahara: Z_rho surjects onto Z_{tau_2} = Z/10.

### 1.5 Signature-defect interpretation [LIT]
Morita, J. Differential Geom. 47 (1997), "Casson invariant, signature defect of framed manifolds...": d(h) is the signature defect of the mapping torus of h with a 2-framing (cited in 1104.2763, Intro). I did not read it.

## 2. Mullins's formula

**lambda(Sigma_2 K) = sigma(K)/8 - V_K'(-1) / (12 V_K(-1))**, Casson normalisation. Equivalently, in Walker's normalisation lambda_W = 2 lambda: lambda_W = sigma/4 - V'(-1)/(6 V(-1)).
- [LIT, secondary] Eisermann arXiv:0802.2287 quotes Mullins Thm 5.1 as lambda(Sigma^2_K) = (1/4) sign(K) - (1/6) V'(-1)/V(-1) in Walker normalisation. It is valid for any link with det ≠ 0. Garoufalidis extended it to all links via Lescop.
- Conventions: V(right trefoil) = t + t^3 - t^4, sigma(right trefoil) = -2, sigma(positive torus knots) < 0, lambda(Sigma(2,3,5)) = -1 with Sigma(2,3,5) = Sigma_2(T(3,5)) oriented as the link of a singularity.
- [CHECK] I computed V from the torus-knot formula in exact arithmetic.
  - T(3,5), T(3,7), T(3,11), T(3,13): V(-1) = 1 and V'(-1) = 0. So lambda = sigma/8 = -1, -1, -2, -2, which matches lambda(Sigma(2,3,6n±1)) = -n.
  - Right trefoil: V'(-1)/V(-1) = -8/3, and the formula gives -1/4 + 2/9 = -1/36. This matches lambda(S^3_{-3/2}(U)) = -s(-2,3)/2 = -1/36, Boyer-Lines convention lambda(L(p,q)) = -s(q,p)/2.
  - T(2,5): the ratio is -24/5 and the formula gives -1/2 + 2/5 = -1/10. This matches -s(-4,5)/2 = -1/10.
  - These checks fix both signs. The opposite relative sign would give 17/36 for the trefoil.
- For V = 1: lambda(Sigma_2 K) = sigma(K)/8.

## 3. Literature linking Casson to Jones's genus-two representation

- [LIT] Kasahara, AGT 1 (2001), arXiv:math/0012216, Section 6.
  - Cor 1.2: tau_2(2) factors through rho (Ker of the first expanded quotient = F_2).
  - Therefore q_f factors through rho, and "rho contains the full information of lambda" in genus 2 iff it contains d_1.
  - Prop 6.1: d_1 factors through rho iff Z_rho ≅ Z.
  - Problem 6.2 asks for the order of Z_rho.
  - Rem 6.3: finite order implies rho is unfaithful on I_2. The order is >= 10 by Kasahara's 4th-root-of-unity preprint. Z_{phi(1)} = Z_{tau_2} = Z/10 and Z_{phi(2)} = Z/10 by computer. The paper says that order exactly 10 "remains possible".
- Mullins (Topology 32 (1993)) is the bridge from Casson to Jones, but only through Sigma_2 and the 3-bridge plat picture.
- I found no paper combining Mullins with Morita on I_2, and no plat analogue of Gambaudo-Ghys (search only; not exhaustive).

## 4. The corrected identity and the Z_rho equivalences

Setup:
- Write M_phi = H ∪_{iota_0 phi} H.
- By Birman-Hilden, M_phi = Sigma_2(K_phi), where K_phi = cap_{P0} o beta_phi o cup_{Q0} is the plat closure of a lift of phi to Mod(S_{0,6}). P0 and Q0 are dual matchings with cap_{P0} o cup_{Q0} = unknot.
- K_phi is well defined from phi, since the lift is unique up to iota.

**Lemma A (V is a function of rho(phi)) [DERIVED].**
- Statement: let P(t) = <v0, rho(phi) v0> be the bracket-type matrix coefficient on W(6,0), where v0 is the cup vector, using the TL form. Then V_{K_phi} = ±t^k P with ±t^k fixed by V(1) = 1 and V'(1) = 0. Hence V'(-1)/V(-1) = P'(-1)/P(-1) + P'(1)/P(1), a function of rho(phi). Fractional powers of t need a fixed branch.
- Reason: the writhe and lift ambiguities change P only by a unit ±t^{k/2}, and the knot normalisation V(1) = 1, V'(1) = 0 removes any unit.
- Assumption to check: V'(1) = 0 for knots is standard. Jones's normalisation of rho is irrelevant to the argument.

**Identity [DERIVED from 1.3, 2 and Lemma A].** For phi in I_2:
sigma(K_phi)/8 = -eps d^(phi)/10 + q(tau_2 phi) + V'(-1)/(12V(-1)), so
**5 sigma(K_phi) + 4 eps d^(phi) = G(rho(phi))**, with G(rho) = 40 q(tau_2) + (10/3) V'(-1)/V(-1). Here tau_2 is a function of rho by Kasahara Cor 1.2.

**Consequences [DERIVED].**
- (a) For kappa in N := Ker rho ∩ I_2 we have rho(kappa) = 1, so P = P(1) and V_{K_kappa} = 1. Also tau_2(kappa) = 0. Then lambda(M_kappa) = sigma(K_kappa)/8 = -eps d^(kappa)/10, so **sigma(K_kappa) = -(4 eps/5) d^(kappa)**.
- (b) Z_rho finite <=> d^(N) ≠ 0 <=> some Jones-trivial 3-bridge knot K_kappa, kappa in N, has sigma ≠ 0. Then |Z_rho| = (5/4)·gcd of |sigma(K_kappa)|, a multiple of 10. "Order exactly 10" <=> some such K_kappa has |sigma| = 8, i.e. lambda(Sigma_2 K_kappa) = ±1.
- (c) Z_rho infinite <=> d^ factors through rho on I_2 <=> sigma(K_phi) is a function of rho(phi) on I_2. For <=, note that d^ = (G(rho) - 5 sigma)/(4 eps) then kills N.
- (d) Direct route: **T => Z_rho ≅ Z**. If T holds, every K_kappa with kappa in N is the unknot, so sigma = 0, so d^(N) = 0. This avoids Ito and the normal-intersection lemma. Conversely, finiteness of Z_rho produces a V = 1 knot whose nontriviality is certified by its signature (it is even non-slice), with no need for Ito's argument.
- (e) Example. Sigma_2(T(3,6n+1)) = Sigma(2,3,6n+1) arises from T_{c'}^n for a suitable separating c' (two full twists on 3 strands lift to T_c). There lambda = -n, d^ = n, sigma = -8n and V'(-1) = 0. No contradiction: this fixes q(tau_2(T_{c'})) = -1 + eps/10 for that particular c'.

## 5. Reading sigma(K_phi) from plat / Heegaard data

- [LIT] Gordon-Litherland: sigma(K) = sign(G_F) - mu(F) for a checkerboard surface F. Here G_F is the Goeritz form, which is the intersection form of Sigma_2(B^4, F pushed in), and mu(F) = -e(F)/2 is a crossing-type correction. For a plat diagram, G_F is read off the checkerboard colouring of the plat word. Kauffman-Taylor / Viro: for any orientable F pushed in, sigma(K) = sign Sigma_2(B^4, F).
- [LIT] Gambaudo-Ghys (Bull SMF 133 (2005)): for closed braids, sigma(closure(ab)) - sigma(closure(a)) - sigma(closure(b)) = -Meyer(Burau_{-1}(a), Burau_{-1}(b)). Hence sigma is a homomorphism on the kernel of the symplectic Burau_{-1}.
- [LIT, secondary: Endo, Math. Ann. 316 (2000), via search] The hyperelliptic Meyer function phi_g: Delta_g -> (1/(2g+1))Z, with delta phi_g = Meyer cocycle, takes the values phi(t_nonsep) = (g+1)/(2g+1) and phi(t_sep,h) = -4h(g-h)/(2g+1). These agree with Matsumoto's local signatures -3/5 and -1/5 in genus 2. Sign conventions vary; check Endo's original.
- [DERIVED] In genus two M_2 = Delta_2. The Meyer cocycle tau(A,B) vanishes when A = I, so tau = 0 on I_2 x I_2, and phi_2|_{I_2} is a homomorphism. It is conjugation-invariant: phi_2 o conj also cobounds tau, and H^1(M_2;Q) = 0. Hence **phi_2|_{I_2} = phi_2(T_c)·d^ = -(4/5) d^**. The identity of Section 4 becomes, up to convention signs, sigma(K_phi) = eps' phi_2(phi) + (function of rho(phi)) on I_2. So the Meyer function, not the cocycle, carries the d^ part.
- **[CLAIM, new, unaudited] sigma(K_.) is a homomorphism I_2 -> Z.**
  - Sketch: for phi, psi in I_2, write K_{phi psi} = cap_{P0} beta_phi beta_psi cup_{Q0}. Insert cup_{P0} o cap_{P0} at the middle level by 3 saddles along the P0 arcs. This gives an orientable cobordism C (Euler characteristic -3) to L_phi ⊔ K_psi.
  - Orientability: the lifts of phi and psi are pure, since I_2 sits in level 2, which is the preimage of PMod(S_{0,6}). So each P0-pair is oppositely oriented and the bands are coherent.
  - Viro additivity: sigma(K_{phi psi}) = sigma(L_phi) + sigma(K_psi) + sign(W), where W = Sigma_2(S^3 x I, C) is M_{phi psi} x I plus three 2-handles. They are attached along the lifts m_i of the P0 arcs, with surface framing.
  - The classes [m_i] lie in the Lagrangian of the upper handlebody, and phi is Torelli. So m_i bounds a surface in the upper part and every framed linking number lk(m_i, m_j^-) is 0. Since M_{phi psi} is a homology sphere, sign(W) = 0.
  - Setting psi = 1 gives sigma(L_phi) = sigma(K_phi), hence additivity.
  - Points to audit: the band-lift framing equals the surface framing, and the induced orientation on L_phi does not depend on psi.
  - Consequences if true: by Mullins, V'(-1)/V(-1) is also a homomorphism on I_2. sigma|_{I_2} is not M_2-invariant: it vanishes on both handlebody-Torelli subgroups but equals -8n on T_{c'}^n. So sigma is an embedding-dependent homomorphism, like q_j, and on N it coincides with the invariant -(4 eps/5) d^.
- Precise obstruction to closing T this way: proving Z_rho infinite needs sigma(K_kappa) = 0 for kappa in N. Neither the additivity claim nor the Meyer-function picture gives this. Both show sigma = (invariant part -(4/5) d^) + (embedding-dependent part). On N, the embedding-dependent part, 8q(tau_2) + (2/3)V'/V, vanishes because rho(kappa) = 1. So the question is exactly whether d^ vanishes on N, i.e. Kasahara's Problem 6.2 restated, with no new leverage unless one has an independent reason that Jones-trivial 3-bridge knots have sigma = 0.
