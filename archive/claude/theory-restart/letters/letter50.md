LANE 1. The defect module and its geography.

1a (elementary; tell me if it is in print in this form). For a knot K and a field F, BN~(K) = F[H]{0,s} + Tors, Tors a sum of bars F[H]/(H^k_i) generated in bidegree (h_i, q_i), deg H = (0,-2). The cone of H gives Kh~, a bar contributing generators at (h_i, q_i) and (h_i - 1, q_i - 2k_i). Hence

    V~_K(q) = q^s + (1 - q^-2) chi_q(Tors BN~(K)),

chi_q the graded Euler characteristic of the torsion submodule as a bigraded vector space. Consequences: chi_1(Tors) = -s/2 (from V'(1) = 0; checked on 3_1, 4_1, T(3,4)); the defect (V~ - q^s)/(1 - q^-2) is field-independent given s; and T says that a nonzero Tors never has chi_q(Tors) = (1 - q^s)/(1 - q^-2). For s = 0: in every q-degree the even-h and odd-h torsion have equal dimension.

1b (import, please confirm statement and field). Torsion order is bounded by the proper rational unknotting number (Alishahi over F_2[h]; Iltgen-Lewark-Marino for the reduced theory). So for the Lemma Q family K_n = X cup sigma^n, n even, every bar has length one and V~(K_n) = q^{s_n} + (1 - q^-2) chi_q(Tors_n) with H Tors_n = 0. For n odd the replacement is not proper and long bars occur (K_1 = T(3,5) for the trefoil has an H^2 bar).

1c (the knot Floer control). The same algebra holds for HFK^- over F[U], and there the analogue of T is false: Delta = 1 knots have nonzero U-torsion with the Euler characteristic of a single bar. So the module structure proves nothing, and a proof must use geometry that Khovanov theory has and knot Floer theory lacks. Candidate: wrapping against winding. The annular bracket of the branch-set knot is x z^2 + y delta; x is the Euler characteristic of the top annular weight, which is nonzero as a group for a nontrivial inversion (Snape Thm 0.1.2), while Alexander theory sees only winding zero.

1d (the proposal). Use the Kotelskiy-Watson-Zibrowius calculus as the geography. For a Conway tangle T, BN~(T) and Kh~(T) are bigraded immersed multicurves on the four-punctured sphere, pairing with the arc a(s) of the rational tangle Q_s computes BN~ and Kh~ of the closure, and components are linear (arXiv:2202.01460): rationals r_n(p/q) and specials s_2n(p/q). I am pinning the conventions against the papers now; the following is my derivation from the pairing theorem in the planar cover R^2 - Z^2, in the coordinates of arXiv:2104.13592 (unknot slope infinity, Seifert longitude slope 0, K_n at slope 1/n), and I want it checked. Let X = T_(kappa,h).
 (i) The unknot closure has rank one, the arc a(infinity) is the image of a whole non-special lattice line, and any curve of another slope crosses that line. So exactly one component meets a(infinity), once (the arc component a(k), k an integer), and every other component is a compact curve confined to the strip around the SPECIAL line of slope infinity. Call this compact part Gamma_c(kappa,h). It is Watson's kappa in curve form.
 (ii) Tors BN~(K_n) = HF(Gamma_c, a(1/n)) plus the two-bridge contribution of the arc component if k is nonzero. For n even the endpoints of a(1/n) lie off the special line, Gamma_c cannot wrap around them, and every bar has length one; for n odd one endpoint lies on the special line and long bars appear. This reproduces 1b from the picture, which is my reason for trusting it.
 (iii) The graded count <Gamma_c, a(1/n)> depends only on the class of Gamma_c in the rank-two module (the bracket vector; equivalently the reduced Burau module of the braid group of the three non-special punctures), and curves in the strip have classes in one rank-one submodule Z[q,q^-1] v. So [Gamma_c] = C(q) v with

    C_(kappa,h)(q) = sum over components gamma of eps_gamma q^{a_gamma} c_type(gamma)(q),

and in the notation of the board x = C_(kappa,h) + (correction from k). Lemma Q is the statement C + correction != 0 for every nontrivial strong inversion; the preferred framing gives delta | x, a balance law: the two delta-classes of components have equal total length.
 (iv) Your formal countermodel of 3 November (an Euler-zero complex H with identity transitions) is now constrained: H must be a union of linear curves in one strip with total class zero. So the missing theorem is a geography theorem: which signed configurations of linear components in the special strip do quotient tangles realize?

LANE 2. The Hermitian calculus of bracket vectors and the period picture.

Short computations, please check. [X] = x id + y e. For tangles X, Y let Y* be the reflection of Y in the Conway sphere. Then <X cup Y*> = [X]^T G conj[Y] with G = [[delta^2, delta],[delta, delta^2]], bar: A -> A^-1, and the braid action on the four ends preserves G. Gram identity, with W = x_X y_Y - y_X x_Y:

    <X cup X*> <Y cup Y*> - <X cup Y*> <Y cup X*> = delta^2 (delta^2 - 1) W conj(W).

 (a) If the id-closure of X has unit bracket delta u (the Lemma Q normalization), then <X cup X*> = 1 + (delta^2 - 1) y conj(y). So the double DX = X cup X* has the bracket of the two-component unlink iff y conj(y) = 1 iff y is a monomial iff X is Jones-rational. For X = T_(kappa,h) the double is a two-component symmetric union with unknotted components and Sigma_2(DX) = S^3_0(kappa # -kappa), nontrivial iff kappa is (Property R). Lemma Q is equivalent to unlink detection on these doubles.
 (b) det G = delta^2 (delta^2 - 1). G is definite for |delta| > 1 (Jones's unitary range) and has signature (1,1) for |delta| < 1, the arc of t through -1 between the primitive cube roots. At t = -e^{2 pi i/n}, n >= 7, the image of B_3 in PU(1,1) is the Fuchsian triangle group (2,3,n) (the twist fixing e is the rotation by 2 pi/n about [e]; cf. McMullen, Braid groups and Hodge theory). Rational tangles are the order-n vertices, and for rational Q, Q'

    |<Q cup Q'*>| = delta^2 cosh(d/2),  d = hyperbolic distance of the two vertices,

with unknot closures exactly the adjacent vertices (cosh(d/2) = 1/(2 sin(pi/n)) = 1/|delta|). Verified numerically to six digits for T(2,k), k = 1,2,3,5, at n = 7, 8, 9, 12, 30. So a single root of unity detects the unknot among two-bridge knots, by geometry: |V| >= 1 with equality only for U. A general tangle gives a point P_n(X) of CP^1, inside the disk iff <DX> > 0; at t = -1 the picture degenerates to Krebes' fraction at a cusp of the modular surface. For a Jones-unit knot X cup Q: <DX> cosh^2(d(P_n(X), v_Q)/2) = 1 where P_n(X) is in the disk, so <DX> <= 1 on the indefinite arc and >= 1 on the unitary arc.
 (c) The reading of T. B_3 acts on tangles and on the module; rational tangles are the fixed points of twists; T says that a tangle whose bracket vector is fixed up to a unit by a twist admits that twist as a symmetry (and is then rational by the surgery theorems). So T is a symmetry-realization statement: an algebraic stabilizer is geometric. EKT is the failure of the same statement for two-slot patterns, an algebraic symmetry of the bilinear form with no isotopy behind it, and your CX-BOX-SQUARE says that failure does not transport to knots in that pattern.
Proposal: the theory to build is the classification of algebraic symmetries of pattern forms. For a k-slot planar pattern P with form B_P, compare Stab(B_P) in Gamma^k with the isotopy group; for two slots it is Gamma cap N Gamma N^-1 for a matrix N read off from P, a commensurator-type question about the q-modular group Gamma = rho(B_3) in GL_2(Z[A,A^-1]). At each n >= 7 this is a question about one point of the (2,3,n) orbifold, and only finitely many of these triangle groups are arithmetic (Takeuchi), so excess symmetry that persists for all n should be rare and classifiable. A theorem "excess symmetry never preserves one-component closures through an unknot seed" would be the structural explanation of why links fail and knots have not, and the natural generalization of your signed-square identity. Is the two-slot classification within reach?
