- At A_0 = exp(pi i/4): delta = 0, G_0 = [[0,1],[1,0]], E_0 = [[0,1],[-1,0]], and with D = diag(1,i):

## 1. Is Gamma_n the Fuchsian triangle group (2,3,n)?  YES, for every n >= 7.

Signature. On |A| = 1, G is a real symmetric matrix with eigenvalues delta+1 and delta-1, so it is
indefinite iff |delta| < 1. At A_n, |delta| = 2 sin(pi/n) < 1 iff n >= 7. For n = 6 the form is
degenerate (delta = -1); for n = 2,...,5 it is definite and the image is the finite group
(2,3,n) (S_3, A_4, S_4, A_5).

Independent derivation (no source used). In PU(G_n) acting on one of the two discs of CP^1 cut out
by the null circle:
- [J] is elliptic of order 2 (eigenvalue ratio -1), [JM] is elliptic of order 3 (since
  (AJM)^3 = -I), and [M_n] is elliptic with eigenvalue ratio exp(-2 pi i/n), i.e. a rotation
  through exactly +-2pi/n (a PRIMITIVE rotation, not 2 pi k/n with k != +-1).
- Let p, q, r be the fixed points of [J], [JM], [M] = [J][JM] in the disc and L the geodesic pq.
  Write [J] = s_1 s_L, [JM] = s_L s_2 with s_1, s_2 reflections in lines through p, q. Then
  [M] = s_1 s_2, the lines meet at r, the triangle pqr has a right angle at p, hence acute angles
  at q and r, hence the angle at q is pi/3 (not 2pi/3) and the angle at r is half the rotation
  angle taken in (0, pi/2), i.e. pi/n.
- By Poincare's theorem the reflections in the sides of a (pi/2, pi/3, pi/n) triangle generate a
  discrete group with that triangle as strict fundamental domain; its rotation subgroup is
  <[J],[JM]> = Gamma_n = Delta(2,3,n) = <a,b | a^2 = b^3 = (ab)^n = 1>.

Numerical confirmation (s2_triangle.py, 50 digits, n = 7..60, 101, 200, 1001, both discs, odd and
even n): the triangle of fixed points has angles pi/2, pi/3, pi/n to 1e-48.

Sources, and what they actually say.
- The group is a Burau image: with P = diag(A^2,1), P(JMJ)P^-1 = A*[[-t,1],[0,1]] and
  P M P^-1 = A*[[1,0],[t,-t]] at t = A^-4 (s3_burau.py; exact). So projectively
  Gamma = reduced Burau image of B_3 at t = A^-4, and at A_n, t = -q with q = exp(2 pi i/n).
- Funar-Kohno, arXiv:0907.0568v4, Section 3 (TeX source read; numbering: Prop 3.3 = discreteness,
  Prop 3.4 = even order, Prop 3.5 = odd order, Lemma 3.4 = "oddl"). Their Gamma_{-q} is generated
  by beta_{-q}(g_1^2), beta_{-q}(g_2^2). Prop 3.3: Gamma_{-q} is discrete in PU(1,1) iff
  q = exp(+-2 pi i/n), n >= 7. Prop 3.4: for n = 2k it is Delta(k,k,k). Prop 3.5: for n = 2k+1,
  k >= 3, it is Delta(2,3,2k+1). Lemma 3.4: for odd n the pure-braid image equals the full image.
  So the citation "Lemma 3.4 / Proposition 3.5" is exactly right for odd n >= 7, and it is the
  reason the text restricts to odd n. For even n the full image of B_3 is still Delta(2,3,n)
  (it contains Delta(k,k,k) with index 6); Funar-Kohno just do not state it in that form.
- McMullen, Braid groups and Hodge theory, Math. Ann. 355 (2013), Section 5, "Examples: n = 3":
  "For n = 3 and p > 6, the image of A_{n-1}(-zeta_p) in PU(1,1) is simply the (2,3,p)-triangle
  group acting on the hyperbolic plane", with a = image of tau_1 tau_2 tau_1, b = image of
  tau_1 tau_2. All p > 6, no parity condition. (Read in the DASH author text.)
- Dlugie, AGT 24 (2024) 2787-2805 (arXiv:2210.06561), Theorem 5.4: for q a primitive d-th root of
  unity, d >= 7, ker beta_3(-q) = ncl(sigma^d) * <tau_3^l>, l = 2d/gcd(12,d+6). He states that this
  corrects Funar-Kohno's Theorem 1.2 "in the cases when d is a multiple of 3". The correction
  concerns only the central (scalar) part of the LINEAR kernel. Projectively it gives
  B_3/(ncl(sigma^d) Z(B_3)) = Delta(2,3,d) for all d >= 7, consistent with the above and
  unaffected by the error. Funar-Kohno's Props 3.3-3.5 are not touched by the correction.

Conclusion for (1): correct; the hypothesis needed is n >= 7 (indefinite form). Parity of n is
irrelevant to the group, only to which sentence of Funar-Kohno one quotes.

## 2. The commensurator

Orientation-preserving part. Comm^+(Gamma_n) taken in PU(G_n) = PSL(2,R).
- Margulis: for a lattice in PSL(2,R), non-arithmetic iff the commensurator is discrete, in which
  case it is a lattice containing Gamma with finite index (hypotheses: irreducible lattice in a
  connected semisimple group with trivial centre and no compact factor; satisfied).
- Takeuchi's criterion for Delta(2,3,n): arithmetic iff cos(2 pi k/n) < 1/2 for every k prime to
  n with k != +-1 mod n. Recomputed (s4_takeuchi.py, n <= 20000): arithmetic exactly for
  n in {7,8,9,10,11,12,14,16,18,24,30}; this is Takeuchi's list for (2,3,n). So n > 30 is
  sufficient; n = 13, 15, 17, 19-23, 25-29 are also non-arithmetic.
- Maximality, re-derived without Singerman's table (s5_areas.py): a Fuchsian group properly
  containing Delta(2,3,n) with finite index k >= 2 is cocompact with area/2pi <= (1/6-1/n)/2 < 1/12
  and has a period divisible by n >= 7. The only cocompact signatures with area/2pi < 1/12 are
  (2,3,7), (2,3,8), (2,3,9), (2,3,10), (2,3,11), (2,4,5); a period divisible by n forces (2,3,n)
  itself, i.e. k = 1. Hence Delta(2,3,n) is maximal for every n >= 7 (agrees with Singerman 1972
  and with Girondo-Gonzalez-Diez Section 2, read in the linked PDF: "les groupes triangulaires de
  signature (2,3,N) sont maximaux").
- Therefore Comm^+(Gamma_n) = Gamma_n for n >= 7 outside the eleven arithmetic values.

The full circle stabilizer.
- Stab_{PGL_2(C)}(circle) has two components: side-preserving (PU(G_n) = PSL(2,R) = Isom^+(H^2))
  and side-swapping. Identifying the two discs by the inversion iota in the circle
  ([v] -> G-orthogonal line), a side-swapping holomorphic map acts on H^2 as an
  orientation-REVERSING isometry. So the holomorphic stabilizer is PGL(2,R) = Isom(H^2).
- E_n is holomorphic (it is a matrix), and E*GE = (delta^2-1)G with delta^2-1 = 4 sin^2(pi/n) - 1 < 0
  for n >= 7, so it swaps the two discs: yes, it is a side-swapping holomorphic involution.
  EJE^-1 = -J sends the (+1)-eigenline of J to the (-1)-eigenline; these are G-orthogonal, one in
  each disc, so iota.E fixes p. E(JM)E^-1 ~ (JM)^-1 and the disc swap give the same for q. Hence on
  H^2, E_n is the reflection in the side pq of the triangle (checked numerically in s2: E p is
  G-orthogonal to p, E q to q, E r not to r). Consequently <Gamma_n, E_n> is the full reflection
  triangle group Delta*(2,3,n), with the triangle pqr as strict fundamental domain; the other two
  side reflections are E J and one of E.JM, JM.E.
- Commensurator in Isom(H^2): if g is in Comm and reverses orientation, then g E_n^-1 is in
  Comm^+ = Gamma_n (Comm is a group and E_n normalizes Gamma_n). So
      Comm_{Isom(H^2)}(Gamma_n) = Gamma_n u E_n Gamma_n = <Gamma_n, E_n>.
  This is the missing one-line justification of the text's sentence; with it, "<Gamma_n,E_n>" is
  the right answer for holomorphic circle-preserving maps.
- If anti-holomorphic circle-preserving maps are admitted as well, the group is Isom(H^2) x <iota>
  with iota central and acting trivially on H^2, and the commensurator is <Gamma_n,E_n> x <iota>.
  This is irrelevant for the proof because S_n is a matrix, hence holomorphic; but the phrase
  "entire circle-preserving extended commensurator" should say "holomorphic".

## 3. One-sided finite index generically, two-sided at each n.  CORRECT.

H = Gamma n [S]Gamma[S]^-1 is finitely generated (finite index in a finitely generated group).
For generators h_i choose bare k_i and alpha_i in K with h_i S = alpha_i S k_i. Then
alpha_i^2 = det h_i/det k_i is a signed monomial in A, so alpha_i is itself +-A^m: no zeros or
poles on the unit circle at all. After discarding the finitely many n at which an entry of S has a
pole, det S vanishes, or c vanishes or is infinite, the relations specialize, so
S_n^-1 H_n S_n is contained in Gamma_n. S_n preserves the null circle (S_n* G_n S_n = c(A_n) G_n,
c(A_n) real and nonzero), hence acts on H^2 as an isometry (orientation-reversing if c(A_n) < 0).
So S_n^-1 H_n S_n is a lattice inside Gamma_n of the same covolume as H_n, namely
[Gamma_n:H_n] covol(Gamma_n) with [Gamma_n:H_n] <= [Gamma:H]. Hence
[Gamma_n : S_n^-1 H_n S_n] = [Gamma_n:H_n] < infinity, and S_n^-1 H_n S_n has the same finite index
in S_n^-1 Gamma_n S_n. Both indices of Gamma_n n S_n^-1 Gamma_n S_n are finite: S_n is in
Comm_{Isom(H^2)}(Gamma_n). The kernel of specialization (the normal closure of M^n) plays no role:
only images are used. By Section 2, S_n is in <Gamma_n, E_n> for every admissible n.

## 4. The Jorgensen-Klein theorem

Statement as found (the 1982 paper itself was not accessible; three concordant secondary
statements):
- Inoue, RIMS Kokyuroku 1022 (1997) 113-116, Theorem 1 (PDF read): "Let {G_m} be a sequence of
  non-elementary r-generator Kleinian groups converging algebraically to the group G. Then G is
  also a non-elementary Kleinian group and the correspondence from the generators of G to their
  approximants in G_m extends for all sufficiently large m to a homomorphism of G onto G_m."
- Alaqad-Gong-Martin, arXiv:2107.07034, theorem labelled [Jorgensen] (TeX read): algebraic limit
  of n-generator Kleinian groups is Kleinian; "for all sufficiently large j the map
  Gamma -> Gamma_j defined by f_k -> f_{j,k} extends to a homomorphism of the groups."
- Marden, Hyperbolic Manifolds, Theorem 4.1.1 [Jorgensen 1976; Jorgensen-Klein 1982] (seen only as
  a search excerpt): same statement, homomorphism phi_n : G -> G_n for large n.
Direction: from the LIMIT group onto the approximants, as the text says. Hypotheses: a fixed
number r of marked generators, each converging in PSL(2,C); each G_m discrete and non-elementary.
No torsion bound is needed in PSL(2,C) (it is needed only in higher dimensions: Martin's theorem,
quoted by Inoue as Theorem 3). This matters here because Gamma_n has torsion of order n -> infinity.

Hypotheses in our case: G_n = <J_n, M_n, E_n, S_n> = <Gamma_n, E_n> (since S_n is in it) is the
reflection triangle group, discrete in the closed subgroup Stab(circle) of PSL(2,C) and
non-elementary; under the older meaning of "Kleinian" (discontinuous somewhere on the sphere) it
also qualifies, since it acts discontinuously off the circle. The four generators converge in
PGL_2(C): J, M, E are Laurent with nonzero determinant at A_0 (det M = -A^-2, det E = 1 - delta^2 = 1
at A_0), and S converges because of the DVR step (Section 6(a) below). Hypotheses hold.

## 5. The limit group.  CORRECT.

L = <J_0, M_0, E_0, S_0> is discrete (Jorgensen-Klein) and preserves the limit circle (S_0 is an
invertible similitude with c_0 != 0). Its side-preserving subgroup L^+ is a discrete subgroup of
PSL(2,R) containing PSL(2,Z). Since PSL(2,Z) is a lattice, L^+ is a lattice with
area(L^+) = (pi/3)/[L^+ : PSL(2,Z)], and it is non-cocompact (it contains a parabolic). The
signature formula area = 2pi(2g - 2 + s + sum(1 - 1/m_i)) with s >= 1 has minimum pi/3, attained
only by (0; 2,3; one cusp); the next values are pi/2 and 3pi/5 (s5_areas.py). Hence the index
is 1 and L^+ = PSL(2,Z), L = PSL(2,Z) u E_0 PSL(2,Z). Remarks:
- only "area >= pi/3 for non-cocompact groups" is used; uniqueness of the minimizer is not needed;
- source of the minimality: it is a direct consequence of the area formula (Beardon, The Geometry
  of Discrete Groups, Section 10.4; the result goes back to Siegel, Ann. of Math. 46 (1945)); a
  2026 arXiv paper found by search states it in exactly the form used. I re-derived it by
  enumeration rather than rely on the citation.

## 6. The conclusion, and the gap hunt

(a) Regularity and invertibility of S at A_0 (DVR step). Checked line by line. O = localization
at Phi_8 is bar-stable (bar(Phi_8) = A^-4 Phi_8), S can be made primitive, alpha is a unit
(indeed a monomial), bare words reduce to invertible matrices, and if S_0 had rank one its image
would be a line invariant under H_0, a finite-index subgroup of PSL(2,Z) (D-gauge), which
contains nontrivial powers of [[1,0],[-1,1]] and [[1,1],[0,1]]; these have no common eigenline.
So S_0 is invertible, [S_n] -> [S_0] in PGL_2(C), and c_0 != 0. No gap. This step is genuinely
needed: without it a rational family could leave every compact set of PGL_2(C).

(b) Projective versus linear. The limit relation is [S_0] = [w(J_0,M_0) E_0^eps] in PGL_2(C); the
eventual homomorphism gives [S_n] = [w(J_n,M_n) E_n^eps]; projective equality of two matrices
over K, one of them invertible, is the vanishing of the 2x2 minors of the 2x4 array of their
entries, which are rational functions vanishing at infinitely many A_n, hence identically. No gap.

(c) Kernel of specialization. Not used: the word w is fixed at the limit, and the identity is
transported to each A_n as a matrix identity at that point.

(d) Independent proof of the limit step, avoiding Jorgensen-Klein and the area bound.
Claim: if g_n in <Gamma_n,E_n> and g_n -> g in PGL_2(C) (g invertible), then there is a word W in
J, M, E with g = W(J_0,M_0,E_0) and g_n = W(J_n,M_n,E_n) for all large n.
Proof. Conjugate by matrices depending continuously on n so that all circles coincide. The
triangles T_n (angles pi/2, pi/3, pi/n) converge, uniformly on compact sets, to the triangle T_0
(angles pi/2, pi/3, 0), which is a strict fundamental domain for <J_0,M_0,E_0> = PGL(2,Z); the
side reflections of T_n are fixed words in J_n, M_n, E_n and converge. Fix x in the interior of
T_0, hence of T_n for large n. The point g x lies in a tile h T_0, h = W_1(J_0,M_0,E_0); put
z = h^-1 g x in T_0 and let F be the finite set of words k with z in k T_0 (one, two, four or six
tiles). For large n the tiles k_n T_n, k in F, cover a fixed ball around z (the local picture at an
interior point, an edge point, or the vertices of angle pi/2 and pi/3 is the same for T_n and T_0),
and h_n^-1 g_n x -> z. So k_n^-1 h_n^-1 g_n x lies in T_n for some k in F; it is in the orbit of the
interior point x; a strict fundamental domain meets that orbit only in x, and x has trivial
stabilizer; hence g_n = h_n k_n. As F is finite and distinct k give distinct limits h k, the same k
occurs for all large n and g = h k. QED.
Applied to g_n = S_n this gives Section 4 of the text at once: S_0 = W(J_0,M_0,E_0) and
S_n = W(J_n,M_n,E_n) for all large admissible n, hence identically. It needs only (i) S_n in
<Gamma_n,E_n> (Sections 1-3) and (ii) convergence of S_n to an invertible similitude (DVR step).
Floating-point sanity check (s6_limit_sanity.py, elements tracked exactly as elements of PGL(2,Z)):
for n = 501, 2001, 20001, 200001 the elements of <Gamma_n,E_n> displacing a base point by at most
3 are exactly the specializations of the 123 elements of PGL(2,Z) doing so at A_0 (121 at n = 501,
the difference being two elements within 0.01 of the boundary), and no others.
(A first version of that script used rounded floating keys and reported spurious duplicates; that
was a defect of the checker, documented in the script header, not of the theorem.)

(e) Things I tried in order to break (A) and that failed:
- even n: the group is still Delta(2,3,n); nothing breaks, the text just does not use them;
- arithmetic n (e.g. n = 7,...,30): Comm^+ is dense there, so the argument must avoid them; the
  text does (n > 30);
- the limit point itself is arithmetic (Comm(PSL(2,Z)) contains PGL(2,Q)): this is why a limit
  theorem, not a pointwise argument at A_0, is required, and discreteness of the limit is exactly
  what excludes S_0 in PGL(2,Q) minus PGL(2,Z);
- sequences such as M_n^{k_n}, k_n ~ n/2, which stay in Gamma_n with unbounded word length: they
  do not converge in PGL_2(C), consistent with (a);
- side-swapping S_n (c(A_n) < 0): covered by working in the holomorphic stabilizer;
- torsion of unbounded order: harmless in PSL(2,C).

## Corrected statement of the part refereed

Let S in GL_2(K) be a G-similitude and suppose H = Gamma n [S]Gamma[S]^-1 has finite index in
Gamma. (i) After scaling, S is regular and invertible at A_0 = exp(pi i/4). (ii) For every n >= 7
(any parity) the image Gamma_n at A_n is the Fuchsian triangle group Delta(2,3,n) in PU(G_n), and
<Gamma_n,E_n> is the reflection group Delta*(2,3,n), E_n being a holomorphic side-swapping
involution acting on H^2 as the reflection in the side joining the fixed points of J and JM.
(iii) For all but finitely many n outside {7,8,9,10,11,12,14,16,18,24,30}, S_n lies in the
commensurator of Gamma_n in the holomorphic circle stabilizer PGL(2,R), which equals
<Gamma_n,E_n>. (iv) Since S_n -> S_0 in PGL_2(C), there is one word W in J, M, E with
S_n = W(J_n,M_n,E_n) projectively for all large such n (by Jorgensen-Klein plus the pi/3 area
bound, or directly by convergence of the triangle tessellations), hence [S] = [W(J,M,E)] over K,
i.e. [S] is in Gamma^ext = Gamma semidirect <[E]>.
