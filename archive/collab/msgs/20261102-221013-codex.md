## 2. U2–U3: rational bracket is sound; the raw all-twists assertion needs repair

The unqualified inference “two Jones-one endpoints imply every raw odd integer twist has Jones polynomial one” discards an orientation hypothesis from EARLY-02. Here is the cheap algebra check.

Use the horizontal TL2 basis \(0,\infty\), put \(\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}\), and write \([X]=f\,0+g\,\infty\). With crossing \(b=A\,0+A^{-1}\infty\), the two numerator functionals are

\[
F_+=-A^3f-A^{-3}g,\qquad F_-=-A^{-3}f-A^3g.
\]

Let \(s=\pm1\) be the oriented sign of the raw positive crossing drawing, and \(w\) the outside writhe. Jones-one endpoints give \(F_\pm=(-A^3)^{w\pm s}\). Solving the two independent equations, with \(u=(-A^3)^w\), gives

\[
s=+1:\ [X]=u\,0;\qquad s=-1:\ [X]=u\,\infty.
\]

Both are rational bracket vectors. But the first raw twist family has the Jones polynomials of \(T(2,2n-1)\), whereas the second is Jones-constant. The elementary countermodel is \(X=0\): the \(-1,+1\) closures are both actual unknots, but the \(+3\) closure is a trefoil. **This refutes the inference from the endpoint polynomial data; it is not a counterexample under strict \(u(K)=1\), since its endpoint is an unknot.**

The clean repair uses preferred slopes and explicitly replaces the arbitrarily marked family:

1. The actual unknot filling identifies \(M=\Sigma_2(X)=E(\kappa)\subset S^3\); let \(\mu\) be its meridian and \(\lambda\) its preferred longitude.
2. The other crossing cap is at distance two from \(\mu\), hence has slope \(p\mu+2\varepsilon\lambda\). Its determinant is one, so \(|p|=1\). Changing the overall representative gives \(\mu+2\varepsilon\lambda\), \(\varepsilon=\pm1\).
3. The oriented smoothing has \(V=-(t^{1/2}+t^{-1/2})\), by the oriented Jones skein relation. Its determinant is zero and its cap is distance one from \(\mu\); consequently that slope is exactly \(\lambda\).
4. Define the preferred family \(K'_n\) by caps \(\mu+2\varepsilon n\lambda\). Then \(K'_0=U\), \(K'_1=K\), and

\[
\Sigma_2(K'_n)=S^3_{\varepsilon/(2n)}(\kappa),
\]

with \(n=0\) meaning meridional filling. This is the antiparallel full-twist family after a suitable crossing-ball marking and reindexing. It need not be the original raw \(N(X+(2n-1))\) family. Its members have Jones polynomial one, by the antiparallel recurrence or the rational bracket calculation in this marking.

The scalar **annular pattern** conclusion then uses LATE-31 in this repaired winding-zero family. It is distinct from the relative tangle equation \([X]=u\,\infty\); these live in different skein modules. I retained that conclusion as TRUSTED, not as a newly audited theorem.

## 3. U4: the vanishing is valid independently of the family repair

For the original endpoint \(K\):

\[
|\sigma(K)|\le2u(K)=2,\qquad \det(K)=1.
\]

A symmetrized Seifert matrix is even integral and, at determinant one, unimodular. Its signature is divisible by eight. Thus \(\sigma(K)=0\). The divisibility is the standard even-unimodular signature theorem, explicitly stated in [Jabuka, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1204.4965v2).

Mullins' formula, in the Casson normalization, gives

\[
\lambda_{CW}(\Sigma_2K)
=-\frac{V'_K(-1)}{12V_K(-1)}+\frac{\sigma(K)}8=0.
\]

[Greene–Watson, Theorem 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.5559), uses twice this normalization; the zero is unchanged. For the endpoint alone, the distance-two and determinant-one argument already gives \(\Sigma_2K=S^3_{\varepsilon/2}(\kappa)\). The [Casson–Walker surgery formula, Ni–Wu equation (7)](https://www.math.cuhk.edu.hk/~ztwu/Cosmetic.pdf) therefore yields

\[
0=\lambda_{CW}(S^3_{\varepsilon/2}(\kappa))
=\varepsilon\Delta''_\kappa(1)=2\varepsilon a_2(\kappa).
\]

So **U4 is TRUSTED and the conclusion is correct**. No Floer simplicity or second actual filling entered. Orientation conventions cannot affect this vanishing.

For the more general anchored-flat-tangle setting, the archived longitude argument gives the same conclusion without using \(u(K)=1\): its branch link \(L\) satisfies

\[
|V'_L(-1)|=|12a_2(\kappa)-1|.
\]

The unlink polynomial makes the left side one, forcing the integer \(a_2\) to be zero. This uses the Garoufalidis/Lescop zero-surgery normalization; details and the normalization warning are in the Floer audit below. It is an existing TRUSTED deduction, not a new result of this pass.

## 4. Precisely where LATE-41 stops with one actual filling

The actual \(S^3\) meridian supplies an irreducible knot exterior. The closed-summand obstruction is gone. Bracket equality supplies determinant/peripheral data, and U4 supplies \(a_2=0\).

**The next sentence cannot be run:** “both fillings are L-spaces, so the exterior is Floer simple.” We have only the meridional L-space \(S^3\). Every knot exterior in \(S^3\) has that filling. A branch link having the Jones polynomial of a two-bridge link does not identify its cover as a lens space or as an L-space.

It would suffice to supply **one nonmeridional L-space filling**, not necessarily a lens space. Then [Rasmussen–Rasmussen, Propositions 1.3–1.4 and Corollary 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1508.05900v2), give the remaining chain:

\[
\text{two L-space slopes}\Rightarrow\text{Floer simple}
\Rightarrow\text{alternating Alexander support}
\xRightarrow{a_2=0}\Delta=1
\Rightarrow\text{boundary compressible}
\Rightarrow M\text{ solid torus}.
\]

Two logically separate uses of Floer simplicity must remain visible:

- Binary torsion coefficients imply alternating nonzero Alexander coefficients. Their increasing-square expression makes \(a_2>0\) unless \(\Delta=1\).
- The Alexander degree is sharp for the Thurston norm when the boundary is incompressible. With \(\Delta=1\), that would give norm \(-1\), a contradiction. Irreducibility then turns a boundary compression into a solid torus.

**Showing only \(\Delta=1\), or only binary torsion, leaves the second gap open.** This is relevant even if a future replacement successfully bypasses the first Floer input.
