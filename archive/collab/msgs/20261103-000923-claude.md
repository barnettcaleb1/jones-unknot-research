**U5-W4 (Claude): faithfulness turns a vanishing coefficient into disjoint arcs.** Let X' = cap_i beta cup_j, with beta in B_4, be the complement of a crossing c of a knot P, and let K be P with c changed.
1. The TL_2 id-coefficient of X' is c_id = <l_i, rho(beta) v_j> in the cell module W(4,2). Up to the scalar A, W(4,2) is reduced Burau of B_4 at t = A^-4.
2. V_K = V_P iff c_id = 0. This uses sigma^2 = A^2 + (1 - A^-4) e and e sigma^2 = A^-6 e.
3. In W(4,2), e_k = |v_k><l_k| has rank one. The top-bottom reflection of cap_i beta cup_j is cap_j beta^-1 cup_i, with barred bracket, so <l_j, rho(beta^-1) v_i> = 0 as well. Put P = rho(beta) e_j rho(beta)^-1. Then e_i P = P e_i = 0, so rho(sigma_i) = A + A^-1 e_i commutes with rho(beta sigma_j beta^-1).
4. If Burau_4 is faithful (the Bharathram-Birman-Brendle claim, arXiv:2607.05283), sigma_i commutes with beta sigma_j beta^-1 in B_4. So the arcs a_i and beta(a_j) are isotopic or disjoint.
5. Isotopic arcs give a closed loop, which is impossible. Disjoint arcs let the cup slide above the cap, so X' is the trivial tangle e.

Conclusion, assuming BBB: if V_K = V_P across a crossing whose complement has plat width 4, then K = P = U. Bigelow (math/0012086) and Ito (1402.2028) prove the other direction: an unfaithful representation gives a V = 1 counterexample. Bigelow wrote that a positive answer for B_4 would have no dramatic consequence; this is one.
