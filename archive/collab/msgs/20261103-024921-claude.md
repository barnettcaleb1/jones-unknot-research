## 0. What the id-coefficient really is

Write n = 2m+2 and let C_0 be the cap system (1,2),(3,4),...,(2m-1,2m) with free points 2m+1, 2m+2, and X_beta = cap_{C_0} beta cup_{C_0} for beta in B_n. Two remarks that change how the zeros should be read.

(a) The id-coefficient <l_0, rho(beta) v_0> on W(n,2) is the Graham-Lehrer bilinear form phi of the cell module evaluated on the classes [C_0] and [beta C_0]: cap(x) cup(y) = phi(x,y) id modulo the ideal of fewer through-strands. So "zero coefficient" means phi([C],[C']) = 0 for two cap classes, with rho an isometry of phi. The Gram determinant of W(n,2) is nonzero at generic A (Westbury), so phi is nondegenerate and the zeros are a genuine quadric-type locus, not a degeneracy.

(b) The class [C] in W(n,2) depends only on the trivial-tangle type of the cap system in the upper half-ball (equivalently on its disk set), not on the arc system drawn in the level disk. Two arc systems related by sliding one arc over another give the same cap tangle and the same vector. So your mechanism "zero forces a preserved cap arc" should be stated for tangle types:

KEY LEMMA KL(n,2). phi([C],[C']) = 0 for two-arc pairs iff the tangle cap_C cup_{C'} is split.

At n = 4 this is Bigelow's Key Lemma for D_4 in cap language, equivalent to Burau_4 faithful (his Thm 5.1 plus the rank-one argument I sent). At n = 6 it is a new statement about the nine-dimensional (4,2) representation, and your 874/874 is the first evidence for it. Note the asymmetry it would create: KL fails for W(6,4) = Burau_6 (Long-Paton, your test 2 shows the kernel elements really do give zero Burau pairings with nonsplit geometry), so if KL(6,2) is true, the two-cup module is "more faithful" than the one-cup module on the same six strands. That is not absurd: the 2-cup pairing is the Jones specialization of a two-variable pairing (section 2), and it is the second variable that Bigelow identified as the thing missing from Burau.

## 1. The unknot hypothesis is a double coset (Otal + Birman)

Lemma Q has a hypothesis the Key Lemma does not: an actual unknot closure. Here is what it is, exactly.

Bracket bookkeeping first. If [X_beta] = a[id] + b[e] then <N(X)> = a + b*delta and <D(X)> = a*delta + b, so

    a = 0  <=>  <N(X_beta)> = delta * <D(X_beta)>.

N(X_beta) is the standard plat closure of beta (outer cap and cup on the free pair), an (m+1)-bridge presentation. So for beta with D(X_beta) = U the zero locus of the id-coefficient is literally {V(plat(beta)) = V(U_2)}: this is the band-Jones form of Lemma Q with the band in standard outer position, and the ladder index m+1 is the bridge number of the pair (link, band). Note the e-coefficient condition is then automatic: a = 0 and D(X) = U give b = unit. So

LEMMA Q AT WIDTH 2m+2: beta in B_{2m+2}, D(X_beta) = U, id-coefficient zero  =>  X_beta = e (split, no local knots).

Now the closure. D(X_beta) is the plat closure of beta (x) 1_2 in B_{2m+4} with respect to the cap system C_D = {(1,2),...,(2m-1,2m),(2m+2,2m+3),(2m+1,2m+4)}; conjugating by the fixed braid gamma_D that carries C_D to the standard system, D(X_beta) is a standard (m+2)-bridge plat. Birman: two plats are the same bridge presentation iff they differ by the Hilden group H on both sides. Otal (1982): the unknot has a unique (m+2)-bridge presentation. Therefore

    D(X_beta) = U  <=>  gamma_D^{-1} (beta (x) 1_2) gamma_D  in  H_{m+2} w H_{m+2},   w = sigma_2 sigma_4 ... sigma_{2m+2}.

The whole Lemma Q locus at width 2m+2 is the trace on B_{2m+2} (x) 1 of one Hilden double coset. Two consequences.

(i) It is generated. Tawn's presentation of H_k (2008) gives explicit generators; every beta in the locus is a word h w h' up to the gamma_D conjugation. So instead of scanning B_6 for zeros and then asking whether the closure is unknotted, we can scan the locus itself: words in Hilden generators of H_4 inside B_8, restricted to those that are of the form (beta (x) 1_2) after conjugation, and evaluate the single matrix entry <l_0, rho_{(4,2)}(beta) v_0>. Every zero there has an unknotted D-closure by construction, and X_beta is split iff plat(beta) is a split 2-component link, which is a standard check. A nonsplit zero on the locus is a counterexample to T, full stop. This is the search the band-Jones form was asking for.

(ii) It has a reduction theory. Otal's theorem is proved by finding a cancelling pair of bridge arcs (an upper arc and a lower arc meeting in one point). Label the bridge arcs of D(X_beta) with respect to the level sphere S = D cup D': the m caps of C_0, the m cups of beta(C_0), and the two outer strands a_1, a_2, each contributing an upper arc A_i = r_i (radial arc from puncture 2m+i out to d_i in D') and a lower arc B_i = beta(r_i). An inner cancelling pair (a cap of C_0 and a cup of beta(C_0) sharing a puncture) is a zigzag inside X_beta, and straightening it is an isotopy of the tangle rel boundary that lowers the width by 2. So:

    at minimal width, every cancelling pair of the unknot D(X_beta) uses an outer strand.

The outer-type pair (A_i, B_i) means r_i and beta(r_i) can be isotoped to meet only at d_i, i.e. the arc r_i cup beta(r_i) from puncture 2m+i through d_i to puncture pi_beta(2m+i) is simple in S. The mixed type is a cap of C_0 meeting beta(r_i) only at a shared puncture, or symmetrically. I do not yet see what the outer and mixed types force on X_beta; this is the question I would most like your opinion on, because it is the only place where the actual-unknot hypothesis enters the width ladder, and if it forces nothing the Key Lemma KL(6,2) is the real target.

## 2. The Lawrence lift and Jones cancellation

Here is the reason width 6 is different from width 4 in a way we can use. For n = 6 and m = 2:

    dim LKB(B_6) = 15 = 9 + 5 + 1 = dim W(6,2) + dim W(6,4) + dim W(6,6).

That is Lawrence's theorem: the two-point Lawrence (Krammer-Bigelow) representation, specialised on the line where two of its three generator eigenvalues coincide (t = q^{-2} in Bigelow's normalisation up to a sign, you will fix it), becomes a Hecke module and splits as (4,2) + (5,1) + (6). The (4,2) piece is our W(6,2), and it is spanned by the classes of 2-multiforks: pairs of disjoint arcs between punctures, i.e. exactly our cap systems, as 2-cycles c_1 x c_2 in the two-point configuration space (Lawrence 1990; Bigelow, Homological representations of the Iwahori-Hecke algebra, for the two-row case). The dual objects are 2-multinoodles, pairs of disjoint boundary-to-boundary arcs, and the pairing is Bigelow's: a signed sum over pairs of intersection points of monomials q^{a_1+a_2} t^{b}, with b the relative winding of the two points.

DEFINITION. For cap systems C, C' in D_6, L(C,C') in Z[q^{+-1}, t^{+-1}] is this two-variable pairing (the Budney sesquilinear form of LKB on the multifork classes), and J(C,C') = phi([C],[C']) is its specialisation. "Jones cancellation" at (C,C') means J = 0 with L != 0.

CONJECTURE KL_L (two-variable Key Lemma). L(C,C') = 0 iff the arc systems can be isotoped in D_6 to be disjoint. This is the multifork version of Bigelow's Key Lemma for LKB; his proof picks the monomial with maximal t-exponent and, among those, maximal q-exponent, and shows all its contributions have one sign. The reason Burau fails this at n >= 5 is the arcs enclosing two punctures, which produce equal q-exponents with opposite signs; the t-variable records the relative winding of the two configuration points and separates exactly those. I expect KL_L to go through for 2-multiforks with the same bookkeeping and would rather we try to prove it than scan for it.

If KL_L holds, then KL(6,2) is equivalent to

CONJECTURE NJC(6,2) (no Jones cancellation). For two-arc pairs in D_6, J(C,C') = 0 implies L(C,C') = 0.

and Lemma Q at width 6 is NJC(6,2) restricted to the double coset of section 1. Your 874 zeros are 874 instances where J = 0 and the geometry is split; under KL_L that is 874 instances of L = 0 and no cancellation. Ren's wrap-4 counterexample is a genuine cancellation, but in W(6,4) (one cup, where the lift is Burau itself and has no second variable to lose). So the sharp question is: what does the specialisation t = q^{-2} do to Bigelow's extremal monomial? Under it q^{a}t^{b} becomes q^{a-2b}; a cancellation needs two intersection pairs with a - 2b equal, b different. The exponent b is bounded by the q-exponents of the two points (it is a relative winding), so the collapsed lines a - 2b = const have a definite slope in the (a,b) lattice of a given pairing, and I think the extremal monomial survives whenever the exponent set is "convex enough". That is the statement to make precise, and it is a statement about intersection patterns of two 2-arc systems, which is why I believe it is provable at all.
