## The Jones pairing as a jet along the TL line

Write the Bigelow pairing of two (multi)fork classes as L = sum_{i,j} eps_{ij} q^{a_{ij}} t^{b_{ij}} (sum over ordered pairs of intersection points, Bigelow eq. (8); for 2-multiforks against 2-multinoodles the same bookkeeping with one point on each arc). Parametrise the normal direction to the TL line by t = -q^{-1}(1+s). Then

    q^a t^b = (-1)^b q^{a-b} (1+s)^b,   and   1 + qt = -s.

Budney's factor (1-t)(1+qt)(q-1)^2 is therefore exactly first order in s. So with Ltilde = L / ((1-t)(1+qt)(q-1)^2):

    Ltilde|_{s=0}  =  -(d/ds L)|_{s=0} / ((1+q^{-1})(q-1)^2),

and your statement "the TL subspace is the radical of Ltilde on the line" says that for cap classes L vanishes to SECOND order along t = -q^{-1}. Your proposed repair, the first normal derivative of Ltilde restricted to the radical, is then the second derivative of L:

    J(C,C')  :=  (d/ds)^2 L |_{s=0}  (on the radical)   =   sum_{i,j} eps_{ij} (-1)^{b_{ij}} b_{ij}(b_{ij}-1) q^{a_{ij}-b_{ij}},

up to the unit (1+q^{-1})(q-1)^2 and your normalisation. The restriction to the radical is what makes it well defined (the second jet of a quadratic form is basis-independent on the radical of its zeroth and first jets). CONJECTURE J: this is the Graham-Lehrer form on W(6,2), up to a unit, on 2-multifork classes. It is a one-line computation for you once the fork matrices are calibrated: for the 9 x 9 basis pairs compare sum eps (-1)^b b(b-1) q^{a-b} with phi. If the radical turns out to need the divided form's own derivative (i.e. my bookkeeping of the common factor is off by one order), the formula is the same with b(b-1) replaced by b; please report which.

Three consequences if Conjecture J holds.

1. The degree functional is a - b, not a - 2b. The Jones pairing of two cap systems is a signed sum over intersection PAIRS weighted by b(b-1): pairs whose two configuration points wind less than a full turn around each other (b in {0,1}) are invisible. That is why W(6,2) is not Burau: it only sees genuinely two-point data.

2. "Jones cancellation" means L vanishes to THIRD order along the TL line at a nonsplit pair. NJC(6,2) is: for a nonsplit two-arc pair, L vanishes to order exactly 2 along t = -q^{-1}. The first-order vanishing is forced by the radical; the second-order coefficient is phi.

3. The monomial strategy changes shape. Bigelow's surviving term is lex-max in (a then b), attained on diagonal pairs (i,i) with one sign. Under the jet it lands at degree A - B with weight B(B-1). Collisions need a' - b' = A - B with a' < A, hence b' < B, i.e. terms strictly below the lex-max in both exponents. His Claim 3.5 argument (b_{ij} - b_{ii} = 2w - eta with w >= 1 forces some a_k > a_i) controls exactly the relation between b-increase and a-increase along chains of intersection points. The question I will work on tonight: is the maximum of a - b over the pair set attained on the diagonal with a coherent sign and B >= 2? If the diagonal pairs of a minimal-intersection position always realise max(a - b), NJC(6,2) has a Bigelow-style proof. If not, the counterexamples to that step are exactly where a cancellation could live, and they can be enumerated.

One remark that bounds the search from the other side: the width-(2m+2) locus is, up to isotopy, the set of quotient tangles of strongly invertible knots kappa whose zero-surgery has an h-invariant genus-m Heegaard splitting, so at width 6 it contains the quotients of strongly invertible tunnel-number-one knots. All strong inversions on knots to 9 crossings already have w != 0 (the KWZ kappa screen). So zeros on the locus can only come from braids whose plat links are beyond that census; worth keeping in mind when choosing the subfamily (long Hilden words, not short ones).
