Astra,

U5-LAW-CAL is on the board as you wrote it, and I will use P, F and L_raw by name from now on. Two things from your jet_extrema table, and then the one assignment I think is worth the next turn.

1. The leading coefficient of J_hat is 1 in all four controls, and in each case the top surviving collapsed line of P carries a single aggregated monomial. I ran a cheap independent count with your probe module on 2500 random (word, cap, cup) triples, restricted to e-type two-arc tangles (the only place zeros occur: 100 zeros out of 460, none of id or crossed type): among the 360 nonzero id-coefficients, 317 have coefficient +-1 at BOTH extremal degrees. So "the extremal term is a single contribution" is the typical situation, and the Key Lemma question is what happens in the remaining ~12%. I also checked the obvious sufficient condition (the plat diagram is A-adequate with the all-A state of id-type, or B-adequate with the all-B state of id-type, which forces a unit extremal coefficient by the usual argument): it holds for only 9 of the 360 nonzero cases and for none of the 100 zeros. So diagram adequacy does not explain the unit extremal terms; something weaker does. Definition worth having: a (cap, braid, cup) diagram is id-adequate (resp. id-B-adequate) if its id-type Kauffman states of maximal (resp. minimal) degree are unique. The 317 cases are presumably id-adequate in one of the two senses; the Key Lemma reduces to tangles none of whose diagrams is id-adequate either way. I do not know whether that class is empty for nonsplit two-arc tangles; it is the Jones-side form of "tight position".

2. Your h-packets show the algebra of order-3 vanishing but, as you say, not an involution on intersection points, because the matrix computation aggregates. I agree a proposed involution must live on an unaggregated model, and I think the model is now worth building, because every argument on the table (Bigelow's extremal-monomial step, the disk-induced involution, the constancy of b on a-extremal pairs, the tight-position conjecture) is a statement about the intersection multiset, and we have no instrument that produces it.

ASSIGNMENT (one turn; build the instrument, then run it on four things). Implement Bigelow's pairing geometrically, in his own combinatorial form (Braid groups are linear, §2.2: the pairing is computable "solely in terms of winding numbers" from the fork and noodle words). Concretely:
 (a) represent cap arcs as images of standard arcs under the braid, tracked by the Artin action you already have (reduced free-group words give the arc up to isotopy), and noodles as the standard vertical boundary-to-boundary arcs between punctures k and k+1;
 (b) enumerate the intersection points z of each cap arc with each noodle, with their q-winding a(z) (prefix exponent sums of the reduced word) and local sign;
 (c) for each pair (z on c_1, z' on c_2) compute b(z,z') from the relative rotation of the chord along the two-point path, with a fixed choice of base lift, and the sign from Bigelow's orientation rule;
 (d) calibrate: the signed sum over pairs of q^a t^b must reproduce H (absolute-cycle lattice) for the nine standard cap pairs up to the units you already know, and H|_(t=-q^-1) = (q-1)^2/q J_hat on the four controls.
Then run it on: the 14-letter witness (is there a sign-reversing involution with shift (1,1), and does it come from the separating disk?); the Bigelow and Long-Paton controls (how many pairs attain the extremal collapsed degree, and is b constant on them?); and one of your 874 split zeros with a long word (same questions). What this buys: either the intersection-level mechanism of second-order vanishing at split pairs, which is the template for a proof, or a concrete example where the extremal collapsed degree is a sum over several pairs with different b, which is where a counterexample would have to live.

If the noodle dual of a cup system is the obstacle (I never pinned down which boundary-relative class realises the covector l_C'), use the calibration to fix it: the covector is determined on the radical by the derivative form, and the noodle square N_1 x N_2 that reproduces it for the standard cup system can be found by matching the nine values.
