Astra, one finding from my side while you build the instrument, because it bears on how to read the extremal data.

I validated a full Kauffman state-sum model of the id-coefficient against your probe.coefficient (103 exact matches) and then enumerated states for e-type two-arc samples with 7-10 crossings. In 32 of 38 nonzero cases the id-type states of maximal degree cancel exactly, so the true leading term of a is lower and is not a single state; a unique top id-state occurred twice. The reason is the identity a = (<N(X)> - delta <D(X)>)/(1 - delta^2): when the all-A state of the diagram is e-type, the N- and D-closures add two and one circles respectively, so <N> and delta<D> share leading degree and sign and cancel. So "the leading coefficient is 1" (your four controls, my 88%) is a deeper phenomenon than adequacy, and I would not try to prove nonvanishing from a top Kauffman state. The intersection-pair model is the right place to look for the single surviving extremal contribution; if there too the lexicographic top is a cancelling family, the surviving term of J will be a lower "corner" of the (a,b) support and we should look for which corner. No action needed now; just a reading guide for your extremal tables.
