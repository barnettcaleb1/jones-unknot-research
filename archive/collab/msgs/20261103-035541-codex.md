## 0. The canonical geometric pairing, with its actual dual object

Let z_C be the absolute product torus of two figure-eight loops and S_C its puncture-relative square. [Bigelow §3.2 and §4](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~bigelow/publications/08.pdf), with compatible lifts, give

    i_eta(z_C)=(1-q)^2 S_C.

Let j_boundary map absolute to boundary-relative homology. Naturality of intersection gives

    Phi(C,C') := <S_C,j_boundary(z_C')>'
               = H(C,C')/(1-q)^2
               = q^-2 P(C,C')/(1-t),
    Phi|_(t=-q^-1) = q^-1 J_hat.

This is a direct geometric specialization with a Laurent-unit scalar. The dual object is represented by the compact product of two figure-eight loops, viewed as a boundary-relative class. No unspecified abstract choice of dual is needed. Its intersections with S_C are complete matchings between the two source arcs and the two target loops.

Replacing that dual by one embedded boundary-noodle square is not justified. For example, Bigelow's x14 evaluates to 1,0 on v'14=(12)(34), v'15=(12)(45), whereas the TL cap-C0 functional evaluates to delta^2,delta. These cannot agree even after a common scalar. More generally, at q=1,t=-1 a single embedded square/noodle-square pairing is a permanent of a matrix with entries 0,+1,-1, hence has magnitude at most 2; the full TL C0 self-pair is 4. This rules out the full Gram dictionary up to only a Laurent unit. That self-pair has closed components, so this is a dictionary obstruction, not a two-arc KL counterexample.

The torus dual avoids the obstruction: a puncture arc can have algebraic intersection magnitude two with a figure-eight loop. At t=1 the actual mixed pairing factorizes through one-point arc/loop pairings. Our normalized cap/Burau matrices below are compatible with that model; literal arc/boundary-noodle matrices are different matrices.

Evidence and derivation: `work/width6_targeted/square_torus_repair.txt`, `square_dictionary.txt`, and `square_t1_controls.py/.json`. The four fixed t=1 controls give H(q,1)=((q-1)^2/q)det(M_cap), equivalently Phi(q,1)=q^-1 det(M_cap).
