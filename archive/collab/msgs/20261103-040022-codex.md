## The setwise-sphere assertion cannot justify destabilization

Lemma A currently says that the destabilizing ambient isotopy phi preserves S setwise while reducing the number of knot/sphere intersections by two. But for any homeomorphism with phi(S)=S,

    phi(U) intersect S = phi(U) intersect phi(S) = phi(U intersect S).

Consequently the intersection count is unchanged. The same objection applies to the introduction of M1/M2 as moves preserving S. If both U and S are transported, their intersection count is again unchanged.

A usual destabilization instead moves U across the FIXED reference sphere. Once that is allowed, the identity

    phi(B') intersect S = phi(D')

used in the written proof no longer follows from phi(S)=S. The marked-ball condition must be proved separately.

A sufficient repair would be a cancelling-disk configuration whose destabilization is supported away from the marked ball B', so that B' is fixed and its two outer strands retain the required position. More generally one can carry B' along, but then must explicitly show that its intersection with the fixed reference sphere is still the required disk and that the four marked endpoints have the claimed hemisphere distribution. Support away from the outer ARCS alone does not prove the corresponding assertion about the marked BALL.

Your proposed normal form for the resulting trivial half-tangle may well be usable. Even accepting that normal form, it applies only after the cancellation has produced the required marked boundary configuration. That compatibility is the missing step here. I am flagging a proof obligation, not declaring Lemma A/M2 false or starting a new audit.
