1. Lemma Q is a statement about strongly invertible knots, and the id-coefficient is an invariant of the inversion.

Absorb the unknotting crossing into the tangle, so the hypothesis is D(X') = U with X' e-type and a(X') = 0. The ball B' = N(alpha), alpha the band core, and U together form a theta-curve theta = U cup alpha with one unknotted constituent, the axis U. Lift to the double cover of S^3 branched along the axis: alpha lifts to a knot kappa with the deck involution tau as a strong inversion, and the quotient tangle of (kappa, tau) is exactly X'. Conversely every strongly invertible knot arises this way (Sakuma's correspondence between strong inversions and theta-curves with an unknotted constituent). So

    a(kappa, tau) := a(X') = (<N(X')> - delta <U>)/(1 - delta^2),   well defined up to units,

is an invariant of the strongly invertible knot, computed from the quotient link N = K_1 cup K_2 (the two constituents of theta other than the axis, pushed apart along the band). Lemma Q, at all widths, reads: a(kappa, tau) = 0 only for the trivial inversion of the unknot. Equivalently: the quotient link of a nontrivial strong inversion never has the Jones polynomial of the two-component unlink. The classical evaluations we already used say this much about N: linking number 0 (derivative of V at 1), det 0, and Sigma_2(N) = S^3_0(kappa) with H_1 = Z, so the surface framing of kappa agrees with the Seifert framing.

What width 6 means in this frame: the level sphere S is the 4-bridge sphere of the axis U (eight points: six inner punctures and the two outer points d_1, d_2), alpha is the arc in S from d_1 to d_2, and kappa is the lift of alpha to the genus-3 Heegaard surface Sigma_3 of S^3 that double covers S, where tau is the hyperelliptic involution. So width-6 Lemma Q is: a tau-invariant simple closed curve kappa on the standard genus-3 Heegaard surface of S^3 through two fixed points of tau, whose quotient link has the Jones polynomial of the unlink, bounds a disk. The width ladder is the genus of this surface. Width 4 (genus 2) is where Gabai and Gordon-Luecke already settle it. I find this a more honest statement of the problem than KL(6,2), because it keeps the unknot hypothesis (the axis) and the Jones hypothesis in one picture, and because it names the real symmetry group: the stabiliser of the 4-bridge presentation of U acting on arcs from d_1 to d_2 in the 8-punctured sphere, which is your Hilden double coset in arc form.

Question A. Is a(kappa, tau), or the Jones polynomial of the quotient link, related to any known invariant of strong inversions? Sakuma's eta-invariant is built from the Kojima-Yamasaki eta-function of a linking-number-zero two-component link attached to the inversion, and our N is a linking-number-zero two-component link attached to the inversion. If Sakuma's link is N (or c cup K_2, the other constituent pair), then the Alexander-type and Jones-type data of the same link are both invariants of (kappa, tau), and the question becomes whether a nontrivial strong inversion can have trivial Jones data on its quotient link. I do not remember Sakuma's construction well enough to say; you may.

2. The quotient link is an encircled pattern, and its Jones polynomial is a single linear functional on the skein of the solid torus.

At width 6 the short-arc component K_1 of N is a one-bridge unknot and K_2 is a two-bridge knot or unknot, with lk(K_1, K_2) = 0. Put V = S^3 - N(K_1), an unknotted solid torus, and expand K_2 in the Kauffman bracket skein of V in the Chebyshev basis: [K_2] = sum_{k even} p_k(A) e_k (even k because the winding number is 0). Encircling e_k by a 1-coloured unknot multiplies it by lambda_k = -(A^{2k+2} + A^{-2k-2}), and lambda_0 = delta. Hence

    <N> = sum_k p_k lambda_k Delta_k,    <K_2> = sum_k p_k Delta_k,    Delta_k = (-1)^k [k+1],

and the Jones-trivial condition <N> = delta^2 is the single linear condition sum_k p_k S_{1k} = delta S_{10} on the skein class of the pattern, S the Hopf pairing. The trivial pattern is p = delta e_0. Nonsplit means the pattern has wrapping number at least 2 in V. So Lemma Q at width 6 says: no two-bridge pattern of winding number 0 and wrapping number at least 2 in the unknotted solid torus, whose band sum with the boundary unknot is unknotted, has Hopf pairing with e_1 equal to that of the trivial pattern. I believe this is the natural home for a "no cancellation" argument, because the Hopf pairing with e_1 is nondegenerate and simple, and because two-bridgeness of K_2 together with winding number 0 is a strong constraint on which skein classes occur. The Whitehead link is the test case to keep in mind: K_1, K_2 unknots, lk 0, wrapping number 2, band-trivial, V(W) nontrivial, width 4 or less. Whatever argument we find must see why its p_2 term survives.

Question B. Is the set of skein classes of winding-number-0 two-bridge patterns in the solid torus describable? Two-bridge knots in S^3 have Jones polynomials with a clean continued-fraction structure (they are the closures of rational tangles, and their brackets come from the TL representation of the rational tangle), and a two-bridge knot sitting in an unknotted solid torus with winding number 0 should inherit a structure of that kind, with the solid torus core as one more generator. If p_2 for such patterns is forced to be nonzero whenever the wrapping number is 2 (which the Whitehead link suggests), that is a theorem, and it would be the first piece of Lemma Q at width 6 proved for a reason rather than checked.
