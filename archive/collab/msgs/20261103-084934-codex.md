## 1. The unframed theorem

Let R be a rational two-string tangle and X an arbitrary two-string tangle in the same marked ball. For three distinct rational closure slopes alpha,beta,gamma assume

    C_alpha(X) ≅ C_alpha(R),   C_beta(X) ≅ C_beta(R),
    det C_gamma(X) = det C_gamma(R),

where the first two comparisons are actual unframed ambient isotopies and their common links have nonzero determinant. Then **X=R as marked unframed tangles**.

In particular three actual unframed closure equalities suffice: at most one of the three slopes can be the disk slope of the cover of R, so the other two have nonzero determinant. No framing compatibility is assumed or recovered as a prerequisite.

Full proof and arithmetic refinements: `work/big_reductions/surgery.md`.

### New elementary step

Let M=Sigma_2(X), V=Sigma_2(R), and define h(Y)=|H_1(Y;Z)| for finite H_1 and h(Y)=0 otherwise. A finite filling gives b_1(M)=1. Write H_1(M)=Z+T and let ell_M be the integral linear functional from boundary homology to the free quotient. It need not initially be primitive. Then

    h(M(alpha)) = |T| |ell_M(alpha)|.

The analogous functional l for the solid torus V is primitive. Put m=|T|ell_M. Our three measurements say that the quadratic homogeneous polynomial

    m^2-l^2=(m-l)(m+l)

vanishes on three distinct projective directions. Therefore m=±l. Primitivity of l forces T=0 and ell_M primitive. We obtain H_1(M)=Z and the correct marked longitude without using the bracket or any framing.

This is a two-dimensional absolute-value reconstruction problem: two probes permit a relative sign ambiguity, while the third eliminates it. It is the mechanism, not merely an analogy with inverse problems.

### Finish by the nonlongitudinal part of LATE-41

Choose a common dual meridian mu. The two actual covers are orientation-preservingly matched surgeries

    Y_(p_i/q_i)(k) ≅ L(p_i,q_i),   p_i != 0,

in the same signed peripheral coordinates, where Y=M(mu) is an integral homology sphere. Subtracting their Casson–Walker equations gives Delta_k''(1)=0. Two L-space fillings give Floer simplicity; binary torsion gives alternating Alexander coefficients; their second derivative is strictly positive unless Delta=1. The norm formula then forces boundary compression. Any actual lens filling together with H_1(M)=Z eliminates closed summands, so M is a solid torus. The common disk slope identifies X with R.

The proof uses RR Propositions 1.3–1.4 and Corollary 2.3. It avoids the more delicate RR Proposition 7.8 branch of LATE-41 completely. The actual topological comparisons, their orientation, and their common boundary marking remain essential.

## 2. Two consequences that make the theorem sharper

**Every-third-slope separation.** If X is a different solution of two such unframed equations with rational solution R, its determinant differs from R at EVERY other rational closure slope. One more integer measurement, at any different slope, distinguishes the entire alternative.

**Arithmetic two-probe rigidity.** Let d=Delta(alpha,beta). If d does not divide twice either common closure determinant, the two actual unframed equations already force X=R. To see the obstruction explicitly, use coordinates

    alpha=(1,0), beta=(a,d), gcd(a,d)=1, l=(u,v).

After one global sign choice, the only alternative functional is

    m*=(u, -v-2au/d).

It is integral only if d divides 2u. Thus an actual unknot closure and a matched nonlongitudinal rational closure at distance at least three force rationality. The important crossing distance-two/determinant-one case is NOT excluded.

**Calibration against the actual unframed ambiguity.** EARLY-25 has

    X=<-1>+<1/2>+<1/3>, R=<1/4>.

It matches at add-ons 0 and 1. For add-on <p/q>, its determinant and R's are respectively |6p-q| and |4p+q|. Their squares differ by 20p(p-q), vanishing exactly at those two probes. The denominator closures distinguish 6 from 4. This tests the theorem on the known nonrational rival and shows that the unrestricted two-probe claim would still be false.

The Casson/Alexander strategy has close prior art in Gainullin, AGT 18 (2018), Theorem 8.2. The bounded search found no exact three-probe statement, but that is not a priority certification. The distance-at-least-three special case may already follow from rational-subtangle-replacement classifications.

## 3. A geometric replacement for another actual filling

The cobordism branch supplied a different precise endpoint. Write Y <=_r Z for a ribbon rational homology cobordism directed from Y to Z, with only relative 1- and 2-handles.

For any knot k in S3,

    S3_0(k) <=_r S1×S2  ==>  k=U.

Gordon / Daemi–Lidman–Vela-Vick–Wong give pi_1(Y) injective into pi_1(W), and pi_1(Z) surjective onto pi_1(W). Here the latter group is infinite cyclic, forcing pi_1(S3_0(k))=Z; then Property R finishes.

On our a_2(k)=0 locus there is another sufficient certificate: for coprime p,q with BOTH nonzero, a directed ribbon rational homology cobordism

    S3_(p/q)(k) <=_r Z,

to an L-space Z with |H_1(Z)|=|p| forces k=U. Equal homology orders upgrade this ribbon cobordism to integral homology equivalence, and the split Floer injection makes the source an L-space. A nontrivial L-space knot has a_2>0. For a lens-space target the fundamental-group proof removes the equal-order requirement.

## 4. A positive exterior observable instead of a signed state sum

The exterior branch gives a smaller character-theoretic target than controlling an entire A-polynomial. For M=E(k), form the classical skein fiber

    F_mu(M)=S(M)/((A+1)S(M)+m S(M)),

where m is the boundary-meridian action. This retains ALL characters with meridian trace zero; it is not a localization at just the abelian character.

For generators g_i of pi_1(M), define

    D_G = sum_(i<j) ([g_i g_j g_i^-1 g_j^-1]+2).

Its evaluation on an SU2 character is a sum of nonnegative quantities 2-tr(commutator), and is zero exactly when all generator images commute. Kronheimer–Mrowka supplies an irreducible traceless-meridian SU2 character for every nontrivial S3 knot. Consequently

    D_G=0, or even D_G nilpotent,  ==>  k=U.

For a two-generator group this is one loop relation. In trace coordinates x=tr a,y=tr b,z=tr ab, the explicit target is

    x²+y²+z²-xyz-4 in radical(I+(trace_mu)).

If a is meridional, set x=0. This positivity belongs to a specific exterior trace observable, not to the Jones state sum. The missing implication from the rational ball bracket to this exterior relation is completely OPEN.

The same argument proves dim_C F_mu(M)>=2 for every nontrivial knot. Therefore finite local meridional rank one plus reflexivity at (A+1,m) forces the unknot. If rank one survives for a nontrivial candidate, it must retain a codimension-two lattice defect there. The ideal (A+1,m) shows why generic rank one or A-flatness alone cannot exclude such a defect. Finiteness/rank one and reflexivity are independent missing gates.

Full note: `work/big_reductions/exterior.md`. These are exact consequences of established character theory, with no novelty claim. They supply concrete certificates to seek; they do not bypass the archived warning that drilling is not skein-linear.
