## 1. Independent read of the unframed three-probe theorem

I checked the new step from scratch and I believe it. Record of the check:

(a) b_1(M) = 1 from one finite filling: correct (half-lives-half-dies gives b_1 >= 1; b_1 >= 2 would make every filling infinite).

(b) h(M(alpha)) = |T| |ell_M(alpha)|: correct. H_1(M(alpha)) = (Z + T)/<(n, t)> with n = ell_M(alpha). For n = 0 the quotient is infinite. For n != 0 the quotient surjects onto Z/n with kernel (nZ + T)/<(n,t)>, and nZ + T = <(n,t)> + T as a direct sum because <(n,t)> meets 0 + T trivially; so the order is |n||T|. Dehn filling kills exactly the image of the filling slope, by Mayer-Vietoris.

(c) The interpolation: m^2 - l^2 = (m - l)(m + l) is a product of two integral linear forms on Z^2 vanishing on three pairwise independent vectors; by pigeonhole one factor vanishes on two independent vectors, hence identically; so m = +-l, and primitivity of l forces |T| = 1 and ell_M = +-l. Correct, and it is the whole content: nothing about the bracket enters.

(d) The hypotheses are correctly scoped. Step (c) needs only the three equalities h(M(s)) = h(V(s)); the two actual isotopies are needed only afterwards, for Casson-Walker and for the two lens fillings. Since l kills exactly one direction, at most one of the three probes is longitudinal, so two nonlongitudinal probes always exist; but the two ACTUAL ones must be the nonlongitudinal ones, as you state, because the det-only probe cannot feed Walker's formula. The "three actual closures suffice" corollary is right because then at most one of the three is longitudinal.

(e) The finish: subtracting Walker's formula at two slopes with the same Y = M(mu) and the Seifert longitude from (c) gives Delta''(1) = 0 and lambda(Y) = 0; two lens fillings give Floer simplicity (Rasmussen-Rasmussen); the torsion then has the L-space form, whose second derivative at 1 is positive unless trivial; Delta = 1 plus Floer simple gives genus zero, so M is a solid torus summed with a closed piece, and a prime lens filling kills the closed piece; a solid-torus double cover means X is rational (Lickorish), and the common disk slope fixes the fraction. I did not re-audit the RR citations; that branch is the one audited in LATE-41.

Two remarks, not objections. First, (b)+(c) is a standalone lemma worth recording on its own: three determinant equalities at distinct slopes against a rational tangle already give H_1(Sigma_2(X)) = Z and the correct longitude. ## 2. Theory page: the wrapping-two reduction

Setting, all widths. N = K_1 cup K_2 with K_1 unknotted and lk = 0 (both forced by V(N) = V(U_2)). Let E be a spanning disk of K_1 meeting K_2 minimally, w = |E cap K_2| the wrapping number. Cut: B = S^3 - N(E) is a ball and T = K_2 cap B is a w-string tangle. Write V for the solid torus S^3 - N(K_1) and P(A, z) for the Kauffman bracket of K_2 in V in the variable z = core.

Lemma 1 (encircling identity). <K_1 cup K_2> = delta * P(A, z = -(A^4 + A^{-4})), and <K_2> = P(A, z = -(A^2 + A^{-2})). Proof: on the Chebyshev basis e_k of the skein of V, <U cup e_k> = Delta_k * lambda_k with lambda_k = -(A^{2k+2} + A^{-2k-2}), and Delta_k lambda_k = delta * S_k(lambda_1) for every k (both sides are (-1)^{k+1}[2k+2]); linearity does the rest. (Convention <empty> = 1, <U> = delta.) So the Jones polynomial of the encircled pattern is the annular bracket of the pattern with the loop value delta(A) replaced by delta(A^2).

Now take w = 2, which is the whole width-6 case (below). Then T is an e-type two-string tangle (lk = 0 forces the two crossings of E to have opposite sign, so each string returns to its own side), the annular closure of the id-state is two parallel cores z^2 and of the e-state one contractible loop, and with <T> = x id + y e:

    P = x z^2 + y delta,   <N> = delta (x lambda_1^2 + y delta),   <K_2> = delta (x delta + y),   <D(T)> = delta (x + y delta),

where D(T) is the two-component link obtained by reclosing the two strings of T inside E. Note lambda_1 = 2 - delta^2.

Theorem 2 (the Jones-trivial line). V(N) = V(U_2) iff

    x (delta^2 - 2)^2 + y delta = u delta   (u a unit).

Consequences: (i) delta divides x, i.e. x vanishes at A^4 = -1, i.e. det D(T) = 0 (because x(A^4 = -1) = <D(T)>/delta at A^4 = -1 up to sign). (ii) Writing x = delta x', y = u - x'(delta^2 - 2)^2, so the whole Jones-trivial locus in the (x,y)-plane is one line through (0, u) with direction (delta, -(delta^2-2)^2). (iii) Eliminating x' gives the single identity between the two closures of T,

    (delta^2 - 3) <K_2>/delta - (delta^2 - 4) <D(T)>/delta^2 = u,

in t: (t - 1 + t^{-1}) V(K_2) - (t - 2 + t^{-1}) V(D(T))/(t^{1/2} + t^{-1/2}) = 1 up to units. (iv) For T rational and e-type, D(T) is a two-bridge link with determinant equal to the denominator, nonzero unless T is the trivial e-tangle; so no pattern that is the annular closure of a rational tangle (twisted Whitehead doubles included) gives a Jones-trivial N. Whitehead check: T = clasp, x = 1 - A^{-4}, y = A^2, x not divisible by delta, consistent.

Width 6 is wrapping two. If K_1 is one-bridge and K_2 two-bridge with respect to the same sphere S, choose bridge disks of K_1 disjoint from K_2 (simultaneous triviality), with shadows s_+, s_- in S; then s_+ cup s_- bounds a region R in S and E = Delta_+ cup R cup Delta_- meets K_2 in |R cap K_2| points, which is 0 (split) or 2 (nonsplit). Moreover lk = 0 forces the two points of R cap K_2 to be the two endpoints of ONE bridge arc of K_2, say alpha_1 (if they were endpoints of different arcs on the same side, K_2 would cross E twice in the same direction). Hence at width 6

    T = A cup B,  A = alpha_1 (a single bridge arc),  B = beta_1 cup alpha_2 cup beta_2,

which is the structure lemma (one short arc) seen from inside the solid torus. Nonsplit means exactly that the shadow of alpha_1's bridge disk leaves R, i.e. crosses the shadows of K_1's bridge disks; if it stayed inside R, A would be parallel into E and the pattern could be pushed off E.

So Lemma Q at width 6 is now: no e-type tangle T = A cup B of this two-level form, other than the trivial one, lies on the Jones-trivial line. The reason I think this is the right object: the line is one linear condition on a rank-two skein module, the first consequence (det D(T) = 0, with D(T) = L_A cup L_B and L_A an unknot, since alpha_1 plus an arc in S is one-bridge) is a topological statement about a simpler link, and the two-level form of T is exactly the kind of structure that controls a bracket vector.
