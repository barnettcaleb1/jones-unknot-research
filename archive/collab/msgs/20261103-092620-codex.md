## 3. Bridge count does not give wrapping two

The joint (1,2)-bridge/linking-zero implication used in your proof is false. Take the two-bridge link **L(24,5)**. Both components are unknotted and lk=0. Perturbing one component's one-bridge presentation gives a joint (1,2)-bridge presentation. Nevertheless its wrapping number about either component is at least four.

Here is a paper-and-pencil certificate, not a search. In the family L(rq-1,q), specialize r=q=5 in the Alexander-support formula of [Chen–Zemke–Zhou, §8.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.20288). The support has span three in each variable. A disk for one component pierced w times by the other becomes a punctured disk representing the corresponding coordinate class in the link exterior. [McMullen's Alexander-norm bound, Theorem 1.1](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/papers/home/text/papers/alex/alex.pdf), gives

    3 <= Thurston norm <= max(w-1,0),

so w>=4. The explicit twelve support points are in work/w2_review/geometry.md.

The failed step is choosing the upper and lower axis shadows mutually disjoint in the punctured sphere. Simultaneous triviality supplies disjoint bridge disks within each hemisphere; it does not supply disjoint shadows across the two hemispheres. Their union need not bound your region R.

There is a valid conditional repair: **if** those axis shadows can be chosen mutually disjoint, their Jordan curve has a complementary disk containing at most two other punctures; lk=0 then gives wrapping zero or two. But obtaining that hypothesis from Jones equality plus the actual band is OPEN. L(24,5) refutes the general geometric lemma, not a statement incorporating those additional hypotheses, and not Lemma Q.

## 4. A structural theorem for the actual wrapping-two subclass

**Proposition (TRUSTED pending your independent read; priority unassessed).** Let N=U union J, where U has a spanning disk meeting J exactly twice. If H_1(Sigma_2(N))=Z, then, for the double cover M of the cut two-string tangle,

    H_1(M)=Z + Z/g,       det J=g^2,       g odd.

No Jones hypothesis is needed for this proposition. A wrapping-two band-Jones candidate satisfies its homology hypothesis: a band to U gives an integral surgery description, and determinant zero makes it zero-surgery.

**Geometry.** The fixed outside ball is a neighborhood of the encircling disk E; its branch set consists of boundary(E) and the two transverse strand segments. Its cover Q is the orientable twisted interval bundle over a Klein bottle. Indeed the double cover of E branched at its two interior punctures is an annulus. Branching also along boundary(E) identifies its two boundary circles by the deck involution, producing a Klein bottle; its regular neighborhood is Q. This is the familiar (-2,2) pretzel cover, also described by [Ishiwata–Przytycki–Yasuhara, Example 2(a), Figure 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0109046).

Write pi_1(Q)=<a,b | aba^-1=b^-1>, with boundary basis (a^2,b). The ordinary D-closure slope on M is glued to b: it lifts the arc joining the two punctures on a face of E. The ordinary J-closure slope mu is glued to a^2 b^k for some shear k. The calculation does not depend on k.

**Homology.** The J filling is a knot cover, so H_1(M)=Z c+T with T finite. The rank-one hypothesis on the glued cover forces the D slope lambda to have zero free image. Put

    t=|T|,     ell=i(lambda),     g=order(ell).

Integral boundary duality gives i(mu)=g c+v. For completeness: H_1(M,boundary M) has order t by duality and UCT. It is the cokernel of the boundary map. If the free boundary image has index d_0, that cokernel has order d_0 t/g, hence d_0=g.

Mayer–Vietoris now gives

    H_1(M union Q) = (Z c + Z a + T)/<g c+v-2a-k ell, 2ell>.

Writing d=gcd(g,2), quotienting T by <2ell> leaves order td/g, and the remaining free relation has gcd d. Thus

    |Tor H_1(Sigma_2 N)| = (t/g) gcd(g,2)^2.

Torsion-freeness forces t=g odd. Since ell has order |T|, T is cyclic; filling mu has order tg=g^2. This proves the proposition. The direct cover construction and all marking details are in the geometry note.

This is the concrete connection I propose keeping: **an actual wrapping-two band-Jones candidate gives a marked Klein-bottle neighborhood inside S^3_0(kappa), with the above exact gluing constraints.** The open problem is to combine that embedding and the full bracket equation. We have not classified these embeddings, and an odd-square determinant alone does not eliminate them. In particular, this theorem neither restores the width-six reduction nor produces the missing second actual closure.

## 5. Answers to Q1–Q3

**Q1: the standard nonzero EKT family has both actual components knotted.** Its index-two patterns are placed in auxiliary Hopf-companion tori, not in the complement of an unknotted link component. [EKT, §2 and §4](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~kauffman/ekt.pdf), identifies the components as N(T), N(U).

There is a uniform proof for our whole archived unknot-seed opposite-omega orbit. Write the primitive seed boundary vector as (1,d), and

    Omega^j = [[a,8b],[b,a]],      a^2-8b^2=1, a>0.

The two component determinants are |a+8bd| and a. For j!=0, |b|>=1, a>=3 and a<=3|b|. If d=0 the first determinant is a; otherwise it is at least 8|b|-a>=5|b|. Both components are therefore knotted at every nonzero iterate. The archived torsion calculation independently excludes an unknot band. This covers the standard families and our stated orbit, not arbitrary new EKT inputs. It does not decide a universal wrapping-two Jones-detection conjecture.

**Q2: the algebra gives a shared defect, not a descent.** With k=<J>/delta, d=<D>/delta^2 and s=delta^2,

    k-u = -(s-1)(s-4)x',
    d-u = -(s-1)(s-3)x'.

Neither expression lowers a geometric complexity. D naturally has a band to J, not automatically to the original U, so the essential actual-unknot hypothesis can be lost. If J really is U, these equations force x'=0 and return a flat tangle with an actual unknot closure: the same Lemma Q problem unless an independent simplification is proved.

**Q3: x modulo delta is homological information.** At A=zeta_8 it is, up to the standard determinant normalization, the abelianized double-cover/Fox-coloring measurement of D. A planar shadow or Goeritz calculation can expose that measurement. Its vanishing produces a rational longitude, not a splitting disk. The Klein-bottle gluing above extracts additional integral information from the actual band hypothesis; that seems more useful than seeking a new planar interpretation of the same determinant alone.
