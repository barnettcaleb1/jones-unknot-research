## 2. The crossing circle: Lemma Q is Snape's open question

Take the unknotting crossing of K_+ in its ball B', with K_- = U. Let D_c be the crossing disk (it meets U in the two points d_1, d_2 of the level sphere, antiparallel since X is e-type, so lk = 0) and c its boundary. Write X = U - B' in the frame where U's strands through B' are straight: <X> = x id_v + y e; the note's coefficient a is x up to a unit, so a = 0 iff x = 0.

Lemma 3. (i) If a = 0, every closure of X is Jones-equal to the same closure of the trivial e-tangle: the whole twist family X cup sigma^n is Jones-trivial, and the crossing-circle link c cup U has V = V(U_2). (ii) Conversely, if U is an unknot and c a crossing circle of U with V(c cup U) = V(U_2), then a = 0. Proof of (ii): U has wrapping number at most two in S^3 - c, so its annular class is P = x z^2 + y delta; <U> = delta(x delta + y) = delta u_0 and <c cup U> = delta(x lambda_1^2 + y delta) = delta^2 u_1 by the encircling identity; eliminating y gives x (delta^2 - 1)(delta^2 - 4) = delta (u_1 - u_0). The left side has a double root at A = 1 unless x = 0; the right side is zero, or delta times a monomial or a binomial, with simple roots only. So x = 0.

Theorem 4. Lemma Q is equivalent to: for every unknot U and every crossing circle c of U (an unknot bounding a disk meeting U in two points), V(c cup U) = V(U_2) implies c cup U is split. Proof: Lemma 3(i), then "c bounds a disk missing U, so the crossing is nugatory"; conversely Lemma 3(ii), Lemma Q for the full-twist knot (unknotting number one, V = 1), then Scharlemann-Thompson (a crossing change on the unknot yielding the unknot is nugatory). Remark: a nonsplit Jones-trivial crossing-circle link of the unknot refutes the Jones unknot conjecture itself, by the same two steps.

Corollary 5. In Sigma_2(U) = S^3, D_c lifts to an annulus with core kappa and boundary kappa' cup tau(kappa'), two parallel pushoffs of kappa swapped by the inversion; its framing is the band framing, which is the Seifert framing exactly when det N = 0, hence whenever a = 0. So c cup U is the axis together with the image of a preferred longitude of kappa and its equivariant mate.

Lemma 3(ii) adds one thing to this: for the branch-set annular knot, annular-Jones-trivial iff Jones-trivial for the Sakuma link, so his two open questions are one question, and it is Lemma Q.

So Lemma Q has a name: the Jones polynomial detecting the strongly invertible unknot, open since 2018, with the categorified version proved by Snape from Hedden-Ni and Batson-Seed. The object is canonical: Watson's quotient tangle T(kappa, tau) is our X, and Lemma Q reads "the Kauffman bracket of Watson's tangle is a multiple of the turnback e only for the trivial inversion". This is the frame you were already working in (U2 to U5, quotient tangles, exterior fillings); the width ladder was a detour from it, and Sakuma and Watson are the right anchors.

## 3. The relative-position statement, in these terms

The crossing circle lies on the level sphere S and bounds a disk in S containing exactly the two outer bridge points. Upstairs its lift is the boundary of a neighbourhood of kappa in the genus-3 Heegaard surface. The marked data is an arc alpha* in the 8-punctured sphere between the punctures 7 and 8, with U in standard 4-bridge position, modulo the stabiliser of that bridge presentation (the hyperelliptic part of the genus-3 Goeritz group; Powell's generators). Width-6 Lemma Q: a(alpha*) = 0 only on the orbit of the standard arc. That uses the band (it is a neighbourhood of alpha* in S, and c is its boundary) and names the group whose orbits we classify. It does not give a mechanism.
