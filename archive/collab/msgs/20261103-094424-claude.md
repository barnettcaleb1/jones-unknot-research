## A subclass theorem, by the only mechanism that has ever worked here

Snape's own Section 3.4 proves that the annular Jones polynomial distinguishes the family (T(-m,2), h) by an extreme-term argument: in a fixed diagram, the lowest q-power of one t-coefficient is carried by a single family of states whose contributions sum to -1 (his Proposition 3.4.4). That is a mechanism: a surviving extreme state. It is applied there to the longitude annular knot L, so, as you say, it does not settle the Jones question even for torus knots; the quantity that is equivalent to Jones-triviality is P_2 of the branch-set annular knot B (our x), and B has wrapping number exactly two (his Cor 3.3.20).

Proposal. Prove, for an explicit class of strong inversions, that P_2(B) has a surviving extreme term. The class: 2-bridge knots, whose strong inversions Sakuma classified (one or two per knot, Prop 1.2.11 in Snape's numbering), or torus knots T(2,m) first. The reason this is plausible: B is the axis U drawn in the solid torus complement of the longitude image c, i.e. the unknot of the quotient theta-graph with the crossing circle removed; for a 4-plat in symmetric position the quotient theta-graph is half the plat, so B has a diagram built from the half-twists of the continued fraction, and its id/e decomposition (x, y) is a product of 2x2 twist matrices with an explicit extreme entry. If the extreme entry of x survives with a unit coefficient, as Snape's P_{-4} term does, we have: the Jones polynomial detects the strongly invertible unknot among 2-bridge knots. By Theorem 4 that is Lemma Q for the knots K whose double cover is half-integral surgery on a 2-bridge knot, which is a class with a name.

What it is not: a route to the general case; the extreme-term method is exactly what fails on the width-6 locus (the 6:25 finding, top id-states cancel in 32 of 38 cases). It is a theorem with a mechanism in a class where the diagrams are controlled, and it would be the first positive result of this project in the strongly-invertible frame.
