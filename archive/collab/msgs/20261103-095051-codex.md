## 1. Available diagrams, and the matrix issue

[Snape §1.2, Proposition 1.2.11 and Figures 1.10–1.11](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/39015/1/2018SnapePhD.pdf), reproduces Sakuma's strong-inversion templates from even continued fractions. This provides an explicit starting diagram for the inversion cases. It does not yet give your asserted recurrence for the marked Watson quotient. In Watson 1311.1085 I found no general two-bridge/(1,1) continued-fraction formula of the remembered kind; his two-bridge branch links in the torus computations arise from lens-space fillings.

There is a decisive distinction: the rational tangle presenting the two-bridge knot kappa is NOT the quotient tangle covering E(kappa). Starting at e and applying the usual rational-tangle boundary-twist and rotation matrices keeps the actual tangle rational, with solid-torus cover. It cannot give E(kappa) for any nontrivial kappa. Even arbitrary rational-tangle sums produce arborescent tangles with graph-manifold covers, so they do not provide the hyperbolic two-bridge exteriors either.

This does not forbid a genuine 2x2 recurrence obtained another way. It requires an actual nonrational operation/seed or a larger intermediate interface, with its effect on the final bracket proved. A two-dimensional final skein module does not imply a two-dimensional interface throughout the construction. This is the first thing the proposed theory page must specify.

For additional explicit inversion diagrams, [Di Prisa–Framba, AGT 25 (2025), arXiv:2303.08794](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08794), treats two-bridge butterfly links and equivariant concordance. It is relevant diagrammatic prior art, not a Jones-detection result. I found no all-two-bridge theorem in this bounded read; that is not an exhaustive novelty determination.

## 2. What the extreme-term argument would need

Your reading of Snape's Proposition 3.4.4 as a mechanism is right: an extremal FAMILY of states has signed sum -1. It is not a unique-state or ordinary-adequacy theorem; the all-zero smoothing is not even in the required annular sector. Moving from that longitude P_-4 calculation to branch-set P_2 needs new sector bookkeeping.

I cannot honestly assert that all two-bridge Sakuma links are adequate, or that their x-coordinate has a unique extremal state. Nor have I found a counterexample to every suitable sector-specific formulation. Two cautions prevent inheriting the conclusion from the alternating input diagram:

- B is an ordinary unknot. Its nontrivial crossing diagram cannot be adequate in the ordinary two-sided sense; a nonzero-crossing adequate knot diagram has nonzero Jones breadth. Annular-sector adequacy is a separate condition.
- A preferred nontrivial Sakuma link has determinant zero, so it cannot be a nonsplit alternating link. This does NOT rule out nonalternating adequacy of the whole link.

Accordingly, aim for a controlled extremal family with a proved nonzero total coefficient, not uniqueness of one state. The missing quotient construction and the noncancellation statement are two separate gates.

## 3. A finished subclass consequence we should state before doing more work

Using the banked U4 implication, flatness forces a_2(kappa)=0. Therefore, for EVERY strong inversion, flatness is already excluded for:

1. **Nontrivial torus knots:** a_2(T(p,q))=(p²-1)(q²-1)/24>0.
2. **Genus-one knots with nontrivial Alexander polynomial:** Conway=1+a_2 z², so a_2!=0. This includes every genus-one alternating knot, hence all genus-one two-bridge/double-twist knots, by the [Crowell–Murasugi genus theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.09850).
3. **Nontrivial knots admitting an integer L-space surgery:** their Alexander exponents satisfy

       a_2 = n_r²-n_(r-1)²+n_(r-2)²-... > 0,
       0<n_1<...<n_r.

   Pair successive square differences, with an extra n_1² if necessary. The exponent form is [Ozsváth–Szabó, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0303017); their knot-Floer description and genus detection exclude r=0 for a nontrivial knot. Mirroring covers either surgery sign.

These are consequences of our existing obstruction, with its inherited status, not claims of new priority. They make T(2,m) a calibration of a mechanism rather than an enlargement of the detected class. They also show why the proposed theorem would not be our first positive strongly-invertible subclass result.

The genuine new two-bridge problem starts at genus >=2 with a_2=0. Our b(31,13), Conway 1-2z^4, is already there. For a concrete genus-two family, the four-band Seifert plumbing with diagonal parameters a,b,c,d has

    Conway = 1+(ab+ad+cd)z²+abcd z^4.

Thus ab+ad+cd=0, with nonzero parameters, supplies an explicit residual locus after conventions and the inversion are fixed. A concrete pilot is

    K_n = C(2,-4n,2,2n),       n>=1,
    fraction = (32n²-1)/(16n²+2n),
    Conway(K_n)=1-2n² z^4.

The four-band diagonal is (1,2n,1,-n), so the displayed polynomial follows by the same continuant identity. Its degree makes the genus exactly two, and a_2=0 for the whole family; n=1 is our 31/18 control. This gives an explicit family where a branch-sector theorem would add something beyond U4. No claim about its Sakuma x or either inversion's extreme term is made here.

## 4. Tunnel number one does not make the map problem thin

The map-level shortcut has a specific obstruction. [Ozsváth–Szabó, Theorem 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0303017), says an alternating knot admitting an integral L-space surgery must be a (2,2m+1) torus knot. Hence a hyperbolic two-bridge kappa has NO nonzero-integer-surgery branch closure of minimal reduced Khovanov rank, for any inversion: such a closure would force an L-space via the spectral sequence. In particular those closures cannot be delta-thin.

One thin nonzero-integer-surgery closure would finish a flat case using U4 and item 3 above. But no such closure is supplied merely by two-bridgeness or tunnel number one. A proof forcing thinness under full bracket flatness would be the substantive new implication, not a standard property of the subclass. I found no uniform tunnel-one description of the required saddle maps in this read.
