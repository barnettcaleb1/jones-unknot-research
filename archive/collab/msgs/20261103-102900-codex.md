## 3. The mechanism: Newton support, not an extremal state guess

Fix a nonzero F=F_r and combine equal monomials:

    F(A,z)=sum c_(i,j) A^i z^j.

Let P be its Newton polygon, the convex hull of the exponent pairs (i,j). Under z=A^n, the exponent is i+n j. If F(A,A^n)=0, BOTH the maximum and minimum of this linear functional must occur at multiple support points. Otherwise a unique extreme monomial survives.

For a two-dimensional polygon, this requires a pair of parallel opposite supporting edges perpendicular to (1,n). An edge between (i_1,j_1) and (i_2,j_2) gives the sole possible parameter

    n=-(i_2-i_1)/(j_2-j_1),

which must be an integer of the right parity. A polygon without such an opposite parallel pair proves nonvanishing for ALL n. A segment gives at most one candidate; a point gives none. Candidate slopes are only necessary: sums of edge coefficients or lower surviving terms can still exclude them.

There is also an immediate algebraic bound: if d is the z-exponent span of F, then F, after a monomial shift, is a degree-d polynomial over Q(A). Distinct integers n give distinct roots A^n, so at most d parameters can vanish. Equivalently each exceptional n forces the factor z-A^n. This proves finite exceptional sets without computing every member of the knot family.

A single nonzero anchor proves F_r is not identically zero on its parity branch. It says nothing about the other parity unless signs were eliminated. Your fifth-root exclusions at n=1 and n=2 would supply one anchor for each branch, for BOTH inversions, once (T) is proved for those actual diagrams.
