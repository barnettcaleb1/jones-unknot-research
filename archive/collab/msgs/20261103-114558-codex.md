## 1. Why the universal mutation mechanism stops at genus two

For a closed oriented surface S_g, g>=1, the kernel of the orientation-preserving mapping-class action on the FULL Kauffman bracket skein module of S_g×I is

    g=1: {I,-I};
    g=2: {1,hyperelliptic involution};
    g>=3: {1}.                                                     (1)

**Proof.** The module has the free multicurve basis. A mapping class acts by permuting these basis elements, without a framing correction. Identity action therefore means fixing every essential unoriented simple-curve class. Such a mapping class commutes with every Dehn twist and hence lies in the mapping-class-group centre. The standard centre theorem gives (1). For the torus directly, a matrix in SL_2(Z) fixing the unoriented primitive slopes (1,0), (0,1), and (1,1) must be ±I. Conversely the listed involutions fix every multicurve. QED.

This is a direct deduction from [Przytycki's Theorem 3.1](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/E0003677/E0003677.pdf) and the curve-action kernel recorded by [Luo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9904020), not a novelty claim. It continues to hold after coefficient specialization in the full free module; finite-dimensional TQFT quotients are a different object.

Consequently “use a higher-genus hyperelliptic mutation with the same universal skein proof” is unavailable. A map preserving one specified closure or a restricted family of states can still lie outside this kernel. That context-specific possibility is not excluded.

An important source correction: [DGST's 2012 erratum](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2012/18-5v.pdf) retracts Proposition 2.7, the assertion reducing arbitrary genus-two mutation to handlebody mutation. All-colored Jones invariance survives by a new proof using the surface skein module. We must not use the retracted topology reduction.

## 2. Mutation cannot hyperbolize the standard doubles

The surviving DGST compression argument, together with Ruberman's theorem, gives hyperbolicity preservation under genus-two mutation, including compressible surfaces. Mutation is reversible, so hyperbolicity is preserved in BOTH directions and through finite sequences. Therefore neither J#J^r nor J#mirror(J), for nontrivial J, can become hyperbolic by this procedure.

There is a sharper deduction. The Alexander polynomial of either double is Delta_J^2 and is preserved by genus-two mutation. A nontrivial torus knot has a nonconstant squarefree Alexander polynomial, as seen from its cyclotomic formula, so it cannot be such a mutant. The unknot is also isolated under genus-two mutation: its solid-torus exterior contains no incompressible closed genus-two surface or incompressible torus, and the surviving compression proposition makes its mutations trivial.

Thus **any PRIME genus-two mutant of either nontrivial double must be a SATELLITE**. I did not prove that primeness itself is preserved; the Conway branched-cover argument does not extend automatically. Proof and precise citations: work/global_design/genus2_gate.md.

Hyperbolic imitation supplies no workaround from the inspected results. Silver–Whitten preserve Alexander information and provide ribbon concordance, not exact Jones. Ito's finite HOMFLY-coefficient matching leaves a high-degree tail after Jones specialization. His full quantum-representation-kernel theorem DOES give exact hyperbolization, but that is the existing faithfulness route; one scalar Jones equality does not produce its required kernel element. Details: work/global_design/hyperbolic_gate.md.

## 3. A clean distinction between algebraic and geometric input control

If an output knot meets each of several disjoint input balls in one proper arc, then it is

    L_0 # K_1 # ... # K_r,                                         (2)

where L_0 replaces those arcs by trivial arcs. The boundary spheres prove (2). The rank-one skein space gives effortless Jones multiplication, but the input knot factors survive topologically. Mirror factors do not cancel under connected sum. This is a statement about actual two-punctured balls, not a converse asserting that every rank-one skein tensor is geometrically split.

Cutting the input into more strands can remove those visible decomposing spheres, but introduces extra skein coordinates. There is an elementary algebraic criterion: a bilinear pairing B(x,y) constant on {ell(x)=1}×{m(y)=1} must factor as c ell(x)m(y). Expand x=x_0+v and y=y_0+w in the two measurement kernels to prove it. Thus universal independent-input control by just two closure values requires the appropriate rank-one pairing. Actual input realizability or correlated inputs may impose additional constraints; neither is assumed away.

For Watson's actual shell the missing coordinate is explicit. With x=(a,b), y=(c,d), D(x)=a+delta b and

    M=[[X,delta],[delta,delta^2]],

one has

    x M y^t = D(x)D(y)+(X-1)ac.                                    (3)

For the original shell X is the square of the figure-eight Jones polynomial, so X-1 is nonzero. Even setting y=bar(x) leaves the term (X-1)a bar(a); in the Laurent domain it vanishes only if a=0. Mirror correlation does not supply the missing control in this shell. This calculation uses bracket framings consistently; it is not an assertion about arbitrary correlated skein pairings. [Watson's construction](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~liam/files/preprint.pdf) preserves the WHOLE pairing (3) under opposing twists, not its replacement by two chosen closure values.

## 4. The genuine mechanism in Kanenobu–Lobb, and its limit

There is a good mechanism here: two specified ribbon resolutions that are actual unlinks let one transfer twists in opposite directions while preserving homology, hence Jones. [Lobb's Theorems 1.4 and 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.3985) make that precise.

But a universal one-band seed identified with K#mirror(K) is impossible. If R has ribbon fusion number f, then H_1(Sigma_2(R)) needs at most 2f generators. For K=#^m trefoil, the double has homology (Z/3)^(2m), so its fusion number is at least m. The bound is recalled in [Sarkar, Section 3](https://msp.org/agt/2020/20-2/agt-v20-n2-p14-p.pdf). Therefore no fixed one-band template covers arbitrary inputs. This does NOT exclude a construction restricted to Jones-unit inputs: their determinant is one, so this particular homology obstruction vanishes. A many-band construction would need an actual seed identification and controlled resolutions, not merely the phrase “braid next to its inverse.”

One recent lead also needs careful reading. Lamm–Tanaka's 2025 result says the partial knot of a one-region symmetric-union presentation of Kinoshita–Terasaka has Jones polynomial one. Such a presentation already exists with partial UNKNOT; it is not a counterexample. A nontrivial partial knot would be the additional, unestablished geometric input. Earlier explicit symmetric-union formulas show the output Jones depends on marked tangle information even when the partial knot is U. Sources and formula: work/global_design/relative_double_gate.md.
