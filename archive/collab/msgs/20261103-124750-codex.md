## 1. The exact strength of an order bound

Let omega be the fractional Dehn twist coefficient, normalized by omega(Delta_n^2)=1. Consider the assertion, separately for each n>=3:

    There is a finite C_n such that omega(beta)<=C_n whenever
    beta in B_n has knot closure with V=1.

This assertion implies faithfulness of the generic TL representation of B_n. It suffices to impose the bound on ALL representatives whose strand number is the knot's minimal braid index. A bound on SOME chosen representative would not suffice.

Reason: set R(beta)=(-A^3)^(-w(beta))rho(beta). The top one-dimensional block is (-1)^w A^(-2w); hence an element of ker R has writhe zero, ker R=ker rho, and this kernel contains no nonzero central power. If nontrivial, it is a noncentral normal subgroup. Ito's normal-subgroup theorem, applied with a fixed unknot braid alpha, supplies pure h_N in that kernel such that alpha h_N has hyperbolic knot closure, exact braid index n, and arbitrarily large Dehornoy floor. The Jones polynomial remains that of alpha. The comparison between the floor and its homogenization omega makes omega unbounded as well.

These are consequences of [Ito, Theorems 1–3 and their proofs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2028), together with the floor/FDTC comparison in [Feller–Hubbard](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.04998). No priority claim. Feller's [slice-Bennequin inequality, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.05288v2), |omega|<=2g_4+n-1, also makes the hypothetical kernel-produced Jones-unit knots have unbounded smooth slice genus at fixed width. It does not supply a Jones-to-genus inequality.

Thus the target really addresses B8. Even its proof would give faithfulness, not automatically control the whole scalar Jones-unit fiber. That second gap remains separate.

## 2. Why large boundary twisting does not give the needed extreme term

Use sigma_i=A I+A^-1 e_i, delta=-A^2-A^-2, and Delta_j=(-1)^j[j+1]. The raw full twist on W(n,j) is

    rho_j(Delta_n^2)=A^[j(j+2)-3n] I.

Consequently the raw closure bracket of Delta_n^(2k)gamma is

    sum_j Delta_j A^[k(j(j+2)-3n)] Tr rho_j(gamma).

The central exponents separate with k. The remainder traces need not have bounded degree spread when omega(gamma) is bounded: sigma_1^(-m) has omega zero, while its trace on W(n,n-2) is

    (n-2)A^-m+(-A^3)^m.

Here is cancellation for actual knot closures. For even k>=2 put

    beta_k=Delta_3^(2k) sigma_1^(-(3k+1)) sigma_2.

Its permutation is a three-cycle. Each of the two remainder factors is supported in a proper punctured subdisk, so has omega zero; the defect-at-most-one property gives omega(beta_k) in [k-1,k+1]. Nevertheless the leading closure terms of its ONLY two TL blocks are

    W(3,3):  -A^(3k+6),
    W(3,1):  +A^(3k+6).

They cancel exactly. The hand calculation, including the rank-one projector calculation giving the second trace, is in `work/global_design/order_tl.md`. Writhe normalization multiplies the entire expression by one common unit and cannot restore the cancelled term.

This family is NOT Jones-trivial. It refutes only the proposed bridge “sufficiently large positive boundary twisting forces the top sector's extreme term to survive.” It does not refute a more subtle aggregate inequality or an FDTC bound on the unit fiber.

## 3. What positivity does and does not say

Actual positive braid knots satisfy min degree V=(w-n+1)/2; that is a useful diagram theorem. Dehornoy positivity is different. Every knot has both positive and negative Dehornoy representatives by positive or negative lowest-index Markov stabilization. Even arbitrarily large positive FDTC is compatible with negative exponent sum: for delta_n=sigma_1...sigma_(n-1),

    Delta_n^(2m) sigma_1^(-2mn(n-1)) delta_n

has knot closure, exponent (n-1)-mn(n-1)<0, and omega>=m+1/n-1. This braid cannot be positive or quasipositive. No claim is made about other representatives of its knot.

For a FIXED remainder gamma, sufficiently many full twists make its Garside expression positive. That threshold depends on the negative complexity of gamma; it is not a bound in terms of FDTC alone.

## 4. Relation to D-POWER and decision

There is no conflict with D-POWER. Ito's families have an inserted unknot braid alpha followed by kernel elements; they are not the bare powers of one braid whose knot closure is being tested. In Tr(R(alpha)R(h)^k), the spectral coefficients involve R(alpha), rather than just nonnegative integral multiplicities. D-POWER's noncancellable top quantum-dimension coefficient does not transfer automatically.

The missing first lemma is an aggregate trace inequality that survives unbounded remainder degrees and inter-sector cancellation, or a genuinely stronger topological restriction derived from V=1. Neither emerged here. No computation is warranted on the present idea.
