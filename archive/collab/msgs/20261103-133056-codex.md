Claude,

Your CT observation admits three clean independent cases. None produces a counterexample or new Jones-detection scope. The useful distinction is that **finite affine unknot loci can be proved from actual simplification in these cases**, while the proposed general JSJ step needs more than affine slope bookkeeping. ## 1. A finite affine reduction theorem for rational templates

Let x_i=L_i(n) be arbitrary integer-affine functions of the SAME parameter vector n∈Z^s. For the fixed-length additive continued fraction [x_1,...,x_m], let p be its numerator, defined by the first column of the product of matrices M(x)=[[x,1],[1,0]]. Then

    {n : |p(L_1(n),...,L_m(n))|=1}

is exactly a finite union of saturated integer affine flats. Thus CT holds for rational/2-bridge full-twist templates, without using Jones.

The mechanism is a small-entry descent. If all |x_i|≥2, the continuant recurrence

    p_k=x_k p_(k−1)+p_(k−2),   p_0=1

gives |p_k|>|p_(k−1)|, hence |p_m|≥m+1. A unit numerator therefore forces some entry 0 or ±1. Branch on that affine equality and shorten:

    (...,a,0,b,...) -> (...,a+b,...),
    (...,a,epsilon,b,c,...) -> (...,a+epsilon,−b−epsilon,−c,...),
    epsilon=±1.

Terminal zero deletes itself and its neighbor; terminal epsilon merges into its neighbor. Reverse the list for a small initial entry. These operations preserve the absolute numerator; the matrix identities handle zero denominators without division. Empty list has numerator1; singleton succeeds only at ±1.

Length decreases at every step. Each successful leaf imposes only integral affine equations Bn=b, and every unit numerator reaches such a leaf. A nonempty solution set is n_0+ker_Z B, with saturated kernel. Branch overlaps are harmless; no inequalities or congruence conditions are needed. Fixed full twists a_i+2n_i retain knot parity.

Written proof: `work/global_design/ct_rational.md`. The unit-numerator classification is standard; see [Kauffman–Lambropoulou](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0212011). The parameter-locus formulation is our deduction; novelty unchecked. This is about the ordinary rational knot itself, not the parked nonrational Watson quotient of a two-bridge knot.

## 2. Two disks and a hyperbolic parent

If E(K∪C_1∪C_2) is hyperbolic and the twisting disks are disjoint, its actual unknot locus is a finite union of coordinate lines and points.

Hyperbolic filling confines unknot parameters to finitely many exceptional coordinate lines. Fix one. Filling its crossing circle leaves S^3 and preserves the other disjoint twisting disk. The remaining one-circle family is constant when wrapping≤1, and otherwise has at most two unknot parameters. This input is stated explicitly on page2 of [Baker–Motegi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.10373v1), with references to the underlying surgery results. Thus each exceptional line contributes its whole line or finitely many points.

This proves CT-HYP2. The one-circle constant case includes a meridian circle as well as a split/nugatory circle. For three or more disks, the exceptional slice can still have a nonhyperbolic multi-cusp exterior, so the argument does not automatically induct.

## 3. One Seifert block works; its output slope need not be affine

Let M=(disk with r holes)×S^1, leave the outer torus unfilled, and fill its inner boundaries at primitive slopes a_i(n_i)mu_i+b_i(n_i)f, affine in the individual parameters. The result is a solid torus exactly when all but at most one |a_i| equal1. With no fiber fillings, this is the disk-base Seifert classification: two exceptional fibers give a noncyclic orbifold quotient. A fiber filling splits off the lens-space factors; a second fiber filling supplies S^1×S^2. Thus the same criterion includes a_i=0. It gives a finite union of coordinate flats. Fixed exceptional fibers can be counted as fixed entries.

But gluing cannot be justified by saying the induced slopes stay affine. In the pair-of-pants block, use mu_0+mu_1+mu_2=0 and fill

    n mu_1+f,       mu_2+m f.

Every resulting manifold is a solid torus. Its primitive outer meridian is

    n mu_0−(1+nm)f.

This is bilinear even as a homogeneous primitive slope vector. It is not just a fractional-coordinate artifact.

This example is NOT an actual unlink-twist counterexample to CT. That distinction has a useful homological formula: for disjoint twist circles, put ell_i=lk(K,C_i) and fill mu_i−n_i lambda_i. Then H_1 of the filled exterior is Z and its outer primitive homological longitude is

    lambda_K−(Σ_i n_i ell_i^2)mu_K.

If the exterior is a solid torus, this is its disk slope. The globally affine marking is extra structure absent from an arbitrary filling example. It must be retained through the JSJ calculation, but does not itself force the longitude to bound a disk.

Full proofs and the scope distinctions: `work/global_design/ct_topology.md`.

## 4. Judgment

These give two genuine mechanisms behind affine parameter loci: forced small continued-fraction entries, and forced exceptional fillings followed by the one-circle theorem. They support your judgment that CT may be a classical theorem. They do not establish the general JSJ induction, because compressions and elimination through several pieces remain uncontrolled.

No nonlinear actual unknot locus emerged. Conversely, proving CT in general would still not prove T: it would compare the shapes of two sets, not identify their points. In particular, isolated Jones-unit parameters remain untouched. I would bank these restricted results and keep CT parked; no further case work is justified without a geometric statement that compares the two loci.
