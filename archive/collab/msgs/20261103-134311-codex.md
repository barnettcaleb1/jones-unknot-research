## 1. Generic Zariski density from the existing first-order calculation

Let rho be the determinant-one Jones representation on I_2, and substitute t=−exp(h). [Kasahara, Theorem1.1 and the proof of Theorem1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0012216) give

    rho(I_2)=I mod h,
    span_Q{first-order terms}=Gamma_(0,2), dimension14,
    [Gamma_(0,2),Gamma_(0,2)]=Gamma_(2,0), dimension10.

These two subspaces are respectively the traceless self-adjoint and skew-adjoint parts of sl_5 for the invariant form at the symplectic point.

Let H be the generic Zariski closure of rho(I_2), extended to Q((h)). For every g=I+O(h) in the image, log g lies in Lie(H). Here is a formal proof that avoids an analytic closure assumption: for a defining polynomial P of H, every h-coefficient of P(exp(z log g)) is polynomial in z. It vanishes at every integer z because g^z∈H. All those coefficient polynomials vanish identically; differentiating at z=0 gives tangent membership.

Choose14 such logarithms whose first-order terms span Gamma_(0,2), divide by h, and choose10 brackets with independent Gamma_(2,0) leading terms. Their24 leading terms are independent, so dim Lie(H)≥24. Since det rho=1 on I_2, H=SL_5. This uses only the displayed first-order module and its bracket, not all orders of Kasahara's filtration.

At a transcendental parameter, the coefficient-field isomorphism and extension of scalars preserve this Zariski-density statement.

## 2. An explicit indefinite interval and a trivial starting point

This can be checked directly in the W(6,0) cap lattice. Let delta=−A²−A^−2 and let G_(C,D)=delta^(number of loops in C∪D). Divide the whole Gram matrix by delta. At delta=0, in the five cap states

    rho(T_c)=t^−9 tau²

A continuous gauge of the nondegenerate Hermitian forms therefore gives a family into a fixed SU(2,3), starting with the trivial representation on I_2. No regular intertwiner to Kasahara's particular basis at the endpoint is required.

## 3. Topological density and the continuity obstruction

Choose a transcendental t on that interval. The real Zariski closure is SU(2,3), by §1 and the invariant form. A separating twist has infinite order and is elliptic, so its powers have an infinite compact closure containing a positive-dimensional torus. The Lie algebra of the topological closure of rho_t(I_2) is therefore nonzero. It is invariant under Ad(rho_t(I_2)); real Zariski density makes it an ideal of su(2,3). Simplicity forces the whole Lie algebra, hence topological density.

Consequently every continuous real character of rho_t(I_2), with its matrix topology, is zero: it extends to the connected semisimple group, which has no nonzero continuous real character. If d_hat does factor through generic rho, its induced character is necessarily discontinuous in this topology. This does NOT rule out such a character: dense subgroups can have discontinuous characters.

## 4. What happens to the rotation idea

The homogeneous Dupont–Guichardet–Wigner function Phi lives on the universal cover of SU(2,3), with Phi(z)=1 on the covering-center generator. A generic lift can send a matrix-kernel element to z^k, giving a nonzero value despite its identity matrix. [Funar–Pitsch, §4 and §5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.0302) explicitly use this central winding; it cannot be discarded.

The deformation in §2 does solve that issue for the GENERIC kernel: for x∈I_2 take the path s↦rho_s(x). Pointwise multiplication makes these paths a homomorphic lift. A generic kernel element has the constant identity path, so Q_t=Phi∘rho_t_tilde kills that kernel exactly. At transcendental t it therefore descends to the specialized Jones image as well.

However, Q_t cannot be a homomorphism on I_2. The lifted image is dense in the universal cover: if C is its closure, C times the covering center is dense; C is normal in that dense subgroup and therefore in the entire cover. Its Lie algebra is zero or all by simplicity. The zero case makes it central, contradicting dense projection. Hence C is the full cover.

If Q_t were additive, the continuous defect Phi(xy)−Phi(x)−Phi(y) would vanish on a dense product set, hence everywhere. Phi would be a continuous homomorphism on a connected semisimple covering group, hence zero, contradicting Phi(z)=1. Thus its bounded defect class is nonzero. The same argument applies to every homomorphic lift at this specialization.

So this candidate reaches the matrix image but does not recover d_hat. Vanishing of the classical Meyer cocycle on Torelli cannot repair it: that cocycle uses the symplectic representation, which is trivial there; the generic Jones image is dense and nontrivial.
