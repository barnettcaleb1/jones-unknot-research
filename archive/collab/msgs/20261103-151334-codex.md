## 1. Lemma C is true for tangles

**Finite separation theorem.** Given a 3|3 curve c and any finite list c_1,...,c_m of 3|3 curves not isotopic to c, there is a trivial three-strand tangle S for which c is reducing and every c_j is nonreducing. No same-splitting hypothesis is needed.

The proof connects compression bodies with reduced words in a free product. Lift to Sigma_2 and cut along the separating lift gamma of c. The two sides Q_1,Q_2 are once-punctured tori. Each other separating lift gamma_j intersects gamma; in minimal position its segments in the Q_i are essential proper arcs. Capping each Q_i turns each segment into a nonzero primitive torus homology class.

Now compress Sigma_2 times I along gamma on the inner side. The resulting compression body C has two inner torus boundaries and

    pi_1(C)=Z^2 * Z^2.

Each gamma_j is a reduced alternating word with nonzero syllables. Choose primitive maps ell_i:Z^2->Z avoiding all the finitely many syllables, and fill the inner tori by solid tori killing ker ell_i. The result is a genus-two handlebody H. Gamma bounds its separating disk, but every gamma_j survives as a nonempty reduced word in pi_1(H)=Z*Z and therefore cannot bound a disk.

This also gives an explicit choice: ell_i(a,b)=a+N_i b, with at most one forbidden integer N_i per syllable. With M_i syllables, one of 0,...,M_i works. We need neither Masur's limit set nor a disk-uniqueness theorem.

The tangle realization is direct. In each three-punctured disk bounded by c, choose an arc whose lift has the selected primitive meridian slope. The B_3 action supplies every slope. Join the two unused punctures by an arc crossing c once, disjoint from the first two, and push all three arcs into the ball. This is a trivial tangle with cover exactly H. Any reducing disk for c_j would lift to a disk in H, contradicting the surviving word.

## 2. Q2: unbounded finite orders are unavoidable

Fix a reducing pair (c_0,S), and normalize Hhat=H/delta at zeta=exp(i*pi/4). For every r>=1 there is f_r in gamma_r(I_2) with

    0 != F_r(A)=Hhat(S,rho(f_r)v_c0),

Then c_r=f_r(c_0) has the same homological splitting as c_0 and is nonreducing. Thus the statement is stronger than producing formal approximate zeros: these are actual curves with certified nonzero pairings of arbitrarily high finite order.

Proof: by G2-DENSE, rho(I_2) is Zariski dense in SL_5. Every lower-central subgroup is still Zariski dense, by the commutator morphism and perfectness of SL_5. The regular endpoint normalization gives rho(I_2)=I mod h, and commutators give rho(gamma_r I_2)=I mod h^r. The regular function

    F(g)=Hhat(S,g v_c0)

Normalization is harmless but essential: P_c=(A^6 tau-I)/(A^12-1) is a regular rank-one projector at zeta, so choose a column giving v_c0 regular and nonzero there. The separating-twist quadratic identity in our density note directly establishes the endpoint normalization in the cap lattice; no regularity of a Kasahara-to-TL change of basis is assumed.
