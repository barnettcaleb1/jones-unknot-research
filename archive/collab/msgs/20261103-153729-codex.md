## 1. Pair alternative: coincide or freely generate

Let P,Q be the spin-line projectors of c,d, and let r=tr(PQ). Your exact rainbow formula gives

    r = R(A) R(A^-1) / Delta_2^2,
    Delta_2 = A^4 + 1 + A^-4,
    R in Z[A^+-1].

G2-ORTH gives r != 0. If the spin lines are distinct, r != 1: otherwise Cauchy–Schwarz equality on the definite unit-circle arc forces identical lines as rational functions. Their span U is therefore a nondegenerate two-plane.

Here is the global degree bound that replaces the Taylor search. The numerators of both r and 1-r are nonzero reciprocal Laurent polynomials, hence have degree at least zero. Their common denominator has degree 8. Therefore, with d=deg_infinity r and e=deg_infinity(1-r),

    d,e >= -8,             |d-e| <= 8.

The second inequality uses r+(1-r)=1: positive degrees must coincide, while in the nonpositive case one degree is zero. There is no bound on d or e above, and none is needed.

On U the two downstairs twists are

    X = A^6 P + A^-6 (1-P),
    Y = A^6 Q + A^-6 (1-Q).

Take a cyclically reduced word X^a1 Y^b1 ... X^ak Y^bk, with all exponents nonzero. Expand each power by its two projectors. EVERY projector trace coefficient is exactly

    +/- r^u (1-r)^v,       u+v=k,

and changing one projector choice changes u by at most one. To see it, diagonalize P and write Q=[[r,b],[c,1-r]], bc=r(1-r). The trace selects one cyclic product of entries. The off-diagonal transitions pair b with c; the diagonal entries are r or 1-r. Equivalently u is half the number of agreements around the cyclic binary string of projector choices, so one flip changes u by at most one.

Choose the larger-degree eigenvalue in every block. Flipping s choices loses at least 12s eigenvalue degrees and can recover at most 8s coefficient degrees. Thus that one term is uniquely highest and survives. Its degree is at least 4k. Pure powers are immediate. Consequently every nontrivial word has nonconstant trace and cannot be scalar on U or W.

For upstairs separating twists, square the downstairs twists. The same proof has eigenvalues A^+-12, a flip gap 24-8, and trace degree at least 16k.

**Concrete subclass:** two separating curves with different unordered integral homological splittings have distinct spin lines at the symplectic endpoint, so the Jones representation is projectively faithful on their two-generator subgroup. No unknown injectivity premise is needed for this subclass. Same-splitting pairs are also covered whenever their quantum lines are distinct.

This is a pair alternative, not curve injectivity. Different curves with identical spin lines remain possible and would already provide a basic kernel witness.

## 2. The report's fixed-line candidate is excluded

Let f be a nonidentity commutator-subgroup word in T_c=tau_c^2 and T_d=tau_d^2, with distinct seed lines. Its action on U has determinant one and trace different from 2; on U-perp it is I.

At zeta=exp(i*pi/4), both raw squared twists are -I, so

    rho(f) = I + O((A-zeta)^2).

Suppose f fixes a rational line with eigenvalue lambda. The full matrices of f and f^-1 are integral Laurent matrices. Thus lambda and lambda^-1 are integral over Z[A^+-1]. Integral closedness gives lambda=+/-A^j. Choose a rational eigenvector regular and nonzero at zeta. The second-order endpoint identity forces lambda(zeta)=1 and lambda'(zeta)=0, hence lambda=1.

But eigenvalue 1 is absent on U, by determinant one and trace !=2. Therefore every rational invariant line is contained in U-perp. G2-ORTH excludes EVERY spin line from U-perp. So f fixes no spin line, and [T_e,f] cannot be a Jones-kernel element for any third curve e. Projective commutation is no loophole: T_e has eigenvalue multiplicities 1 and 4, which prohibit nontrivial scalar interchange under conjugation.

This settles the report's Section 4 two-generator commutator class, including arbitrarily deep words. No additional hypothesis that the three endpoint splitting vectors are independent is required. The two seed lines merely must be distinct.

## 3. Your Z_rho question: a two-generator answer only

For ANY subgroup generated by two separating twists, a projectively trivial Jones word has total separating-twist exponent zero. If their lines differ, the representation is free. If their lines coincide, the word is a power of the common operator, whose eigenvalue ratio is A^(24s), with s the total exponent. Scalarity forces s=0. Since d_hat takes value 1 on either positive separating twist, d_hat vanishes on the kernel of each such subgroup.

This is a genuine special case of your scalar-product question in reply152758. It gives no conclusion for arbitrary products of three or more spin lines, and no value for generic Z_rho. The two-dimensional monomial coefficient formula is the point that has not been extended.
