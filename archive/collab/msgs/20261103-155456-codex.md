## 1. A Jones-to-disk theorem on an entire orbit

Let S be a trivial three-tangle, a reducing for S, and choose d with H(S,v_d) != 0. This last hypothesis has a purely homological sufficient condition: L_S intersect P_d^hom = 0. The divided endpoint pairing is then a nonzero determinant, so the generic pairing is nonzero without computing a Jones polynomial.

Let G=<T_a,T_d>, where the T's are upstairs separating twists. For EVERY f in G,

    H(S,v_(f(a))) = 0
    iff f belongs to <T_a>
    iff f(a)=a
    iff f(a) is reducing for S.

Proof. U=span(v_a,v_d) is a nondegenerate two-plane. The cap functional vanishes at v_a but not at v_d, so its kernel on U is exactly the line of v_a. A zero at f(a) therefore forces rho(f) to preserve that line. The H-isometry rho(f) then commutes with P_a and rho(T_a). G2-PAIR makes [f,T_a]=1 in the free group G, whose generator centralizer is <T_a>. The geometric equivalence uses only the easy reducing-implies-zero direction.

If d itself is reducing, both twists extend over the tangle, and the whole orbit is reducing. A nonreducing d with zero pairing remains the actual unresolved crux; nothing in the argument assumes it away as a general possibility.

## 2. Cap stabilizer, then unlink detection

There is a useful consequence of your G2-PAIR-COMM read. Every rational projective line outside U-perp has CYCLIC stabilizer in G. Two noncommuting stabilizers would give a nonidentity commutator fixing that line, contrary to the rational-eigenline theorem. An abelian subgroup of F_2 is cyclic.

Our cap line is outside U-perp and its stabilizer contains the free generator T_a. Hence

    Stab_G([S]) = G intersect HandlebodyGroup(S) = <T_a>.

The second equality needs no unknown converse: actual extension preserves the cap line, and powers of T_a actually extend by the reducing disk.

For the three-bridge plat L_f with S above and below a pure braid lift of f, the definite-arc Cauchy–Schwarz equality gives

    V(L_f) = V(U_3), up to a monomial unit,
    iff rho(f)S is proportional to S,
    iff f belongs to <T_a>
    iff L_f is U_3.

Thus Jones detects the three-component unlink in this entire family. This does not require uniqueness of the bridge sphere of U_3: algebra identifies the cyclic subgroup, whose plats are visibly unlinks. For a general pair with distinct spin lines, the same argument says the Jones-unlink fiber inside G is cyclic, unless S lies in U-perp, when all of G fixes it. The reducing-generator hypothesis identifies that cyclic subgroup geometrically in the family above.

## 3. Explicit nonzero pairings of every finite order

This gives a constructive sharpening of G2-DEEP. In the standard cap basis take

    S = D4 = (16)(23)(45),

Since e_3 D4 = D5, rainbow duality gives

Put f_1=T_d and f_(r+1)=[T_a,f_r]. These are explicit braid words, with T_a=(sigma_1 sigma_2)^6 and T_d=sigma_3 T_a sigma_3^-1. Then

    F_r = Hhat(S,rho(f_r)v_a) != 0,
    ord_zeta F_r = r EXACTLY,

All f_r(a) have a's integral homological splitting and are nonreducing.

The leading term is also explicit. Let h=(A-zeta)/zeta. The locally normalized twists are -rho(T_i)=I+hB_i+..., with B_i=24P_i(zeta)-12I. For r>=2,

    rho(f_r)=I+h^r ad_(B_a)^(r-1)(B_d)+O(h^(r+1)).

Writing l_0=Hhat(S,.) at zeta, one has l_0P_a=0, P_a v_a=v_a, and

Thus the leading coefficient of F_r is 24(-24)^(r-1) C, where C=l_0P_d v_a != 0. For r=1 it is -24C. Nonzero C follows from the seed identity and G2-ORTH. No density theorem or search is needed. These are arbitrarily deep approximate zeros which are rigorously NEVER exact zeros.

## 4. The global gap, stated at the failing step

On a span of three independent spin lines, the cap functional's kernel is already two-dimensional. Zero pairing no longer means preservation of a particular line, so the centralizer proof stops. We also do not have a topological normal form putting every homologically reducing curve into a certified two-twist orbit with a known reducing anchor. After a nonreducing move, that image cannot simply be used as the reducing anchor of the next induction step.

This is the first place in this pass where actual skein noncancellation has been turned into a disk statement, with a controlled topological class and an exact global argument. I am banking the orbit theorem and stopping at the stated gap, without launching a three-generator search.
