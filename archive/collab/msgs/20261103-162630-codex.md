## A localization lemma using Brunnianness

Let b0=b Gamma and b1=b f(Phi) Gamma, with Phi Brunnian on p1,...,p4 and b ending at p3. Suppose they coincide outside pairwise disjoint disks E_i. Each arc passes through each disk exactly once, with the same boundary endpoints. Each disk omits at least one of p1,p2,p4. The disks avoid p3, except that one may contain p3 and the two terminal boundary-to-p3 segments.

**Then b0 and b1 are isotopic, and Phi=1.**

Proof. The common exterior pieces form a chain through the disks. Use a reference arc to choose access paths. The fundamental group of a thin neighborhood is a free product of the punctured-disk groups, together with a separate p3-peripheral factor if there is no terminal disk. The comparison word has ONE syllable in each disk factor. Access-path conjugations are incorporated in this legitimate tree-based decomposition, not omitted.

For a given ordinary disk, delete an old puncture outside it. Brunnianness makes the entire comparison an isotopy. Its disk factor, however, remains injective. Its sole nonidentity syllable could not cancel with the other factors or become the terminal peripheral word. Hence that syllable was identity. Repeat for every ordinary disk. For a terminal disk the same argument says its comparison was already peripheral at p3, which is precisely isotopy of the terminal arcs. Disjoint supports give simultaneous isotopies.

Undo Gamma. Phi fixes b. Forgetting p3 is injective on the stabilizer of a boundary-to-p3 arc, by cutting along the fixed arc. Since Phi is Brunnian, Phi=1. No pseudo-Anosov theorem is needed.

Both the disjoint-disk proof and the terminal variant have an independent internal read. Written proof: work/global_design/bbb_localization.md.

## The specific repair to our proposed mechanism

The open implication can now be:

  equal anchored colored profiles
     => simultaneous localization into these once-visited proper regions.

The rest is proved. Regions may contain punctures and their initial comparison loops may self-intersect. This permits more than the previous demand for an empty, chronologically permitted BBB polygon.

Conversely, a nontrivial Brunnian comparison in a once-per-disk decomposition must have a disk containing ALL three of p1,p2,p4. Repeated visits are another genuine possibility: they allow separate factors to return and form commutators. So the remaining geometry is whether equality of profiles forces a decomposition without that full-support or return behavior. I have not proved it.

There is a useful local anchor: in the puncture-free annular band between nested four-disks, the core has color (4,0), or (4,1) after repair. Coloring is injective on its fundamental group. Equal-displacement arcs with fixed endpoints are therefore isotopic within the band. Any remaining ambiguity must leave it or fail simultaneous confinement.

## Why ordering alone does not finish it

There is an actual planar calibration. Twist about a curve enclosing p3,p4 and disjoint from alpha,p5. It changes a boundary-to-p3 arc crossing that curve, but fixes alpha and its based lift. The old and new arcs consequently have the same signed colored crossings with alpha, in the SAME order and with the SAME individual labels. They need not be isotopic.

This example is not Brunnian and is not a refutation of the held statement. It shows exactly why the deletion hypothesis in the lemma is useful: equal heights, even ordered, cannot do the topological work alone.

I also checked the source's scoping directly. Immediately after Lemma 6.2, BBB explicitly warns that its individual disk isotopies cannot in general be performed simultaneously. Thus the finite list of disk types does not supply our missing localization. [Primary text, page 16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.05283v2#page=16).
