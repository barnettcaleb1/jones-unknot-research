## Positive re-entry is insufficient

The eight-letter word w=(xyz y^-1)^2 has four cyclic sign runs and is neither allowed positive-fraction form. Nevertheless its Burau matrix is strictly positive for EVERY t>1. Indeed

    rho(xyz)=tI+t(t-1)(t²,t,1)^T(1,1,1),

and the only potentially problematic row of rho(xyz y^-1) is

    ((t-1)², t-1+t^-1, (t-1)²(t²+1)/t),

which is strictly positive. Thus even re-entry of the entire cone does not produce a positive-fraction normal form. This example is not Brunnian; it rules out only the unrestricted inference.

## Four-run Brunnian words can have arbitrarily large commutator length

Set

    B_n=(xy)^n (yx)^-n (zyx)^n (zxy)^-n.

These actual words are Brunnian and cyclically reduced, with exactly four cyclic sign runs. Define q as the number of xyz subwords minus the number of z^-1 y^-1 x^-1 subwords in the reduced word. Maximal-cancellation accounting at three seams gives defect at most6, so a commutator has |q|<=18, and a product of r commutators has |q|<=24r-6. But q(B_n)=-(n-1). Hence

    cl_K(B_n)>=ceil((n+5)/24).

B_20 cannot be a single commutator in K=ker(P4->P3), even after conjugacy or inversion. Therefore the Brunnian-only proposal 'four runs => [positive fraction, positive word]' fails substantially. No claim that B_n lies in BI_4, or about its commutator length in the whole B4, is made. A classification using the EXTRA t=-1 condition remains untested.

## A finite target from cyclic symmetry and hypergeometric monodromy

In our same basis set

    C=[0 0 t^-1; 1 0 0; 0 1 0],
    R=R_1=[t² t-1 t-1; 0 1 0; 0 0 1].

Then C³=t^-1I and C R_i C^-1=R_(i+1). Also RC is the companion matrix of (s-t)(s²+s+1), while C is the companion matrix of s³-t^-1. Their difference has rank one. This is an explicit calibration of the known Burau/hypergeometric connection, not a novelty claim for the connection.

Projectively these matrices give Z*C3; its index-three free subgroup maps to our canonical R1,R2,R3. Projective injectivity on that subgroup is equivalent to injectivity on Z*C3: an additional kernel element would have cube1 and thus be conjugate to a nontrivial C3 element, which C does not kill. Generic scalar kernels on the free subgroup are excluded by differentiating at t=1: off-diagonal row-i derivatives give the three exponent sums, hence all vanish; determinant then forces the scalar to be1.

A SINGLE rational specialization t0>2 would suffice if we could construct nonempty projective domains X+,X-, put X=X+ union X- and Y=CX union C²X, and verify

    X intersect Y=empty,
    RY subset X+, R X+ subset X+,
    R^-1 Y subset X-, R^-1 X- subset X-.

These finitely many inclusions imply full free-product ping-pong. Finite unions of rational polyhedral cones would make the required checks exact and finite. The Long/Brunnian reduction then gives generic Burau4 faithfulness. This would still NOT prove T; faithfulness is only the necessary rung we are addressing.

No domains or cone search are supplied. Related finite cone constructions exist for a different three-dimensional hypergeometric group (Frieden-Gelinas-Soucy, arXiv:2111.14692); they do not automatically apply to these parameters. I have parked this explicit certificate as an alternative if the geometric route does not produce a mechanism, without changing the held target.
