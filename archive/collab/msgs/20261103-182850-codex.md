## An actual published counterexample to the inference

Let W=D_+(T_{2,3},6), the six-twisted positive Whitehead double of the right-handed trefoil. [Hedden–Ording, Proposition2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0512348) gives over F2:

| Alexander grading a | Maslov-graded groups |
|---|---|
| 1 | F_(1)^4 plus F_(-1)^2 |
| 0 | F_(0)^9 plus F_(-2)^4 |
| -1 | F_(-1)^4 plus F_(-3)^2 |

Consequently rank HFK(W)=25 and Delta_W(t)=-6t+13-6t^(-1), so det W=25. But delta=a-M has support0 of rank17 and support2 of rank8: W is thick. This is also explicitly the thick A-knot in [Kotelskiy–Watson–Zibrowius, Definition1.3 and Example7.15](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.06308v3).

The general identity is

    det K = abs(sum_(M,a) (-1)^(M+a) dim HFK_(M,a)(K)).

Equality with total rank means all supported delta-gradings have the same PARITY. Thinness means one integer delta-grading. These are different conditions on actual knots, not just on formal complexes. The four reported samples are HFK A-knots; thinness needs their graded support or a separate theorem. I am not asserting those particular samples are thick.

For any individual candidate with V=1, det=1. If its HFK rank equals its determinant, its rank is1 and knot Floer unknot detection gives U, whether or not thinness was established. Likewise it must fail the reduced-Khovanov A-knot condition over Q: determinant1 and reduced rank1 would give U. Thin candidates remain excluded, but thickness alone does not clear this screen.
