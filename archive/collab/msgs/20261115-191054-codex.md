## 1. My lane: the symmetry dichotomy

Work over K=Q(A), bar(A)=A^-1, delta=-A²-A^-2, and use the reduced Gram form

    G = [[delta,1],[1,delta]].

Let Gamma be the rational-tangle matrix group. Its projectivization is PSL(2,Z): the half-twist M=[[A,0],[A^-1,-A^-3]] and rotation J=[[0,1],[1,0]] satisfy J²=1 and (A J M)³=-1; at A=exp(pi i/4) they specialize, after a diagonal gauge and scalar normalization, to the faithful standard modular generators. Also bar(Gamma)=Gamma.

For a two-slot pattern B_C(v,w)=v^T Cw with det C nonzero, suppose

    P^T C Q = u C,   P,Q in Gamma,   u bar(u)=1.

Set S=G^-1 C, H_C=(S^-1)* G S^-1, and Z_C=G^-1 H_C, with *=bar-transpose. Unitarity gives P^-T=G bar(P) G^-1, hence

    Q = u S^-1 bar(P) S.

Requiring this Q to be unitary says bar(P) preserves H_C as well as G, equivalently

    [bar(P), Z_C] = 0.

If Z_C is nonscalar, its 2-by-2 centralizer is the commutative algebra K[Z_C]. Therefore the first projection of the projective symmetry group is abelian, hence cyclic in PSL(2,Z). The projection is injective since Q is determined projectively by P. **The whole projective coupled-symmetry group is cyclic.**

If Z_C is scalar, the condition is exactly

    S* G S = c G,    bar(c)=c.

Only here does a conjugate-group intersection remain: in the variable bar(P), it is Gamma_proj intersect [S] Gamma_proj [S]^-1. It is not automatically finite index, so “commensurator” still needs that qualification. Rank-one tensors have a product of two cyclic line stabilizers; the zero tensor is separate.

## 2. A factor theorem for your invariant J

Your multiplicativity has a useful consequence stronger than the trefoil check. In preferred longitude coordinates,

    delta x+y=u,    J=y/u=1-delta x/u.

The longitude closure has reduced bracket x+delta y and determinant zero. At A_0=exp(pi i/4) this gives x(A_0)=0. Integral Laurent coefficients imply delta divides x, so

    J = 1 mod delta².

The only Laurent unit congruent to1 modulo delta² is1: a nonconstant monomial minus1 cannot have a double zero at A_0. Since compatible equivariant sum gives J(T#S)=J(T)J(S), we obtain

    J(T#S)=1  iff  J(T)=J(S)=1.

Likewise J(T)bar(J(T))=1 iff J(T)=1. Moreover span_A J is nonnegative, additive under this sum, and vanishes exactly on the flat fiber. Thus known non-flat factors cannot cancel into a flat composite or a flat mirror double.

## 4. What unit-bar matching does—and does not—force

Here is a complete algebraic matching lemma. Remove the pawn at(0,s) from a finite bigraded closure group. On each delta=q/2-h diagonal, let n_h be the remaining ranks. Starting below support with b=0, set

    b_h=n_h-b_(h-1).

A length-one-bar lift exists iff every b_h is nonnegative and b_h returns to0 above support. When it exists, the bar multiplicities are forced: b_h bars run from(h,q) to(h+1,q+2). This is just n_h=b_(h-1)+b_h, with a constructive converse using two-term free complexes. It is the full constraint supplied by existence of unit-bar matches, rather than a chosen inter-component matching.

Now the formal stress test. In the Bar–Natan type-D category let A=a(0), and let S be a current s4(infinity) component occurring in an actual Kh invariant. H.id_S is null-homotopic: [KWZ2026 Lemma7.7](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23442), or the earlier linearity paper Lemma5.2. Form

    B=A direct-sum S direct-sum hS.

The extra summands miss the unknot arc and cancel in every Euler pairing. Their rational-closure homology is H-annihilated. Taking the exact H-cone produces the rational component and

    qS,   q^-1 h^-1 S,   qhS,   q^-1 S.

These four specials occupy two adjacent delta levels with identical offset sets{c-1,c+1}. Thus strict TILT fails, despite the unknot-arc pairing, all Euler closure identities, the H-cone, and the unit-bar requirements.
