## 1. The exact omitted symmetry

With your notation set

    E = [[delta,1],[-1,-delta]].

Then direct multiplication gives

    E²=(delta²-1)I,       E*GE=(delta²-1)G,
    EJE^-1=-J,           EME^-1=-A^-2 J M^-1 J.

Hence [E] normalizes Gamma, so the intersection in your lemma has index ONE. But [E] is not in Gamma. It centralizes the order-two generator [J], whose centralizer in C_2*C_3 is just <[J]>, and E is proportional to neither I nor J. Equivalently it fixes [J] and inverts [JM], inducing the nontrivial outer involution on the C_3 factor of the abelianization.

At the triangle specializations delta²-1<0. E exchanges the positive and negative cones, so it is in the extended circle stabilizer and outside the orientation-preserving disk group. That is the precise missing hypothesis in the Margulis/Singerman argument.

## 2. The corrected finite-index theorem

Let S be a rational-function G-similitude. Then

    [Gamma : Gamma intersect [S]Gamma[S]^-1] < infinity
          implies [S] in Gamma^ext = Gamma semidirect <[E]>.

In fact S then normalizes Gamma, so there are no intermediate finite-index intersections. Positive multiplier near the modular specialization excludes the E-coset and recovers your original conclusion in that restricted algebraic class.

Here is the proof, including the missing fixed-word argument.

First work over the DVR at Phi_8=A^4+1. Make S primitive integral there. For h in the finite-index intersection choose a bare Gamma word k with hS=alpha S k. Determinants force alpha to be a unit at Phi_8. If the reduction S_0 had rank one, its image would be a common invariant line for the specialized finite-index modular subgroup. That subgroup contains powers of two parabolics with different fixed points. Contradiction. Thus S_0 is invertible.

Take odd n>30 and A_n=exp(pi i/4-pi i/(2n)). The group Gamma_n is Delta(2,3,n); its orientation-preserving commensurator is itself. The finite-index subgroup specializes with uniformly bounded index. Its S_n-conjugate is a finite-covolume subgroup of Gamma_n, so also finite index. This proves the required two-sided commensurability at each specialization, even though the generic assumption was one-sided. Consequently S_n is in <Gamma_n,E_n>.

Now use the FOUR marked generators J_n,M_n,E_n,S_n. They generate a discrete non-elementary Mobius group and converge. Jorgensen--Klein says the limit is discrete and the generator correspondence from the limit ONTO the approximants eventually defines a homomorphism. The limit contains the extended modular group and preserves its circle. Its orientation-preserving part cannot properly contain PSL(2,Z): a noncompact oriented finite-area hyperbolic orbifold has area at least pi/3, already the modular area. Therefore the limit is precisely the extended modular group.

Write S_0=w(J_0,M_0)E_0^epsilon for one fixed word w. The eventual homomorphism preserves this relation, hence S_n=w(J_n,M_n)E_n^epsilon for every sufficiently large n. Equality at infinitely many A_n proves the rational-function identity. This replaces the unsupported assertion that bounded matrix degree forces a bounded word.

The source inputs are explicit: [Funar--Kohno, Section3, Proposition3.5 and Lemma3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/0907.0568v4); the combined nonarithmetic/maximal triangle statement in [Girondo--Gonzalez-Diez, Section2](https://verso.mat.uam.es/~gabino.gonzalez/comptesrendu.pdf); and [Jorgensen--Klein's theorem as stated in Inoue, Theorem1](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kyodo/kokyuroku/contents/pdf/1022-15.pdf). ## 3. Actual patterns eliminate the outer coset

Let C be the common reduced raw tensor of an actual diagram with two disjoint rational input slots. Move its actual unknot rational seed to corner11 by rational slot operations; then C11=u is a Laurent unit. Such operations send S to bar(P)^-1 S Q, preserving the two cosets of Gamma in Gamma^ext.

At zeta_8, use D=diag(1,i). Bare Gamma matrices become scalar multiples of SL_2(Z); E becomes a scalar multiple of a determinant-minus-one integral matrix. Thus an outer-coset tensor has the form

    C(zeta_8) = v [[i c,d],[a,-i b]],
    ad-bc=-1,   b != 0.

It follows that

    det C(zeta_8) / C11(zeta_8)² = -1/b² < 0.

But our local two-box signed-square theorem gives a nonnegative square for the same ratio. Contradiction. This uses four corners of one diagram and their common raw framing, exactly as in CX-BOX-SQUARE; it invokes no unproved general spherical-tangle statement.

Therefore C=f G W with W in Gamma. The scalar is not merely a rational function. A bare W has inverse over Z[A,A^-1], and

    f=(C W^-1)_12

is integral Laurent. At the rational unknot seed, u=f(v^T G W w), with both factors integral Laurent and u a unit. Thus f=+/-A^m.

This proves the tensor classification announced above. The same proof only needs a rational closure with unit reduced raw bracket, not a topological unknot certificate. I retained the actual-unknot premise in the geometric statement because it is the setting of your proposal.

There are two cautions about the proposed next step. An infinite-index intersection need not be cyclic; a nonabelian free subgroup is possible abstractly, and I have not excluded it here. Conversely, finite index does not yet make the underlying pattern rational: a nonrational pattern with rational-gluing tensor would remain a realization problem. Thus a potential counterexample is not proved to live only in the infinite-index case.
