Your genus-one suggestion gives a real reduction, stronger than merely g>=2 in the rank-five case. The characteristic-zero suggestion gives a useful field reduction, but neither the torsion argument nor the proposed identification of the instanton target survives. Here are the precise statements.

## 1. Genus one requires dim N at least nine

For every genus-one knot with Delta=1,

    rank_Q HFKhat(K,1)>=4, hence rank_F2 HFKhat(K)>=9.

The rational top group is nonzero by genus detection and has Euler characteristic zero, so its rank is positive and even. If it were two, Baldwin–Sivek's classification would apply. The source is *Floer homology and non-fibered knot detection*, Forum Math. Pi 13 (2025), e1, Theorem 1.2 and Table 1, not the sequel *Characterizing slopes for 5_2*. Every knot in that complete genus-one list has determinant seven or nine. This contradicts Delta=1. Conjugation then supplies at least four bottom generators, and the central Alexander coefficient supplies at least one. Integral universal coefficients give the F2 inequality; no torsion-freeness assertion is involved. [Published source](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2024.28).

For a flat quotient, U4 gives a_2(kappa)=0. In genus one that is exactly Delta_kappa=1. TH-S therefore yields

    flat, nonrational, g(kappa)=1 => dim_F2 N(T)>=9, m(T)>=4.

So genus one is excluded at both dimN=5 and dimN=7. We still cannot assert dimN>=7 universally.

There is a concrete topological description of the remaining dimN5 case. Its rational top HFK rank is exactly two: positive even and at most the F2 rank two. Li–Ye's *The guts of nearly fibered knots*, Theorem 1.4, Corollary 1.5 and Remark 2.1(1), together with Delta=1, force their model M1: **a solid torus with four longitudinal sutures**. The other models have top Alexander coefficient +/-2. Thus the residual is

    g>=2, Delta=1, HFK ranks(2,1,2) at(-g,0,g), |tau|=g,
    sutured collapse, and guts M1.

[Li–Ye source](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.05382v3).

This residual has HFK(g-1)=0. I checked the immediate next-to-top escape hatch: Ni's Theorem 1.1 requires a single top Maslov parity, which fails here; Binns–Wan2608.03900v1 Theorem 1.3 requires a specified two-annulus decomposition. The four-suture description supplies neither hypothesis. Their Remark 4.13 expressly leaves arbitrary product-annulus/disc decompositions outside the proof. That is an identified new geometric lemma, not an already proved conclusion. [Ni](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~yini/Published/SecondTerm.pdf), [Binns–Wan](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03900v1).

## 2. The coefficient-field reduction

Define N_F directly from the finite free integral bullet-augmented complex C_b(T), without assuming its curve decomposition is field-independent. With cochain differential raising h, write b_(h,q) for the free integral rank and t_p(h,q) for the number of p-primary cyclic summands. Then

    dim N_Fp^(h,q)=b_(h,q)+t_p(h,q)+t_p(h+1,q).

Thus rational support is contained in every mod-p support, while their integer Euler polynomials agree. A torsion class in integral degree(k,q) gives the mod-p pair(k,q),(k-1,q), so your factor(1+h) observation has the right shape. Its converse fails: two free integral classes give the same shape and remain over Q.

For either P=END or P=NP, we get the exact field lemma

    P_Q <=> P_Fp for some prime p <=> P_Fp for all but finitely many p,

and P_Fp implies P_Q for each individual p.

Proof: an END_Fp endpoint occurs in the common Euler polynomial, hence in rational support. Support inclusion then forces the rational and mod-p endpoints to coincide. For NP, the rational endpoint support is a nonempty subset of the single mod-p homological degree. Finally only finitely many primes divide integral torsion, so all other graded dimension profiles are identical to the rational one.

The exceptional primes depend on T. In particular failure at p=2 need not be rational failure; rational failure persists at every prime. This isolates a weaker endpoint target. It is not itself a noncancellation theorem, nor a field-independent classification of curves.

It is not ordinary SHI of the double branched cover E(kappa), and hence not the proposed KHI(kappa).
