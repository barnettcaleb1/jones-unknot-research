## 1. Theorem N is an exact marked skein identity

Work generically over Q(A), with the TH-2BR crossing convention. Fusion gives

    V_1 tensor V_2 = V_1 direct-sum V_3,
    End(V_1 tensor V_2) = K P_1 direct-sum K P_3.

Each summand has multiplicity one, so a box is aP_1+bP_3 and stacking multiplies a and b separately. This proves commutativity of the skein algebra; it does not assert that reordered boxes are isotopic. The primary recoupling input is Masbaum–Vogel, *3-valent graphs and the Kauffman bracket*, Pacific J. Math. 164 (1994), Sections 1–2 and Theorem 3, https://msp.org/pjm/1994/164-2/pjm-v164-n2-p07-p.pdf.

Bending the arc strand gives an isomorphism from this endomorphism space to H. If h_c is the bent projector, h_1+h_3=e_1; phi kills h_3; and Y has eigenvalues 1,t^-3 on h_1,h_3. Solving gives

    h_1=(1,1/(t^3-1)),  h_3=(0,1/(1-t^3)).

Therefore your channel convention is correct:

    a(v)=v_1,  b(v)=v_1+(1-t^3)v_2,
    v*w=(v_1w_1,v_1w_2+v_2w_1+(1-t^3)v_2w_2).

The integral statement has an important scope. The algebra Lambda[s]/(s²-(1-t³)s) is the coordinate lattice, not a theorem that every element is realized by an actual box. For actual body states, your written proof's projected-TL_3 argument establishes integrality: with p=1 tensor f_2, the sandwich has form c_1p+c_e pe_1p; Laurentness of a forces delta|c_e; at A=1 crossing changes trivialize the through-through box, forcing the additional 1-t factor. This supplies v_2 in Lambda after a common unit. No generic semisimplicity at t³=1 is being used.

The marking, not merely dimension two, determines the closure functional. For the actual word

    s.r2.(y1)^m.B_1...B_n.u1,

slide the outside vertex through infinity and read from the opposite end. The cup becomes e_1, the bodies form the composite box, and the y crossings become X^-1 before phi. A half-turn fixes the two channel projectors. Hence mu=-m and

    F_mu=e_1^T X^mu(v_1*...*v_n)
        =[w_1 A+w_3 B]/Phi_3,
    w_1=t+(1+t²)(-t)^mu,  w_3=(1+t²)(1-(-t)^mu),
    A=product a_i,  B=product b_i,  Phi_3=1+t+t².

## 2. Rational necklaces: the cancellation argument closes

The precise accepted algebraic theorem is:

> Let n>=2, let v_i=rho(w_i)e_1 be rational box states with every a(v_i) nonunit, and let N be any integer and mu any nonzero integer. Then e_1^T X^mu Y^N(v_1*...*v_n) is nonunit.

The proof permits cancellation. It does not try to bound the entire cancellation cascade at the bad end.

First remove the degenerate prefixes by the repaired two-bridge normal form, and collect the leading Y powers into N. For a normalized box u, let r=v_2/v_1. The old states are

    R: r=t^-1(1+...),
    S_j: r=(-1)^j t^(-4-j)(1+...).

At the upper end, b/a has degree 2 and leading coefficient -1 in R, and degree 0 and leading coefficient 1 in S_j. The mirror exchanges R and S and obeys

    bar(a(u))=t^-4k a(u''),
    bar(b(u))=t^(-4k-3) b(u''),
    u''=u(-m_0-1,-m_1,...).

Let r_count be the number of R-top boxes and d=2r_count-3N. The three possible cancelling upper ties are exactly

    mu>=1:  d=0,  r_count even;
    mu<=-2: d=-1, r_count odd;
    mu=-1:  d=-3, r_count even.

The mirror changes d to -n-d and r_count to n-r_count. Its corresponding lower ties cannot coexist with an upper tie when n>=2. Degree coincidences would require n=1, or n=3 with incompatible parities. If neither end ties, the two-bridge span formula already gives span J>=4n-2.

For the remaining cases, write rho(f) for the normalized coefficient one degree below the leading term of f. Refine R to R_c, c in {0,1}, by recording r=t^-1(1+c t^-1+...). The second-order transition table in the referee report is closed. Its key potential is

    rho(a)+1 on R,   rho(a) on S or the initial state.

It never increases. Consequently rho(a)<=-1 on R, rho(a)<=0 on S, and rho(b)<=0 for every box. Products preserve these inequalities because rho is additive. At the clean end these bounds force the second coefficient of J to be nonzero, except in the single configuration

    n divisible by 6, mu=-2, N=n/3,
    all boxes alternating S-top with rho(a_i)=0,

or its mirror. In that configuration the normalized third coefficient is Y_2-1, where Y_2=sum x_2(a_i)<=0. It is therefore nonzero. The complete six-case check and the alternating-box third-order calculation are in `necklace_extremes_referee.md`; I have checked the exceptional case as well, not just the generic potential.

Thus two nonzero coefficients survive in every case. This avoids the stronger span conjectures, the proposed uniform cascade bounds, and every homological-outline assertion. No large verification count is used in the proof.

The marked topological dictionary now gives Lemma Q for the horizontal inversions on

    N(T_1+...+T_n+1/(2mu)),

where the T_i are genuine odd-denominator rational tangles, in the coordinated sign convention above. The equivalent halfword has m=-mu. All signs of the rational inputs and the collected integral part are allowed. With fewer genuine boxes, or mu=0, the existing two-bridge/product results handle the relevant nontrivial cases separately.

This is the reciprocal-even-special-tangle subclass. A general special slope such as 3/8 is not covered merely by “moving integral parts.” The nesting calculus may represent it, but then the inputs to the last frame need not obey the rational-box potential. The claimed result for every horizontal Montesinos inversion, and the iterated arborescent extension, remain open.

## 5. The odd-closure corollary needs another input

The divisibility argument in120708 does not prove that nonflatness implies V(T(1/n))!=1 for every nonzero n. In its displayed formula take j=1, n=1, and the formal Laurent polynomial

    J_* = 1+t(t+1)(t^-12-1)/Phi_3
        = t^-11-t^-9+t^-8-t^-6+t^-5-t^-3+t^-2.

It is nonunit, satisfies J_*(-1)=1 and J_*'(-1)=0, and (t+1)² divides t(J_*-1). Nevertheless

    t^12 {1+(1+t^-1)Phi_3/(1+t)² (J_*-1)} = 1

identically. This is an exact counterexample to the claimed algebraic inference. It is not asserted to be realized by a tangle, and hence does not refute the topological corollary or T. The fixed certificate is in `necklace_fixed_checks.py/.json`.

For even n!=0 the prefactor in the quoted formula is one, so J!=1 does give V!=1 directly. The same is true when j=0. For general odd n, either a realizability restriction excluding the displayed formal possibilities or another argument is needed. Please keep that distinction in the two-bridge and necklace conclusions.
