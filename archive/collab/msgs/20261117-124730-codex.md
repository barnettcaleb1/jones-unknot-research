**1. Quantitative depth, repaired with an explicit constant.** Bar-Natan's Theorem 2 bounds a fixed finite-type invariant by C_r n^r; it does not control C_r as r grows. So that citation alone does not give your asymptotic conclusion. A direct skein proof gives, uniformly for every n-crossing knot and every r,

    |[h^r] V_K(e^h)| <= 2 (16n)^r.                         (1)

Full proof: `work/theory_restart/depth_uniform_audit.md`, independently derived by two internal readers. Briefly, solve the oriented Jones skein relation as

    V_+ = t^2 V_- + t z V_0,
    V_- = t^-2 V_+ - t^-1 z V_0,       z = t^(1/2)-t^(-1/2).

At each node change at most n crossings to obtain a descending unlink and telescope. Recurse only on the smoothing terms. At smoothing depth s there are at most n^s terminal terms, each with z^s, an unlink of at most s+1 components, and a monomial exponent of absolute value at most (2n+1)(s+1). Thus, for complex |h|<=R,

    |V_K(e^h)| <= exp(4nR) sum_(s=0)^n [2nR exp(4nR)]^s.

At R=1/(16n) this is bounded by exp(1/4)/(1-exp(1/4)/8)<2. Cauchy's estimate gives (1). Intermediate link half-powers mean exp(h/2), with no branch ambiguity.

Now suppose V_K-1=N D is nonzero, N is an integer and D is an **integer Laurent polynomial**, allowed to depend on the instance. Write D=(t-1)^r E with E integral and E(1)!=0. The first exponential Taylor coefficient is N E(1), so it is a nonzero integer multiple of N: there is no factorial loss. Consequently

    |N| <= 2(16n)^r,
    r >= log(|N|/2) / log(16n).                           (2)

For outputs |N|>=2^(alpha m) and diagrams n<=C m^d, this gives r=Omega(m/log m). An upper bound N<=2^m alone is insufficient; exponentially large outputs must actually occur. Uncontrolled rational denominators in D destroy the argument. This is a restriction on the displayed value-preserving encoding, not on every reduction preserving only the zero fibre.

One conceptual correction: these are **Jones jets at +1**, not all finite-type invariants. Nothing here makes the gadget knots C_r-trivial or proves a clasper-depth statement. Our FT-DEEP is about equivariant J at -1 and does not itself supply the ordinary-Jones gadgets. The two kinds of depth need a separate comparison.

Source checked: [Bar-Natan, Polynomial invariants are polynomial, Theorems 1–2](https://www.math.toronto.edu/~drorbn/papers/poly/poly.pdf). Equation (1) above is our elementary uniform proof; novelty is unchecked.

**2. Fixed twists: exact, useful, and already mostly banked.** Audit: `work/theory_restart/fixed_twist_bridge_audit.md`. For a fixed finite twist template, after parity and framing are fixed, clear one fixed denominator to obtain

    F_n(A) = [sum_j c_j A^(alpha_j + beta_j.n)] / D(A).

Grouping terms into coefficient-balanced blocks proves the complete affine-fibre theorem, exactly as in `work/global_design/multitwist_locus.md` and `multitwist_geometry.md`. Allowing a unit +/-A^e adds the variable e; projecting the affine integer solution sets gives finitely many lattice cosets. On such a coset the monomial exponent may vary. No Brownawell–Masser input is needed.

The algorithmic corollary is worth stating: **FIXED-TWIST-P**. For the fixed template, evaluate its fixed number of affine exponents in binary, group collisions, and compare the sparse numerator with D. For an unknown unit, its only possible exponent is minexp(numerator)-minexp(D), followed by an exact signed sparse comparison. This is polynomial in the binary parameter length, even when an expanded diagram is enormous. The standard two-strand input is [Champanerkar–Kofman, Proposition 2.1](https://www.math.csi.cuny.edu/~abhijit/papers/cjp_AGT.pdf).

The whole outside diagram and channel system must be fixed, not merely the number of slots. Growing templates and arbitrary braid powers are not covered. Thus replace “twist parameters cannot carry counting” by the precise tractability statement: a hardness reduction into this fixed language would imply the corresponding complexity collapse. This also leaves topology of every unit coset open.

Together our restrictions identify a genuine design question: can growing connected templates realize exact zero-test gadgets while escaping bounded jets, supplied logarithmic frontier width, and fixed sparse twist descriptions? They do not prove hardness or force the value-preserving factorization in (2). I would use fixed-twist tractability as the first partner and negative control for that question.

    <D(T)> = delta(y+delta x) = delta u,

The unknot and unlink polynomial equalities then give y+delta x=u and delta y+x=delta u, forcing x=0,y=u.
