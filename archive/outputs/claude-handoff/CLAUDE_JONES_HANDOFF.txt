THE STRONGEST PARTIAL RESULT

Let P,Q be rational closing tangles with distinct underlying unframed slopes.
Let R be rational and X an arbitrary two-string tangle with the same marked
boundary. If C_P(X)=C_P(R) and C_Q(X)=C_Q(R) as BALANCED FRAMED LINKS,
then X=R as balanced framed tangles.

This matches Conjecture 2 in Sikora arXiv:2005.08162v2, or Conjecture 1.2
in the published paper. It is stronger than uniqueness among rational
solutions, but weaker than rational-tangle detection by bracket alone.

Proof architecture:
  two actual framed closure equalities
    -> equality of relative bracket vectors (Sikora)
    -> auxiliary determinant-one and determinant-zero fillings
    -> H_1(M)=Z and a common preferred longitude for the double covers
    -> actual lens filling gives irreducibility
    -> if both original slopes are nonlongitudinal: two same-signed-p/q
       lens comparisons force Delta''(1)=0 by Casson--Walker
    -> two L-space fillings imply Floer simplicity
    -> binary torsion coefficients imply alternating Alexander coefficients
    -> Delta''(1)=0 forces Delta=1
    -> Floer-simple norm formula forces boundary compressibility
    -> irreducibility makes the cover a solid torus
    -> rationality and bracket equality recover the marked balanced framing.

The longitude/unlink branch instead uses Rasmussen--Rasmussen Proposition
7.8, followed by the same norm/compressibility endpoint. The full argument
and references are included later in this file.

Most delicate checks already made: equality of the signed peripheral
coefficients; validity of Casson--Walker in an arbitrary auxiliary homology
sphere; equality of RR's projected polynomial with the ordinary Alexander
normalization when H_1(M)=Z; irreducibility; and balanced framing.

Attribution: Gainullin's 2018 Theorem 8.2 already uses a closely related
Casson--Walker -> second Alexander derivative -> alternating coefficients
-> genus-detection strategy. The potential new step is the two-filling
adaptation and application to the full framed tangle conjecture. His
ambient-L-space/null-homology assumptions do not immediately cover our
auxiliary Y. No claim that this adaptation is unprecedented is established.

Recent status evidence: Sikora's 15 October 2026 IPAM talk abstract still
calls the exact uniqueness assertion a conjecture. The abstract was read,
not the whole recording. This is evidence of recent conjectural status,
not proof that no prior or unpublished argument exists.

WHY THAT RESULT DOES NOT SOLVE THE ORIGINAL PROBLEM

Its key hypotheses concern actual links and actual filled manifolds.
Polynomial equality alone does not provide them. Sikora proves
Jones unknot detection -> framed rational-solution uniqueness. Proving
the consequent is not a proof of the antecedent.

Pass 51 makes the gap concrete. Tie an arbitrary knot J into one arc of R,
adjusting framing for Jones normalization. Then

 [X_J]=V_J(A^(-4))*[R],
 Sigma_2(X_J)=(S^1 x D^2)#Sigma_2(J).

A hypothetical nontrivial Jones-one knot would preserve the bracket while
surviving as a closed connected-sum factor in the cover. The auxiliary
filling-core knot is then an unknot in a ball of Sigma_2(J); its Alexander
polynomial is already 1 and gives no information about J. The actual lens
fillings in the candidate proof remove closed summands; Jones equality
does not. Assuming irreducibility simply excludes this generic construction.

There is a recorded restricted extension: bracket equality with a rational
tangle plus irreducibility AND Floer simplicity of the double cover implies
the same rationality conclusion. Its derivative argument uses a
Garoufalidis/Lescop zero-surgery formula. It was checked in pass 51 but did
not receive the same fresh audit as the main candidate. Crucially, neither
extra hypothesis follows from bracket equality in the record.

Source: outputs/jones-generic-route-audit.txt and work/research51/.

THE ORIGINAL CLAIMED JONES PROOF

The starting paper was Avishy Carmi and Eliahu Cohen, On trivial
Jones--Vassiliev polynomials, arXiv:2606.22410v1, submitted 21 June 2026.
The initial audit found that its Section 5 package would give a uniform
p-degree bound 22 on a trefoil-based odd anti-parallel twist family. Direct
skein computation gives degree 2L+5 and b_(2L+5)=-1; at L=23, b_51=-1.
Thus the asserted uniform compression and residual-twist identification
cannot simultaneously hold. Replacing the overhead 11 by another fixed
constant does not fix an unbounded degree contradiction.

Later work reconstructed the source's specific G2 locked-clasp move and
found a concrete endpoint mismatch, with separate exact bracket checks.
This is stronger localization of the failure than the original audit,
which expressly did not identify G2 alone as the offending step.
See the early and late ledgers for the exact formula, state diagrams and
verification paths; do not conflate a schematic diagram with that later
source-grounded reconstruction.

GLOBAL CONDITIONAL SCAFFOLDS THAT REMAIN OPEN

The most useful common endpoint is connected-sum amplification. If a
nontrivial K has V_K=1 and J is any fixed anchor, then

                  V_(J#K#...#K)=V_J.

Consequently a finite upper bound throughout ONE nonempty whole Jones
fiber on bridge number, reduced rational Khovanov rank, or its quantum
spread would imply unknot detection. For example

 b(J#K^(#m))=b(J)+m*(b(K)-1),
 r(J#K^(#m))=r(J)*r(K)^m.

These implications are complete. No such unrestricted whole-fiber bound
has been established here. Restriction to prime knots misses the very
connected sums used in the argument. On the unit fiber, the boundedness
assertion is equivalent to the original problem.

A related conditional endpoint uses bounded total hyperbolic volume of
exteriors throughout one whole fiber, with a separate graph-knot argument
for the zero-volume case. Another uses a genuine nontrivial kernel element
of a generic Jones braid representation plus Ito's topological endpoint.
Neither the required bound nor the required kernel element was found.

Other complete conditional chains need a general reduction to unknotting
number one AND rigidity there, or an essential Jones-invisible satellite
pattern plus its nontriviality certificate. The record proves useful
equivalences or necessary conditions in those settings, not the missing
general construction.

Many purportedly different approaches share exactly the same missing
geometric-realizability or whole-fiber-boundedness premise. They should not
be counted as independent indications that a solution is near.

NOTATION AND NORMALIZATION WARNINGS

* U is the unknot; V_U=1 is the ordinary normalized Jones polynomial.
* A common convention is t=A^(-4), x=t^(1/2), p=x-x^(-1),
  delta=-A^2-A^(-2). Some source notes use q or x differently; each local
  statement must be read with its own definitions.
* The skein convention used in many notes is
  t^(-1)V_+ - t V_- = (t^(1/2)-t^(-1/2))*V_0.
* Raw Kauffman bracket may give the unknot delta; the reduced closed bracket
  gives 1. Divide by delta BEFORE specializing to delta=0 for determinants.
* Colored-Jones conventions differ between notes. In the common reduced
  convention H_1=1 and H_2=V. Annular S_n corresponds to representation
  dimension n+1. Never identify ordinary color 2 with all colors.
* Tangle brackets carry framing. A single global framing adjustment acts on
  both closures; independently fixing the two closure framings changes the
  framed problem.
* Balanced framed isotopy is Sikora's convention. Do not replace it by a
  different componentwise framing equivalence without checking the theorem.
* A slope p*mu+q*lambda is p/q surgery in the common marked convention.
  Mirror/orientation changes can alter signs and must be tracked on both
  sides of a surgery comparison.
* Delta_K(t) in surgery formulas is symmetric, Delta_K(1)=1. A shifted
  polynomial D=t^h*Delta_K must be symmetrized before taking Delta_K''(1).

WHAT AN INDEPENDENT AGENT SHOULD TRY FIRST

The highest-value immediate task is adversarial verification of the finite
rational-tangle proof, together with a focused novelty search. Check the
actual cited hypotheses rather than trusting this summary. If rejecting a
step, identify the exact implication, missing hypothesis or counterexample.
If accepting it, distinguish logical validity from publication priority.

Second, inspect the pass-51 generic obstruction before proposing to extend
the result to all Jones-one knots. Any proposed construction must explain
how it removes or controls the local-knot closed summand without assuming
Jones detection in another form.

Third, select one remaining question with a falsifiable output: a concrete
geometric realization, an exact representation-kernel certificate, a
genuine new whole-fiber bound, or a proof/counterexample for a precisely
stated intermediate lemma. State the full route to Jones before investing
in the local calculation. A conditional theorem whose premise is merely
Jones detection renamed is not such an input.

The record does not support a prediction that another unrestricted pass,
more parallel agents, or additional runtime will solve the conjecture.
There may be worthwhile partial results here, especially the candidate
tangle theorem. The previous process failure was repeatedly treating new
reformulations and narrow exclusions as momentum toward a full proof.

RATIONAL SOLUTIONS OF FRAMED TANGLE EQUATIONS: AUDITED ARGUMENT
1 November 2026

STATUS

This is a candidate deduction of Sikora's framed rational-solution uniqueness
conjecture from published results. A fresh audit found no fatal gap. The
argument has not received external specialist review, and priority is
unverified. This version supersedes the pass-50 proof for review purposes;
the earlier file is retained as a historical record.

The target is Conjecture 2 of [S, arXiv v2], numbered Conjecture 1.2 in the
published paper. It is not the Jones unknot-detection conjecture or the
stronger assertion that the bracket detects rational tangles among all
tangles. The distinction is essential: the hypotheses below are actual
link equivalences, not equalities of link polynomials.

The Casson-Walker/Alexander-derivative strategy has a close antecedent in
Gainullin [G, Theorem 8.2]. The potential contribution here is its use with
two matched fillings and Rasmussen--Rasmussen's results, then its application
to the full framed tangle statement. No new Floer-theoretic machinery or
new general surgery formula is claimed.

STATEMENT

Let P and Q be framed rational closing tangles with distinct underlying
unframed slopes. Let R be a framed rational tangle and X an arbitrary framed
2-tangle, with the common boundary marking used to define tangle equations.
Suppose

                  C_P(X) = C_P(R),     C_Q(X) = C_Q(R)

by balanced framed isotopy in the convention of [S, Section 4]. Then X=R
as balanced framed tangles.

Here C_P(X) denotes attachment of the closing tangle; this is equivalently
Sikora's N(X+P) notation. The ambient balls are oriented and link isotopies
preserve ambient orientation. The link components need not be oriented.
These are the usual conventions of the framed problem, rather than extra
restrictions on its solutions. Merely changing a probe's framing does not
provide a second underlying slope.

PROOF

1. The two equations determine the bracket vector.

By [S, Theorem 21], the two distinct rational closure functionals determine
the relative Kauffman bracket vector. Hence [X]=[R]. Every further rational
closure therefore has the same bracket as its counterpart for R, and in
particular the same determinant.

For clarity about normalization: use the reduced closed bracket with unknot
value 1. If using the convention in which a single circle contributes
delta=-A^2-A^(-2), first divide the bracket by delta as a polynomial. Its
absolute value at A=exp(pi*i/4) is the link determinant. Framing factors
have absolute value one there. Substituting into the unreduced bracket
before dividing would incorrectly erase the determinant information.

2. Recover the integral peripheral data and irreducibility.

Let V be the oriented double branched cover of R and M that of X. The
manifold V is a solid torus. The marked tangle boundaries identify the
oriented slope lattices of their covers. Let lambda be the primitive
disk-bounding slope in V. Choose a slope mu intersecting lambda once,
and choose it distinct from the two original probes BEFORE making any
fillings. There are infinitely many choices mu+n*lambda, so this is possible.

The rational closures associated with lambda and mu give respectively the
two-component unlink and an unknot for R. Their covers are

                  V(lambda)=S^1 x S^2,     V(mu)=S^3.

Step 1 gives determinants 0 and 1 for the corresponding closures of X.
The determinant-one closure is a knot: a link with c components has
determinant divisible by 2^(c-1) [St, Proposition 6.5]. Thus X has no closed
components, since each such component would survive every closure alongside
at least one component formed from the two arcs.

The order of first homology of a double branched cover is the determinant,
with determinant zero indicating infinite first homology [D, Section 3].
Consequently Y=M(mu) is an integral homology sphere. Let K be the core of
the attached solid torus. Its exterior is M, and therefore

                       H_1(M;Z)=Z,     H_2(M;Z)=0.

One can also see this directly from the Mayer--Vietoris sequence of the
filling: H_1(boundary M) maps isomorphically to H_1(M) direct-sum Z.

For a knot in an integral homology sphere, the unique filling slope with
infinite first homology is its primitive preferred longitude. The
determinant-zero lambda filling therefore identifies lambda with this
longitude. Thus mu and lambda form a common meridian/longitude basis for
the exteriors M and V. No assertion that Y is S^3 or an L-space is made.

At most one original probe equals lambda. At every other original probe,
the actual shared branch link is a nonsplit rational link. Its cover is an
actual lens space with finite first homology, possibly S^3. In particular,
M has at least one actual lens-space filling.

This also implies that M is irreducible. In a prime decomposition, the
summand M_0 carrying its torus boundary has b_1(M_0)>=1. Since H_1(M)=Z,
all closed summands have trivial first homology; a nonseparating sphere,
which would contribute an S^1 x S^2 factor, is excluded as well. Every
closed summand persists after filling the boundary. Prime decomposition
of an actual lens-space filling forces each closed homology-sphere summand
to be S^3. Hence no nontrivial sphere decomposition of M remains.

3. Suppose both original slopes are nonlongitudinal.

Write alpha_i=p_i*mu+q_i*lambda, with (p_i,q_i) primitive and p_i nonzero.
Distinctness implies q_1/p_1 != q_2/p_2. The common peripheral data and
the actual link isotopies give orientation-preserving homeomorphisms

            Y_(p_i/q_i)(K)  ~=  S^3_(p_i/q_i)(U),   i=1,2.       (1)

Here U is the unknot. The SAME SIGNED coefficients on both sides are
essential. Both covers inherit orientation from the same oriented tangle
ball, and the boundary identification preserves orientation. Its two
lifts differ by the deck involution, acting by -I on boundary homology;
this changes (p,q) to (-p,-q), leaving p/q unchanged. Step 2 identifies
both preferred longitudes, as well as the common chosen meridian.
Thus there is no independent peripheral shear or sign change between
the two exteriors. A link isotopy lifts to an orientation-preserving map
of the closed covers. That map need not preserve the filling cores:
the peripheral coefficient comparison has already been fixed independently.

Normalize the Alexander polynomial by

                 Delta_K(t)=Delta_K(t^(-1)),   Delta_K(1)=1.

The Casson-Walker surgery formula [NW, Proposition 3.1] applies to a
null-homologous knot in any rational homology sphere, including Y. Applying
it to (1), the lens-space terms cancel, giving

           0 = lambda_CW(Y) + (q_i/(2p_i))*Delta_K''(1),  i=1,2.

Subtracting yields Delta_K''(1)=0. It also gives lambda_CW(Y)=0, which
alone would not identify Y with S^3.

Both fillings in (1) are L-spaces. By [RR, Proposition 1.3], M is Floer
simple. Proposition 1.4 supplies a torsion normalization

             tau(M)=D(t)/(1-t)=sum_(n>=0) a_n*t^n,

where a_0=1, a_n belongs to {0,1}, and a_n=1 for all sufficiently large n.
Since H_1(M)=Z, D is the polynomial normalization of Delta_K. Also the
projection defining RR's bar-tau is the identity, so

                       bar-Delta(M)=D(t).                       (2)

The coefficients of D are a_n-a_(n-1), with a_(-1)=0. Their nonzero terms
therefore alternate +1,-1,...,+1. Write D=t^h*Delta_K, with degree(D)=2h.
Here h is an exponent shift; no genus-detection assertion has yet been used.
Symmetry and alternation give

 Delta_K(t)=(-1)^k + sum_(j=1)^k (-1)^(k-j)*(t^(n_j)+t^(-n_j)),
                         0<n_1<...<n_k.

The central coefficient is present: there are an odd number of nonzero
coefficients, while noncentral ones occur in symmetric pairs. Consequently

              Delta_K''(1)=2*sum_(j=1)^k (-1)^(k-j)*n_j^2.

If k>0, pairing from the largest exponent downward expresses the sum as
positive differences of squares, and possibly one extra positive square.
It is strictly positive. Its established vanishing forces Delta_K=D=1.

If the boundary of M were incompressible, [RR, Corollary 2.3] and (2) would
give the Thurston norm of a generator of H_2(M,boundary M) as
degree(D)-1=-1, contrary to nonnegativity. Thus the boundary is compressible.
An irreducible orientable manifold with a single torus boundary and a
compressing disk is a solid torus. Hence M is a solid torus.

4. Suppose one original slope is lambda.

The actual shared product at this slope is the two-component unlink, so
M(lambda)=S^1 x S^2. The other probe supplies an L-space filling. Regard
M as the exterior of the filling core in S^1 x S^2.

By [RR, Proposition 7.8], this exterior is a generalized solid torus. In
RR's notation, the order g_M of the homological longitude in H_1(M) is 1,
because H_1(M)=Z and the longitude represents zero. Definition 7.2 gives
Floer simplicity and degree(bar-Delta(M))<g_M=1. This nonzero normalized
polynomial therefore has degree zero.

If the boundary were incompressible, Corollary 2.3 would again give
Thurston norm -1. The boundary is compressible, and irreducibility from
Step 2 makes M a solid torus. This argument does NOT assert that all
generalized solid tori are actual solid tori: the torsion-free homology
and irreducibility hypotheses are crucial.

5. Recover the marked framed tangle.

In either case, the double branched cover of the given two-arc tangle X
is a solid torus. The classical branched-cover criterion makes X rational
[BM, Theorem 3.5(2)]. This criterion concerns the given branched cover and
its deck involution; it does not require replacing that involution by a
different one. Finally, [S, Corollary 20] distinguishes balanced framed
rational tangles by their bracket vectors. Since [X]=[R], it follows that
X=R with the required boundary marking and balanced framing. End proof.

RELATION TO EARLIER WORK AND TO JONES UNKNOT DETECTION

Gainullin [G, Theorem 8.2] already uses the closely related chain from a
surgery comparison through Casson-Walker and Delta''(1), then alternating
Alexander coefficients and genus detection. That theorem assumes an
ambient L-space and a null-homologous knot, and compares with Y#L(p,q).
Here the auxiliary Y has not been shown to be an L-space, and the comparison
is with L(p,q). Two equations remove the unknown lambda_CW(Y), while
Floer simplicity of the exterior supplies the coefficient and norm facts.

This is a possible new application/generalization of existing methods,
not an established priority claim. The bounded literature audit located
no prior exact proof. Sikora's 15 October 2026 talk abstract still states
the exact framed uniqueness assertion as a conjecture [T]. The recording
itself was not reviewed; neither this evidence nor unsuccessful searching
excludes an unpublished or overlooked proof.

The argument cannot be substituted into a proof of Jones unknot detection
without an additional, unproved step. Polynomial equality alone supplies
neither the actual lens-space fillings used in Steps 2--3 nor the actual
S^1 x S^2 filling used in Step 4. For example, tying a hypothetical nontrivial
Jones-one knot locally into R could preserve its bracket while changing
the actual closure links. The required topological identifications would
then be missing. Sikora proves that Jones detection implies framed
uniqueness; proving that consequent does not establish the antecedent.

WHY THE ARGUMENT CURRENTLY HOLDS UP

1. Correct scope and exact bracket equality.

Sikora Section 4.1 explicitly requires different unframed probes and uses
balanced framing. Theorem 21 gives equality of bracket vectors from the
two actual framed equations. Thus the deduction does not silently assume
the stronger rational-tangle bracket-detection conjecture.

2. Integral homology and the longitude are genuinely recovered.

Two auxiliary closures of the rational comparator are an unknot and a
two-component unlink. Their matching brackets for X give determinants 1
and 0. The first produces an integral homology-sphere filling, so the
exterior has H_1=Z, without hidden torsion. The second identifies its
preferred longitude with the comparator's longitude. Determinant one
also excludes closed components in X.

The proof never identifies the auxiliary integral homology sphere Y with
S^3, nor assumes that Y is an L-space. Either assertion would be an error.

3. The signed surgery coefficients match.

This was the most delicate topological check. The common oriented boundary
marking, common chosen meridian and identified longitude give the same
signed p/q on both exteriors. The two lifts of the boundary marking differ
by -I, which leaves the ratio unchanged. Actual link isotopies give
orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of the filled covers. Those maps
need not preserve the filling cores. Homology orders alone, or unoriented
lens-space identifications, would not justify the calculation.

4. Casson-Walker is applicable in the auxiliary ambient manifold.

Ni--Wu Proposition 3.1 explicitly applies to null-homologous knots in
rational homology spheres, despite the narrower title of their paper.
For two nonlongitudinal probes it gives

       0=lambda_CW(Y)+(q_i/(2p_i))*Delta_K''(1),   i=1,2.

The two ratios are different, so subtraction eliminates the unknown
lambda_CW(Y) and forces Delta_K''(1)=0. No assumption about its sign,
or about Y being S^3, is needed.

5. The Alexander-polynomial step is sound.

Two distinct L-space fillings imply Floer simplicity by Rasmussen--
Rasmussen Proposition 1.3. Their torsion coefficient restriction makes
the polynomial's nonzero coefficients alternate +1,-1,...,+1.
Symmetrizing BEFORE differentiating gives a strictly positive second
derivative for every nonconstant polynomial of this form. Hence the
vanishing derivative forces Delta_K=1.

An independent algebraic check gives a useful cross-check. Write
D=t^h*Delta_K and tau=D/(1-t)=sum a_n*t^n, with a_0=1 and a_n in {0,1}.
Let E={n>=0:a_n=0}. It is a finite set of positive integers. Then

 D=1+(t-1)*sum_(n in E)t^n,
 |E|=D'(1)=h,
 Delta_K''(1)=2*sum_(n in E)n-h*(h-1) >= 2h.

Thus zero derivative forces h=0 and D=1. This is a verification of the
algebra, not a separately established novelty claim.

6. The final norm and irreducibility steps use the necessary hypotheses.

Because H_1(M)=Z, the projected polynomial bar-Delta in RR is exactly D.
If the boundary were incompressible, Corollary 2.3 would force Thurston
norm degree(D)-1=-1, a contradiction. Boundary compressibility plus
irreducibility gives a solid torus.

Irreducibility is proved earlier from an ACTUAL lens-space filling:
every closed summand must be a homology sphere, survives filling, and
therefore must be S^3. It is not inferred merely from Floer simplicity.

7. The unlink case is covered without a missing surgery hypothesis.

The original use of Ni--Vafaee Theorem 1.1 and fiber capping was checked
and found valid. The revised proof uses the shorter RR Proposition 7.8:
a knot in S^1 x S^2 admitting an L-space surgery has generalized-solid-
torus exterior. H_1(M)=Z forces its longitude-order parameter to be 1;
the degree bound and Corollary 2.3 again force boundary compressibility.
Together with irreducibility this gives an actual solid torus.

8. Returning to the given tangle is justified.

The classical solid-torus-cover criterion applies to the actual two-arc
tangle and its given deck involution. Rationality then lets Sikora's
Corollary 20 recover the marked balanced framing from bracket equality.

HYPOTHESIS STRESS TESTS

* A solid torus connected-summed with a nontrivial integral homology
  L-space sphere can have H_1=Z, Delta=1 and many L-space fillings without
  being a solid torus. It is reducible; its fillings retain the closed
  summand and cannot be the actual lens spaces required here. This checks
  why the irreducibility argument cannot be omitted.

* A trefoil exterior has H_1=Z and is Floer simple, but its symmetric
  Delta=t-1+t^(-1) has Delta''(1)=2. It fails the matched-surgery constraint.
  Multiple lens fillings alone would not be enough.

* Non-solid generalized-solid-torus examples do not refute the argument:
  the familiar irreducible examples have homological torsion, excluded
  by H_1(M)=Z. Reducible examples are separately excluded above.

* A hypothetical Jones-one knot tied locally into R would preserve
  polynomial information without supplying the actual rational-link
  closures. This is precisely why the partial result does not prove
  Jones unknot detection.

MID-01  D/N  Reversible finite classical positivity cannot model Jones
------------------------------------------------------------------------
At A=i*r, r>1, q=r^-4, d=r^2+r^-2, write B_i=rI-r^-1 e_i.
Its eigenvalues are r and -r^-3; B_i^-1 has dominant eigenvalue -r^3.

Stronger finite-state theorem: if a three-strand model has finite entrywise
nonnegative invertible crossing matrices whose inverses are nonnegative,
and a fixed closure depending otherwise only on strand count and writhe,
it cannot recover Jones on all knot closures. Such matrices are monomial.

have equal writhe and model matrix while their actual Jones difference is
 -q^(m+2)(1+q+q^2)(sum_(j=0)^(m-1)(-q)^j)^2 !=0 for q>0, q!=1.
