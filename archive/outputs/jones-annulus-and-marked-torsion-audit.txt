JONES UNKNOT DETECTION: ONE-PUNCTURED ANNULI AND MARKED TORSION
1 November 2026. Research deductions, not a proof or counterexample.

Outcome
-------
The recent closed torsion relation does not yet supply a new knot
construction. Its canonical cuts are geometrically trivial after filling,
and arbitrary banding returns to the previously identified one-band ribbon
problem. A separate annulus calculation gives a stronger restriction:
in a once-punctured unknotted annulus twist family, infinitely many Jones-one
members force the complementary relative arc to have exactly the skein
class of an elementary arc, up to framing. This is an algebraic statement;
it does not show that the embedded arc is elementary.

The complete remaining endpoint is explicit. An actual nontrivial relative
arc with that skein class, the appropriate winding, an unknot closure, and
Taylor's incompressibility hypothesis would give counterexamples after
twisting. No such arc or marked torsion lift was constructed.

1. One-punctured annulus restriction
-----------------------------------
Let B be an annulus lying on an embedded sphere P in S^3, and let a knot K
meet the interior of B transversely in exactly one point. Its boundary is
disjoint from K. Denote the complementary disks of P by D1,D2. Let K_n be
the knot obtained by opposite n and -n disk twists at their boundaries,
with the zero-framing normalization used for the ordinary Jones polynomial.

THEOREM (research deduction). If V(K_n)=1 for infinitely many integers n,
then V(K_n)=1 for every n. The absolute linking numbers with the two annulus
boundaries are 0 and 1 in some order. In the solid torus complementary to
a neighborhood of B, the relative skein of the remaining arc is a framing
unit times one of the two elementary arcs.

Proof. Write R=Z[A,A^-1], F=Q(A), delta=-A^2-A^-2, and
Delta_r=(-1)^r(A^(2r)+A^(2r-4)+...+A^(-2r)).
Choose boundary orientations so w1=w2+1=w+1. This follows by algebraic
intersection with the once-punctured annulus.

The complement of a neighborhood of B is a solid torus with two marked
endpoints. Over F its relative skein has the trivalent graph basis
g_(j,l), where the three incident colors j,l,1 are admissible. Hence
|j-l|=1. The two colored edges meet D1,D2 respectively. Opposite disk
twists act with eigenvalue theta_j^n theta_l^(-n), where
theta_r=(-1)^r A^(r(r+2)). Removing transported framing contributes
(-A^3)^(-n(w1^2-w2^2)). Parity cancels the signs. Thus the normalized
frequencies in the finite graph expansion are

 d_(j,l)=j(j+2)-l(l+2)-3(w1^2-w2^2).

For j=l+1 the first difference is 2l+3; for l=j+1 it is -(2j+3).
These integers are distinct over all ordered adjacent pairs. The initial
short complementary arc closes g_(j,l) to its theta evaluation
Theta(1,j,l)=Delta_max(j,l), which is nonzero.

Consequently V(K_n) is a finite sum of distinct Laurent-monomial
exponentials in n, each with a nonzero closure weight. Such a sum equals
1 infinitely often only if its sole surviving frequency is zero and its
coefficient is 1. To see this without analytic assumptions, include the
constant term and evaluate at as many distinct integers as there are
frequencies. The generalized Vandermonde determinant is nonzero: after
ordering both integer lists, the rearrangement inequality gives a unique
largest exponent in its determinant expansion. Thus all coefficients of
the difference from 1 vanish.

It follows that the relative skein is c*g_(j,l) for one adjacent pair.
Here c is actually integral; this does NOT assert that arbitrary graph
expansions have integral coefficients. Use the free crossingless-diagram
basis of the marked annulus. The identity terms of the Jones-Wenzl
projectors give one highest diagram with coefficient 1. For g_(i,i+1)
this is the short arc with i core circles; for g_(i,i-1) it is the long
arc with i-1 core circles. Their minimal intersection pairs with the two
meridian cuts are respectively (i,i+1) and (i,i-1). Every other projector
term has a turnback and reduces at least one cut count, without increasing
the other. It cannot contribute to this highest diagram. The corresponding
coefficient of c*g is therefore exactly c. Since the original arc has
an integral skein class, c belongs to R.

The initial closure now gives

 c*Delta_max(j,l)=u(A)*delta,

where u is a framing unit and u(1)=+1 or -1. At A=1, integrality says that
max(j,l)+1 divides 2. Adjacency implies max(j,l)>=1, so max(j,l)=1.
The only pairs are (1,0) and (0,1), whose graph representatives are the
two elementary arcs. The zero-frequency equation then forces
{|w1|,|w2|}={0,1}. This proves the stated necessary conditions. End proof.

The graph basis and theta-closure conventions come from Abernathy--Gilmer,
Section 4 and Proposition 5.2. The leading-diagram argument above is the
additional lattice argument; the paper's graph basis is localized, and
must not be treated as an integral basis. The actual graph diagram on
printed page 1044 was inspected, alongside its integral crossingless basis.

The theorem concerns an entire marked family. It neither excludes an
isolated Jones-one knot nor proves a global reduction to annulus twists.
It also does not cover annuli meeting the knot zero times or more than
once. In particular, the earlier two-braid example uses zero punctures
and equal linking numbers, so its many diagonal channels are unavailable
here. The quantum-dimension denominator from that example is not ignored.

2. Complete conditional counterexample endpoint
-----------------------------------------------
Taylor's Theorem 1.3 states that if K_0 is an unknot and B is an unknotted
sufficiently incompressible twisting annulus meeting K_0 once, then K_n
is nontrivial for every |n|>=2. His Definition 5.1 supplies the precise
geometric incompressibility condition.

Therefore a sufficient construction is:
 - an actual pair (K_0,B) satisfying those geometric hypotheses;
 - complementary relative arc skein u*g_(1,0) or u*g_(0,1);
 - corresponding zero normalized twist frequency (linking magnitudes 0,1).

The unknot anchor fixes the scalar; the displayed spectral calculation
then gives V(K_n)=1 for all n. Taylor supplies nontriviality for |n|>=2.
All subsequent implications are accounted for. What remains unknown here
is existence of the actual marked geometry with those exact skein data.
Neither a formal graph nor an arc merely homotopic to the elementary arc
would supply it. No claim of existence or geometric detection is made.

3. Why the canonical torsion-band construction is finished
---------------------------------------------------------
For M=ST#ST, the explicit closed relation is

 (A^2-A^-2)([x1 disjoint-union x2]-[y disjoint-union U])=0,

with y the connected sum of the two core circles. To band the two sides,
one would need a relation after deleting a band ball while retaining its
four endpoint markings. The closed relation does not imply that relation.

After an S^3 filling, the complement of the deleted band ball is a ball.
Its four-point skein module is free of rank two on h,v. With coordinates
chosen so h has unlink numerator and unknot denominator, the two closure
maps on alpha*h+beta*v are

 N=delta^2*alpha+delta*beta,
 D=delta*alpha+delta^2*beta.

The determinant delta^2(delta^2-1) is nonzero. Thus a relative torsion
difference would force equality of both coordinates. The original closed
relation controls only one closure and cannot supply the other.

For the canonical cuts, both filled tangles have numerator the two-component
unlink and denominator the unknot geometrically. Scharlemann's theorem
implies that such a tangle is rational. Its numerator determinant is zero,
so its rational fraction is 0 in these fixed coordinates. Both tangles are
therefore the same trivial unframed tangle. Any common replacement inside
the band ball produces isotopic unframed links. This excludes all common
local replacements after these cuts, not just the first elementary band.

A different cut would require an actual nonrational unlinkable tangle T
with the full framed bracket vector [T]=[h]. Its denominator would have
Jones polynomial 1; Scharlemann's theorem would show that denominator is
nontrivial. This is a complete conditional endpoint, but no such T was
constructed. It lies in the one-band ribbon subclass already isolated in
earlier work. The closed torsion witness by itself imposes no restriction
on the arbitrary band and does not solve that older problem.

4. Further geometric filter on torsion candidates
------------------------------------------------
If an actual knot P in ST#ST intersects its reducing sphere in two points,
it factors as P1#P2 across the sphere. Write p1(x1),p2(x2) for the integral
annular skeins of the capped pieces. Generic connected-sum skein theory
gives delta[P]=p1(x1)p2(x2). If [P]-[y] is torsion, then

 p1(x1)p2(x2)=x1*x2.

Separate polynomial variables and integrality force
p1=+/-A^r*x1 and p2=+/-A^(-r)*x2, with reciprocal signs. Any nontrivial
Jones-one filling would already yield a nontrivial Jones-one one-axis
factor. Thus a new interacting torsion construction must use at least
four intersections with this sphere. Sphere-supported homeomorphisms
preserving filling slopes do not help: they extend over filling and
carry an unknot's spanning disk with them.
