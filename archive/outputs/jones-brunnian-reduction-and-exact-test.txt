JONES UNKNOT DETECTION: A BRUNNIAN REDUCTION AND AN EXACT TEST
Research pass 19 — 31 October 2026

STATUS

The general Jones unknot-detection problem is not resolved here. This pass
establishes a reduction within the unknotting-number-one case, repairs a
geometric gap in our previous approach, and evaluates an actual construction
exactly. It also gives the conditional amplification theorem below. The
mathematical deductions have been independently checked within
this investigation; we make no claim of literature priority.

The central distinction is between converting a hypothetical counterexample
into a useful form and constructing the first counterexample. The Brunnian
construction accomplishes the former. Our explicit test does not accomplish
the latter.

1. THE REDUCTION

The following three existence statements are equivalent:

  (i) A nontrivial knot K has unknotting number one and V_K(t)=1.

 (ii) A nontrivial strongly invertible knot J has a preferred exterior
      quotient tangle whose entire Kauffman bracket equals that of the
      zero rational tangle, up to the unavoidable framing unit.

(iii) There is a nonsplit two-component link P union D, with both components
      unknotted, linking number zero, and one involution strongly inverting
      both components, for which the normalized quotient operation has
      identically zero transmission factor d(A).

The new implication in this pass is (ii) => (iii). Our earlier Whitehead-clasp
attempt did not prove it: the inherited symmetry acts freely on the standard
clasp circle. The new construction uses a different, actually realizable
three-component link.

Here are precise meanings for the algebra. Put

  R = Z[A,A^-1],   delta = -A^2-A^-2,   v = Q_infinity-delta Q0.

For a preferred quotient T_J, its meridional closure is an actual unknot.
Divide by that closure's framing unit. The normalized bracket is

                         T_J = Q0 + x(J)v,       x(J) in R.

We call this quotient flat when x(J)=0. This condition is stronger than
asserting that the ordinary Jones polynomial of J is 1.

A two-component pattern as in (iii) induces an R-linear operation F with

                   F(Q0)=Q0,       F(v)=d(A)v.

Thus x(output)=d(A)x(input). A zero factor means exactly that the operation
erases this entire polynomial coordinate, for every input.

The previously established connection to (i) is concrete. Filling a flat
quotient at slope +/-1/2 gives a knot with Jones polynomial 1; a crossing
change gives the meridional unknot. Nontriviality follows because a nontrivial
S3 knot admits no nonmeridional S3 surgery. Conversely, removing an unknotting
crossing from a Jones-one knot gives such a flat quotient. The exact cap
formula, in one choice of signs and t=A^-4, is

                  V_B(A^-4)-1 = (A^2-A^-10)x(J).

This reduction does NOT show that every hypothetical Jones-one knot can be
replaced by an unknotting-number-one example. That remains a missing bridge
to the unrestricted problem.

1A. A STRONGER CONDITIONAL AMPLIFICATION THEOREM

If (i) holds, then for every positive integer n there is a nontrivial knot
K_n with V_K_n=1 and n marked crossings such that changing ANY nonempty
subset of those crossings produces the unknot.

The knots K_n depend on n. This does not assert that a single nontrivial knot
has this property for all n. In fact, the Howards–Luecke genus bound implies
g(K_n)>n/3, so this construction gives arbitrarily high Seifert genus. For
n>=3 these knots also have Alexander polynomial 1, by the established
adjacency theorem cited by Stoimenow immediately after equation (10).

Consequently, for any fixed n>=2, Jones detection among n-adjacent-to-the-
unknot knots would imply Jones detection among ALL unknotting-number-one
knots. A counterexample in the latter class could not remain an isolated
exception to the stronger adjacency restriction.

Proof outline. Use the lift of a nontrivial (n+1)-adjacent source knot to
obtain an (n+1)-component, componentwise strongly inverted Brunnian link.
Infect one component C with the flat companion J supplied by (ii). Every
proper sublink of the n remaining components is still an actual unlink.
The original n crossing-change neighborhoods survive the infection.

Perform the source's prescribed +/-1/2 surgeries on all but one of these
remaining components P. Before infection, the surviving pair P_* union C
is nonsplit: if it were split, its two unknotted components' remaining
prescribed surgeries would give S3, contradicting the nontrivial original
source knot's double branched cover. Hence P_* is an essential pattern.
After infection, P_*(J) is a nontrivial satellite. Its final nonmeridional
surgery cannot give S3, so the final branch knot K_n is nontrivial.

Undoing any nonempty subset of the n prescribed crossing changes leaves
surgeries on a proper unlink, and therefore returns the branch unknot.
Replacing the inserted flat quotient by Q0 preserves the complete bracket
and gives an unknot closure. Thus V_K_n is a monomial; V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0
force exactly V_K_n=1. This proves the conditional theorem without a
limiting argument or finite-order approximation.

2. WHY THE BRUNNIAN CONSTRUCTION WORKS

Choose an actual three-component link P union C union D with these properties:
every pair is the two-component unlink, the full link is nonsplit, and a
single involution strongly inverts all three components. Such links exist.
One construction starts with a nontrivial 3-adjacent-to-the-unknot knot:
three marked crossings have the property that changing any nonempty subset
gives the unknot. Change all three and lift the reverse crossing arcs in the
double cover of that unknot. Lidman–Moore's proof of Theorem 1.4 establishes
the required Brunnian property of this lifted link. Stoimenow's explicit
construction and Lemma 6 supply a nontrivial source knot.

Now replace the solid torus complementary to C by the corresponding satellite
embedding around the hypothetical flat knot J. Denote the two remaining
components by P(J) and D(J).

Each component remains an actual unknot: before replacement, each was local
in the solid torus because its pair with C was split. Their linking number
is zero, and the componentwise strong inversion extends over the companion
exterior. Thus P(J) union D(J) satisfies all the geometric requirements for
a normalized pattern operation.

Its transmission factor is zero. In the quotient, replacing T_J by its equal
skein vector Q0 turns the whole operation into that of the original split
pair P union D. Skein composition is linear, so this replacement preserves
the entire operation, after the fixed framing normalization.

The important remaining point is that P(J) union D(J) is NONSPLIT. Perform
the source knot's prescribed +/-1/2 surgery on D in the original triple.
The remaining pair P_* union C consists of unknots but is nonsplit. If it
were split, the remaining prescribed surgeries on P_* and C would give S3,
whereas the full surgeries give the nontrivial source knot's double branched
cover. This is a contradiction by Smith's unknot theorem.

Consequently P_* is an essential pattern. For nontrivial J, P_*(J) has an
incompressible companion torus and is nontrivial. But twisting the D(J)
component of a split pair of unknots could not change P(J) into a nontrivial
knot. Therefore the infected pair is nonsplit. This completes (ii) => (iii).

This proof uses a generalized twist, not an invariant two-puncture clasp disk.
It therefore avoids the specific geometric obstruction that invalidated the
earlier Whitehead construction.

The archive also proves an optional stronger geometric fact for arbitrary
such Brunnian triples: every twist of denominator absolute value at least two
leaves a nonsplit pair. That statement uses the precise CGLS reducing-filling
theorem and its no-lens-space-summand hypothesis. The chosen-source proof
above needs only its prescribed slopes and avoids that additional input.

3. THE EXACT ALGEBRA EXPLAINS BOTH THE OPPORTUNITY AND THE LIMIT

The quotient of the three-component Brunnian link has two input slots.
Pairwise splitness forces its normalized operation to have the form

        F(Q0+xv, Q0+yv) = Q0+c_B(A)xyv,       c_B(A) in R.

Proof: the terms containing exactly one v vanish because filling the other
slot by Q0 leaves a split pair. The meridional closure functional is the
product of the two input meridional functionals, so the remaining all-v term
lies in the rank-one kernel Rv. Its coefficient is c_B.

In particular,

                         d_(P(J) union D(J)) = c_B x(J).

This makes the reduction transparent. A flat input gives a zero factor.
If c_B is nonzero, a nonflat input cannot give one. The Brunnian construction
multiplies the defects; it does not add contributions with opposite signs.

For a componentwise strongly inverted Brunnian link with more input slots,
the same argument gives c_B times the product of all input defects. A seed
with c_B identically zero would be decisive. Its topological nontriviality
alone does not establish that coefficient's value.

4. AN ACTUAL SEED, COMPUTED EXACTLY

We reconstructed Stoimenow's n=3 diagram directly from his equation (10).
Start with the pure braid

                   beta_3 = [sigma_1^2, sigma_2^2].

Double its strands and add the six crossings in the specified clasp wiring.
The resulting diagram has 38 crossings; this is a diagram count, not a claim
about the knot's minimal crossing number. Its three marked crossing changes
were checked in all seven nonempty combinations. Every result simplifies by
actual diagram moves to a crossing-free, one-component unknot.

In the recorded Jones convention, the exact polynomial satisfies

 V_K3(t)-1 = -t^-12 (t-1)^7 (t+1)^3 (t^2+1)
                         (t^2+t+1)(t^2-t+1)^2.

In particular it is nonzero. Extracting the two input factors and the output
cap factor gives

 c_B(A) = unit * (t-1)^4 (t+1)(t^2+1)(t^3+1)^2,
                                                        t=A^-4,

where 'unit' means a signed power of A determined by the chosen peripheral
markings and chirality. The nonzero conclusion is independent of that unit.

Therefore this specific seed cannot create the first flat input, for ANY
nonflat companion. This is an exact exclusion of the whole companion family.
The same seed still proves the conversion theorem when a flat companion is
assumed.

Verification: Regina computed the polynomial directly from the original PD
diagram. A separate integer Kauffman-bracket contraction, using neither
Regina nor diagram simplification, reproduced all eight polynomials from the
38-crossing PD data. Independent exact Laurent multiplication checked the
displayed factorization. The data, complete diagram, and both computation
paths are included in the evidence archive.

5. TWO OTHER EXACT OBSTRUCTIONS

Equivariant connected sums cannot create a first flat preferred quotient.
Writing y(J)=1-delta*x(J), composition multiplies the y coordinates. For a
flat total, the factors must all be Laurent units. The preferred longitude
has determinant zero, which gives x(exp(pi*i/4))=0. Together these conditions
force every unit factor to equal 1, hence every individual x to be zero.
The proof is included in the archive; its scope is the specified equivariant
connected sum, without an unproved claim about every possible involution on
a composite knot.

There is also a geometric locality obstruction. In a strongly inverted solid
torus, invariant zero-linking two-puncture crossing-disk twists preserve the
property that a knot lies inside a ball in that torus. An equivariant sphere
gives an invariant containing ball, and the unique fixed-axis interval
between the disk's punctures allows its surgery circle to be moved into that
ball. Thus such local clasp moves cannot turn an essential pattern into a
local unknot. The proof requires the disk itself to be invariant and contained
in the solid torus. The Brunnian construction escapes precisely this scope.

6. WHAT REMAINS

The concrete target is still an essential pattern whose whole transmission
factor vanishes. We now know that this is equivalent to a Jones counterexample
with unknotting number one, rather than merely a sufficient construction.
The tested Brunnian seed, connected sums, and the audited local clasp repairs
do not supply such a pattern.

Two separate open steps must remain distinct: finding or excluding a zero
factor in this restricted setting, and connecting the restricted setting to
an arbitrary Jones-one knot. No finite collection of Taylor coefficients,
ordinary polynomial equality promoted to colored equality, or braid-matrix
faithfulness theorem supplies either missing step here.
