Jones unknot problem — concrete construction verdict
31 October 2026, America/Chicago

No proof or counterexample was found. The following are failed constructions,
not evidence that a solution is close. The tests used actual knot/link diagrams
and full polynomials, rather than additional conditional reductions.

1. ROTOR MODIFICATION OF AN EXPLICIT UNKNOT
Four copies of the tangle [-1]+[1/2]+[1/3] give a 24-crossing unknot completion.
Its rotor flip is also an unknot. Independent full bracket evaluation confirms
Jones=1 for both; geometric simplification reduces each to zero crossings.
The two tested asymmetric completions already have nonunit Jones polynomials.
This two-string necklace assembly is retired.

2. TWO-BAND FUSION OF A JONES-TRIVIAL THREE-COMPONENT LINK
The EKT LLL(1) link has 19 crossings and Jones polynomial t+2+t^-1, equal
to the three-component unlink. Its branched-cover homology and torsion linking
form do not obstruct two-band fusion to a determinant-one knot.

The explicit, bounded experiment generated 52,485 two-band closures from
355 first-band choices. Of these, 88 had determinant one and 22 had Jones
polynomial one. All 22 simplified to the crossing-free unknot. A separate
frontier state-sum implementation checked their full Jones polynomials;
fresh simplifications again gave unknots.

Scope: face bands with at most two half twists, on the chosen input and
simplified intermediate diagrams. These are counts of generated constructions,
not distinct knot types. Arbitrary embedded bands were not exhausted.

3. OVERLAPPING BRAID-KERNEL CONSTRUCTION
Four explicit six-strand closures built from overlapping Bigelow Burau-kernel
commutators have Alexander polynomial one but nonunit Jones polynomials,
with 146 or 290 nonzero terms. Full TL6 evaluation rejects all four.

CONCLUSION
The earlier mistake was treating conditional implications and the amount of
analysis as indications that a solution was approaching. These concrete tests
provide stopping points for the tested constructions. They do not resolve
whether the ordinary Jones polynomial detects the unknot.
