JONES UNKNOT DETECTION: A CONCRETE REPAIR ATTEMPT AND ITS LIMIT
1 November 2026

RESULT

We tested a repair of the Carmi--Cohen argument that removes its
component-orientation error. The repair genuinely works at that local
step, and a squared version retains the required finite-type coefficient
property. However, a general span argument shows that it cannot restore
the twist-length-independent compression needed by the proof.

No proof of Jones unknot detection and no nontrivial Jones-one knot has
been obtained. The statements below concern this specific repair. They
do not exclude every modified machine or every nonlinear descent argument.

1. THE REPAIR THAT ACTUALLY WORKS

Write x=A^-2, p=x-x^-1, and let ell(L) be the sum of the pairwise linking
numbers of an oriented link. In the normalization V_unknot=1, define

  F_L(x) = x^(-3 ell(L)) V_L(x),
  H_L(x) = F_L(x)^2.

The usual Jones normalization removes total writhe. F instead removes
only self-writhe: w_total=w_self+2ell and

  F_L=(-A^3)^(-w_self) <L>.

Reversing one component does not change its self-crossing signs or the
unoriented bracket. Thus F is orientation independent. It removes the
reorientation factor found in the preprint's exceptional local move.
The move also changes clasp choices; the original hypothesis ranges over
those choices, so this aspect alone need not defeat a revised argument.

On a knot ell=0, so F_K=V_K and H_K=V_K^2. Therefore H_K=1 is equivalent
to V_K=1: the Laurent polynomial ring is a domain and V_K(1)=1 excludes
V_K=-1. This modification retains the original detection target.

F itself fails a required finite-type condition. For a two-component
link, the constant coefficients in F=a(p)+xb(p) are

  a_0=-1+(-1)^ell,       b_0=-1-(-1)^ell.

They change when a mixed crossing is switched, so are not invariants of
order zero. In fact linking parity has nonzero finite differences of
arbitrarily high order.

Squaring fixes this problem. H is even in x. Every coefficient of h^n in

  H(e^h)=exp(-6ell h) V(e^h)^2

has finite-type order at most n: ell has order one, the Jones exponential
coefficients have the degree/order bound, and products add orders. To
transfer this statement to the finite p-expansion, put

  s(p)=sqrt(p^2+4),
  C(p)=H((p+s(p))/2).

The substitution h=arcsinh(p/2) preserves the order bound. Evenness gives

  b(p)=[C(p)-C(-p)]/s(p),
  a(p)=[C(p)+C(-p)]/2-pb(p)/2.

Consequently a_n(H) and b_n(H) have order at most n. The constant value
is H(1)=4^(number of components-1), which still records component count.
Thus this is a valid partial repair; it was not rejected on a superficial
finite-type objection. These deductions are not claimed as new literature.

2. WHY THE EXISTING DESCENT CANNOT SIMPLY BE REUSED

The normalization changes the skein relations. At a crossing between
components whose linking number in the positive state is m, one obtains

  F_+ - x F_- = p x^(2-3m) F_0.

At a self-crossing whose oriented smoothing produces two components with
linking number m, one obtains

  x^-2 F_+ - x^2 F_- = p x^(3m) F_0.

These follow by substituting V=x^(3ell)F into the Jones skein relation.
The coefficients involve global linking information. Furthermore, if
F_+=aF_-+bF_0, then

  H_+ - a^2 H_- - b^2 H_0 = 2ab F_-F_0.

The additional product term is nonzero in general. The preprint's linear
three-state recursion is therefore not a recursion for H. This alone
does not exclude a more elaborate replacement; the next argument gives
the decisive obstruction to preserving its compression conclusion.

3. A GENERAL OBSTRUCTION TO NORMALIZATION-AND-POWER REPAIRS

Suppose Laurent polynomials T_m admit an expression

  T_m=A(x)+B(x)x^(dm),

where A and B are nonzero rational functions and d>0. Clearing a fixed
denominator leaves two nonzero Laurent polynomials whose supports are
disjoint for sufficiently large m. Since Laurent span is additive under
multiplication, it follows that

  span(T_m)=dm+O(1).

For any integer shifts e_m and any fixed positive integer s,

  span((x^(e_m)T_m)^s)=s dm+O(1).                       (1)

Monomial normalization preserves span and a positive power multiplies
it. Both nonzero terms in the expression for T_m are essential: a lone
moving monomial could be removed by normalization.

Application to the source construction.

The earlier audit used the paper's directly cancelable clasp states and
its double-smoothing identification. In the fixed three-crossing unknot
diagram obtained by switching the trefoil's unknotting crossing, these
identities give a locked clasp state

  T_m=C(x)-p^2 R_m,
  R_m=[x^2(x^4+1)+(1-x^6)x^(4m)]/(x^2+1),

where C is a fixed Laurent polynomial. R_m is the actual ordinary twist
family with R_0=1 and fixed smoothing -x-x^5. The exceptional move under
dispute is not used in deriving these identities.

Here

  A=C-p^2 x^2(x^4+1)/(x^2+1),
  B=-p^2(1-x^6)/(x^2+1).

Both are nonzero for every possible Laurent C. Indeed, multiplying by
x^2+1 and setting x=i gives respectively -8 and 8. A Laurent polynomial
C has no pole at i and cannot cancel the displayed rational pole.
Therefore (1) applies with d=4. Whatever the state's linking number,

  span(F_m)=4m+O(1),        span(H_m)=8m+O(1).

A JVP degree bound D in the basis {1,x}, p=x-x^-1, confines Laurent
exponents to [-D,D+1], giving span at most 2D+1. Thus no fixed D can
bound the repaired family. Enlarging a constant overhead does not help.

Scope of the geometric input: this application uses the source's direct
clasp reductions and double-smoothing identification, as documented in
the prior audit. It is not presented as a new full planar-diagram
reconstruction of every host-machine member. If one of those source
identifications is rejected, that is another required change to the
geometric package. The algebraic conclusion from the identities is exact.
The chosen host is a nonminimal unknot diagram, so this does not refute
a new lemma restricted solely to minimal diagrams of hypothetical
nontrivial Jones-one knots. Such a lemma would require a new proof and
would also need to handle the generated hosts in the two-machine descent.

4. TWO OTHER GLOBAL BRIDGES CHECKED IN THIS PASS

Categorical units. For an actual cut knot, tensor-invertibility of its
reduced Khovanov complex forces rank-one homology by the Kunneth formula,
and hence the unknot. But asserting that a unit Jones polynomial forces
that invertibility is exactly equivalent to the original detection
problem. Passing to a field of rational functions makes every nonzero
Jones polynomial a unit and loses the relevant distinction. This branch
was stopped as a reformulation without a new mechanism.

Four-dimensional modules. The square knot R=trefoil#mirror(trefoil)
is slice but nontrivial. Ren--Willis Proposition 1.12 implies that, for
every integer framing n, the Lee skein lasagna modules of X_n(R) and
X_n(U) agree with their gradings and quantum filtration. Nevertheless,
zero surgery on R is a closed genus-two surface mapping torus and is
aspherical; it has no nonseparating sphere. A slice disk capped by the
two-handle core does give a sphere inside X_0(R), illustrating the
difference between an interior sphere and the required boundary sphere.
Thus even this full filtered Lee-module equality cannot by itself force
the geometric handle cancellation. No equality of Jones polynomials or
of Khovanov lasagna modules is asserted here.

VERIFICATION AND CONSEQUENCE

The normalization, finite-type transfer and span argument received
independent algebraic audits. Exact integer arithmetic checked the Hopf
and trefoil skein identities, the extra product term, the two residues
at i, and the residual twist recurrence. These checks support the proofs;
they are not substitutes for the stated general arguments.

The original orientation error is not the only missing ingredient.
Removing it leaves an unbounded polynomial term that the proposed
compression would have to eliminate. This repair therefore does not
complete the claimed proof. The main unknot-detection objective remains
unresolved.
