JONES UNKNOT DETECTION: A BOUNDED PASS AT PROOF MECHANISMS
31 October 2026

Outcome

This pass produced a short, independently checked proof for an infinite
class, and identified precise obstructions to several proposed extensions.
It did not prove Jones unknot detection for arbitrary knots and did not
find a nontrivial knot with Jones polynomial 1. No novelty is claimed.

The most useful connection was to sparse moment recovery: a fixed twist
pattern produces finitely many exponential terms, and vanishing Jones
derivatives imposes rigid equations on their integer frequencies. In the
three-strand pretzel case those equations reduce to a positive definite
quadratic form. This is an actual calculation, not a physical analogy.

1. A COMPLETE RESTRICTED PROOF

Proposition. For every three-strand pretzel knot K=P(p,q,r),

             V_K''(1)=V_K'''(1)=0  implies  K is the unknot.

Here V_U(t)=1. There is no bound on the integer twist parameters. Jones
detection for pretzel knots was already established in the literature;
the point of this calculation is the short proof and the method it tests.
Nicholson: https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609584
Low-order pretzel Vassiliev formulas also appear in Sleptsov:
https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.07192
We have not established priority for this exact two-derivative formulation.

Conventions and normalization

P(p,q,r) is the numerator closure of the sum of rational tangles with
slopes 1/p, 1/q, 1/r. A zero parameter means the infinity tangle. This
closure is a knot precisely when all parameters are odd or exactly one
is even. Set

 y=A^4,  d=y+2+y^(-1),  X_j=(-1)^j y^(-j),  S=p+q+r.

The twist-bracket formula gives

 <P(p,q,r)> = A^S E/d,
 E = X_p X_q X_r + (d-1)(X_p+X_q+X_r) + (d-1)(d-2).

Input: Champanerkar--Kofman, Proposition 2.1 and the pretzel example:
https://arxiv.org/html/math/0605631
We also recovered this identity independently in the two-dimensional
Temperley--Lieb algebra. The choice of A convention can replace the
Jones variable by its inverse; simultaneous vanishing of the second
and third derivatives at 1 is invariant under that replacement.

Write D=y d/dy. Jones normalization has the form

                 E = epsilon d y^m V_K(y or y^(-1)),

where epsilon is a sign. Since V_K(1)=1 and V_K'(1)=0, the zeroth and
first moments determine epsilon and m. If F=E-epsilon d y^m, then
F(1)=DF(1)=0. The desired joint vanishing is equivalent to
D^2F(1)=D^3F(1)=0. A nonzero analytic normalization factor does not change
the order of vanishing. The following computations use exact integers.

Case A: p,q,r all odd

E(1)=-4 and DE(1)=4S, so epsilon=-1 and m=-S. Factor

 F=(d-1)Q,
 Q=y^(-S)-y^(-p)-y^(-q)-y^(-r)+y+y^(-1).

Q(1)=DQ(1)=0, and

 D^2Q(1) = 2(pq+pr+qr+1),
 D^3Q(1) = -3(p+q)(q+r)(r+p).

Thus some pair must sum to zero. Taking p=-q, the quadratic equation
becomes 1-p^2=0. All possibilities are permutations of (k,1,-1).

Case B: exactly one parameter even

Reorder so p is even, and write q=a+b, r=a-b. Since q and r are odd,
a and b are integers. Now epsilon=1 and m=a-p. Differentiation yields

 D^2F(1) = 6(1-a^2-b^2+2pa),
 D^3F(1) = 6a(1+3b^2-a^2-3p^2).

Eliminating b^2 gives

                    a[4-a^2-3(p-a)^2]=0.

If a=0, then b^2=1, again giving (k,1,-1).
Otherwise a^2+3(p-a)^2=4. Its integer solutions have:

 * a=+/-2 and p=a, which would require b^2=5 and are impossible;
 * a=+/-1 and p=0, giving (0,1,1) or its mirror;
 * a=+/-1 and p=2a, giving permutations of (1,-2,-3) or its mirror.

This exhausts the possibilities.

Geometry of the survivors

Rotating and reversing the three boxes permits all parameter permutations.
In (k,1,-1), the adjacent integer tangles +1 and -1 cancel; the remaining
numerator closure of slope 1/k is an unknot. The same is true for k=0.
The infinity tangle with two unit twists gives the unknot P(0,1,1), and
mirroring preserves this conclusion. Finally a pretzel with a unit box
is a two-bridge link. P(1,-2,-3) has determinant
|pq+pr+qr|=|-2-3+6|=1. A two-bridge link of determinant 1 is the unknot.
This also handles its mirror and finishes the proposition.

Verification and its role

The proof above is symbolic and covers all integer parameters. Separate
checks recovered the bracket for 2,197 triples, classified all solutions
with an exact affine-exponent solver, and independently audited all 60
solver branches. Direct Reidemeister simplification checked the exceptional
geometric cases. These checks support the algebra and conventions; finite
testing is not the reason the proposition holds for arbitrary parameters.

2. THE REUSABLE CONNECTION: SPARSE MOMENTS AND INTEGER LATTICES

Fix a wiring diagram containing finitely many two-strand twist sites.
After fixing twist parities and clearing denominators, its bracket-minus-
target condition is a finite Laurent sum of the form

        F_n(A) = sum_{i=1}^M c_i A^(a_i + b_i dot n).

The coefficients c_i and integer affine exponent functions are fixed;
the entries of n can be arbitrarily large. If the target is an arbitrary
signed monomial, include its exponent as one further integer variable.

Two elementary facts make this suitable for exhaustive proofs.

(i) Exact collision classification. The identity F_n=0 holds precisely
when the terms can be partitioned into blocks whose coefficients sum to
zero and whose exponents agree. There are finitely many partitions. Each
one imposes only integer linear equations in n. Each nonempty solution
set is a translate of an integer lattice. Projecting away an auxiliary
monomial exponent again gives a translate of a lattice. Consequently the
identity locus is a finite union of such sets, including the parity
constraints. No cutoff on twist magnitude is required.

This is also an algorithm: choose an uncancelled term and branch on its
exponent coinciding with an opposite-sign term. Every new nontrivial
equation increases the rank of the linear system, so a branch has bounded
depth. It may be computationally expensive, but it terminates.

(ii) Finite moments certify an identity. If a Laurent polynomial has at
most M nonzero monomials, then

          (A d/dA)^j F(1)=0 for j=0,...,M-1  implies F=0.

After collecting equal exponents e_i, these equations have matrix
(e_i^j), a Vandermonde matrix. Distinct exponents make it invertible.
This bound depends on the number of terms, not their exponent sizes.

In the pretzel example, only two nonautomatic moments were needed to
force the geometry, through the small integer ellipse above. This suggests
seeking positive definite combinations of moments in other fixed templates.

Limitation: arbitrary knots require arbitrarily complicated templates.
Nothing here supplies a universal finite template list, a universal
derivative order, or a geometric interpretation of every cancellation
lattice. Those are substantive missing steps, not implementation details.

3. WHY TWO TEMPTING GENERAL PROOFS FAIL

Extreme coefficients and diagram genus

For a connected diagram with c crossings and Turaev genus g_D, let k_A
and k_B count the potential outer bracket layers that vanish at the two
ends. The exact state-sum identity is

                 span V = c-g_D-k_A-k_B.

A genus-only bound on cancellation depth would be attractive. It fails:
standard Kanenobu diagrams D(n,-n) have c=2n+8, g_D=2, and constant Jones
polynomial V_(4_1)^2 of span 8. Therefore k_A+k_B=2n-2 is unbounded.
For n>=3 these diagrams are within one crossing of minimal. This rules
out that bound even for nearly minimal diagrams; it makes no claim about
all possible presentations of those knots.

Primary inputs: Qazaqzeh--Mansour, Proposition 1.2,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.0683 ; del Valle Vilchez--Lowrance, Section 6,
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21274 .

The obstruction is especially relevant because Turaev genus one is already
covered by Lowrance--Spyropoulos:
https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2017/23-71p.pdf
Any genus-two extension must control long cancellation mechanisms, not
simply inspect a genus-dependent fixed number of outer layers.

Quantum positivity and vacuum rigidity

We checked a useful full-TQFT endpoint: a boundary mapping class preserves
the handlebody vacuum line at infinitely many unbounded SU(2) levels if
and only if it extends over the handlebody. The proof uses a fixed finite
colored-spine expansion, specialization of the integral skein module at
A=-1, faithful free-group representations, and disk detection. Its full
argument and assumptions are included in the evidence archive; no novelty
is claimed.

But ordinary Jones=1 does not imply vacuum saturation. For a g-bridge plat,
normalized cup state Omega, and unitary level r with d=2cos(pi/r),

       |V_K| = d^(g-1) |<Omega,U_beta Omega>|.

Thus V_K=1 gives overlap d^(1-g), strictly less than 1 when g>1 and r>3.
The squared norm outside the vacuum is 1-d^(2-2g), which is positive.
The missing equality cannot be supplied by Cauchy--Schwarz. Moreover the
full handlebody theory contains data beyond the fixed-color ordinary
Jones representation. Deriving a sufficient rigidity premise from the
ordinary Jones polynomial remains unproved.

Sources for the standard ingredients and normalization:
https://arxiv.org/html/math/0209150
https://arxiv.org/abs/q-alg/9604014
https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v011a006/v011a006.pdf

4. A NARROWER GEOMETRIC TARGET THAT SURVIVES THIS PASS

For a ribbon knot formed from the two-component unlink by one band,
Sarkar's ribbon-distance theorem bounds the generalized Lee torsion
blocks to length one. With reduced grading conventions this gives

 P_red(t,q)=1+(1+t q^2)S(t,q),
 V_K(q^2)-1=(1-q^2)S(-1,q).

Here S has nonnegative coefficients, but S(-1,q) is a signed sum. A
sufficient geometric lemma would say that at some extreme source quantum
grade, even and odd homological source multiplicities are unequal.
This lemma is unproved. It would settle this restricted ribbon subclass.

Actual Kinoshita--Terasaka homology has complete internal cancellations
at two source grades, so positivity at every grade is false. Its extremes
do survive. We also built a formal rank-nine reduced complex obeying the
length-one restriction, slice normalization, a free-summand retraction,
and the full-twist grading law, but having Euler polynomial 1. No knot
realization is asserted. This demonstrates why the existing algebraic
constraints alone do not prove the needed geometric lemma.

Primary inputs: Sarkar https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.11095 ; Wang
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01070 ; Kinoshita--Terasaka data in Sano
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.17435 . Exact calculations are in the archive.

The direction I would prioritize is classifying cancellation lattices in
additional fixed twist templates, then asking which lattices are forced
by genuine geometric simplifications. The pretzel proof supplies a working
example; the Kanenobu family supplies a necessary stress test. Neither
currently provides the missing global theorem.
