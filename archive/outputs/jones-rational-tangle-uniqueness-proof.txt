RATIONAL SOLUTIONS OF FRAMED TANGLE EQUATIONS ARE UNIQUE
Research deduction, 1 November 2026 — pass 50

STATUS AND SCOPE

This note gives an internally checked argument for the rational-solution
uniqueness statement formulated as Conjecture 2 in Sikora's paper [S]. It
extends the preceding pass's result by removing the unknot-product hypothesis.
The argument combines published results; literature novelty and external
refereeing are not claimed.

It does NOT prove that the Jones polynomial detects the unknot. Sikora proves
that Jones detection implies this uniqueness statement. That implication
cannot be reversed by logic alone. The result instead closes the entire
proposed counterexample route through a rational/nonrational ambiguity in
two framed rational tangle equations.

STATEMENT

Let P and Q be framed rational closing tangles with distinct underlying
slopes. Let R be a framed rational tangle, and X any framed 2-tangle, all
with the same boundary marking. Suppose

                  C_P(X)=C_P(R),     C_Q(X)=C_Q(R)

by balanced framed isotopy, in the convention of [S, Section 4]. Here
C_P(X) is the link obtained by attaching P to X; equivalently one can use
Sikora's notation N(X+P). Then X=R as balanced framed tangles.

The ambient balls are oriented, and link equivalences preserve the ambient
orientation. Link components need not carry chosen orientations. Distinct
framings of the same rational slope do not count as distinct probes.

PROOF

1. Equality of bracket vectors.

The two rational closure functionals are linearly independent over Q(A).
Thus the hypotheses imply [X]=[R] in the rank-two relative Kauffman bracket
module [S, Theorem 21]. In particular every rational closure of X and the
corresponding closure of R have equal determinants.

Normalization: if a circle has unreduced bracket delta=-A^2-A^-2, divide
the closed bracket by delta as a polynomial before evaluating at
A=exp(pi i/4). The absolute value gives the determinant; framing and writhe
factors have modulus one there. Direct evaluation of the unreduced bracket
would vanish for every nonempty link and would give no information.

2. The cover is an integral homology solid torus with the correct longitude.

Write V for the oriented double branched cover of R and M for that of X.
The cover V is a solid torus. Use the common boundary marking to identify
their slope lattices. Choose lambda bounding a disk in V and choose mu with
oriented intersection one with lambda. Thus

                     V(lambda)=S^1 x S^2,    V(mu)=S^3.

The corresponding branch closures of R are the two-component unlink and
the unknot. Step 1 therefore gives

              det C_lambda(X)=0,      det C_mu(X)=1.

The determinant-one closure is a knot: an m-component link has determinant
divisible by 2^(m-1) [St, Proposition 6.5]. Hence X has no closed components,
since any closed component of X would persist alongside a component made
from its two arcs in every closure. This also handles the convention in
which a 2-tangle may initially contain closed components.

The filled manifold Y=M(mu) is an integral homology sphere. Its filling core
is a knot K with exterior M. Consequently H_1(M;Z)=Z and H_2(M;Z)=0.
The determinant-zero lambda filling has infinite first homology. For a knot
in an integral homology sphere, the unique slope with this property is its
preferred longitude. Thus lambda is also the preferred longitude of K.

We have now identified a common meridian/longitude basis for M and V.
Changing mu by a multiple of lambda is allowed; choose it distinct from
the two original probes. No assertion that Y is S^3 is made.

At most one original probe can be lambda. All other rational-link products
are nonsplit and their covers are lens spaces with finite first homology,
including S^3 when the product is the unknot. In particular at least one
filling of M is an actual lens space.

We may also conclude that M is irreducible. A closed connected-sum summand
of M has trivial first homology, since H_1(M)=Z and the torus-boundary
summand already has positive first Betti number. Such a closed summand
persists in every filling. An actual lens-space filling, together with
prime decomposition, forces each of these summands to be S^3.

3. Two nonzero-determinant products.

First suppose neither original probe is lambda. Write their slopes as
p_i mu+q_i lambda, where p_i is nonzero and (p_i,q_i) is primitive. Their
distinctness means q_1/p_1 differs from q_2/p_2.

The actual equivalences of the closed branch links lift to orientation-
preserving diffeomorphisms

                Y_(p_i/q_i)(K) = S^3_(p_i/q_i)(U),   i=1,2.       (1)

The coefficients in (1) are the SAME signed coefficients. The common
oriented boundary marking and the longitude identification in step 2 ensure
this. Knowing only the orders of the two lens-space homology groups, or
only unoriented homeomorphism types, would not suffice.

Let Delta_K(t)=Delta_K(t^-1), Delta_K(1)=1. The Casson-Walker surgery formula
[NW, Proposition 3.1] applied to (1) gives

        0 = lambda_CW(Y) + (q_i/(2p_i)) Delta_K''(1),   i=1,2.

Subtracting the two equations yields Delta_K''(1)=0. It also yields
lambda_CW(Y)=0, but we do not infer that Y is S^3 from this.

Both fillings in (1) are L-spaces. Rasmussen--Rasmussen [RR, Proposition 1.3]
therefore imply that M is Floer simple. Their Proposition 1.4 gives a
normalization of its Turaev torsion

                 tau(M)=D(t)/(1-t)=sum_(n>=0) a_n t^n,

where a_0=1, every a_n is zero or one, and a_n=1 for sufficiently large n.
Since H_1(M)=Z, D(t) is the polynomial normalization of the Alexander
polynomial. Its coefficients are a_n-a_(n-1), with a_(-1)=0. Accordingly
its nonzero coefficients alternate +1,-1,+1,...,+1.

Symmetrize BEFORE differentiating: D(t)=t^g Delta_K(t), where 2g=deg D.
Here g denotes the exponent shift, not an assumed Seifert genus. Symmetry,
alternation and Delta_K(1)=1 give

 Delta_K(t)=(-1)^k + sum_(j=1)^k (-1)^(k-j)(t^(n_j)+t^(-n_j)),
                         0<n_1<...<n_k.

The middle coefficient is present because symmetric noncentral terms occur
in pairs, while the sum of the alternating coefficients is one. Hence

               Delta_K''(1)=2 sum_(j=1)^k (-1)^(k-j) n_j^2.

For k>0, pairing the terms from the largest exponent downward writes this
as a sum of strictly positive differences of squares, and possibly an
additional positive square. Therefore Delta_K''(1)>0 unless k=0. Its
vanishing forces Delta_K=1 and D=1.

If M were boundary incompressible, [RR, Corollary 2.3] would give its
Thurston norm as deg D-1=-1, impossible. Thus M is boundary compressible.
As M is irreducible with torus boundary, it is a solid torus.

4. A two-component unlink product.

It remains to consider an original probe equal to lambda. Its actual shared
product is the two-component unlink, so M(lambda)=S^1 x S^2. The other
probe supplies a lens-space filling and hence an L-space filling.

View M as the exterior of the filling core in S^1 x S^2. Ni--Vafaee
[NV, Theorem 1.1] imply that M fibers over the circle. Choose a connected
fiber F, so the fibration induces an isomorphism H_1(M)=Z -> Z.

The inclusion H_1(boundary M) -> H_1(M) is surjective: its cokernel lies in
H_1(M,boundary M)=H^2(M)=0. Therefore the boundary fibration is surjective
on H_1 as well. A torus bundle over the circle with k circle components
in its fiber has image kZ. It follows that k=1 and F has exactly one
boundary component, of slope lambda.

Capping this boundary with a disk extends the fibration over M(lambda).
The result is a closed orientable surface bundle over the circle whose
total space is S^1 x S^2. Its fiber group injects into the total fundamental
group Z. No closed orientable positive-genus surface group can inject into
Z. The capped fiber is therefore S^2, F is a disk, and M is a solid torus.

5. Return to tangles.

In every case M is a solid torus. The standard branched-cover criterion
therefore makes X rational [BM, Theorem 3.5(2)]. The bracket distinguishes
balanced framed rational tangles [S, Corollary 20]. Since [X]=[R], we obtain
X=R, as claimed. End proof.

WHAT THIS RESULT DOES AND DOES NOT PROVIDE

The statement matches the framed rational-solution uniqueness assertion of
[S, Conjecture 2], including its distinct-probe convention and the possibility
of closed components in an initially arbitrary 2-tangle. The internal audit
found no remaining gap in this deduction from the cited results. An external
specialist review remains appropriate before treating it as a new theorem
in the literature.

This closes the proposed two-probe rational/nonrational construction without
any search through the Baker--Doleshal--Hoffman lens-filling families. Any
unframed ambiguity of this form must have different total framing defects
at the two probes; otherwise one common framing adjustment would contradict
the result.

It does not settle Jones unknot detection. A Jones-one knot, tied locally
into a rational tangle, could preserve its bracket while making its closures
connected sums with that unknown knot. Those closures have the appropriate
POLYNOMIALS, but are not established rational links. Our proof needs actual
lens-space or S^1 x S^2 fillings. Polynomial equality cannot replace those
topological hypotheses. No generic reduction supplying them has been proved.

The remaining general claim that the bracket detects rational tangles among
all tangles is stronger than the two-equation uniqueness statement. Confusing
these two claims would falsely turn this partial result into a proof of the
original conjecture.
