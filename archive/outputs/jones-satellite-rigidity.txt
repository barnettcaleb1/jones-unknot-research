JONES UNKNOT INVESTIGATION: A STRUCTURAL SATELLITE RESULT
31 October 2026

Status: the general unknot-detection problem remains unresolved in this
investigation. This note proves a restriction on a proposed method of attack.
It does not prove Jones unknot detection or exhibit a counterexample. No claim
of novelty is made; the argument is a deduction from standard skein and
colored-Jones formulas, checked independently during this pass.

1. THE RESULT

A satellite construction fixes a knot-shaped pattern P inside a solid torus,
then ties that torus around an input knot K. The companion longitude is always
zero-framed. Let w be the algebraic winding number of P.

Suppose one rational function f, with coefficients depending on the polynomial
variable q but not on K, satisfies

                      V_{P(K)}(q) = f(V_K(q))

for every knot K at which the expression is defined. Then:

  * If w is even, f is constant.
  * If w is odd, f(X) = c(q) X for some fixed c(q).

If P sends the unknot U to U, these alternatives are f = 1 and f(X) = X.
In particular, no fixed satellite pattern can universally square the ordinary
Jones polynomial. No winding-zero pattern can universally preserve it.

In fact, a finite linear combination of colored Jones polynomials can be a
rational function of any ONE fixed color only when that function is affine.
This last statement concerns algebraic dependence, not the topology of the
pattern.

2. WHY THIS MATTERS FOR A PROOF ATTEMPT

One proposed reduction was to turn an arbitrary hypothetical Jones-one knot
into a ribbon knot, or a ribbon knot with a single fusion band, while retaining
its Jones information. These classes have additional geometric and homological
structure that might make detection easier.

Any fixed satellite P that produces a slice knot for EVERY companion has
winding zero. Indeed, Litherland's signature formula gives

  sigma_{P(K)}(omega) = sigma_{P(U)}(omega) + sigma_K(omega^w).

If w is nonzero, choose K to be a trefoil and a regular omega for which the
last term is nonzero. Since both P(U) and P(K) would be slice, their signatures
vanish, a contradiction. Ribbon knots are slice, so this applies to universal
ribbon constructions as well.

Combining this fact with Section 1 rules out any such fixed construction with
a NONCONSTANT universal rational rule for its ordinary Jones polynomial.
This includes exact preservation V_{P(K)} = V_K and squaring V_{P(K)} = V_K^2.

The weaker implication

                 V_K = 1  ==>  V_{P(K)} = 1

is NOT excluded. It makes a claim only on one fiber of the invariant, whereas
the theorem assumes an identity for all companions. A reduction also needs
nontriviality to survive: a pattern that always gives U is of no use. Nor does
a constant annular skein expression prove that its pattern is geometrically
trivial. Constructions depending on K, or using K and its mirror as separate
inputs, lie outside the fixed one-companion argument.

3. PROOF BY TWO COEFFICIENTS

Use reduced zero-framed colored Jones polynomials H_N, normalized by
H_N(U) = 1, H_1 = 1, and H_2 = V. Work over F = Q(q^(1/4)).

The Kauffman bracket skein of the solid torus is F[z]. Its Chebyshev basis
S_j(z) evaluates on a companion to the color j+1 invariant, up to a quantum
dimension absorbed in its coefficient. Thus every fixed pattern has a finite
expansion

                    V_{P(K)} = sum_j c_j(q) H_{j+1}(K).

Smoothing preserves homology modulo two. Therefore c_j = 0 unless j and w
have the same parity. The zero-framing convention makes these coefficients
independent of the companion.

Test the identity on the two-strand torus knots K_m = T(2, 2m+1), choosing
the chirality in the following standard formula. Set

       [n] = (q^(n/2) - q^(-n/2))/(q^(1/2) - q^(-1/2)).

Then

  H_{j+1}(K_m)
    = 1/[j+1] sum_{a=0}^j (-1)^(j-a) [2a+1]
        q^((2m+1)(a(a+1)-j(j+2))/2).

At m=0 this equals 1. At j=1, m=1 it gives q^-1 + q^-3 - q^-4.
Changing chirality replaces q by q^-1 and leaves the proof unchanged.

Write x = q^-m. The right side is a polynomial G_j(x) over F. For every
j >= 1, its two highest powers of x have degrees

                         j(j+2),   j(j+2)-2.

The ratio of their coefficients, second divided by first, is always

                              R = -q[3],

independent of j and nonzero. Lower colors cannot change these two
coefficients: the degree gap from color label j to j-1 is 2j+1 >= 3.
In particular, G_1(x) = b x^3 + a x, with a/b = R.

Our assumed identity implies the rational-function identity

                         sum_j c_j G_j(x) = f(G_1(x)).

To see this, clear denominators. The resulting polynomial vanishes at the
infinitely many distinct elements x = q^-m of F, so it vanishes identically.

First f must be polynomial. Write f = P/Q with coprime P,Q in F[X]. A Bezout
identity remains valid after substituting G_1, so P(G_1) and Q(G_1) remain
coprime. Since their quotient is polynomial, Q(G_1), hence Q, is constant.

Let d > 0 be the degree of f, and J the largest color label with c_J != 0.
Comparison of highest degrees gives

                              3d = J(J+2).

On the left, the ratio of the next relevant coefficient to the leading one
is R. On the right it is dR: one chooses the a x term in exactly one of d
factors of (b x^3 + a x)^d. Lower powers of G_1 have degree at most 3d-3,
so they cannot affect this comparison. Therefore R = dR, forcing d = 1
and then J = 1. The distinct leading degrees of the remaining G_j imply

                             f(X) = c_0 + c_1 X.

Parity eliminates c_1 for even winding and c_0 for odd winding. If f was
constant initially, the same distinct-degree argument eliminates all
positive color labels. This proves the stated result.

Replacing G_1 by G_s, s >= 1, gives the single-color extension: its leading
degree is s(s+2) >= 3 and its coefficient ratio is the same R. The comparison
again forces d=1, then J=s. Only H_1 and H_{s+1} can occur.

4. OTHER CHECKED RESULTS FROM THIS PASS

Unitarity does not supply the missing positivity. For the actual four-strand
braids

  beta_m = sigma_2^(2m) (sigma_1 sigma_2) sigma_2^(-2m) sigma_3^(-1),

every closure is U: destabilize once, then conjugate to sigma_1 sigma_2.
All have writhe 1 and identical full Jones polynomial. Nevertheless the
two-component closure of beta_m^2 has linking number 2m, and, in t=A^-4,

                 (d/dA) V_{closure(beta_m^2)}(1) = 24m.

Thus even the entire first Jones trace does not bound higher trace moments
at fixed strand number and writhe. These representatives are not minimal;
the example does not disprove a special theorem about minimal Jones-one
representatives, and is not a counterexample to unknot detection.

Mirror doubling retains cancellations. If e_j and o_j are the even and odd
homological dimensions of reduced rational Khovanov homology at quantum
degree j, the quantum-degree-zero part of the mirror double has

  Euler coefficient E_0 = sum_j (e_j-o_j)^2,
  actual dimension  D_0 = sum_j (e_j+o_j)^2.

Their difference is 4 sum_j e_j o_j. Jones supplies E_0, not D_0. Actual
knots 5_1 and mirror(10_132) have the same Jones polynomial but reduced
ranks 5 and 11. Their mirror doubles have ranks 25 and 121, respectively,
while retaining equal Jones polynomials. The two D_0 values are 5 and 21;
both E_0 values are 5.

Twist sampling needs geometric control. Two actual unknot members of a
single antiparallel two-strand full-twist family force all members to be
unknots. But three unknot samples do not suffice for arbitrary coupled
twist parameters. With a=sigma_1, b=sigma_2 and [x,y]=xyx^-1y^-1, the family

                [a^(2n), [b^(2(n-1)), a^(2(n-2))]] ab

closes to U at n=0,1,2, yet has determinant

       |1 + 8n(n-1)(n-2)[8(n-1)^2(n-2) + 6n - 8]| > 1

for every n>=3. The n=3 determinant is 2017. This family does NOT have
constant Jones polynomial; it refutes sampling alone as a certificate.

5. REMAINING RESEARCH TARGET AND LITERATURE CHECK

The best geometric lead in this pass is a constrained banded-link question:
if a two-component link has the unlink's Jones polynomial and an oriented
band turns it into an actual unknot, must that link be split? A positive
answer would settle a particular unknot-anchored twist-family problem.
Known split-band theorems supply the subsequent geometric conclusion.
Neither the splitness assertion nor a reduction of arbitrary knots to this
case has been proved here.

Khovanov multicurve theory restricts a tangle with an unknot closure to one
rational component and special components of a common slope. The cited
proof uses homology dimensions and gradings. Its deductions cannot simply
be transferred to Jones Euler characteristics. Formally, a special sector
C together with its homological shift C[1] cancels under every Euler pairing;
no assertion that such a sector is realized by a classical tangle is made.
Geometric realizability is an additional question, not an algebraic detail.

Carmi--Cohen's arXiv record remains v1 as checked on 31 October 2026. The
earlier identified failure of simultaneous uniform diagrammatic control is
not repaired by the finite-channel twist formula: that formula leaves an
uncontrolled second channel. No validated generic proof was found here.

A separate September 2026 revision by Bharathram, Birman and Brendle claims
faithfulness of the four-strand Burau representation. Its stated corollary
concerns the Jones representation, not detection by the scalar Jones
polynomial of a closure. This pass inspected the claim and its parity
strategy, but did not independently verify the entire 28-page proof.
