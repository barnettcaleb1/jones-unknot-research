Uniform band twists: a surgery and linear-algebra obstruction
31 October 2026, America/Chicago

This is an obstruction to a class of counterexample constructions. It does
not resolve whether the ordinary Jones polynomial detects the unknot. No
novelty or external expert-verification claim is made.

GENERAL STATEMENT
Start with an actual unknot U and r disjoint marked two-strand twist balls.
The strands at each site are antiparallel. Let L be the oriented link obtained
by the cup-cap replacements (the inverse bands) at all marked sites. Let K_k
insert k full twists at every site, with the same local handedness in
orientation-preserving charts. Thus K_0=U, and all K_k remain knots.

The common-handed convention is essential. In the local normalized
Temperley-Lieb convention used here, all sites use the same eigenvalue
z=A^8=t^-2; reversing every local convention is harmless, but reversing just
one site changes the hypothesis.

There is a symmetric integral r-by-r surgery matrix B such that

 H1(Sigma_2(L);Z) = coker B,
 |V(K_k;t=-1)| = |det(I + epsilon*2k B)|,  epsilon in {+1,-1}.       (1)

If V(K_k)=1 for infinitely many integers k, then B=0. In particular
H1(Sigma_2(L);Z) must be free abelian of rank r. The same necessary condition
already follows from determinant one at infinitely many k.

PROOF OF THE SURGERY FORMULA
The double branched cover of the actual unknot is S3. The inverse-band arcs
lift to a framed r-component link in S3. On its ith boundary torus, let mu_i
be the original meridian filling and lambda_i the band-smoothing filling.
They have distance one, so lambda_i is an integral framed longitude. Write
lambda_i=preferred_longitude_i + B_ii*mu_i; the off-diagonal B_ij are the
pairwise linking numbers of the lifted components. Therefore B is symmetric,
and filling all lambda_i presents the homology of Sigma_2(L) by B.

A local half twist lifts to a Dehn twist; a full twist lifts to its square.
The kth full twist changes the filling to mu_i+epsilon*2k*lambda_i. The
same-handed local charts give one common epsilon. In meridian generators
these simultaneous filling relations form I+epsilon*2kB, proving (1).
The usual identity |V_K(-1)|=|H1(Sigma_2(K))| then gives the determinant.

PROOF OF THE OBSTRUCTION
Put P(s)=det(I+epsilon*2sB). If |P(k)|=1 for infinitely many integers, the
polynomial P(s)^2-1 has infinitely many roots, hence vanishes identically.
Since P(0)=1, P is identically1.

Its linear coefficient gives tr B=0. Its quadratic coefficient gives

             (tr B)^2-tr(B^2)=0.

For a real symmetric matrix,

             tr(B^2)=sum_i B_ii^2 + 2 sum_(i<j) B_ij^2.

Every summand is nonnegative, so B=0. The r=1 case follows already from
the linear coefficient. Equivalently, the real eigenvalues of B must all
vanish. This argument requires neither positivity nor definiteness of B.

If B is nonzero, P has degree rank(B). Consequently there are at most
2*rank(B) real parameter values, hence at most that many integer values,
at which the determinant can be1. This is an upper bound, not an existence
statement about Jones-one knots at those parameters.

APPLICATION TO EVERY QUALIFYING TWO-BAND PRESENTATION OF EKT LLL(1)
For the concrete three-component link LLL(1), exact diagram calculations give

                    H1(Sigma_2(LLL(1))) = Z + Z/144.

Thus every disjoint oriented pair of inverse bands based at an actual unknot
and producing this link has a rank-one integral symmetric matrix B. It can
be written B=d*v*v^T with v a primitive integer vector and |d|=144, because
the nonzero Smith invariant is the gcd of the entries. Consequently

 |tr B| = 144*(v_1^2+v_2^2) >=144,
 det(I+epsilon*2kB) = 1+epsilon*2k*tr B.

For every nonzero integer k this has absolute value at least288*|k|-1,
and therefore at least287. In particular EVERY nonzero uniform coherent
twist in EVERY such marked two-band presentation has nonunit Jones polynomial.
This conclusion is independent of the length, placement, or complexity of
the qualifying embedded bands. It improves the prior result for one band pair.

ESSENTIAL LIMITS
Opposite twist signs are different. For example,
 B=2[[1,1],[1,1]], D=diag(1,-1)
has B nonzero but DB nonzero nilpotent, so det(I+2kDB)=1 for every k.
This is a matrix example, not a Jones-one knot construction. It explains why
the symmetric-matrix proof must not be applied to mixed signs.

For two bands with double smoothing Jones delta^2, the separate exact skein
formula and signature argument also exclude a whole linear Jones-one family
twisting both bands at unequal nonzero rates when b1(Sigma_2L)=1. They do
not exclude isolated mixed-sign parameter choices, coordinate families,
different starting links, or the remaining general Jones unknot problem.
Finally, B=0 is only a necessary condition: it does not establish Jones1
or topological triviality.
