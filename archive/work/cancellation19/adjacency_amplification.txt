CONDITIONAL AMPLIFICATION TO ARBITRARILY HIGH UNKNOT ADJACENCY
31 October2026. Internally audited research deduction; no priority claim.

THEOREM

Suppose a nontrivial knot with unknotting number one has Jones polynomial
identically1. Then, for every integer n>=2, there exists a nontrivial knot
K(n) with Jones polynomial identically1 that is n-adjacent to the unknot.

Here n-adjacent means a single diagram contains n specified crossings
such that changing ANY NONEMPTY subset of those crossings produces the
unknot. This is an exact diagrammatic property. It is stronger than
having n different unknotting crossings, and does not mean unknotting
number n: the resulting nontrivial knots still have unknotting number1.

The theorem is conditional. It does not establish existence of its input
or outputs, and it does not reduce an arbitrary Jones-one knot to the
unknotting-number-one case.

INPUT FROM THE EARLIER REDUCTION

The hypothesis supplies an actual nontrivial strongly invertible knot J
whose preferred exterior quotient T_J is skein-flat:

             [T_J]=u[Q0],

where Q0 is the preferred rational quotient of the unknot exterior and
u is a signed framing monomial. The meridian branch closure is actual U.
The fact that u is a Laurent unit, rather than an arbitrary rational
function, follows by applying that meridian closure: f_inf(Q0)=1 and the
bracket of an unknot diagram is a framing monomial.

This input is the established equivalence between a Jones-one u=1 knot
and a nontrivial preferred flat strongly inverted core. The proof below
does not assume any generic ordinary-Jones-to-flat-core reduction.

1. CHOOSE A FIXED ACTUAL (n+1)-COMPONENT BRUNNIAN SEED

Stoimenow's K_(n+1), equation(10) and Lemma6, is a nontrivial knot that is
(n+1)-adjacent to U. Change every specified crossing to U and lift its
(n+1) reverse crossing arcs in Sigma2(U)=S3. As proved in Lidman--Moore's
Theorem1.4 proof, the lifted link

           B = C union D1 union ... union Dn

is componentwise strongly inverted and Brunnian, with prescribed slopes
beta_i=epsilon_i/2, epsilon_i in {+1,-1}, corresponding to the crossing
changes downstairs. Every proper sublink is an ACTUAL unlink. The entire
link is nontrivial: otherwise all prescribed surgeries would give S3,
forcing the original nontrivial knot K_(n+1) to be U by Smith's theorem.
A split Brunnian link is an unlink, so E(B) is irreducible.

The surgery/crossing balls associated to all components are disjoint.
All are retained with their original markings in what follows. The
component C is chosen as the infection site, not surgered to beta_C.

2. THE PRESCRIBED PARTIAL SURGERY MAKES AN ESSENTIAL PAIR

Perform the original prescribed surgeries on D1,...,D_(n-1), retaining
C and Dn. The ambient manifold stays S3: the surgery sublink is a proper
sublink of B and hence an unlink. Call the remaining pair C union P.
Each component is an unknot. For either retained component, its union
with the surgered components was still a proper sublink of B, hence an
unlink before the surgeries. Its image therefore remains an unknot.
The pair has linking number zero, since all initial pairwise linking
numbers were zero and the surgery corrections vanish.

The pair C union P is NONSPLIT. If it were the two-component unlink,
completing the two original prescribed +/-1/2 surgeries on C and P would
give S3. But that full surgery is Sigma2(K_(n+1)), which is not S3 because
the original seed knot is nontrivial and Smith's theorem detects the
unknot from its S3 double branched cover. This contradiction proves that
P is an essential winding-zero pattern in E(C).

This proof uses the chosen seed's prescribed slopes, not arbitrary twist
signs. It is shorter than the optional general reducing-filling argument
recorded at the end. No CGLS theorem is needed for the main construction.

3. INFECT C BY THE FLAT NONTRIVIAL CORE J

Before doing those surgeries, replace N(C) with E(J) by the preferred
untwisted satellite gluing

             mu_J -> lambda_C, lambda_J -> mu_C.

The new ambient manifold is S3 because C was an unknot. The strong
inversions glue equivariantly; the surviving components retain their two
fixed points. Write the resulting n-component link as

             L_J = D1(J) union ... union Dn(J).

Every proper sublink of L_J is an unlink. Indeed, take such a sublink S;
S union C was a proper sublink of B and hence an unlink. One ball in
E(C) contains the whole S unlink and is disjoint from C. Infection does
not change this ball or its unlink. Thus L_J is Brunnian. Its pairwise linking numbers remain zero under
the preferred infection, including when n=2 (where being Brunnian alone
would not imply zero linking number).

L_J is nontrivial. Perform the prescribed surgeries on D1(J),...,D_(n-1)(J).
They commute with the C infection, since their surgery tori are disjoint.
Before infection they turn B into the essential pair C union P from
Section2. All linking numbers involved are zero, so the preferred
longitude corrections vanish; equivalently each intermediate surgery
component is local in E(C), and its1/2 surgery fixes the outer boundary
marking. The final remaining knot is exactly the untwisted satellite
P(J). It is nontrivial because its companion torus is incompressible on
both the essential-pattern side and the exterior of nontrivial J.
If L_J were the unlink, those surgeries would leave its last component
an unknot, a contradiction.

4. FULL SURGERY GIVES A NONTRIVIAL BRANCH KNOT

Carry out all n prescribed beta_i=epsilon_i/2 surgeries on L_J, and call
the resulting closed3-manifold Y. Every proper subset of these surgeries
gives S3 because the corresponding sublink is an unlink. However Y is
NOT S3. By Section3, doing the first n-1 prescribed surgeries leaves
the nontrivial knot P(J) in S3. The last surgery is still the prescribed
+/-1/2 slope: pairwise linking zero preserves its preferred longitude.
The Gordon--Luecke no-nonmeridional-S3-surgery consequence of the knot
complement theorem says that this surgery cannot produce S3.

There is a separate check using Lidman--Moore Proposition3.1. If Y were
S3, every nonempty sublink surgery of L_J would give S3. That proposition
would make the pairwise-zero-linking L_J an unlink, contradicting
Section3. Either argument proves the required nontriviality.

The simultaneous strong inversion extends across these fillings. Its
quotient is obtained from S3 by the n inherited local crossing changes,
so Y=Sigma2(K(n)) for the resulting branch set K(n). Before all changes,
the branch set was U: the initial infected ambient manifold is S3, and
its orientation-preserving involution has nonempty fixed set; Smith's
theorem identifies the branch set as an unknot.

K(n) has ONE component. Most directly, the inherited local replacements
are actual crossing changes and preserve the component count. An
independent homological check is available: pairwise linking numbers in
L_J vanish and every rational surgery numerator is +/-1, so Y is an
integer homology sphere. For a nonempty c-component branch link,
 dim_F2 H1(Sigma2(link);F2)=c-1;
hence c=1. There is no hidden multi-component branch-link exception.

Since Y is not S3, K(n) is not an unknot. In the resulting diagram, undo
any nonempty subset of the n specified crossing changes. A proper subset
of the surgeries remains, whose cover is S3; the branch knot is therefore
U by Smith. This proves n-adjacency in the stated simultaneous sense.

5. EXACT JONES NORMALIZATION

The exterior of the original B modulo its simultaneous inversion is an
actual multi-input tangle shell. Insert T_J at the C input and the
specified rational fillings at the other n inputs. Skein multilinearity
and [T_J]=u[Q0] imply that the final bracket is u times the bracket
obtained by inserting Q0 at C instead.

That reference closure performs the n prescribed surgeries on the
original B while leaving C meridionally filled. It is a PROPER subset
of the (n+1) prescribed original crossing changes, so its branch knot is
an actual unknot. Its bracket is consequently a framing monomial.
Therefore V_K(n) is a signed monomial.

For every knot, V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0. The first identity fixes the sign of
a signed monomial to+, and the second fixes its exponent to0. Thus

                       V_K(n)(t)=1

as a full Laurent-polynomial identity. This avoids assuming that all
intermediate diagrams have equal writhe, and excludes a residual framing
or orientation factor. The local surgery markings were inherited from
the seed, while the companion insertion used preferred meridian and
longitude coordinates throughout.

CONSEQUENCE

To establish Jones unknot detection for all unknotting-number-one knots,
it would suffice to establish it in the much more constrained class of
n-adjacent-to-U knots for any ONE chosen n>=2. Conversely, a hypothetical
u=1 counterexample cannot be ruled out simply by imposing arbitrarily
high finite adjacency. Howards--Luecke's Theorem1.4 supplies that bound: a nontrivial knot of
genus g fails to be strongly m-trivial for m>=3g-1. Their Definition1.3
identifies n-adjacent with strongly(n-1)-trivial, so

                    n <= 3g-1,
                    g(K(n)) >= ceil((n+1)/3).

Thus the hypothetical Jones-one outputs have UNBOUNDED GENUS and include
infinitely many distinct knots. We do not assert K(n) and K(n+1) are
always different. This remains conditional on a u=1 Jones counterexample
and still does not settle generic Jones unknot detection.


OPTIONAL GENERAL REDUCING-FILLING LEMMA

If B_r is a nontrivial Brunnian link with r>=3 components, performing
+/-1/2 surgery on ANY one component yields a nontrivial Brunnian
(r-1)-component link in S3.

Proof. The surgery component is an unknot, so the ambient manifold stays
S3. Each proper subset S of the remaining r-1 components, together with
the surgery component, was a proper sublink of B_r and hence an unlink.
The image of S after surgery is consequently an unlink. Thus the new
(r-1)-link is Brunnian in the inclusive sense that it could a priori be
an unlink.

Suppose it were an unlink. E(B_r) is irreducible; meridional filling and
+/-1/2 filling on that torus would both give E(U_(r-1)). Since r-1>=2,
both manifolds are reducible and neither has a nontrivial lens-space
summand. Their slopes have distance2. This contradicts Culler--Gordon--
Luecke--Shalen, Corollary2.4.7, precisely in the no-lens-summand form used
in Lidman--Moore's Proposition3.2 proof. Hence the new link is nontrivial.
The argument needs no hyperbolicity or asymptotic surgery estimate. QED.

Apply this lemma successively to D1,...,D_(n-1), keeping C and Dn.
It produces an essential two-component link C union P, where both
components are unknots and lk(C,P)=0. The individual prescribed signs
may vary: only distance2 is used at each step.


The preceding optional argument gives the same essentiality conclusion
for arbitrary choices of the half-integral signs, rather than just the
original prescribed seed slopes. It is not needed for amplification.
