PASS 19: A LOCALITY OBSTRUCTION TO EQUIVARIANT SATELLITE REPAIRS
31 October 2026. Bounded research deduction, internally audited.
No claim of literature priority; no general Jones-detection proof.

MAIN RESULT

An essential pattern in a strongly inverted solid torus cannot be turned
into a local unknot by a finite sequence of invariant winding-zero
crossing-disk twists supported entirely in that solid torus.

Here 'local' means contained in a 3-ball in the interior of the solid
torus. Each disk is genuinely invariant, meets the knot transversely in
exactly two points, and its boundary circle is strongly inverted. These
hypotheses cannot be replaced by invariance of the boundary circle alone.
The allowable operation is Boyle--Chen type C: its two punctures are the
two fixed points on the knot.

This is a geometric obstruction independent of the Jones polynomial. It
rules out a proposed universal repair of the anchored-flat-tangle to
zero-shell construction: changing the Whitehead pattern to any other
fixed essential pattern with a correct-type LOCAL unknotting disk will
not work. A construction using a disk that leaves the companion solid
torus is outside the conclusion.

1. WHY THE CORRECT CROSSING DISK IS TYPE C

Let tau preserve the orientation of S^3 and strongly invert a knot P and
a circle C=boundary D. Suppose D is invariant and intersects P in exactly
two transverse points. The restriction of tau to D is a reflection.
It reverses both the orientation of D and the orientation of P, so it
preserves the oriented intersection sign of each puncture. If the two
punctures are exchanged, they have the same sign, giving lk(C,P)=+/-2.
Consequently lk(C,P)=0 forces the two punctures to be separately fixed.
They are exactly the two fixed points of P. In Boyle--Chen's terminology,
this is the type C operation, not type B.

Primary reference:
Keegan Boyle and Wenzhao Chen, Equivariant unknotting numbers of strongly
invertible knots, arXiv:2412.09797v2, Definition2.1 and Section6,
https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09797v2 .
The published version is J. London Math. Soc.113(2026), e70506,
https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70506 .
Lemma6.5 explicitly describes such a disk as an interval bundle around
an axis interval, and explains why its two punctures are fixed.

2. INVARIANT CONTAINING BALL

Let A=Fix(tau), let C_0 be a strongly inverted unknot, and let
V=S^3\int N(C_0), with invariant tubular neighborhood. Thus V is a
strongly inverted solid torus and A intersect V consists of two properly
embedded intervals. Let P subset int V be a strongly inverted LOCAL knot.
Then there is a tau-invariant ball B subset int V containing P.

Proof. The two-component link P union C_0 is split. Its exterior is
reducible. The equivariant sphere theorem supplies an essential sphere S
such that S=tau(S), or such that S and tau(S) are disjoint. Every essential
sphere in a two-component link exterior separates the two components:
otherwise one side is a ball disjoint from the link and the sphere would
be inessential.

Choose the ball B_S bounded by S that contains P and excludes C_0. Its
image tau(B_S) has the same property because both components are preserved
individually. If the two boundary spheres are distinct and disjoint,
these two balls must be strictly nested: they share P, and their union
cannot be all S^3 because both exclude C_0. Applying tau to a strict
inclusion reverses that inclusion, a contradiction. Hence S is invariant.
The chosen ball side is invariant as well, since it is characterized by
containing P and not C_0. S lies in the exterior of the chosen invariant
neighborhoods, so B_S is disjoint from N(C_0), as required.

The restriction of tau to boundary B is a nonidentity orientation-
preserving involution of S^2, and therefore has exactly two fixed points.
The global fixed set A is a circle, so A intersect B is a single interval.
The action cannot be the identity on all boundary B, since a nontrivial
finite-order smooth action is linearizable and cannot fix a surface
pointwise while preserving ambient orientation.

Primary reference for the exact equivariant sphere formulation:
Marc Lackenby, Links with splitting number one, proof of Proposition4.1,
printed p10, states that a reducible manifold with an involution contains
one or two disjoint essential spheres invariant as a set, and cites
Meeks--Simon--Yau, Theorem3 and its proof:
https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/lackenby/spl29121-geom-ded.pdf .
Original input: W.H. Meeks III, L. Simon, S.-T. Yau, Embedded Minimal
Surfaces, Exotic Spheres, and Manifolds with Positive Ricci Curvature,
Annals of Mathematics116(1982),621--659.

3. LOCALITY-PRESERVATION LEMMA

Continue the preceding setup. Suppose D subset int V is an invariant
crossing disk with two fixed punctures p,q in P. Then any integral
full-twist surgery on boundary D produces another LOCAL knot in V.

Proof. The reflection fixed set of D is an interval in A intersect V.
Both p and q lie in this interval. Let beta be the subinterval between
them. Because each component of A intersect V is an interval, there is
only one interval in A intersect V joining p and q. Since the invariant
containing ball B has A intersect B a single interval containing p,q,
beta lies in B.

Choose a slightly longer closed subinterval of Fix(tau|D), still inside
B, with p and q in its interior. A sufficiently narrow invariant regular
neighborhood D' of this interval in D is a disk contained in B. It
contains both punctures and no other intersections with P. The region
between boundary D and boundary D' is an annulus disjoint from P.
Sliding boundary D across this annulus is an isotopy in V\P. It carries
the disk framing to the disk framing of D'. Equivalently, shrink the
reflection-invariant disk to D' relative to small neighborhoods of its
two punctures and use isotopy extension relative to P.

We may consequently perform the same framed surgery on boundary D'
inside B. Since this is 1/n surgery on an unknot in B, the surgered ball
is again a ball with the same boundary marking. P is contained in this
ball before and after the twist. Thus the resulting knot remains local.
For n=+/-1 this is the usual crossing-change surgery. QED.

The proof requires no assertion that the original disk already lies in
B. Its boundary is moved into B through an annulus disjoint from P.
Any twisting of the disk around its fixed interval is retained as disk
framing during this isotopy; it cannot evade locality.

4. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PROPOSED SATELLITE REPAIR

Suppose an actual essential winding-zero pattern P in V has P(U)=U and
an inherited componentwise strong inversion compatible with a companion.
One seeks an invariant zero-linking crossing disk, entirely inside V,
which unknots P to a LOCAL unknot in V. Reverse that operation. The
locality-preservation lemma says that the result is still local, which
contradicts essentiality. The same reasoning applies to any finite
sequence of such type-C moves supported in V.

Thus the failure of the Whitehead clasp is structural for this entire
class of repairs, not just an unfortunate axis placement. A standard
Whitehead clasp can unknot the pattern inside V precisely because its
crossing circle is free under the inherited inversion; it does not meet
the lemma's componentwise-strong/type-C hypothesis.

This does NOT exclude all essential zero-factor shells. In a general
shell P union C_0, the axis C_0 need not be the boundary of an invariant
two-puncture disk in the pattern solid torus. Nor does it exclude a
companion-dependent equivariant unknotting operation whose disk enters
the companion exterior. Those operations are not fixed local satellite
repairs, and need separate actual geometry.

5. ADDITIONAL GLOBAL RESTRICTIONS, NOT USED IN THE PROOF

Boyle--Chen Theorem1.8 classifies strongly invertible knots unknottable
by a finite sequence of type-C moves: they are (1,2)-knots whose symmetry
axis is the core of a handlebody in that decomposition. Here (1,2) means
genus-one bridge position with TWO boundary-parallel arcs in each solid
torus; it does NOT mean an ordinary two-bridge knot or Seifert genus one.
Their Corollary6.3 implies tunnel number at most2. Their Theorem1.3
excludes any connected sum with three nontrivial summands.

The braid track independently proves that preferred anchored quotient
flatness is preserved by equivariant connected sum, and in fact a
connected sum is flat if and only if each factor is flat. Thus a
hypothetical flat core could be copied into arbitrarily many nontrivial
summands, where type-C unknotting is impossible. This already blocks a
claim that every flat core itself admits the required crossing circle.
It does not alone block applying a different satellite to that core.

No unverified lower bound comparing tunnel numbers of a satellite and
its companion is used here. The standard bridge-number inequality does
not suffice because genus-one bridge number2 does not bound ordinary
bridge number.
