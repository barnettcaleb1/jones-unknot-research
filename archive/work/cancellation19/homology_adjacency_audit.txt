INDEPENDENT AUDIT: CONDITIONAL AMPLIFICATION OF A JONES-ONE u=1 KNOT
31 October 2026. Research deduction; no novelty or unconditional existence claim.

VERDICT

The construction in adjacency_amplification.txt is sound, conditional on
the previously established equivalence between a nontrivial Jones-one
unknotting-number-one knot and a nontrivial strongly inverted core J with
preferred flat exterior quotient [T_J]=u[Q0], u a framing monomial.

There is a shorter proof of nontriviality than the reducing-filling proof
in that file. It needs no repeated CGLS estimate and no application of
Lidman--Moore Proposition 3.1 at the end. Both replacements are recorded
below. The original proofs were also checked and no contradiction found.

PRECISE CONDITIONAL CONCLUSION

If a nontrivial knot K has u(K)=1 and V_K(t)=1, then for every n>=2 there
is a nontrivial knot K_n with V_K_n(t)=1 and n specified ordinary crossings
such that changing any nonempty subset gives U. Furthermore

                       n <= 3 g(K_n)-1.

Thus the output genera are unbounded, and there are infinitely many
distinct output knot types. This is not a proof of Jones unknot detection
and not a reduction of an arbitrary hypothetical counterexample to u=1.

1. SEED AND MARKINGS

Choose a nontrivial (n+1)-adjacent knot H. Stoimenow's 2005 construction,
Eq.(10), and Lemma 6 give such an H for every n+1>=3. Change all specified
crossings to get U; lift the n+1 reverse crossing arcs to Sigma_2(U)=S3.
The result B=C union D_1 union ... union D_n is componentwise strongly
inverted. Its preferred crossing-change slopes are epsilon_i/2.

The proof of Lidman--Moore Theorem 1.4 supplies the actual Brunnian
property: every proper sublink is an unlink. In particular this is not
just an algebraically split link. The full link is not the unlink, since
otherwise the full prescribed surgeries give S3 whereas they give
Sigma_2(H), and Smith's theorem would force H=U.

Keep the original disjoint crossing balls throughout. Their lifts are
the chosen component neighborhoods. Infection at C replaces only the
C ball in the quotient; the other n balls, their two strand endpoints,
and their two actual crossing pictures are untouched. Consequently the
later operations are LITERAL ordinary crossing changes. The proof does
not infer this merely from a distance-two assertion about arbitrary
rational tangles.

2. SHORT ESSENTIALITY PROOF

Perform the prescribed surgeries on D_1,...,D_(n-1), leaving C and
P=D_n. The ambient manifold is S3: the operated sublink is an unlink.
Each of C and P is still an unknot, because that component together with
the operated components forms a proper unlink in B. Their linking number
is zero. The preferred slopes remain epsilon/2: all original pairwise
linkings vanish, so neither longitude receives a surgery correction.

Suppose C union P were split. It would then be the two-component unlink,
so the remaining two prescribed epsilon/2 surgeries would give S3. That
would be the FULL prescribed surgery on B, contradicting H nontrivial
and Sigma_2(H)!=S3. Therefore C union P is nonsplit, and P is an essential
pattern in E(C), with winding zero and P(U)=U.

This proves essentiality for the particular inherited surgery signs
needed here. Unlike the longer proof, it does not assert an arbitrary
signs/arbitrary Brunnian preservation theorem, and does not need one.

3. INFECTION AND FULL SURGERY

Insert E(J) at C, with mu_J->lambda_C and lambda_J->mu_C. Because C is
an unknot, the ambient manifold is S3. The boundary strong inversions
are both -I and can be glued equivariantly. The remaining n components
retain fixed points, so the ambient involution has nonempty fixed set.
The standard involution/Smith theorem identifies its quotient with S3
and its branch set with U.

For any proper subset S of the remaining n components, S union C was
an unlink. The whole S unlink is contained in a ball disjoint from C,
so it remains an actual unlink after infection. This works collectively,
not merely component by component. Pairwise linking remains zero; for
n=2 this also follows directly from the zero-linking infection formula.

Now perform the n-1 surgeries from section 2 after infection. Surgery
and infection commute, since their tori are disjoint. The remaining knot
is exactly P(J), with its preferred marking. The companion torus is
incompressible on both sides: P is essential and J is nontrivial.
Therefore P(J) is nontrivial.

The last prescribed surgery has slope epsilon_n/2, so is nonmeridional.
The knot-complement theorem implies that nonmeridional surgery on a
nontrivial knot in S3 cannot give S3. Thus the full surgered manifold Y
is not S3. This is the desired nontriviality certificate for the final
branch knot; no self-adjacency classification is needed at this step.

4. BRANCH CONNECTIVITY AND ADJACENCY

The quotient of Y is obtained by the n retained ordinary crossing
changes in disjoint balls. The initial branch set was U, and ordinary
crossing changes preserve component count, so the full branch set is a
knot K_n. Independently, its surgery matrix has zero off-diagonal linking
entries and numerator +/-1 on every component, so Y is an integer
homology sphere; its mod-2 first homology also rules out a multi-component
branch link.

Undo any nonempty collection of those n changes. Only a proper sublink
of the infected Brunnian link is then surgered; that sublink is an actual
unlink, so its surgeries give S3. The resulting branch knot is U by
Smith. Full Y!=S3 makes K_n nontrivial. All n changes appear in one
diagram because their balls were retained simultaneously.

5. JONES NORMALIZATION

The quotient of the original link exterior is a genuine multilinear
skein shell. Replacing T_J by u Q0 makes the final bracket u times the
bracket of the original seed with all n D crossings changed but C left
unchanged. That is a proper subset of the original n+1 changes, hence U.
Its bracket and u are framing monomials. Thus V_K_n is a signed monomial.
The knot identities V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0 force that monomial to equal 1.
No equal-writhe assumption for the intermediate diagrams is required.

6. GENUS AND DISTINCTNESS

Howards--Luecke Definition 1.3 calls an n-adjacent knot strongly(n-1)-
trivial. Their Theorem 1.4 excludes a nontrivial genus-g knot from being
strongly m-trivial when m>=3g-1. Substituting m=n-1 gives n<3g, or
n<=3g-1. Hence g(K_n)>=floor(n/3)+1. The collection contains infinitely
many distinct knots; this conclusion is stronger than mere existence
of outputs for every n, and does not require guessing whether two
particular constructed outputs coincide.

SCOPE WARNING

This is a conditional propagation theorem within the u=1 Jones-one
sector. No actual nontrivial flat J or actual Jones-one counterexample
has been supplied. The generic Jones-unknot problem remains unresolved
by this argument.
