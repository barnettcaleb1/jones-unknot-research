1. THE EXPLICIT SHELL

For odd q put m=(q-1)/2, let Q_a denote the integer rational tangle, and
let r be the quarter-turn with rational-slope action z -> -1/z. Consider

 C_q(T) = r(Q_-1 + Q_(1/2) + r(Q_-2 + r(Q_m+T))) + Q_(-2q).

This is an actual annular tangle: replace T by an unfilled four-ended
inner ball. Its only nonintegral fixed rational piece is Q_(1/2).
The other operations are integer twists and quarter-turns in collars;
they change boundary markings and sections, not the number/order of
exceptional Seifert fibers.

The standard double-cover construction of a Montesinos tangle therefore
identifies its cover W_q as a Seifert manifold over an annulus with one
exceptional fiber of order 2. This is the structural certificate missing
from an argument based solely on fitting several rational input values.
Such a manifold is a cable space; its specific solid-torus embedding and
peripheral identification still need to be fixed as follows.

2. WHAT THE RATIONAL PROBES REALLY CERTIFY

With T=Q_n, the inner expression is a rational tangle of slope

             (m+n)/(2(m+n)+1).

Thus the Montesinos part has rational pieces

       -1, 1/2, (m+n)/(2(m+n)+1),

and its cover has base disk with exceptional orders 2 and |q+2n|.
This is the standard torus-knot-exterior description, with the outer
boundary marked by the final rotation and Q_(-2q) twist. At n=0 it is
the quotient of E(T(2,q)), up to the simultaneous mirror convention.

These probes are used to identify slopes inside an already certified
cable space; they are not being used to assert that arbitrary tangles
are determined topologically by their bracket values.

At n=0 the output meridian is the ordinary torus-knot meridian. Its
distance from the Seifert-fiber slope is one. Filling that outer boundary
therefore changes the base annulus to a disk and adds no new exceptional
fiber. The result is a solid torus, with the original order-2 exceptional
core. The core of the filling just added is a regular Seifert fiber in
this solid torus, so it winds twice around its core. Its complement W_q
is exactly the exterior of a (2,q)-type pattern in a solid torus.

The q label and relative input twist are fixed by the displayed slope
formula: inserting Q_n changes the torus parameter from q to q+2n. A
meridional twist of the input would change that reference q, and has
therefore not been left free.

3. MERIDIANS AND PREFERRED LONGITUDES

After the outer meridional filling, the quotient of the resulting solid
torus is a rational cap on the input boundary (the standard solid-torus
branched-cover criterion). The direct operator computation gives

              f_infinity M_q = u_q f_infinity,

where u_q is a framing unit. Because this cap is already known to be
rational, its bracket functional identifies its slope as the input
meridian. This uses injectivity for rational tangles only; it does not
use the open claim that an arbitrary tangle with rational bracket is
rational. Hence an arbitrary actual input meridian-unknot quotient is
sent to another actual meridian-unknot quotient.

The final Q_(-2q) marking makes the n=0 output zero closure have determinant
zero, so it identifies the output longitude with the preferred longitude
of T(2,q). For a genuine satellite gluing, input preferred longitude is
null-homologous. The same outer marked longitude is therefore preferred
for the arbitrary companion satellite as well. It would be WRONG to call
it preferred for every Q_n probe: changing the companion framing shifts
the output longitude by the winding-square framing correction. The code's
fixed outer shift -2q, rather than -2(q+2n), correctly keeps the shell's
output marking fixed while probing its matrix.
