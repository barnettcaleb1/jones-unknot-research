SATELLITE AFFINE DEFECT AUDIT (b)
2026-11-02, defect_wh_gate. Status: TRUSTED within the specified compatible
strong-inversion geometry; not a reduction of every satellite inversion.

1. Exact normalization

Use R=Z[A,A^-1], meridian f=(1,delta), reference Q0, and v=delta Q0-Qinf.
Thus normalized [T]=Q0+w(T)v. A compatible two-boundary quotient shell
gives an R-linear map M. Filling the output pattern meridian deletes P
and leaves the axis exterior E(A). Its compressible slope lambda_A is
identified by satellite gluing with the input companion meridian mu_J.
The quotient cap is rational and has this exact slope, so
  f M=u f
as functionals on the whole rank-two module, for one framing unit u.
No span of realizable knot quotients is needed.

Set Mhat=M/u. In the ordered basis (Q0,v),
  Mhat = [[1,0],[w0,d]],
where w0=w(P(U)) in the physical preferred output marking and
  d=det(M)/u^2.
The raw eigenvalue Mv=m_raw v is det(M)/u, so d=m_raw/u.
Consequently the EXACT normalized formula is
  w(P(J))=w(P(U))+d*w(J).

In the original basis (Q0,Qinf), this matrix is
  [[1+delta*w0, delta*(1-d)+delta^2*w0],
   [-w0,       d-delta*w0]].
Its determinant is d and its meridional row is (1,delta), directly
verifying the formula. Once normalization and generator are fixed, the
affine identity is exact. Writing two independent "up to units" terms
is inadequate for a cancellation or divisibility computation.

2. Winding zero and the proposed F divisibility

The classical satellite formula a2(P(J))=a2(P(U))+p^2*a2(J), where p is
the algebraic winding, becomes companion-independence for p=0. That
first-order statement alone does NOT logically force divisibility by
F=(1-A^4)(1+A^8)(1+A^12). It controls determinant-root derivative data,
not all roots of F. This inference must be replaced.

Here is a conditional repair depending explicitly on part (a): if every
actual preferred strongly invertible quotient has F|w, then winding zero
does imply F|d. Insert rational Q0 and Q1 (or Q-1) into the shell.
They are independent, and the latter has defect a Laurent unit. The
corresponding filling of the unknotted axis is 1/n surgery, so the
output is an actual strongly invertible knot P_n in S3. For winding zero,
the fixed output longitude remains preferred. Therefore
  F | w(P_1)-w(P(U))=d*w(Q1),
and the input defect is a unit, yielding F|d. This is a full-polynomial
root/evaluation argument, not a consequence of a2 alone.

Even without part (a), the archived determinant argument proves
(A^4+1)|d in winding zero: the fixed longitude closures of P_0 and P_1
both have determinant zero, while Q1 has unit defect at the determinant
root. The archives already contain this weaker unconditional factor.

For nonzero winding p, 1/n axis filling changes the preferred output
longitude by a meridian shear of magnitude n*p^2 (sign follows surgery
conventions). A fixed shell's output coordinates therefore need not be
the preferred coordinates of P_n. Using w(P_n) in the same affine
formula without applying this boundary transformation is wrong. This
is particularly relevant for cable probes.

3. Equivariant JSJ scope

The stated hypothesis is sufficient: an invariant separating companion
torus with hyperelliptic restriction has four branch points downstairs,
and the region between it and the exterior boundary becomes a four-ended
two-boundary shell. Compatible filling on the companion side gives its
strong inversion and preferred quotient; the physical satellite framing
fixes the reference w(P(U)).

But this does NOT yet justify a general reduction from every strongly
invertible satellite to a smaller strongly invertible companion through
one such matrix. One needs an equivariant decomposition statement for
the GIVEN inversion and a classification of all relevant pieces. An
involution may permute JSJ tori; an invariant torus may have a free
restriction instead of the four-fixed-point hyperelliptic action.
Those cases are not described by the assumed two-boundary sphere shell.
This audit does not assert that every such formal possibility occurs,
nor silently exclude it without a theorem. Multi-input pieces and
connected-sum annuli also need their specified equivariant gluing.

The Whitehead result is correspondingly for inherited inversions,
not every axis on the same underlying satellite knot.

4. Scope of any claimed hyperbolic reduction

Even with the affine formula, general patterns have an offset w(P(U)).
Their exact cancellation condition w(J)=-w(P(U))/d remains an independent
open problem. Checking Whitehead and finitely many cable patterns does
not settle all pattern pieces. The archived zero-shell equivalence is
also explicitly equivalent to the anchored unknotting-one problem; it
cannot be treated as a routine subsidiary nonvanishing theorem.

Thus no unconditional reduction of U5 to hyperbolic companions follows
from the present shell identity alone. Needed additional hypotheses are
an equivariant decomposition coverage theorem and no-cancellation for
all remaining pattern types, not only a finite cable table.
