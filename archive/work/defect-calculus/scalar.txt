1. INTEGRAL SCALAR AND NORMALIZATION

Use the archive convention: R=Z[A,A^-1], delta=-A^2-A^-2, relative basis
(Q0,Qinfinity), meridian closure f_infinity=(1,delta), longitude closure
f_0=(delta,1). The actual meridian unknot bracket is a framing unit, so
normalizing it to 1 remains in R^2. Then

  ker(f_infinity)=R*(delta Q0-Qinfinity).

Indeed a+delta*b=0 iff (a,b)=(-delta*b,b). Therefore a unique w in R gives

  [T]^ = Q0 + w*(delta Q0-Qinfinity),
  f_0([T]^)=delta+(delta^2-1)w,
  w=(f_0([T]^)-delta)/(delta^2-1).

This w is -x in the previous Whitehead archive. The displayed denominator
does not assert division is numerically permissible at its roots; polynomial
divisibility follows from the rank-one identity itself.

If Claude names the meridian closure N, his kernel generator is correct
after relabeling to these conventions. If N literally means the archive's
numerator f_0=(delta,1), its kernel generator instead is Q0-delta Qinfinity.
Do not mix those two closure names.

In preferred meridian/longitude coordinates, w=0 is exactly the normalized
rational-zero bracket condition. If the bracket is that of ANY rational
tangle, the determinant-zero preferred-longitude closure forces its solid
torus cover's disk slope to be lambda; thus its rational tangle is Q0 up to
framing, and the actual meridian normalization fixes that framing scalar.
The solid-torus-cover criterion then identifies Lemma Q with
  w_kappa=0 implies kappa is the unknot.
The defect depends on the chosen strong inversion and peripheral marking;

2. UNIVERSAL FACTOR F: A PROOF USING ONE BRANCH KNOT

For an arbitrary strongly invertible kappa with preferred quotient T,
attach the standard +1/2 cap. The branch set B is a knot and
Sigma_2(B)=S^3_(1/2)(kappa), so det(B)=1. The archived exact cap calculation,
valid for every such meridian-normalized T, is

  V_B(A^-4)-1 = C(A)*x(T),
  C(A)=A^-10*(A^4-1)*(A^8+A^4+1),    x=-w.

There is no unknown additive monomial: B and the meridional unknot have
the same endpoint pairing and can be oriented compatibly on T, so their
writhe difference comes solely from the fixed cap. The rational reference
cap closure normalizes to 1. The opposite half-cap changes C only by a unit.

Write P(t)=V_B(t)-1. The following standard facts imply

  G(t)=(t-1)^2*(t^2+t+1)*(t^2+1)*(t^3+1) divides P(t).

Detailed factors and phases:

  (i) Every knot has V(1)=1, V'(1)=0 and V(exp(2pi*i/3))=1.
      Thus (t-1)^2*(t^2+t+1) divides P.

 (ii) det(B)=1 gives V_B(-1)=+1, not merely an undetermined sign.
      Here is an elementary sign check: if V=sum c_j t^j, then
      V(-1)=1-2*sum_(j odd)c_j. V'(1)=sum j*c_j=0 implies the odd
      coefficient sum is even, hence V(-1)=1 mod 4. Since |V(-1)|=1,
      V(-1)=1. Thus t+1 divides P.

(iii) A determinant-one knot has Arf=0. For completeness, normalized
      Delta(t)=Conway(t^1/2-t^-1/2) gives Delta(-1)=1-4*a2 mod 8.
      Also Delta(-1)=1 mod 4 and |Delta(-1)|=1, so Delta(-1)=1 and
      a2 is even; Arf=a2 mod 2. The classical evaluation
      V(i)=(-1)^Arf therefore gives V(i)=V(-i)=1. Thus t^2+1 divides P.

 (iv) At eta=exp(pi*i/3), Lickorish--Millett give V_B(eta)=+/-1 because
      H_1(Sigma_2(B);Z/3)=0. The sign is fixed without an extra topology
      theorem: if V_B(eta)=epsilon in {+1,-1}, then phi_6(t)=t^2-t+1
      divides V_B(t)-epsilon in Z[t,t^-1]. Evaluate at -1; since
      phi_6(-1)=3 and V_B(-1)=1, epsilon=1 mod 3, hence epsilon=+1.
      Therefore t^2-t+1 divides P.

The factors are pairwise coprime over Q except for the deliberately
specified double root at 1. They are monic, so Gauss's lemma gives the
displayed product divisibility over Z[t,t^-1].

Substitute t=A^-4. Up to Laurent units,

  G(A^-4)=(A^4-1)^2*(A^8+A^4+1)*(A^8+1)*(A^12+1),
  C(A)=(A^4-1)*(A^8+A^4+1).

Cancellation in the integral domain R gives

  F(A)=(1-A^4)*(1+A^8)*(1+A^12) divides x(T), hence divides w(T).

This proves the requested universal factor for every chosen strong inversion,
not just for Whitehead towers or a2(kappa)=0 companions. In particular the
one-half branch knot's determinant is one even when a2(kappa) is nonzero;
do not confuse its Arf invariant with Arf(kappa).

The zero set is exactly the preimage under t=A^-4 of
  {1,+i,-i,-1,exp(pi*i/3),exp(-pi*i/3)}.
Every root is simple. The three factors of F have disjoint roots.
Universal factor divisibility is about those evaluations; arbitrarily high
orders of vanishing in the Whitehead tower are strictly stronger than the
classical single evaluations.

3. CORRECTIONS TO THE PROPOSED EXPLANATION

The assertion that 'all three agree with the unknot's values for every
closure' is false literally: a p/q closure has determinant |p|, and even
the rational reference can close to a nontrivial two-bridge knot. The
appropriate comparator is the matching rational closure, not always U.
At the listed A roots, F|w implies equality of the meridian-normalized
bracket vectors, hence equality after EVERY fixed bracket closure functional
with its rational comparator. Normalized Jones polynomials for arbitrary
different-connectivity closures still require compatible orientation/writhe
bookkeeping. No such broad normalization assertion is needed for this proof.

The root-evaluation proof also shows why the proposed claim in (b), that
a2(P(J)) being independent of J alone forces F|m_P, cannot be taken on
faith: one derivative condition does not automatically impose all the
listed evaluation roots. A separate rational-input/satellite argument may
prove F|m_P for winding-zero patterns, but that implication needs its own
proof. This report does not audit (b).
