1. Exact conventions and multiplication

Use R=Z[A,A^-1], delta=-A^2-A^-2, and horizontal TL2 algebra
R[e]/(e^2=delta e), with Q0=1, Q_infinity=e. The meridian functional is
f_infinity(1)=1, f_infinity(e)=delta, hence it is an algebra homomorphism.
The archived kernel generator is v=e-delta and v^2=-delta v.
An actual meridian-normalized quotient is T_hat=1+xv.
Claude's sign convention w=-x uses v_Claude=delta-e. Thus

 x_(1#2)=x1+x2-delta*x1*x2,
 w_(1#2)=w1+w2+delta*w1*w2,
 m_(sum with kappa)=1+delta*w_kappa,
 c0=-delta,
 H_kappa=1+delta*w_kappa=1-delta*x_kappa,
 H_(1#2)=H1*H2.

This directly computes c0; commutativity alone would only show equality
of fractions where nonzero denominators occur, not its polynomial value.
Framings and preferred mu/lambda markings are fixed before normalizing.
No independently chosen Laurent units can be inserted into these identities.

2. Why the geometric composition is connected sum

Choose directions/half-axes of the two strong inversions, then remove a
small invariant neighborhood of the terminal fixed point of the first
and initial fixed point of the second. Glue the boundary data so the
directions concatenate. This is the directed equivariant connected sum.
Downstairs its quotients are joined by the horizontal two-tangle gluing.
The covering gluing disk is a meridional annulus; gluing the two exteriors
along this annulus gives E(kappa1#kappa2). Preferred longitudes concatenate
without a meridian twist. The two meridional unknot closures become their
connected sum, again U, so f_infinity normalization is multiplicative.

3. Unit exclusion needs LESS than full F divisibility

Suppose w_(1#2)=0. Then H1*H2=1 in R, so each Hi=epsilon_i A^ki.
For every actual preferred quotient, the longitude closure has determinant
zero. At zeta=exp(pi*i/4), delta(zeta)=0, and

 f0(T_hat)=delta+(1-delta^2)x

therefore gives x(zeta)=0. Consequently

 H(zeta)=1, H'(zeta)=-delta'(zeta)x(zeta)-delta(zeta)x'(zeta)=0.

For the Laurent unit epsilon A^k, the derivative at nonzero zeta vanishes
only if k=0; its value then forces epsilon=1. Hence H=1 and x=w=0 for
both factors. Conversely, zero input defects plainly give zero output.
The same proof applies to every finite number of directed equivariant
summands. F|w is unnecessary; Phi8=A^4+1 dividing w already suffices.

This is not merely a truncated-jet claim. Exact multiplicativity first
forces each entire Laurent polynomial to be a unit; the one-jet test
then excludes all those units except 1.

5. Essential scope caveat for reduction to hyperbolic cores

This does NOT classify every strong inversion on a composite knot.
An inversion can exchange two prime summands. Miller--Powell Example
2.5(iii) explicitly gives the flipping involution on J#J^r for arbitrary
J; it need not be a directed sum of separately strongly inverted copies.
The archived double-tangle calculation assigns that block

 H_(flipping J#J^r)=H3(J),

where H3 is the reduced 3-dimensional colored Jones polynomial.
The archived global decomposition source gate (Sakuma's primary theorem)
was not reopened here. Thus generic composite elimination requires both
that decomposition theorem AND control of flipping blocks; plain U5-hyp
for strongly invertible hyperbolic knots does not automatically cover
arbitrary J with H3(J)=1. Keep this extra gap visible.
