GEOMETRIC FANOUT: A PROVED EXCLUSION AND A SMALLER CONSTRUCTION TARGET
31 October 2026. First bounded pass, approximately 20 minutes.

OUTCOME

No Jones-one nontrivial knot, useful unlink rotant, or completely certified
new rotor candidate was constructed. There is a proved geometric exclusion:
an unknot rotor whose rotation extends to an actual global periodic rotation
of that unknot has an unknotted flip. This holds for every order, independently
of Jones polynomial preservation. Parent and tangle agents independently
audited the compression and branched-cover steps below.

The construction effort narrowed the useful target from FOUR orbit surgery
circles to ONE setwise-invariant surgery circle. Shared Borromean interactions
with that circle give an explicit small clasper template. The simplest direct
inverse-cancellation anchor for this template fails a component-count test,
proved below. No more elaborate anchor has been certified.

A. GLOBAL PERIODICITY EXCLUSION

Hypotheses. Let U be an unknot in S^3. Let B be a rotor ball, with U intersect B
in n arcs forming one free orbit under the local standard order-n rotation rho,
n>=2, and with no closed components. Suppose rho extends to a standard finite
rotation of S^3 preserving U, with fixed axis A disjoint from U. Require the
actual rotor flip mu to normalize rho on B and to induce its usual half-turn
on the marked rotor boundary. The stator is therefore invariant under the
global rotation, although it need not be geometrically planar.

Conclusion. Replacing R=U intersect B by mu(R) gives an unknot.

Proof, including the markings.

1. The distinguished pair U union A is a Hopf link. A periodic symmetry of U
preserves its orientation because an orientation-reversing circle symmetry has
fixed points, whereas the rotation axis is disjoint from U. Paoluzzi's Claim2.1
proves the Hopf-link statement using an equivariant disk fibration of the
unknot exterior. The quotient pair bar(U) union bar(A) is likewise a Hopf link:
in that disk fibration the rotation fixes the disk centers and rotates each
disk, and its quotient again has the core-axis configuration.

2. The quotient of B is a ball meeting each Hopf component in exactly one arc.
The quotient sphere is a Conway sphere, with two axis marks and two U marks.
The flip mu descends because mu rho mu^-1=rho^-1. Its boundary action swaps
the two axis marks with each other and the two U marks with each other. It is
an ordinary Conway half-turn with these component colors retained.

3. Remove tubular neighborhoods of the Hopf components. The four-punctured
Conway sphere F cannot be pi_1-injective into the Hopf exterior: pi_1(F)=F_3
and pi_1(T^2 times I)=Z^2. The loop theorem provides a compressing disk, whose
interior lies on one side of F. Its boundary is essential and nonperipheral,
so it separates the four endpoint marks into two pairs. Since the disk misses
the tangle, it cannot separate the ends of either tangle arc. Cutting that
ball along the disk therefore gives two balls, each containing one complete
arc, one from each Hopf component.

4. Each such one-arc ball is an unknotted ball-arc pair. Its boundary sphere
meets a globally unknotted Hopf component twice, and the resulting connected-
sum decomposition of U has only trivial summands. Rejoining the balls along
the compression disk makes their two arcs a boundary-parallel, hence rational,
two-string tangle. Thus at least one side of the quotient Conway sphere is
rational. The other side is not assumed rational or boundary-parallel.

5. Rational tangles are invariant, relative to their marked boundary, under
the Conway horizontal and vertical half-turns and their product. If the
mutated side is the other tangle, transfer the half-turn to the rational side
by a rotation of the entire closed diagram. This shows that the mutated Hopf
pair is the original Hopf pair. The isotopy retains the axis color, because
the boundary action preserves its endpoint pair and each tangle arc has a
unique endpoint pair. No exchange of the two component roles is required.

6. Take the n-fold cyclic branched cover over the distinguished axis component.
The lift of the other component of a Hopf link is an unknot. Cutting the cover
along the lifted Conway sphere and applying the lifted boundary half-turn
recovers exactly the prescribed rotor flip. Possible choices of deck lift
differ by powers of rho and do not change the rotor. Consequently the rotant
of U is U.

Scope. This does not assume an arbitrary local cyclic symmetry extends across
an asymmetric stator. That extension is precisely the extra hypothesis. It
excludes quotient constructions whose whole closed anchor is a periodic
unknot, and equivariant surgery constructions retaining that global symmetry.
It does not exclude a local rotor with an asymmetrically glued stator.

Primary sources:
Luisa Paoluzzi, Three Cyclic Branched Covers Suffice to Determine Hyperbolic
Knots, JKTR14 (2005), 641--655, Claim2.1, p643.
  https://www.i2m.univ-amu.fr/perso/luisa.paoluzzi/jktr05.pdf
  https://doi.org/10.1142/S0218216505003981
Kauffman--Lambropoulou, On the classification of rational tangles, Lemma2.

B. CONSTRUCTION ATTEMPT: WHY SIMPLE TELESCOPING DOES NOT HELP

With four free-orbit unlink circles eta_i, a tempting group-theoretic design
gives the four rotor arcs words w_i=x_i x_(i+2)^-1 in the unlink-complement
group F_4. Close the arcs in order0,2,1,3. The total word is
  w_0 w_0^-1 w_1 w_1^-1 =1.
To upgrade this to an actual unlink certificate, make opposite arcs parallel
and fill the two resulting pairs by disjoint ribbons, then connect their
boundary disks by a standard exterior band. This gives a genuine spanning
disk disjoint from the surgery circles, provided the specified ribbons exist.

The direct planar realization is useless: its opposite-pair ribbons and
exterior band are preserved or interchanged by the flip. The same disk
cancellation survives. Also, a trivial word alone cannot replace the ribbon
certificate: a null-homotopic embedded curve in an unlink complement need not
bound an embedded disk there. This attempt therefore supplies no candidate.
The exclusion is for this explicit ribbon realization, not all geometries
with the same free-group word or merely the same opposite pairing.

C. ONE INVARIANT CIRCLE: A STRICTLY SMALLER GEOMETRIC TARGET

Instead of four distinct surgery circles, use one embedded unknot eta in
int(B) which is preserved SETWISE by rho. The action on eta is free. Let the
initial rotor R_0 be boundary-parallel and its stator S complete it to U.
Require the augmented two-component link U union eta to be the unlink, with
disk framing on eta. Then every 1/k surgery on eta leaves the global U
unchanged. Its rotational symmetry makes the local surgery equivariant.
One must still identify the surgered ball equivariantly with the standard
rotor ball, keeping the boundary marking; this is a geometric obligation in
any completed diagram, not something to omit from a certificate.

If the flipped augmented link is nontrivial and some specified 1/k filling
gives a nontrivial remaining knot, this would yield a counterexample for an
order4 or order5 Jones-preserving rotor. This is a TWO-component unlink-rotant
target instead of the previous five-component one. Nonsplitness alone does
not certify that a particular filling is nontrivial.

Why previous exclusions do not automatically apply:
- eta is a closed marked component. The bridge-disk theorem for R_0 alone
  does not supply bridge disks avoiding eta.
- eta itself and the rotation axis form a Hopf link, but U is not assumed
  globally periodic. SectionA therefore cannot simply be applied to U.
- In the quotient rotor ball one sees an axis arc, one rotor arc, AND a
  closed eta component. The rational two-string flipping argument does not
  apply to this marked three-object configuration.
- If eta bounds a disk already in B minus R_0, the construction is locally
  harmless. A useful global splitting disk must essentially leave B.

Elementary filter. With coherently transported rotor-arc orientations, the
homology class of an invariant eta in H_1(B minus R_0)=Z^n is
  m(1,...,1).
If the global U orientation induces signs epsilon_i, unlinking requires
  m sum_i epsilon_i =0.
For n=5 this forces m=0. For n=4 one may have m=0 or two signs of each kind.
This is only an abelian obstruction. It does not prove splitness or relative
flip invariance, and length4 binary reflection symmetry explains why the
corresponding linking-number test is especially weak.

D. EXPLICIT SHARED-CLASPER TEMPLATE AND ITS DECISIVE FIRST TEST

Begin with four standard small boundary-parallel rotor arcs a_0,...,a_3 and
a central round eta, all disjoint, with eta split from the unmodified closure.
In sector i put a Borromean (Y-clasper) interaction G_i with its three disk
leaves meeting eta, a_i, and a_(i+1), each once. Rotate G_0 to obtain the other
three, arranging them as disjoint claspers. The eta component is SHARED by all
four interactions. In a local three-strand description the elementary
interaction can be represented by a pure-braid commutator such as
  [sigma_1^2, sigma_2^2].
The exact braid/sign convention must be fixed in a diagram before claiming
pairwise inverses; antisymmetry of Jacobi diagrams alone is insufficient.

Two useful facts are exact for this template. If eta is deleted, each G_i
has a trivial disk-leaf, so its surgery leaves the rotor arcs unchanged.
If all rotor arcs are deleted, each G_i likewise has trivial disk-leaves,
so eta remains an unknot. Thus R_0 remains a boundary-parallel tangle when
eta is forgotten, and eta remains individually unknotted. The interaction
is entirely in their joint embedding. These statements use Habiro's
trivial-leaf move and hold for the disjoint forest, not just in a finite-type
approximation.

The initially intended anchor was an asymmetric stator connecting the four rotor arcs
into U so that two neighboring commutator interactions become exact inverse
string-link operations, and likewise for the other two. The flip changes the
attachment order. Because eta is shared and each box has six ends, the
independent FOUR-ended-box/Conway-mutation exclusion does not immediately
apply. However, the direct inverse-channel cancellation mechanism fails the
connectedness test below. This is a precisely specified geometric TEST TEMPLATE,
not an asserted unlink, candidate counterexample, or existence theorem.

Decisive first test: give the eight-endpoint stator and the three-strand
commutator embeddings, then perform actual braid/Reidemeister cancellation
showing the entire augmented link is U_2. If cancellation also survives the
flip, discard this template. If the pre-flip unlink is certified but the
flipped link is nontrivial, proceed to a filling/nontriviality certificate.
Testing polynomial equality is not the first unresolved step: rotor theory
already supplies the appropriate bracket equality.

E. EXECUTED CONNECTIVITY TEST: DIRECT INVERSE PAIRS CANNOT GIVE ONE U

Specify the economical cyclic incidence model: G_i lies on eta,a_i,a_(i+1),
and along each a_i its two interactions are G_(i-1) and G_i, with the two
remaining ends of a_i on the rotor boundary. Along eta the boxes occur in
cyclic order G_0,G_1,G_2,G_3. Require direct inverse cancellation along three
matching channels, without sliding a box through another interaction.

The only initial perfect pairings of adjacent boxes on eta are
  (G_0,G_1),(G_2,G_3), or (G_0,G_3),(G_1,G_2).

For the first pairing, G_0 and G_1 already share the eta channel and a_1.
Their remaining a_0 and a_2 channels must therefore join through the stator.
Similarly, G_2 and G_3 share eta and a_3, and their remaining channels require
the OTHER ends of a_0 and a_2 to join through the stator. Thus both ends of
a_0 are connected to a_2: their union is an entire closed component, separate
from a_1 and a_3. The latter contribute at least one more component.
The second pairing gives the same obstruction with a_1 and a_3 interchanged
with a_0 and a_2. Since the commutator interactions are pure, they do not alter
these component connections.

Consequently this direct pairwise inverse-channel construction cannot have
the required SINGLE unknot U as its non-eta component. It may produce an
unlink with more components, which is not the target and cannot be silently
band-summed without rechecking rotor symmetry and every disk certificate.

Exact scope: this excludes the specified direct pairwise cancellation anchor
for the four-Y incidence template. It does NOT exclude all embeddings of four
Y-claspers, higher-order cancellation identities, intervening strand moves,
or all invariant-circle constructions. No such more elaborate anchor was
constructed during this bounded pass. The test therefore ends this attempt
with a concrete obstruction rather than an unexecuted promise.

Do not implement an EMPTY disk-leaf wholly inside B as the anchor: Habiro's
move would make the construction trivial relative to B before closure. A
nonlocal trivial-leaf disk must actually leave the rotor ball, and must avoid
the entire remaining clasper forest to certify simultaneous cancellation.

FINAL STRATEGIC VERDICT

The global-periodic subclass is rigorously excluded, with no reliance on
Jones detection. The one-invariant-circle target is smaller and avoids the
known four-independent-box trap, but its essential unlink anchor remains
unproved. The shared-clasper template's simplest anchor has now been ruled out
by connectedness; a higher-order geometric relation would be necessary.
No large knot-table enumeration was performed, and no unverified diagram is
presented as a successful construction.
