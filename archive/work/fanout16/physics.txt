REVERSIBILITY, POSITIVE CONES, AND AN EXACT JONES SIGN-PROBLEM OBSTRUCTION

Status: an elementary obstruction to a proposed proof mechanism, not a proof
or disproof of Jones unknot detection. The general strategy tested was to turn
Jones amplitudes into a finite classical partition function with nonnegative
local weights, then use positivity to prevent cancellation. A precise class of
such models is impossible. The obstruction has an explicit pair of knot tests.
The arguments below make no claim of novelty.

1. CONVENTIONS AND A PHYSICALLY USEFUL REAL SPECIALIZATION

Use the bracket representation rho(sigma_i)=A I+A^{-1}e_i, with
e_i^2=delta e_i, e_i e_(i+1)e_i=e_i, and delta=-A^2-A^{-2}.
The normalized Markov trace has tau_n(I)=1, tau_n(e_i)=delta^{-1}.
For an n-braid beta of exponent sum w,

  V_cl(beta)(A^{-4})=(-A^3)^{-w} delta^(n-1) tau_n(rho(beta)).

Set A=i r, r>1, q=r^{-4} in (0,1), and d=r^2+r^{-2}>2. Removing the scalar i
from each positive generator gives real operators

  B_i=r I-r^{-1}e_i,       rho(sigma_i)=i B_i.

They still satisfy the braid relations. Their eigenvalues are r on ker(e_i)
and -r^{-3} on im(e_i). In the usual fundamental tensor representation these
have multiplicities three and one. Both are present already in the nontrivial
two-dimensional TL_3 block. The Jones normalization simplifies to

  V_cl(beta)(q)=d^(n-1) r^{-3w} tau_n(B_beta).                  (1)

The TL trace is positive for d>2. One can check everything directly in the
two-state tensor representation, without appealing to operator-algebra theory:
in the basis 00,01,10,11 take

          [0   0     0      0]
  e =     [0   r^2  -1      0],       D=diag(r^2,r^{-2}),
          [0  -1     r^{-2} 0]
          [0   0     0      0]

and let e_i act on adjacent tensor factors. These satisfy the TL relations;
e is positive rank one, since its middle block is (r,-r^{-1})^T(r,-r^{-1}).
The matrix D tensor ... tensor D commutes with every e_i, and the D-weighted
partial trace of e on its second tensor factor is I. Consequently

  tau_n(X)=Tr(D^{tensor n} X)/d^n

is the positive normalized Markov trace. This specialization thus retains
genuine operator positivity; it does not make both crossing directions
positive. In this explicit basis B_i is entrywise nonnegative for r>=1,
whereas its inverse has the negative entry r^{-1}-r^3 when r>1.

Writing P_i=e_i/d, one has the exact factorization

  B_i=r (I-2P_i) exp(-4 log(r) P_i).

A crossing is a reflection multiplied by imaginary-time attenuation. The sign
on the projected channel is essential to its reversible braid behavior.

2. EVEN A CHANGE OF POSITIVE CONE DOES NOT MAKE BOTH DIRECTIONS POSITIVE

A proper cone is closed, convex, pointed, and spanning in a finite-dimensional
real vector space. A linear map preserving such a cone must have its positive
spectral radius as an eigenvalue (the cone form of Perron--Frobenius).

For r>1, B_i has eigenvalues r and -r^{-3}. Its inverse has eigenvalues r^{-1}
and -r^3. The inverse's unique largest eigenvalue modulus is r^3, but +r^3 is
not an eigenvalue. Therefore B_i^{-1} cannot preserve any proper cone on a
space carrying just these two spectral channels. Choosing an overall negative
scalar fixes the inverse's spectral-radius sign but makes B_i itself have a
negative eigenvalue of strictly largest modulus. Thus no real scalar gauge,
including a writhe scalar, makes BOTH B_i and B_i^{-1} preserve a proper cone.
The r<1 case exchanges the roles of the two directions. The r=1 degeneration
is excluded and is consistent with V_K(1)=1 for every knot.

In particular, there is no common basis making a crossing and its inverse
entrywise nonnegative. This is a representation-level assertion. Adding extra
spectral channels is not ruled out by this paragraph alone; the next argument
handles finite classical auxiliary states under explicit assumptions.

3. FINITE REVERSIBLE NONNEGATIVE LOCAL MODELS FAIL EVEN ON KNOT CLOSURES

Precise model assumptions, at one fixed q in (0,1):

* There is a fixed finite state space of dimension D for three-strand tangles.
* Positive crossings have matrices G_1,G_2 with nonnegative real entries;
  negative crossings have nonnegative matrices G_1^{-1},G_2^{-1}.
  These are full inverses on that state space. This is tensor-level RII,
  not merely equality after projection or after one particular closure.
* The same closing contraction is used for every three-braid. The only
  diagram normalization is allowed to depend on strand number and exponent
  sum. In particular, equal matrices with equal exponent sum give equal
  claimed knot polynomials.

Then this model cannot reproduce the Jones polynomial on all knot closures.

Proof. A nonnegative matrix with nonnegative inverse is monomial: it maps the
extreme rays of the positive orthant bijectively, so it is a permutation times
a positive diagonal matrix. This classical result is also proved in Ding--Rhee.
Choose an even m divisible by the orders of the permutation parts of G_1,G_2;
for example m=2D! suffices. Then G_1^m and G_2^m are diagonal and commute.
Consequently the two words

  beta_+=sigma_1^m sigma_2^m sigma_1 sigma_2,
  beta_-=sigma_2^m sigma_1^m sigma_1 sigma_2

have the same model matrix and the same exponent sum 2m+2. Because m is even,
their permutations are both the three-cycle sigma_1 sigma_2, so both closures
are knots. Nevertheless their actual Jones polynomials satisfy

 V_+(q)-V_-(q)
   = -q^(m+2) (1+q+q^2) (sum_(j=0)^(m-1) (-q)^j)^2.          (2)

For every q>0, q!=1 and even m this is nonzero. This is the contradiction.
In fact the same pair also disproves the model if arbitrary equal-matrix
closure contractions are allowed; positivity of the closure contraction was
not used.

Derivation of (2), entirely inside TL_3. Let z=-r^{-4}=-q and

  c=(z^m-1)/d=-r^{-2} sum_(j=0)^(m-1) z^j.

Functional calculus for the two eigenvalues gives B_i^m=r^m(I+c e_i). Hence

  B_1^m B_2^m-B_2^m B_1^m=r^(2m)c^2(e_1e_2-e_2e_1).

For normalized tau_3,

  tau(e_1e_2)=d^{-2},
  tau((e_1e_2-e_2e_1)I)=0,
  tau((e_1e_2-e_2e_1)e_i)=0,
  tau((e_1e_2-e_2e_1)e_1e_2)=d^{-2}-1.

The last identity uses e_1e_2e_1e_2=e_1e_2 and
e_2e_1^2e_2=d e_2. Since
B_1B_2=r^2 I-e_1-e_2+r^{-2}e_1e_2,

  tau(B_beta+ - B_beta-)=r^(2m-2)c^2(d^{-2}-1).

Multiply by the factor d^2 r^{-3(2m+2)} from (1), substitute c, and use
1-d^2=-(q^{-1}+1+q). This gives exactly (2).

Independent verification: the preexisting exact diagrammatic TL engine,
which uses planar matching composition rather than this matrix calculation,
verified (2) for m=2,4,6,8,10,12. Results are in physics_check.json. At m=2,
the polynomial difference is the familiar difference between 5_1 and the
connected sum of two trefoils:

  V_51-(q+q^3-q^4)^2=-q^4(1+q+q^2)(1-q)^2.

This last identification is not required for the proof: the explicit braid
words, their permutations, and the exact nonzero difference suffice.

4. DOES A PROJECTED PHYSICAL SUBSPACE EVADE THE OBSTRUCTION?

It can evade the full-inverse premise, but a POSITIVE finite classical retract
still fails under natural invariance assumptions.

Let P be a nonnegative idempotent, W=im(P), and let nonnegative crossing maps
preserve W and be mutually inverse there. Then

  C=P(R_{>=0}^D)=W intersect R_{>=0}^D

is a closed pointed spanning polyhedral cone in W. The equality follows because
P is positive and fixes every element of W. The crossing maps are cone
automorphisms of C. They permute its finitely many extreme rays. Some common
even power fixes every ray. Choosing a basis of W from independent extreme
rays makes both such powers diagonal, hence commuting on W. Therefore the
same braid-pair contradiction applies whenever all physical closure data
factor through W. This argument allows extra physical channels and does not
assume W is exactly the fundamental Jones representation.

More generally, the argument applies to any finite-dimensional pointed
polyhedral physical cone preserved reversibly by both generators. Its
automorphism group is virtually diagonal in the sense used above.

What genuinely escapes this theorem:

(a) A nonpositive projection or a quotient on which the image of the classical
    positive cone is not pointed/spanning. This reintroduces cancellation into
    the physical-state extraction; its positivity cannot then be used without
    a separate argument.
(b) A different quantum representation on density operators. Conjugation
    X -> B X B* and its inverse preserve the positive-semidefinite cone, which
    is not polyhedral. This is not the original amplitude representation:
    B and -B give the same map. Recovering coherent Jones amplitudes requires
    additional interference information, so channel positivity alone is not
    an unknot-detection argument.
(c) Infinitely many geometric states, or projections depending on the whole
    diagram. The permutation-order argument no longer applies. Such a model
    would need a new topological theorem relating its nonlocal positive
    quantity to ordinary Jones=1. No such theorem is established here.

5. A CONSTRUCTIVE CONTROL CASE: WHY ONE-SIGN BRAIDS DO WORK

For d>2, the positive Markov trace satisfies |tau(X)|<=||X||. Since B_i is
self-adjoint with eigenvalues r,-r^{-3}, ||B_i||=r. If beta is a positive
n-braid of length p whose closure is a knot, (1) gives

  |V(q)| <= d^(n-1) r^{-2p}
          = q^((p-n+1)/2)(1+q)^(n-1),       0<q<1.

If p>n-1, the right side tends to zero as q tends to zero, contradicting V=1.
If p=n-1, every generator must occur once: omitting sigma_i would disconnect
the braid permutation across that cut, whereas a knot closure has one cycle.
An outermost generator occurring once can be cyclically moved to the end and
Markov-destabilized; induction gives the unknot. Thus this operator-norm
argument proves Jones detection for positive braid knots, a known restricted
result, without a fictitious positivity assumption for inverse crossings.

With p positive and b negative crossings, ||B_i^{-1}||=r^3 instead gives

  |V(q)| <= q^((p-3b-n+1)/2)(1+q)^(n-1).

Therefore V=1 requires p-3b<=n-1; applying the mirror argument also requires
b-3p<=n-1. These are elementary necessary inequalities, not generic detection.
They pinpoint the defect in extending the one-sign proof: inverse crossings
expand precisely the spectral channel that forward crossings attenuate.

6. STRATEGIC CONSEQUENCE AND THE EARLIEST TEST OF A NONLOCAL ESCAPE

A generic proof cannot obtain its crucial positivity merely by changing local
bases, appending finitely many classical auxiliary labels, or positively
projecting onto a finite physical sector. Reversibility forces the finite
classical cone to forget noncommuting powers that Jones actually distinguishes.

A spanning-disk-state proposal is a logically different possibility: use actual
isotopy classes of disks/arcs as an infinite set of positive states. Braid
actions can then be genuine nonperiodic permutations, so Section 3 does not
apply. But to be useful it must specify, before assuming unknottedness, a
Jones-determined quantity that forces existence of an embedded spanning disk.
An algebraic intersection number or a single vacuum overlap is not that
quantity. The earliest kill test is to state exactly where the required
positive projection is computed: if it is a fixed finite positive retract,
Section 4 rules it out; if it uses a spanning disk already, it assumes the
desired conclusion; if it needs all matrix elements/cables, it uses more than
the ordinary Jones polynomial. No candidate passing these tests emerged here.
