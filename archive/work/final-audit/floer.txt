STEP 3: INDEPENDENT CHECKS

1. The surgery formula has the necessary ambient generality. Once Y is an
integral homology sphere, K is null-homologous. No L-space assumption on Y
is needed. Each asserted oriented equality to the unknot p_i/q_i filling
cancels exactly the corresponding lens-space Casson–Walker term. The two
remaining linear equations have distinct coefficients q_i/p_i, because
the slopes are distinct and neither is the longitude. Their subtraction
does force the symmetric Delta_K''(1) to vanish.

This cancellation depends on the common marked, oriented slope basis.
An equality of filling homology orders, an unoriented lens-space equality,
or an independently chosen basis on each solid torus would not suffice.
Subject to Step 2's common basis, there is no additional hidden choice in
this step. A common reversal of surgery convention changes both sides
together. Choosing mu distinct from both probes avoids q_i=0; even q_i=0
would be harmless algebraically, but the stated choice is convenient.

2. With H_1(M)=Z, the projection used to define bar-tau is the identity.
Consequently bar-Delta=(1-t)tau=D, in the chosen polynomial normalization.
This is the specific reason Corollary 2.3 can be applied to D. For general
H_1=Z plus torsion this identification would be false. Add one explicit
sentence identifying bar-Delta with D before the norm argument; the
current statement is mathematically correct but leaves that point implicit.

3. Set a_{-1}=0. Then d_n=a_n-a_{n-1}. As a binary sequence changes
between its two values, its nonzero successive changes alternate signs.
It starts at 1 and eventually remains 1, so D's nonzero coefficients are
+1,-1,...,+1, with constant coefficient 1. There is no possibility of
a larger coefficient, a leading negative coefficient, or infinitely many
nonzero d_n. The argument does not assume the chosen filling core K is
itself Floer simple in Y; Floer simplicity of its exterior is sufficient.

4. Symmetry of the knot Alexander polynomial gives D=t^g Delta_K, with
deg(D)=2g. Here g is initially a nonnegative integer exponent shift.
Since D has an odd number of nonzero coefficients and symmetry pairs all
noncentral terms, the coefficient at its center cannot vanish. The
displayed signed expansion for Delta_K follows. Differentiating a pair
t^n+t^(-n) at 1 gives 2n^2. For even k, the alternating sum of squares is
(n_k^2-n_{k-1}^2)+...+(n_2^2-n_1^2). For odd k it is the analogous sum
ending with +n_1^2. Every summand is positive. Thus the positivity claim
is sound, including the central-coefficient issue.

An independent algebraic check gives a quantitative strengthening. Let
G(t)=sum_{a_n=0}t^n, summing only n>=0. This is a finite polynomial, with
no constant term, and D(t)=1+(t-1)G(t). Symmetry gives D'(1)=g, so there
are exactly g missing exponents. Hence

  Delta_K''(1)=D''(1)-g(g-1)
              =2 sum_{a_n=0} n - g(g-1) >= 2g.

The inequality uses that g distinct positive integers have sum at least
g(g+1)/2. Thus vanishing directly forces g=0 and D=1. This supplies a
second proof of the algebraic step; either version is adequate.

5. The norm argument is a contradiction under a temporary assumption of
boundary incompressibility. Since bar-Delta=D=1, it gives norm=-1.
The Thurston norm is nonnegative by definition. Therefore a compressing
disk exists. Irreducibility, already obtained from an actual lens filling,
then makes M a solid torus. No fibering inference from Delta=1 is being
smuggled into this argument. Irreducibility must not be deleted from the
last implication.

STEP 4: INDEPENDENT CHECKS AND SIMPLIFICATION

The Ni–Vafaee application is direct: the lambda filling gives an actual
S^1 x S^2 and its core is a knot whose exterior is M. The other filling is
an L-space. If a fibration initially has disconnected fibers, pass to its
connected-fiber factorization over a circle; the total manifold is connected.
For that fibration, H_1(M)=Z maps onto Z and therefore maps isomorphically.

The boundary-surjectivity calculation is correct. Poincare–Lefschetz
duality and the universal coefficient theorem give
H_1(M,partial M)=H^2(M)=0 from H_1(M)=Z and H_2(M)=0. The long exact
sequence makes H_1(partial M)->H_1(M) surjective. Its composition with
the fibration map is therefore onto Z. A connected torus mapping-torus
whose circle fiber has k components has image kZ on fundamental groups
and H_1: going once around the base permutes those k components cyclically.
Thus k=1. The boundary circle is null-homologous in M and consequently
has slope lambda.

Capping the boundary is legitimate: an orientation-preserving circle
monodromy extends over a disk. It gives a closed orientable fiber bundle
over S^1 with total space M(lambda)=S^1 x S^2. Its fiber group injects
into pi_1(S^1 x S^2)=Z. Positive-genus closed orientable surface groups
cannot inject into Z. The capped fiber is S^2, the original fiber is a
disk, and its oriented mapping torus is a solid torus.

Shorter replacement, using only a source already needed in Step 3:

  By RR Proposition 7.8, the exterior of the filling core in S^1 x S^2,
  which admits the other L-space filling, is a generalized solid torus.
  Since H_1(M)=Z, the homological longitude has order g_M=1. RR
  Definition 7.2 therefore gives deg(bar-Delta(M))<1. If partial M were
  incompressible, Corollary 2.3 would give a negative Thurston norm.
  Thus partial M is compressible, and irreducibility makes M a solid torus.

The order of the zero element here is 1, not 0. RR's g_M is unrelated to
the exponent shift g in Step 3. Use distinct notation if inserting this
replacement. This route removes an entire dependency and avoids needing
the boundary-fibration argument. It does not rely on a claim that every
generalized solid torus is an actual solid torus.

STEP 5: INVOLUTION AND FRAMING

The converse in BM 3.5(2) already concerns the given double branched cover
of the given two-arc tangle. It is not merely an existence assertion that
some involution of a solid torus has rational quotient. Thus no extra
assumption that the particular deck involution is standard is needed.
Excluding circle components earlier is necessary to fit BM's stated
definition. Rationality as an unmarked pair is enough: the inherited
boundary marking selects its rational slope, and its arbitrary allowed
framing makes it a framed rational tangle in Sikora's convention. Then
Corollary 20 applies to [X]=[R]. The conclusion would require additional
care if one silently replaced balanced isotopy with componentwise strict
framed isotopy; the target correctly retains balanced isotopy.

ATTEMPTED COUNTEREXAMPLES / HYPOTHESIS STRESS TESTS

(a) M=(S^1 x D^2)#Sigma, where Sigma is a nontrivial integral homology
L-space sphere, is the standard obstruction to overclaiming. It has
H_1=Z, Delta=1 and many L-space fillings, but is not a solid torus. It is
boundary compressible and reducible. Its nonlongitudinal fillings have
the nontrivial Sigma connected summand, so they cannot be actual lens
spaces. Therefore it does not violate the target, but shows exactly why
the earlier irreducibility argument and actual lens topology are needed.

(b) Nontrivial L-space knot exteriors, such as the trefoil exterior,
have H_1=Z and are Floer simple. For the trefoil Delta=t-1+t^(-1),
Delta''(1)=2, so they fail the Casson–Walker conclusion. More generally,
the independent algebraic inequality above obstructs every nonconstant
polynomial allowed by the binary torsion condition.

(c) RR's non-solid generalized-solid-torus examples have nontrivial
homological torsion or different longitude order. For instance the
Seifert examples N_g have H_1=Z plus Z/g. For g>1 they are outside the
target's H_1=Z hypothesis. At g=1 the norm obstruction forces boundary
compressibility; in the irreducible case this is the ordinary solid torus.
They cannot be used to falsify the proof by conflating rational and
integral homology solid tori.

(d) A hypothetical nontrivial Alexander-one knot in an integral homology
sphere does not defeat the norm step: if its irreducible exterior were
Floer simple, Corollary 2.3 would already exclude incompressible boundary.
No assumption that Y itself is S^3 or an L-space is used.
