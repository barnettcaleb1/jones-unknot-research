1. EXACT CONJECTURE SCOPE AND BRACKET EQUALITY

Sikora's Conjecture 2 concerns framed systems with a rational solution and
claims uniqueness among all solutions. Section 4.1 explicitly requires the
two rational probes to be unequal as UNFRAMED tangles. Section 4 uses balanced
Reidemeister isotopy. Thus the report has not silently strengthened the
distinct-probe assumption. Section 2.1 allows loops in general tangles;
the display defining framed tangles in Section 4 mentions only two intervals,
but the report's explicit treatment of loops safely covers the larger scope.
Theorem 21 gives uniqueness of the bracket vector, not topological uniqueness.
These are the precise source statements being used:
[S] https://arxiv.org/html/2005.08162v2
Conjecture 2; Sections 2.1, 4 and 4.1; Theorem 21 and its proof in Section 8.

There is NO extra global framing or orientation hypothesis narrowing
Conjecture 2 here. A single framed unknown used in both equations is part of
the definition of a framed system. Ambient isotopy, including the balanced
Reidemeister equivalence after forgetting the framing, preserves ambient
orientation. The report merely states that convention explicitly. Independent
framing adjustments at the two probes would instead describe a different,
unframed problem. Common boundary marking is likewise inherent in fixed-end
tangle equations, not an additional identification of the closed links.

The linear algebra can be checked directly. Write a probe bracket as (a,b)
and an unknown bracket as (x,y), in the horizontal/vertical basis. With the
closed bracket normalized to give the unknot value 1, the numerator closure
is (delta*a+b)x+(a+delta*b)y. For two probes the matrix determinant is
(delta^2-1)(a_1*b_2-a_2*b_1). The rational-probe determinant is nonzero for
distinct slopes. Hence the two equalities force [X]=[R] over Q(A), and therefore
as Laurent-polynomial vectors. All subsequent common rational closures have
identical brackets.

The normalization warning is correct. In the convention where a single
circle contributes delta, a nonempty closed bracket has a polynomial factor
delta. Divide BEFORE substituting A=exp(pi*i/4). The resulting absolute value
is the link determinant. The identities with Jones evaluation and branched
cover homology are also recorded in Dasbach–Futer–Kalfagianni–Lin–Stoltzfus,
"Alternating Sum Formulae for the Determinant and Other Link Invariants,"
Section 3, printed p. 769:
https://faculty.cst.temple.edu/~dfuter/research/determinants.pdf

2. HOMOLOGY, LONGITUDE AND IRREDUCIBILITY

Fix the oriented boundary identification first. Let lambda be the meridian
of the comparator solid torus V. Choose a primitive mu intersecting lambda
once. The corresponding rational closures of R are respectively the
two-component unlink and an unknot. Thus the matching closures of X have
determinants 0 and 1. Stoimenow's Proposition 6.5 says odd determinant occurs
exactly for knots; its proof gives divisibility by 2^(c-1) for c components.
The reference is correct (printed p. 619):
https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2005/0013/0003/CAG-2005-0013-0003-a005.pdf
The publisher open request returned 403, but the search tool supplied the
publisher's indexed text of the exact proposition and proof.

Consequently the determinant-one closure is a knot. Any closed component of
X would remain a separate component in every closure, alongside at least one
component formed from the two arcs. This proves X has no closed components.

Let Y=M(mu). The branched-cover homology presentation gives H_1(Y)=0, and
orientation and duality give H_2(Y)=0. If N is the attached solid torus,
Mayer–Vietoris gives an isomorphism

 H_1(boundary M)=Z^2 --> H_1(M) direct-sum H_1(N)=H_1(M) direct-sum Z.

Thus H_1(M)=Z, without a torsion ambiguity. The preceding part of the same
sequence, using H_3(Y)-->H_2(boundary M), gives H_2(M)=0. The filling core K
is a knot in the integral homology sphere Y, with exterior M and meridian mu.

Let lambda_K generate the kernel of H_1(boundary M)-->H_1(M). A slope
p*mu+q*lambda_K gives first homology Z/pZ (Z if p=0). The determinant-zero
lambda filling therefore forces lambda=+/-lambda_K as a primitive slope.
This identifies an actual integral preferred longitude, rather than merely
a rational multiple of one. There is no inference that Y is S^3.

At least one original slope is different from lambda. Its comparator filling
is a finite-homology lens space, including S^3. The actual link equivalence
makes the corresponding M filling the same lens space.

Here is the irreducibility argument in full. In a prime decomposition of M,
there is exactly one summand M_0 carrying the torus boundary. For any compact
orientable manifold with that boundary, chi(M_0)=0, so b_1(M_0)=1+b_2(M_0)>=1.
Since H_1(M)=Z, every closed summand is an integral homology sphere. Filling
the boundary gives M_0(alpha) connected-sum those same closed summands. A lens
space has no nontrivial homology-sphere prime factor. Prime decomposition
(and Poincare for a simply connected closed factor) forces all those factors
to be S^3. Nonseparating spheres would give an S^1 x S^2 factor and are already
excluded by the rank calculation. Thus M is irreducible.

3. WHY THE SIGNED p/q REALLY IS THE SAME

Both branched covers inherit orientation from the SAME oriented tangle ball.
The fixed boundary identification lifts to an orientation-preserving torus
identification. Its two lifts differ by the hyperelliptic deck involution,
which acts by -I on H_1; this changes (p,q) to (-p,-q) and leaves p/q unchanged.

After mu filling, the filling cores in M(mu) and V(mu) have exteriors M and V.
Their meridian slopes are the same marked mu. Their preferred longitude
slopes are the same marked lambda, by the homology argument above. Orient the
two peripheral pairs compatibly with the common torus orientation and the
standard surgery convention. Any convention-wide sign change must be made
on BOTH exteriors. There is no independent longitude sign or meridional shear
left over. The core in V(mu)=S^3 is an unknot.

Hence an original filling slope alpha_i=p_i*mu+q_i*lambda represents the
same signed p_i/q_i surgery on each core. An ambient orientation-preserving
isotopy of the closed branch links lifts to an orientation-preserving map of
their covers: off the branch locus this follows immediately from the
orientation-preserving local covering maps, and it extends over the branch
set. Therefore

 Y_(p_i/q_i)(K) is orientation-preservingly homeomorphic to
 S^3_(p_i/q_i)(U).

The lifted homeomorphism need NOT preserve the decomposition, the core, or
the marking. The coefficient comparison was fixed independently beforehand;
the closed-manifold homeomorphism is used only to equate invariants. This
distinction prevents a possible, but ultimately unsuccessful, objection.

Ni–Wu Proposition 3.1, equation (7), printed p. 9, explicitly treats a
null-homologous knot in a rational homology sphere and defines L(p,q) by
p/q surgery on the unknot. Its formula is

 lambda_CW(Y_(p/q)(K)) = lambda_CW(Y) + lambda_CW(L(p,q))
                       + q*Delta_K''(1)/(2p),

with symmetric Delta_K and Delta_K(1)=1. It is not necessary for Y to be an
L-space or S^3:
https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/301/8839-Cosmetic.pdf

For the two nonlongitudinal original slopes p_i is nonzero. Subtracting the
unknot reference value gives 0=lambda_CW(Y)+(q_i/2p_i)Delta_K''(1). Distinct
unoriented primitive slopes with p_i nonzero have different q_i/p_i, so the
two equations force Delta_K''(1)=0 and then lambda_CW(Y)=0.

The remainder of step 3 has the correct cited hypotheses: Rasmussen–Rasmussen
Proposition 1.3 uses more than one L-space filling, Proposition 1.4 gives
zero/one torsion coefficients, and Corollary 2.3 gives the stated norm formula
under boundary incompressibility. Its extra irreducibility assumption is for
the separate fibering conclusion. These exact statements were checked:
https://arxiv.org/html/1508.05900
The binary sequence has alternating nonzero first differences. Symmetry and
sum 1 force a central term; pairing the positive exponents in decreasing
order makes the displayed second derivative strictly positive unless the
polynomial is constant. Thus no algebraic sign exception appeared here.

MINOR CLARIFICATION RECOMMENDED, NOT A GAP

Choose mu to avoid both original slopes BEFORE defining Y and K. There are
infinitely many choices mu+n*lambda. If mu is changed afterward, explicitly
redefine Y=M(mu) and its core K; the old and new Y need not be identical.
The report's wording permits the intended choice but leaves this bookkeeping
implicit. This also avoids q_i=0, although the meridional case itself can be
handled directly.

LIMITS

The argument would fail if actual link isotopies were weakened to polynomial
equality, or if ambient orientation reversal were allowed independently in
the two equations. Neither weakening occurs in the report's statement.
No external person was contacted. Steps 4–5 and novelty remain outside this
audit's principal scope.
