# Generic homology attack on Jones unknot detection

Bounded research pass, 31 October 2026. This deliberately leaves the EKT families aside and asks whether their surgery obstruction gives leverage for an arbitrary hypothetical knot with Jones polynomial 1.

## Exact generic lemma: the obstruction becomes automatic

Let K be any knot of determinant 1. At any crossing, let L0,L1 be its two smoothings, and K' the knot obtained by switching the crossing. Then the double branched covers of both L0 and L1 have cyclic first homology (possibly infinite), and their determinant magnitudes differ by one. More precisely, for a suitable integer n and choice of signs, the four groups are

- H1(Σ2(K)) = 0;
- H1(Σ2(L0)) = Z/n;
- H1(Σ2(L1)) = Z/(n+1);
- H1(Σ2(K')) = Z/(2n+1).

The convention Z/0=Z is used. Thus the two smoothing determinants are |n| and |n+1|, which differ by 1. In particular, a determinant-zero smoothing necessarily has torsion-free H1=Z.

Proof: Remove the ball around the crossing and take the branched double cover M of the complementary tangle. The original crossing filling gives the integral homology sphere Y=Σ2(K), so M is the exterior of a knot in Y. Hence H1(M)=Z, generated by the meridian μ, while a preferred longitude λ maps to zero. Choose μ to be the original filling slope. The two smoothing slopes each have distance one from μ, and distance one from one another. Up to orienting slopes and interchanging the resolutions, they are nμ+λ and (n+1)μ+λ. The switched crossing slope has distance two from μ and distance one from each smoothing, so it is (2n+1)μ+2λ. Dehn filling along pμ+qλ imposes pμ=0 in first homology and gives Z/p. This proves the formulas. This is the ordinary Montesinos correspondence, not a novel theorem.

Reference for the integral-surgery/banding correspondence: Baker, *The Poincaré homology sphere, lens space surgeries, and some knots with tunnel number two*, Pacific Journal of Mathematics 305 (2020), pp. 1–51, §2.1:
https://msp.org/pjm/2020/305-1/pjm-v305-n1-p01-s.pdf

Consequence: V_K=1 implies determinant 1, so the entire homology constraint holds automatically at every crossing of a hypothetical counterexample. It excludes proposed links before banding, but does not supply a decreasing complexity measure, a nugatory crossing, or a contradiction for arbitrary knots.

## Exact test on nontrivial determinant-one knots

`work/generic/smoothing_homology.py` constructs the standard Spherogram PD diagrams of Kinoshita–Terasaka 11n42 and Conway 11n34. It computes the integral universal Fox coloring presentations of all 44 resolutions, removes the constant-color summand, and computes their Smith forms using Regina. It verifies cyclicity and the consecutive determinant statement at all 22 original crossings. The output is `smoothing_homology.json`.

For the Kinoshita–Terasaka diagram the pairs of determinants are

(30,29), (8,7), (8,7), (17,16), (17,16), (27,26), (1,0), (14,13), (20,19), (9,8), (9,8).

The Conway diagram gives the same list up to permutation. Both knots have nonconstant Jones polynomial

V = -x^8+2x^6-2x^4+2x^2+x^-4-2x^-6+2x^-8-2x^-10+x^-12,

in Regina's x=sqrt(t) normalization. This polynomial itself certifies their nontriviality. Thus a generic argument using only determinant-one, the cyclicity of the resolution homology, and these surgery constraints cannot conclude unknotness.

At PD crossing index 6 (zero-based), in each diagram, switching the crossing produces an unknot: Regina's Reidemeister simplifier takes the resulting 11-crossing knot to a single crossing-free circle. Its oriented smoothing L has

H1(Σ2(L))=Z,

but its exact Jones polynomial is

H = x^9-x^7+x^5-x^3-x-x^-1-x^-3+x^-5-x^-7+x^-9.

The unlink polynomial is -x-x^-1. So even a nontrivial link with H1=Z and a band to the unknot is common enough to occur in these small standard examples. A homological replacement of the exact Jones-unlink condition would be invalid.

## Precise remaining gap

The actual smoothing route requires the exact Laurent-polynomial identity H=-(x+x^-1), together with a nonnugatory crossing in an unknot diagram. The surgery test sees only a specialization of this identity at x=i and the resulting integral homology. It discards the entire nonzero polynomial defect in the example above. No argument in this pass recovers that missing polynomial information from homology or forces the crossing to be nugatory.

This is an explicit stopping criterion for the homology approach: do not search further bands or promote cyclic homology to a global Jones proof without a new invariant controlling the full polynomial defect.

## Stronger test: the derivative at -1 and the Casson-Walker invariant

I checked the natural next invariant, rather than continuing to search bands. Mullins' formula, reproduced as Theorem 13 on p. 6 of Greene–Watson, *Turaev torsion, definite 4-manifolds, and quasi-alternating knots*, reads

    λ_CW(Σ2(K)) = -V'_K(-1)/(6V_K(-1)) + σ(K)/4.

Here V is the ordinary Jones polynomial in t (not x=sqrt(t)), and the normalization is λ_CW(P)=-2 for the oriented Poincaré homology sphere bounding the negative E8 plumbing. Thus Jones polynomial 1 forces λ_CW=σ/4.

Primary source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.5559 , Theorem 13. The source itself explicitly calibrates the formula on T(3,5), with V'(-1)=0, σ=-8, λ_CW=-2.

This does not force the cover to be S3, or the knot to be trivial. For the positive (3,5) torus knot,

    V(t)=t^4+t^6-t^10,
    V(-1)=1,
    V'(-1)=0,

so it satisfies the entire determinant/Casson-Walker consequence while being visibly nontrivial by V. The exact Regina calculation agrees with this polynomial. More generally, for coprime odd p,q, the usual torus-knot formula

    V_T(p,q)(t) = t^((p-1)(q-1)/2) [1-t^(p+1)-t^(q+1)+t^(p+q)]/(1-t^2)

contains only even powers of t. Since V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0 for every knot, it follows that V(-1)=1 and V'(-1)=0 for every such torus knot. Thus even this next refinement has an infinite supply of nontrivial examples satisfying it.

A connected sum with its mirror also shows that adding zero signature does not solve this: if J is any nontrivial determinant-one knot, K=J#mirror(J) has σ(K)=0, V_K(-1)=1, and V'_K(-1)=0, because V_K(t)=V_J(t)V_J(t^-1). Its Jones polynomial remains nonconstant (its span is twice the span of V_J). Therefore its double branched cover has λ_CW=0 while K is nontrivial.

Conclusion: the generic homology branch is stopped. It provides rigorous filters, but the filters cannot be promoted to a proof of unknot detection. One must control the full polynomial, or a topological invariant not determined by these specializations, rather than calculate additional examples of the same constraints.
