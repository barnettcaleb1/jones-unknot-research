# Two-twist freeness and exclusion of the two-twist commutator mechanism

Date: 2026-11-03. Nodes: G2-PAIR, G2-PAIR-COMM.

Status: complete written argument, independently checked by the internal reader `skein_pattern_design`; TRUSTED with Claude's independent read in154307. Priority/novelty unverified. The inputs about the five-dimensional module and its spin lines are the consolidated genus-two lane's exact skein formulas and G2-ORTH, stated explicitly below. No faithfulness assumption enters.

## 1. Statements and scope

Work over $K=\mathbb Q(A)$ in the generic five-dimensional Temperley–Lieb module $W=W(6,0)$, whose restriction to $B_5$ is $W(5,1)$. For a $3|3$ curve $c$ on the six-punctured sphere, write $\ell_c=Kv_c$ for its spin-$3/2$ line and $P_c$ for the invariant-form orthogonal projector onto that line. Let $\tau_c$ denote a positive downstairs full twist: in standard position it is $(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^3$. Its square lifts to an upstairs separating Dehn twist.

**Theorem 1 (pair alternative).** For any two such curves $c,d$, exactly one of the following holds:

1. $\ell_c=\ell_d$, and their canonically normalized twist operators coincide.
2. $\ell_c\ne\ell_d$, and the projective operators $\rho(\tau_c),\rho(\tau_d)$ freely generate a rank-two free group. In particular, the same holds for their squares.

The qualification is essential: **different geometric curves are not known to have different spin lines.** Establishing that is the original faithfulness crux. The theorem shows that, after excluding a collision of the two generators themselves, no longer relation in those two generators can create a kernel.

In particular, the Jones representation is projectively faithful on the subgroup generated by any two separating twists with **different unordered integral homological splittings**. Their spin lines are distinct already at the symplectic specialization: the line represented by $2\omega_P-\omega$ determines the unordered splitting $P\oplus P^\perp$. Thus this subclass needs no unknown line-injectivity premise.

**Theorem 2 (no spin line fixed by a two-twist commutator word).** Suppose $\ell_c\ne\ell_d$. Put $T_c=\tau_c^2,T_d=\tau_d^2$, and let $f$ be a nonidentity element of the commutator subgroup of the free group generated by $T_c,T_d$. Then $\rho(f)$ fixes no spin line $\ell_e$, for any $3|3$ curve $e$. Consequently

\[
\rho([T_e,f])\ne I
\]

even projectively, for every $e$. This excludes exactly the commutator-word construction proposed in `g2-proof/build-kernel/kernel.md`, Section 4, for pairs of noncoincident seed lines. It includes arbitrarily long words and pairs with the same homological splitting.

**Corollary 3 (two-generator case of the scalar-product question).** On a subgroup generated by two upstairs separating twists, any projectively trivial Jones word has total separating-twist exponent zero. Thus $\widehat d(N\cap\langle T_c,T_d\rangle)=0$ for each such subgroup, where $N$ is the generic Jones kernel. This does not decide it on the whole Torelli group.

## 2. Exact module inputs

Set

\[
\delta=-A^2-A^{-2},\qquad \Delta_2=\delta^2-1=A^4+1+A^{-4},
\qquad \Delta_3=\delta^3-2\delta.
\]

Let $H(x,y)=\bar x^{\,t}Gy$, with the involution $\bar A=A^{-1}$. The standard cap basis is

\[
D_1=(12)(34)(56),\ D_2=(12)(36)(45),\ D_3=(14)(23)(56),\
D_4=(16)(23)(45),\ D_5=(16)(25)(34).
\]

For $c_0$ surrounding $1,2,3$, the Jones–Wenzl calculation gives

\[
v_0=\Delta_2^{-1}(-\delta,1,1,-\delta,\Delta_2)^t,
\quad H(v_0,v_0)=\Delta_3,
\quad H(v_0,y)=\Delta_3[y]_{D_5}.
\tag{1}
\]

The twist spectra are

\[
\rho(\tau_c)=A^{-6}(I-P_c)+A^6P_c,
\qquad
\rho(T_c)=A^{-12}(I-P_c)+A^{12}P_c.
\tag{2}
\]

Here and below these are raw TL lifts. The scalar used to descend to a normalized genus-two representation does not affect projective claims and cancels in commutator words.

Conjugate $c$ to $c_0$, write $d=g(c_0)$, and put

\[
R(A)=[\rho(g)(\Delta_2v_0)]_{D_5}\in\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}].
\]

All braid matrices and their inverses have integral Laurent entries. Equation (1) gives the invariant overlap

\[
r:=\operatorname{tr}(P_cP_d)
=\frac{H(v_c,v_d)H(v_d,v_c)}{H(v_c,v_c)H(v_d,v_d)}
=\frac{R(A)R(A^{-1})}{\Delta_2^2}.
\tag{3}
\]

The lane's G2-ORTH theorem says that at $\zeta=e^{i\pi/4}$,

\[
r(\zeta)=(2D-1)^2\ne0,
\tag{4}
\]

where $D\in\mathbb Z$ is the determinant formed from symplectic bases of the two homological splitting planes. In particular $r\ne0$ as a rational function.

For distinct spin lines, $r\ne1$ as well. Indeed, the cap form is definite on a unit-circle arc near $A=1$. If $r=1$ identically, equality in Cauchy–Schwarz on that arc makes the two vectors parallel at every regular point. Their rational $2\times2$ minors then vanish identically. Conversely, equal lines have $r=1$. Definiteness here is the usual generic TL definite specialization near $\delta=-2$; it is also the definite-arc input used in the lane's single-identity certificate.

Hence for distinct lines,

\[
0\ne r\ne1,\qquad U=\ell_c+\ell_d,
\qquad W=U\oplus U^\perp,
\tag{5}
\]

with $U$ two-dimensional and nondegenerate. The determinant of its Hermitian Gram matrix is the product of the two norms times (1-r).

## 3. A bounded difference of degrees

For a nonzero rational function, let $\deg_\infty$ be numerator degree minus denominator degree. Put

\[
d_r=\deg_\infty r,\qquad d_s=\deg_\infty(1-r).
\]

Both $R\bar R$ and $\Delta_2^2-R\bar R$, where $\bar R(A)=R(A^{-1})$, are nonzero reciprocal Laurent polynomials. A nonzero reciprocal Laurent polynomial has degree at least zero. Since $\deg_\infty\Delta_2^2=8$,

\[
d_r,d_s\ge-8.
\tag{6}
\]

Also

\[
|d_r-d_s|\le8.
\tag{7}
\]

To see this, if either degree is positive, the identity $r+(1-r)=1$ forces both positive degrees to be equal. If both are nonpositive, at least one is zero, and (6) applies to the other. The bound is independent of the length or geometric complexity of the braid relating the curves.

This bounded **difference** is the important fact. The two degrees themselves may be arbitrarily large.

## 4. The two-dimensional trace lemma

Let (P,Q) be the restrictions of (P_c,P_d) to $U$. They are rank-one idempotents in dimension two, with $\operatorname{tr}(PQ)=r\notin\{0,1\}$. For an integer $m>4$, define

\[
X=A^mP+A^{-m}(I-P),\qquad
Y=A^mQ+A^{-m}(I-Q).
\tag{8}
\]

Both have determinant one. We will prove that every nontrivial free word in (X,Y) has nonconstant trace.

Consider a cyclically reduced alternating word

\[
w=X^{a_1}Y^{b_1}\cdots X^{a_k}Y^{b_k},
\qquad a_i,b_i\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}.
\tag{9}
\]

Expand each block by its two spectral projectors. A term consists of a Laurent eigenvalue monomial times a coefficient

\[
C(\epsilon,\eta)=
\operatorname{tr}(P_{\epsilon_1}Q_{\eta_1}\cdots P_{\epsilon_k}Q_{\eta_k}),
\quad P_0=P,\ P_1=I-P,
\quad Q_0=Q,\ Q_1=I-Q.
\]

**Projector coefficient lemma.** Every such coefficient is exactly

\[
C(\epsilon,\eta)=\pm r^u(1-r)^v,
\qquad u,v\ge0,\quad u+v=k.
\tag{10}
\]

Changing one projector choice changes $u$ by at most one.

**Proof.** In a basis diagonalizing $P$,

\[
Q=\begin{pmatrix}r&b\\c&1-r\end{pmatrix},\qquad bc=r(1-r),
\quad
I-Q=\begin{pmatrix}1-r&-b\\-c&r\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The trace is the single product

\[
\prod_{i=1}^k(Q_{\eta_i})_{\epsilon_i,\epsilon_{i+1}},
\qquad \epsilon_{k+1}=\epsilon_1.
\]

Cyclic transitions $0\to1$ and $1\to0$ occur equally often, so every $b$ pairs with a $c$. The remaining diagonal entries are $r$ or (1-r), proving (10). More explicitly, $u$ is half the number of agreements on adjacent edges of the cyclic binary string

\[
\epsilon_1,\eta_1,\epsilon_2,\eta_2,\ldots,\epsilon_k,\eta_k.
\]

A single change toggles two edge agreements, changing $u$ by at most one. This also proves that all coefficients in (10) are nonzero. $\square$

Now choose the larger-degree eigenvalue separately in every block of (9). Its eigenvalue factor has degree

\[
m\sum_i(|a_i|+|b_i|).
\]

If another term differs at $s\ge1$ blocks, its eigenvalue degree is lower by at least (2ms). By (7) and (10), its coefficient degree can exceed the selected coefficient degree by at most (8s). Thus its total degree is strictly smaller, by at least

\[
(2m-8)s>0.
\tag{11}
\]

The selected term is therefore the unique highest-degree term in the entire trace expansion and cannot cancel. Using (6) and $u+v=k$, its degree satisfies

\[
\deg_\infty\operatorname{tr}(w)
\ge m\sum_i(|a_i|+|b_i|)-8k
\ge(2m-8)k>0.
\tag{12}
\]

A cyclically reduced pure power has trace (A^{ma}+A^{-ma}), also of positive degree. Every nonidentity free word is conjugate to one of these forms. Hence every nonidentity word has nonconstant trace and is not a scalar in $\mathrm{SL}_2(K)$. This proves freeness in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(K)$.

For the downstairs twists $m=6$, (12) gives at least (4k). For their squares $m=12$, it gives at least (16k).

## 5. Proof of Theorem 1

If the spin lines coincide, their nonisotropic orthogonal projectors coincide, and (2) gives identical operators.

Otherwise (5) supplies a two-dimensional invariant summand. On this summand the twist operators are exactly (8), with $m=6$. The trace lemma proves that no nonidentity word restricts to a scalar on $U$, and hence none acts projectively trivially on $W$. This proves Theorem 1. Taking the squares gives the corresponding conclusion for upstairs separating twists. $\square$

## 6. Rational eigenvalues of a commutator word

Let $f$ be as in Theorem 2. Because its exponent sum in each of $T_c,T_d$ is zero, the scalar actions on $U^\perp$ cancel:

\[
\rho(f)|_{U^\perp}=I,\qquad
\det(\rho(f)|_U)=1,\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(\rho(f)|_U)\ne2.
\tag{13}
\]

At $\zeta=e^{i\pi/4}$, the raw matrices of both $T_c,T_d$ are (-I). This follows either from (2), since the projectors are regular there by (1), or from the full-twist quadratic identity. Conjugation gives regularity for every curve. Commutators of matrices scalar at the endpoint are identity through first order. Consequently any word with exponent sum zero in both generators satisfies

\[
\rho(f)=I+O((A-\zeta)^2).
\tag{14}
\]

Suppose $\rho(f)$ has a rational invariant line with eigenvalue $\lambda\in K$. Since $\rho(f)$ and its inverse both have matrices over $R=\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$, the numbers $\lambda$ and $\lambda^{-1}$ are integral over $R$. The Laurent ring is integrally closed in its fraction field, so

\[
\lambda\in R^\times=\{\pm A^j:j\in\mathbb Z\}.
\tag{15}
\]

Rescale an eigenvector to be regular and nonzero at $\zeta$. Applying (14) to a coordinate nonzero there gives

\[
\lambda=1+O((A-\zeta)^2).
\]

For a unit in (15), the zero derivative forces $j=0$, and its endpoint value forces the positive sign. Thus **every rational eigenvalue of $f$ is 1**.

But determinant one and trace different from 2 exclude eigenvalue 1 on $U$. In fact the quadratic characteristic polynomial on $U$ is irreducible over $K$: any rational root would also be a rational eigenvalue of the full integral matrix and hence equal 1. Therefore every rational eigenvector of $\rho(f)$ belongs to

\[
\ker(\rho(f)-I)=U^\perp.
\tag{16}
\]

G2-ORTH says $H(v_c,v_e)\ne0$ for every spin vector $v_e$. No spin line can lie in $U^\perp$. Thus $f$ fixes no spin line.

Finally, projective commutation with $T_e$ implies actual commutation. Indeed, the two generic eigenvalues of $\rho(T_e)$ have unequal multiplicities 1 and 4. A conjugate cannot be a nontrivial scalar multiple of this operator: the multiplicity-four eigenvalue must match itself, forcing the scalar to be 1. Actual commutation preserves its unique multiplicity-one line, contradicting what was just proved. This establishes Theorem 2. $\square$

## 7. Exponent sum and the remaining gap

For Corollary 3, if the seed lines are distinct, Theorem 1 rules out every nonidentity projectively trivial word. If they coincide, a word in the two twists acts as the common twist raised to its total exponent $s$. Its two eigenvalues have ratio $A^{24s}$, so projective triviality forces $s=0$. Since $\widehat d$ takes value 1 on every positive separating twist, it vanishes in either case.

This answers the two-generator instance of the question in Claude's reply152758. A follow-up elementary argument in g2_proper_span.md extends ONLY the exponent-sum conclusion to every proper spin-line span, including any four separating-twist generators. No freeness or fixed-spin-line exclusion is established here for arbitrary products involving three or more distinct spin lines. The trace coefficient monomial (10) is a specifically two-dimensional fact; we have not proved a counterpart on a larger span.

The unresolved possibilities are:

- A basic collision $\ell_c=\ell_d$ for geometrically different curves.
- A kernel involving more generators, not reducible to the pair mechanism above.
- The stronger curve–cap vanishing criterion G2-TWIST, and the separate Haken-curve assertion needed to turn faithfulness into three-bridge unknot detection.

In particular, this note proves neither faithfulness of $W(5,1)$, generic $Z_\rho=\mathbb Z$, nor the Jones unknot conjecture.
