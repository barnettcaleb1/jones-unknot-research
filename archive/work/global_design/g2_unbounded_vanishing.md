# Arbitrarily high finite vanishing at nonreducing curves

2026-11-03. Response to Q2 of the consolidated genus-two lane letter. Status: TRUSTED deduction from G2-DENSE, checked by an independent internal reader; Claude152408 independently checked the argument; novelty unverified. No computation or enumeration is used.

## Statement and normalization

Fix a trivial three-strand tangle S and a 3|3 curve c_0 reducing for S. Put zeta=exp(i*pi/4). In the regular Temperley-Lieb cap basis, let H be the invariant Hermitian form and use

    Hhat = H/delta,     delta=-A^2-A^-2.

This division removes the common zero of all Gram entries at zeta. Hhat is regular and nondegenerate there. Choose the spin vector v_0 for c_0 regular and nonzero at zeta. This is possible without a singular change of lattice: if tau is the downstairs full twist about c_0, its rank-one projector is

    P_0 = (A^6 tau - I)/(A^12-1),

whose denominator equals -2 at zeta. It is a regular rank-one idempotent, and a suitable column supplies v_0.

**Theorem.** For every integer r>=1 there is an element f_r in the r-th lower-central subgroup gamma_r(I_2) such that c_r=f_r(c_0) has the same integral homological splitting as c_0, is nonreducing for S, and the regular matrix coefficient

    F_r(A) = Hhat(S, rho(f_r)v_0)

is not identically zero but vanishes to order at least r at zeta. In particular its order is finite, and these finite orders are unbounded.

The genus-two action is normalized to be identity on I_2 at zeta. Multiplying any chosen curve vector by a local unit does not change the order. A scalar with a zero or pole at zeta is not an allowed change of this normalization.

## Two algebraic facts

**Density persists down the lower central series.** Let Gamma be Zariski dense in a connected perfect algebraic group G over a characteristic-zero field. Then gamma_r(Gamma) is Zariski dense in G for every r.

For the induction, Gamma times gamma_r(Gamma) is dense in G times G. The commutator morphism is regular. Consequently the closed subgroup generated by its values on these two dense subgroups contains the commutators of all of G. Those generate G because G is perfect. This proves the induction.

**Orders add under commutators.** In a formal matrix group,

    [I+h^a X+O(h^(a+1)), I+h^b Y+O(h^(b+1))]
       = I mod h^(a+b).

Thus a representation sending Gamma into I+hM_d sends gamma_r(Gamma) into I+h^rM_d.

## Application to the Jones representation

The already checked G2-DENSE theorem gives generic Zariski closure SL_5 for rho(I_2). Its proof uses [Kasahara's first-order image](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0012216), which spans the fourteen-dimensional traceless selfadjoint space; brackets span the ten-dimensional skewadjoint complement. See `g2_density_gate.md`, Sections 1-3, for the written argument.

There is a regular local normalization in the cap lattice with rho(I_2)=I at zeta. This was proved directly in Section 6 of that note from the polynomial identity

    (tau-A^-6 I)(tau-A^6 I)=0.

At zeta, tau^2=-I; the normalization of the upstairs separating twist cancels this scalar. The separating twists generate I_2. Thus this assertion does not assume the Kasahara-to-TL intertwiner is regular at the endpoint. A local uniformizer h can be used; the parameter relation to Kasahara's t+1 has nonzero derivative.

By the two facts above, rho(gamma_r(I_2)) is Zariski dense in SL_5, and all its elements equal I modulo h^r.

Over the generic field, consider the regular function

    F(g)=Hhat(S,g v_0),       g in SL_5.

It is nonzero as a function on SL_5: its row covector is nonzero, v_0 is nonzero, and SL_5 moves v_0 through all nonzero vectors. But F(I)=0, since c_0 is reducing. Zariski density therefore gives an f_r in gamma_r(I_2) with F(rho(f_r)) nonzero. The congruence at the endpoint gives

    F(rho(f_r)) = F(I)+O(h^r)=O(h^r).

Since the coefficient is a nonzero rational function regular at the endpoint, its order is finite. The Torelli condition preserves the integral splitting of c_0. Finally, if c_r were reducing for S, the easy skein implication would force its pairing to vanish identically. Its nonzero coefficient proves it is nonreducing. QED.

## Precise consequence for the research target

There is no uniform finite Taylor order that distinguishes reducing from nonreducing curves, even with the initial homological splitting fixed. High order of vanishing alone is not evidence of an actual kernel: the theorem produces arbitrarily high orders while certifying that the pairings are nonzero.

This does not refute an upper bound depending on geometric complexity. It also does not prove twisted-unlink rigidity, faithfulness, or their negations. An upper bound valid for every nonreducing curve, without first assuming its pairing is nonzero, would itself require the unresolved nonvanishing mechanism. The report's Q2 can therefore be narrowed to such a genuinely geometric mechanism, rather than a uniform truncation cutoff.
