# Exact Jones transfer to hyperbolic knots: the local-replacement gate

2026-11-03. Bounded theory and primary-literature pass; no computation and no scaffold changes. This supplements `hyperbolic_gate.md` rather than proposing another approximation scheme.

**Outcome.** No exact construction was found transferring an arbitrary hypothetical Jones-trivial prime satellite to a hyperbolic Jones-trivial knot. There are two concrete geometric obstructions to the simplest local constructions. The scalar information V_K=1 makes the one-strand tangle of K skein-invisible, but that tangle retains K as a connected-sum factor. Separately, local replacements avoiding a companion torus of winding magnitude at least two cannot remove that torus. A viable transfer must cross those separating surfaces and preserve Jones in that placement; neither condition follows from the existing extension theorems.

## 1. The reduction that is actually unconditional

Prime knots suffice for unknot detection. If K is a connected sum and V_K=1, multiplicativity makes each prime factor's Jones polynomial a unit in Z[t,t^-1]. Write the unit as epsilon*t^a. The identities V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0 give epsilon=1 and a=0. A nontrivial counterexample therefore has a nontrivial prime counterexample as a summand.

Prime torus knots are excluded by their Jones polynomial, or by their nonzero Conway coefficient a_2=(p^2-1)(q^2-1)/24. The remaining transfer problem is precisely the prime-satellite case. A theorem saying that every *nontrivial* knot has a hyperbolic Jones-equivalent knot would suffice, but no such theorem was found in the primary sources checked here. A theorem including the unknot as input would already manufacture a counterexample.

## 2. A complete local obstruction: scalar transparency preserves a summand

**Lemma (one-strand obstruction).** Let T_K be the oriented (1,1)-tangle obtained by deleting a trivial open arc from K. If V_K=1, its zero-framed skein class is the identity strand. However, inserting T_K into a strand of any knot L produces K#L, up to the usual orientation convention. If K is nontrivial and nonhyperbolic, this insertion never produces a hyperbolic knot.

**Proof.** The relative bracket module of a ball with two marked boundary points has rank one. After the zero-framing normalization, closing its basis strand gives the unknot, so the coefficient of T_K is exactly V_K. The sphere bounding the insertion ball meets the output knot twice and exhibits the connected-sum decomposition K#L. If L is nontrivial, the output is composite; if L is trivial, it is K. Both possibilities are nonhyperbolic under the hypothesis. QED.

The same argument handles any fixed assembly that retains one or more input copies inside such balls: each retained copy is a local summand, regardless of how complicated the outside tangle is. The statement does not cover later moves that cross the sphere and change the input tangle itself.

This identifies the cost of moving to a multi-strand encoding. In a two-strand relative skein module with basis id,e, one closure gives one functional

    phi(a id+b e)=a+delta*b

after choosing the corresponding convention. Its kernel is nonzero, generated by delta*id-e. Thus knowing the Jones polynomial of that closure does not identify the tangle vector. Cutting K in more places can remove the protected connected-sum geometry, but it also loses the implication “the input equals the trivial tangle in the skein module.” A transfer must address that missing coordinate by an actual identity, not by the scalar equality alone.

This is an algebraic underdetermination statement. It does not assert that every vector in the affine fiber is realized by a knot tangle.

## 3. A second complete obstruction: winding protects a companion torus

**Lemma (support outside the companion torus is necessary).** Let V be a knotted solid torus with nontrivial core J, and let K lie in int(V) with algebraic winding number w satisfying |w|>=2. Perform finitely many oriented tangle replacements in balls contained in int(V), preserving the endpoints and leaving a knot K'. Then T=boundary(V) is an essential torus in E(K'). Consequently K' is not hyperbolic.

Equivalently, replacements supported in balls disjoint from T cannot hyperbolize K: every such ball meeting K lies on the V side.

**Proof.** Inside each ball the old and new oriented strands differ by a cycle bounding a two-chain in that ball. Hence [K']=[K] in H_1(V), and K' has winding w.

The torus T is incompressible on the outside because E(J) is the exterior of a nontrivial knot. If T compressed in V minus K', a compression disk would be a meridional disk of V disjoint from K'. Its algebraic intersection with K' would then be zero, contradicting w!=0. Thus T is incompressible on both sides.

Finally, T cannot be boundary-parallel in E(K'). Such parallelism would make the region V minus int N(K') a torus product, which makes K' a core of V and forces |w|=1. The hypothesis |w|>=2 excludes this. Therefore T is essential. QED.

This lemma uses no polynomial. It applies to Jones-preserving replacements, crossing changes, or any other oriented local substitutions with the stated support. At winding one the product exception must be retained; at winding zero the meridional-disk argument does not apply. No claim is made for either excluded case.

For a nested satellite, every companion torus satisfying these hypotheses must be crossed by the support of a successful hyperbolization. The statement concerns the actual embedded support, not whether the move looks local in a planar diagram.

## 5. Independent check of the single-disk twist theorem

This section checks the parent's newly proposed theorem; it is a deduction from the standard annular expansion, with no novelty claim.

Let K_n be obtained from a fixed knot by n full twists about one fixed disk. Let r be the disk's geometric intersection number and w the signed winding. Write the integral annular bracket in the Chebyshev basis as

    [K] = sum_{j=r,r-2,...} p_j(A) e_j,   p_j in Z[A,A^-1].

With the exact writhe correction, the zero-framed ordinary Jones expansion is

    delta V_{K_n}(A^-4)
      = u(A) sum_j p_j(A) Delta_j(A) A^{n E_j},
    E_j = j(j+2)-3w^2,

where u is one fixed framing unit and Delta_j=(-1)^j[j+1]. Reversing the twist convention negates all E_j and does not affect the conclusions. The full annular twist contributes a sign (-1)^j, while the framing correction contributes (-1)^(w^2); their product is 1 because j, r, w and w^2 have the same parity. Thus positive real A gives genuinely positive exponential bases.

**Check A: infinite repetition of one knot Jones polynomial.** Suppose V_{K_n}=F for infinitely many integers n, where F is the Jones polynomial of a fixed knot. Choose transcendental real A_0>1. Nonzero Laurent coefficients remain nonzero upon evaluation. The E_j are distinct. A nonzero linear combination of M exponentials with distinct real exponents has at most M-1 distinct real zeros, by dividing by one exponential and applying Rolle's theorem inductively. Hence infinite repetition forces the difference to vanish identically, leaving only a mode with E_j=0. The same conclusion holds over the Laurent field, since evaluation at A_0 is injective on rational functions over Q.

There is at most one resonant j. Its identity is

    u(A) p_j(A) Delta_j(A) = delta(A) F(A^-4).

At A=-1, the right side is -2 because F(1)=1. Since u(-1)=+/-1, p_j(-1) is an integer, and Delta_j(-1)=(-1)^j(j+1), we obtain j+1 dividing 2. Therefore j=0 or j=1. Resonance then forces w=0 or w=+/-1 respectively. In particular, the annular class is supported only on e_0 or only on e_1, and the entire twist family has the same Jones polynomial F.

This integrality step is essential: the resonance equation alone has larger solutions, such as j=6,w=4. Those cannot support a constant family of ordinary *knot* Jones polynomials because their quantum dimension evaluates to a multiple of 7, whereas the unreduced knot value evaluates to +/-2.

**Check B: finite fibers.** Set d=floor(r/2)+1, the number of allowed colors. If the family is not identically F, then V_{K_n}-F contains at most d+1 distinct exponential modes at A_0, including the constant target. Therefore at most d real parameters, and hence at most d integer parameters, can have V_{K_n}=F. If w=0 or +/-1, exponent zero is already among the allowed color modes, so the sharper bound is d-1. Vanishing coefficients only improve these bounds. In particular |w|>=2 always has finite fibers bounded by d.

The argument needs integrality of p_j, the fixed-unit convention, and the knot normalization F(1)=1. These hypotheses must remain explicit; arbitrary rational skein vectors or arbitrary link targets do not satisfy the same divisibility conclusion.

**Primary prior art check.** The correct historical paper is [Yoshiyuki Yokota, *Twisting formulae of the Jones polynomial*, Math. Proc. Cambridge Phil. Soc. 110 (1991), 473–482](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematical-proceedings-of-the-cambridge-philosophical-society/article/abs/twisting-formulae-of-the-jones-polynomial/2875F896EBB3186864393B8208B7052A). Its online publication date is 2008; it is not a Kalfagianni–Lin paper. The accessible extract gives the winding/wrapping setup, but the full theorem text was not obtained, so no priority judgment is made against it.

[Champanerkar–Kofman, *On the Mahler measure of Jones polynomials under twisting*, Section 2, equations (3)–(4), and Theorem 5](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0404236v2), proves the finite full-twist spectral expansion and eventual coefficient-block separation. It explicitly credits Yokota's Main Theorem. Its displayed eigenvalues suppress a common monomial, which is harmless for Mahler measure but must be restored before claiming exact normalized equality. The winding-zero/one and integer-fiber statements above were not found explicitly in the checked text; they are not asserted to be new.

## Decision

The hyperbolic transfer remains unestablished. The new local-support lemmas identify geometric conditions an operation must address. The single-disk twist result, checked independently above, gives a precise restriction on exact Jones-preserving twisting: only winding zero or one and a one-color annular class can sustain an infinite constant fiber. No scaffold changes.
