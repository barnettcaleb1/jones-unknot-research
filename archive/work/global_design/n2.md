# N2 audit: the proposed diagram-existence statement fails

Design round, 2026-11-03; theory and primary sources only, no computation. The counterexample argument below is a written deduction from published facts, awaiting the parent agent's check. No priority claim.

## 1. Precise corrections to the dictionary

In the [Gonzalez-Meneses–Manchon–Silvero paper, arXiv:1511.05845, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.05845), the graph records admissible smoothing chords: those whose two endpoints lie on the SAME all-A circle. Adjacency is interleaving of two chords. Thus A-adequacy means the Lando graph has NO VERTICES. Its independence complex is the formal sphere S^-1, with nonzero reduced cohomology in degree -1. It is not a point. A graph with one isolated vertex has a cone as its independence complex and zero reduced Euler characteristic. “No loops in the Lando graph” is not the adequacy condition; the Lando graph is already simple. The loop condition belongs to the all-A STATE graph.

The extremal complex is the POTENTIAL diagram extreme. It need not be the true extreme of the link's homology or its Jones polynomial. A nonzero coefficient at this potential degree supplies an obstruction to V=1 only if its NORMALIZED degree is outside the unknot support (unreduced Khovanov Euler polynomial: degrees -1 and +1; normalized Jones: degree 0). Claude's (D) does not state that additional condition.

The field of study already has a name: “Khovanov A-adequacy.” [Buchanan–Guo–Montoya-Vega–Rong–Silvero, arXiv:2502.17637, section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.17637) studies the existence of diagrams with nonzero potential extreme homology. Nonzero Euler characteristic is a stronger requirement. Even a wedge of spheres does not supply it if the spheres occur in different parities.

## 2. A nontrivial classical knot on which BOTH ends cancel in EVERY diagram

The GMS paper itself supplies the seed. Its Theorem 13, taking n=1, constructs a classical knot K whose actual lowest nonzero quantum row has two free rank-one groups in consecutive homological degrees. Hence that row is nonzero but has Euler characteristic zero. See the construction from Figure 11 and the adjacent-degree calculation preceding Theorem 13. This survives reduction modulo two, since the stated row is free and no lower quantum homology occurs.

Work over F_2. Two standard facts are used:

1. Unreduced Khovanov homology splits as reduced homology tensor the two-dimensional unknot factor, with quantum shifts +/-1. See [Shumakovitch, Torsion of the Khovanov homology, section 3.2, arXiv:math/0405474](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405474).
2. Reduced Khovanov homology of a connected sum is the tensor product over the field. This follows directly by identifying the marked state circles in the two reduced complexes; see also the standard isomorphism in [Baldwin–Hedden–Lobb, On the functoriality of Khovanov-Floer theories](https://maths.dur.ac.uk/users/andrew.lobb/BLH14.pdf), condition (3) and its applications.

Let R=Kh_red(K;F_2), with lowest and highest quantum rows R_a,R_b. The splitting identifies the bottom unreduced row with R_a, shifted, so chi(R_a)=0 and R_a is nonzero. Let H=K#mirror(K). Its reduced homology is R tensor the bigraded dual of R. Its lowest quantum row is R_a tensor R_b^*, and its highest is R_b tensor R_a^*. Both are nonzero, and BOTH have Euler characteristic zero. Splitting back to unreduced homology preserves this statement at the two extremes.

Now take ANY diagram D of H. Its hypothetical minimum quantum degree is at most the actual minimum homology degree. If it is lower, the homology there is zero; if it equals the actual minimum, the preceding argument gives zero Euler characteristic. In either case the potential minimum Euler coefficient vanishes. The same argument at the maximum gives zero there too. By the GMS identification, both Lando independence complexes therefore have zero reduced Euler characteristic for every D.

H is nontrivial: its reduced homology has rank greater than one, already clear from R_a having two generators, or use nontriviality of K and connected-sum genus additivity. Thus this is a classical one-component counterexample to the proposed statement (D): it has NO diagram with a nonzero hypothetical all-A OR all-B extreme Euler coefficient.

This is NOT a Jones-trivial-knot construction. It disproves a stronger proposed diagram-existence property, irrespective of the interior Jones coefficients. The endpoint cancellations are an invariant obstruction, so searching for a cleverer projection of H cannot cure them.

## 3. Why the splitting used above is safe

Over F_2, the sum of the state-circle operations sending X to 1 and 1 to 0 is a chain map nu. If X_p is multiplication by X at the marked circle, then nu X_p+X_p nu=1 and nu^2=X_p^2=0. These identities split the complex into two quantum-shifted copies of the marked reduced complex. This supplies an elementary chain-level explanation of fact 1, rather than using a false characteristic-zero reduced/unreduced splitting.

The reduced connected-sum tensor identity is over the field, so there are no Tor terms. Mirroring reverses both gradings and dualizes. At an extreme quantum sum only the two extreme factors can contribute; hence the nonzero product rows and their Euler products are exact. Taking F_2 dimensions still computes the ordinary integer-valued Euler characteristic, not merely Euler characteristic modulo two.

## 4. What can be salvaged, and what remains a real gap

This does not refute a PRIME-knot-restricted analogue: the constructed H is composite. Jones detection can be reduced to prime knots because V is multiplicative, a product equal to one forces each Laurent polynomial factor to be a unit, and V(1)=1 plus V'(1)=0 forces a knot's monomial Jones polynomial to equal one. However, no theorem supplied here shows that prime knots avoid the same two-ended homological cancellation. That is the next cheap falsification question before building a prime version of N2.

A repaired target would have to address more than the first chain degree. One could ask for a geometric, Reidemeister-compatible cancellation procedure that removes all homologically acyclic outer rows and then produces a nonzero Euler row outside the unknot support. But the seed above demonstrates why noncontractibility or sphere-wedge structure is insufficient: actual surviving homology can cancel in Euler characteristic. Such a procedure needs an additional theorem controlling the homological parity of the FIRST EULER-VISIBLE row, not just the first homology-visible row. No such mechanism is established by the Lando dictionary.

My recommendation is to reject (D) as stated and avoid promoting “choose a better diagram” into a route until an invariant obstruction test is passed. I do not have a justified new all-knot mechanism from these graph complexes alone. A discrete-Morse matching idea would merely restate the existing Euler-to-homology gap unless one proves a geometric restriction on which matchings are realizable.
