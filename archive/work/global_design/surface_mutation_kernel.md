# Kernel of the full surface-skein action

2026-11-03. Bounded theory check. This is a direct combination of standard theorems, not a novelty claim.

## Precise statement

Let Sigma_g be a closed connected oriented surface of genus g>=1, and let Mod^+(Sigma_g) denote orientation-preserving mapping classes, with no marked points. Over R=Z[A,A^-1] (or Q(A)), consider the natural action

    rho_g : Mod^+(Sigma_g) -> Aut_R KBSM(Sigma_g x I).

Then

    ker rho_g = Z(Mod^+(Sigma_g))
              = {I,-I}                    if g=1,
                {1,iota_hyperelliptic}    if g=2,
                {1}                      if g>=3.

Here the module is the **full, unreduced Kauffman bracket skein module**, not a finite-dimensional TQFT state space or a quotient specialized to a fixed set of colors.

## Proof

1. Przytycki's Theorem 3.1 gives a free basis of the surface-product skein module indexed by isotopy classes of unoriented multicurves on Sigma_g without contractible components, including the empty multicurve, with surface framing. An orientation-preserving homeomorphism takes each basis vector exactly to the vector of the image multicurve; it introduces no framing twist.

2. Therefore rho_g(f)=id iff f fixes every essential unoriented simple-curve class. The forward implication follows by applying rho_g(f) to single-component basis vectors. The converse follows because a multicurve is determined by its component classes and multiplicities.

3. This curve-action kernel equals the center of the orientation-preserving mapping class group. Indeed, f fixing every curve implies

    f T_c f^-1 = T_(f(c)) = T_c

for every essential curve c; Dehn twists generate the group. Conversely, centrality gives T_(f(c))=T_c, and equality of twists identifies their unoriented curve classes. The standard center/kernel classification gives the g>=2 cases. Luo explicitly records that for closed surfaces the curve-action kernel is trivial except in genus two, where it is generated by the hyperelliptic involution.

4. For the closed torus there is an elementary proof avoiding any convention about its curve complex. Identify Mod^+(T^2)=SL(2,Z), and unoriented essential curve classes with primitive vectors in Z^2 modulo sign. A matrix fixing the classes of e_1 and e_2 is diag(epsilon_1,epsilon_2), with epsilon_i in {+1,-1}; fixing the class of e_1+e_2 forces equal signs. Thus it is I or -I, and both fix every unoriented slope. The ordinary disjointness curve complex of a closed torus is discrete; the commonly modified Farey graph uses intersection-one edges. Their vertex sets, and hence the kernel relevant here, agree.

## Sources checked

- [J. H. Przytycki, Fundamentals of Kauffman bracket skein modules, Kobe J. Math. 16 (1999), 45–66](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/E0003677/E0003677.pdf), Theorem 3.1, printed p. 52: the multicurve basis for an I-bundle over a surface.
- [Feng Luo, Automorphisms of the Complex of Curves](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9904020), introduction, p. 2: explicit kernel classification, attributed there to Birman and Viro. Unlike the full automorphism-surjectivity theorem, this is exactly the kernel fact required here. The torus calculation above is independent of that paper's negative-Euler-characteristic conventions.
- [DGST, Erratum: Behavior of knot invariants under genus 2 mutation, NYJM 18 (2012), 75–77](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2012/18-5v.pdf): repaired colored-Jones invariance proof uses trivial action of the genus-two hyperelliptic involution on the full skein of F x I, rather than the false handlebody reduction in the original paper.

## Exact consequence for proposed higher-genus mutation

A construction whose universal skein justification is literally “the gluing map acts as identity on the full surface skein” has no new nontrivial closed-surface gluing maps in genus >=3. Every nonidentity mapping class there moves some single essential curve, already a basis-level witness to its nontrivial action. The special genus-two hyperelliptic mechanism does not continue to higher genus merely by choosing a higher-genus hyperelliptic involution.

This does **not** classify all Jones-preserving mutations. Equality of one scalar closure, equality on a restricted family of states, or equality after passage to a quotient does not imply identity on the full skein module. A mapping class outside this kernel might preserve those particular measurements. Establishing separation by a proposed collection of closures would be an additional theorem, not part of this result. Surfaces with punctures, marked arcs, boundary constraints, or orientation-reversing maps also require their own statements.

Optional strengthening: the same kernel calculation works over any nonzero commutative coefficient ring with A invertible for which we use Przytycki's full surface-product module. That basis remains free and the action remains a permutation of it. Specializing A alone therefore does not enlarge this full-module kernel; the larger kernels familiar from root-of-unity TQFT concern further quotients.
