# A concrete four-braid realization problem

Bounded investigation, 31 October 2026. This identifies and tests a counterexample target; it does not construct a counterexample or prove unknot detection.

## Primary literature and what it does establish

Korzun–Lanina–Sleptsov, *Closed 4-braids and the Jones unknot conjecture*, [arXiv:2402.02553v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.02553v2), [published version](https://ems.press/journals/aihpd/articles/14299028), gives hypothetical HOMFLY polynomials and necessary spectral tests, rather than a braid realizing them. Section 4.3 reports six surviving three-braid quotient words. They are survivors of a bounded four-block search and sampled eigenvalue tests, **not a classification of all possible quotient braids**.

[Kassel–Reutenauer, Proposition 1.13 and Remark 1.14(a)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0507219) supplies the exact group-theoretic parametrization used below. [Morton, Theorem 1](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~su14/papers/seifertcircles.pdf) supplies the nontriviality certificate.

## Entire kernel cosets, not a Burau-kernel search

Let

\[
\pi:B_4\longrightarrow B_3,\qquad
\sigma_1,\sigma_3\mapsto a,\quad\sigma_2\mapsto b.
\]

The kernel is freely generated by

\[
u=\sigma_1\sigma_3^{-1},\qquad
v=\sigma_2u\sigma_2^{-1}.
\]

Thus every lift of a fixed quotient word \(\gamma\) is uniquely \(k(u,v)\gamma\). This exhausts a kernel coset when all freely reduced words are allowed. It does not require the Burau matrix of \(k\) to be the identity. Both generators have writhe zero.

Use the reduced Burau representation \(B_i(t)\), with

\[
B_1=\begin{pmatrix}-t&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\t&-t&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
B_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\0&t&-t\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Set \(R_i(q)=qB_i(q^{-2})\), and write \([n]=(q^n-q^{-n})/(q-q^{-1})\). The two-dimensional character factors through \(\pi\). Denoting its trace by \(z\) and the three-dimensional trace by \(T\), the character formula gives

\[
V_{\widehat\beta}(q)=q^{-2w}\frac{q^w[5]+T[3]+z}{[2]}.
\]

At writhe five, therefore,

\[
V_{\widehat\beta}=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
(q^2+1+q^{-2})T+z=q^{11}-q^7-q^5-q^3-q.
\]

For the particularly short quotient

\[
\gamma=a^{-1}ba^2b^3,
\]

exact multiplication gives

\[
z=-q^5+q^3-2q+2q^{-1}-q^{-3}+q^{-5},
\]

and the required trace is

\[
T_*=q^9-q^7-q^5+2q^3-3q+2q^{-1}-q^{-3}.
\]

The concrete problem is to find a freely reduced word \(k(u,v)\) with

\[
\operatorname{tr}R(k\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2\sigma_1^2\sigma_2^3)=T_*.
\]

Its closure is a knot precisely when the total exponent of \(v\) is odd: the kernel permutation image is the Klein four group, with \(u=(12)(34)\), \(v=(13)(24)\), while this seed has permutation \((12)\).

Any knot produced here is automatically nontrivial. A closed four-braid has four Seifert circles; Morton's inequality and the unknot HOMFLY polynomial imply \(|w|\leq3\) for any four-braid representative of the unknot. Our writhe is five. Thus a word meeting the exact trace equation and the permutation condition immediately supplies a genuine counterexample.

The six published quotient tuples, interpreted as \(a^{c_1}b^{b_1}a^{c_2}b^{b_2}\), are

\[
(-5,2;6,2),\quad(-2,-1;1,7),\quad(-2,-1;3,5),
\quad(-2,2;3,2),\quad(-1,1;2,3),\quad(-2,2;1,4).
\]

All have writhe five. Exact Laurent targets for all six are in `literature_trace_targets.json`, generated by `literature_trace_targets.py`. Seeds 1, 4, 5, 6 require odd total \(v\)-exponent for a knot; seeds 2, 3 require odd total \(u\)-exponent. Seeds 5 and 6 have the same character target, but this alone does not identify their braid cosets.

## Replacing a numerical spectral check by an exact check

For each of the six targets, the required characteristic polynomial passes the **entire** positive unitary arc, not merely the sampled values in the paper. Here is a reproducible exact certificate.

On \(|q|=1\), the determinant is \(D=-q^5\), and the inverse trace is \(\overline T\). Choose \(d^3=D\) and normalize eigenvalues by \(d\). Their polynomial is

\[
\lambda^3-z\lambda^2+\bar z\lambda-1,\qquad z=T/d.
\]

Its real discriminant is

\[
\Delta=|T|^4+18|T|^2-8\operatorname{Re}(T^3/D)-27.
\]

Negative discriminant implies that all three roots are distinct and on the unit circle. Indeed, self-inversiveness pairs every root with its reciprocal conjugate. If an off-circle pair existed, it could be written as \(re^{i\phi},r^{-1}e^{i\phi}\), with the third root \(e^{-2i\phi}\); its discriminant would be

\[
(r-r^{-1})^2\bigl(2\cos(3\phi)-r-r^{-1}\bigr)^2>0.
\]

After substituting each Laurent target, \(\Delta\) is a polynomial in \(x=q^2+q^{-2}\). The positive unitary arc \(0\leq\arg q\leq\pi/4\) corresponds to \(0\leq x\leq2\). Exact rational Sturm sequences give equal endpoint variations in all six cases:

| Seed | Degree | Variations at 0 and 2 | Discriminant at 0 and 2 |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1 | 22 | 11, 11 | −8, −16 |
| 2 | 16 | 8, 8 | −8, −16 |
| 3 | 16 | 8, 8 | −16, −16 |
| 4 | 14 | 7, 7 | −8, −16 |
| 5 | 12 | 6, 6 | −16, −16 |
| 6 | 12 | 6, 6 | −16, −16 |

There are no roots in the interval and the discriminant is strictly negative. Conjugation and \(q\mapsto-q\) cover the other positive unitary arcs. The exact polynomials and certificates are in `literature_unitarity.json`; the computation uses rational arithmetic only. This validates a necessary eigenvalue test; it does **not** show that any target is realized by a braid matrix.

## A whole finite image also fails to obstruct realization

At a primitive sixth root \(t\), calculate over \(\mathbb Z[t]/(t^2-t+1)\). Exhaustive group closure under the four matrices \(B(u)^{\pm1},B(v)^{\pm1}\) produces exactly 27 matrices. The queue exhausts; this is the full specialized kernel image, not a word-length sample.

Every target \(T_*/q^5\) specializes to \(-1\). In each of the six cosets, 9 of the 27 kernel-image matrices give the target trace. Hence this exact finite specialization excludes none of the candidates. Code and results are `literature_finite_image.py/.json`.

A stronger infinitesimal test works over the 16-element ring

\[
\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^2+t+1)^2.
\]

This retains both the value and first-order information at the reduced cyclotomic root, modulo two. The full kernel image has 6,912 matrices, and each of the six cosets has exactly 576 matrices with the required target trace. Again the group-closure queue exhausts, so this is a whole-image test. The image has twelve possible trace values, each occurring 576 times; all six targets belong to that set. Code and exact results are `literature_jet_image.py/.json`. These finite tests impose trace conditions only; they make no claim of Laurent-polynomial realization.

## What remains missing

The remaining question is a Laurent-polynomial trace realization question in a specified free-group image. Passing unitary spectral conditions or finite specializations is insufficient. Bounded word enumeration can exclude a specified search radius, but not the full coset.

In particular, raw free-word length cannot be bounded directly by Jones degree. Conjugating \(k\gamma\) by \(h\in\ker\pi\) replaces the kernel prefix by

\[
h\,k\,\varphi_\gamma(h^{-1}),\qquad
\varphi_\gamma(h)=\gamma h\gamma^{-1},
\]

without changing the closed braid. These twisted conjugates can have arbitrarily long reduced prefixes with exactly the same Jones polynomial. A finite completeness theorem would need a bound on suitable representatives of twisted conjugacy classes, together with a proof that the trace equation has no larger representatives of that type. No such bound has been obtained here.

The root task's separate exact Temperley–Lieb search tested all freely reduced kernel words through length 16 in the six cosets, comprising 258,260,640 knot-closure instances. Its 245 first-modulus survivors all failed exact full-Jones verification. This excludes that bounded search only.
