THREE-SAMPLE UNKNOT CERTIFICATION IN FIXED TWIST FAMILIES
31 October 2026. Deduction from classical 3-manifold theorems; no novelty claim.

Statement. Fix a knot diagram outside one two-strand twist ball. Let K_n
be the knots obtained by inserting n full twists in that ball, for n in Z,
with any fixed initial half-twist parity. If three distinct K_n are certified
unknots, then every K_n is an unknot.

Proof. The double branched cover M of the complementary two-string tangle
has torus boundary. Filling the twist ball corresponds to filling M along
a family of distinct slopes alpha_n=alpha_0+n beta in a fixed integral
homology basis (the coefficient in beta may include the full-twist factor2).

One unknot filling is S^3. Its filling-core circle identifies M with a knot
exterior in S^3. A second distinct unknot filling is a nonmeridional S^3
surgery on that core knot. Gordon--Luecke's theorem says a nontrivial knot
admits no such surgery. Hence the core is an unknot and M is a solid torus.

A two-string tangle with solid-torus double branched cover is rational.
The K_n are therefore two-bridge closures. If mu is the solid-torus meridian,
their determinants are |intersection(mu,alpha_n)|=|an+b| for fixed integers
a,b. A nonconstant affine function can equal either+1 or-1 at at most two
distinct integers. Three unknot fillings force a=0 and |b|=1. All closures
then have determinant1, and a two-bridge link of determinant1 is the unknot.

Sharpness of the sample count. The coherent family T(2,2n+1) has unknots
at n=0 and n=-1, but a trefoil at n=1. Two arbitrary samples cannot suffice.
This is why one must not apply the nugatory-crossing theorem without
checking the orientation and surgery assumptions of that theorem.

Several independent sites. With d disjoint two-strand full-twist sites,
certifying the 3^d diagrams n_i in{0,1,2} as unknots certifies all integer
tuples. Extend the first coordinate by the preceding lemma for every fixed
choice of the other grid coordinates; then extend the second, and so on.
The hypothesis is actual unknot certificates, not merely Jones polynomial1.

Use with the exact affine-exponent solver. An axis-aligned product family
inside a Jones-one locus can thus receive a finite geometric certificate.
Affine loci coupling several physical twist sites, such as (n,-n), are not
covered by this argument: changing that parameter is not a single
two-strand local replacement. Arbitrary finite tables of knots are also
outside its hypotheses.

The present proof was checked independently by another agent. It is a
finite certification tool for a specified family, not a deduction from
V_K=1 for an arbitrary individual knot.
