ALL-q JONES IDENTITY DOES NOT CONTROL HIGHER MARKOV MOMENTS
2026-10-31. An explicit family of actual unknot braids.
This is an obstruction to a proposed proof mechanism, not a counterexample
to Jones unknot detection. No novelty claim is made.

1. EXACT FAMILY AND TOPOLOGY
For every integer m let
 beta_m = sigma_2^(2m) (sigma_1 sigma_2) sigma_2^(-2m) sigma_3^(-1)
in B_4. Each has writhe1. A negative Markov destabilization removes the
last sigma_3^(-1), leaving a conjugate in B_3 of sigma_1 sigma_2.
That conjugate closes to the unknot. Therefore
 V_(closure beta_m)(A)=1 for every m, identically as a Laurent polynomial.
No unproved Jones-detection assumption enters this topology argument.

2. THE SECOND MOMENT VARIES WITHOUT BOUND
The closure of beta_m^2 has two components, and their linking number
with the braid orientation is2m.

Here is a crossing-count proof for every signed integer m. Color initial
strand labels {0,2} red and {1,3} blue. The permutation of beta_m is the
4-cycle represented by bottom labels[1,2,3,0], so these are exactly the
two component classes after squaring. In the first copy of beta_m, the
initial sigma_2^(2m) contributes2m mixed-color signed crossings; sigma_1
contributes+1; the following sigma_2 and sigma_2^(-2m) involve strands of
the same color; the final sigma_3^(-1) contributes-1. Thus the signed
mixed-color count is2m. The second copy gives the same count. Dividing
the total4m by2 gives linking number2m.

For an oriented two-component link L, in the convention t=A^(-4),
 V_L'(1) in variable t = -3 lk(L).
One can derive this directly from the Jones skein relation:
 t^-1 V_+ - t V_- = (t^(1/2)-t^(-1/2))V_0.
At t=1 the two-component value is-2. Differentiation shows that changing
a crossing between the components changes V'(1) by-3, just as it changes
linking number by+1. Changing a self-crossing changes this derivative
by0. The two-component unlink has derivative0. Hence the formula follows.
Consequently
 d/dA V_(closure beta_m^2)(A) at A=1 =24m.
In particular the second Jones/Markov moment is unbounded even to first
order, although all Taylor coefficients of the first Jones moment agree.

The same construction works with a positive last crossing instead:
 beta_m^+ =sigma_2^(2m)(sigma_1 sigma_2)sigma_2^(-2m)sigma_3.
All closures are still unknots, now with writhe3=n-1, and
 lk(closure (beta_m^+)^2)=2m+2, so the derivative is24m+24.
Thus even requiring the writhe to be n-1 does not restore moment
rigidity.

3. COMMON ANALYTIC UNITARY REALIZATION
Write delta=-A^2-A^-2 and use the standard TL_4 representation
 rho(sigma_i)=A I+A^-1 e_i,
with normalized Markov trace tau(1)=1. Put
 R_m=(-A)^(-3)rho(beta_m),
since every beta_m has writhe1. The ordinary Jones formula gives
 tau(R_m)=delta^-3,
 tau(R_m^2)=delta^-3 V_(closure beta_m^2).
These identities hold as rational functions, not merely at sample roots.

On the common arc A=i exp(i theta), for sufficiently small real theta,
the TL_4 Markov form is positive and faithful, and all R_m are unitary
for that same q-dependent form at each parameter value. Indeed, at delta=2 the TL action on(C^2)^(tensor4)
realizes a faithful positive trace; positivity persists on a neighborhood.
The standard adjoint has e_i*=e_i and
 (A I+A^-1 e_i)*(A I+A^-1 e_i)=I
because A^2+A^-2+delta=0. The writhe-normalization scalar has modulus1.
No m-dependent choice of an arc is necessary.

At theta=0,
 tau(R_m)=1/8, tau(R_m^2)=1/4,
 d/dtheta tau(R_m)=0,
 d/dtheta tau(R_m^2)=-3 i m.
For the last equation, the two-component Jones exponents are2 mod4,
so V'(i)=i V'(1)=24im; delta'(i)=0 and dA/dtheta=-1.
This is an explicit family of analytic unitary braid images, on a fixed
finite-dimensional algebra with a common q-dependent positive trace, whose complete
first-trace function agrees but whose second-moment velocities are
unbounded. Thus unitarity, analyticity, a common dimension and the full
ordinary Jones identity do not imply spectral rigidity or a bound on
higher moments. Differentiating tau(R(A)) is not the same operation as
forming tau(R(A)^k).

4. A NONDESTABILIZABLE UNKNOT TEST
For a sharper test of a one-step simplification claim, use Morton's
irreducible4-braid
 X=sigma_3^-2 sigma_2 sigma_3^-1 sigma_2 sigma_1^3
   sigma_2^-1 sigma_1 sigma_2^-1.
Its closure is the unknot, and its writhe is1, as for
 C=sigma_1 sigma_2 sigma_3^-1. Exact TL calculation gives
 V_(closure X)=V_(closure C)=1,
but
 support V_(closure C^2)=[-14,6] in A,
 support V_(closure X^2)=[-42,26] in A.
Thus their second Markov moments differ. In particular their images
cannot be conjugate in the generic Temperley--Lieb algebra. Morton's
example also illustrates why a Jones-one braid need not admit an
immediate destabilization: exchange moves can be needed first.
The original topology result is Morton's1983 paper; the word and explicit
exchange simplification are reproduced by Birman--Brendle, Example3.1.

5. WHAT SURVIVES AS A RESEARCH TARGET
An ordinary-Jones proof via braid matrices must work modulo closure-
preserving changes, including destabilization and exchange. Neither
matrix conjugacy nor a bound on matrix moments is such a property.
One possible target would be a Markov-invariant variational quantity
whose minimum over all representatives is forced to its unknot value
by V=1. This family explains why fixing strand count and writhe does
not remove the need for that minimization. No such quantity or theorem
has been established here. This does not rule out rigidity specifically
at the level of the closed knot.

7. MINIMAL-BRAID QUALIFICATION AND AN EXACT REFORMULATION
The beta_m examples are nonminimal braid representatives of the unknot.
They do not refute a theorem restricted to minimal representatives.
However, minimal braid dimension is not controlled by the ordinary
Jones polynomial in general: Takioka's Kanenobu family k(n) has the
same HOMFLYPT polynomial(v^-2-1+v^2-z^2)^2 for all n, while
 n+3 <= b(k(n)) <=2n+1 for n>=3.
Therefore even the full HOMFLYPT polynomial does not bound minimal
braid index. A minimality theorem at V=1 would require special rigidity
of that fiber rather than a general scalar-trace dimension argument.

A precise sufficient statement using only the original Jones hypothesis:
 If beta is an n-braid with knot closure, V_(closure beta)=1, and n>1,
 then finitely many conjugacies and exchange moves take beta to a braid
 admitting a destabilization.
Iteration would prove Jones unknot detection. Conversely, if Jones
detects the unknot, Birman--Menasco's unknot theorem gives exactly this
statement. Thus it is an equivalent reformulation, not an independent
new lemma. Its missing input is a trace-to-exchange-move theorem; neither
unitarity nor the moment identities proved here supply that input.
