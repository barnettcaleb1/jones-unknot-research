FIXED SATELLITES CANNOT APPLY NONLINEAR RATIONAL FUNCTIONS TO JONES
2026-10-31. Independent audit and generalization of the torus-frequency
argument from the homology agent. No novelty claim is made.

CONVENTIONS AND CLAIM
Let q be indeterminate, F=Q(q^(1/4)), and H_N(K;q) the reduced, zero-
framed N-dimensional colored Jones, with H_1=1 and H_2=V. Let P be a
fixed knot pattern in a solid torus, satellites formed using the
zero-framed companion longitude. Suppose there is a rational function
f(X) in F(X), independent of K, such that
 V_(P(K))(q)=f(V_K(q))
for every knot K for which the right side is defined.
It is enough to assume this for all T(2,2m+1), m>=0.

Then f is constant or has degree1. More precisely, writing the annular
skein expansion in the reduced-colored basis as
 V_(P(K))=sum_(j=0)^J c_j(q) H_(j+1)(K;q),
all coefficients with j>=2 vanish. Thus f(X)=c_0+c_1 X.
For a single-component pattern with winding number w, parity gives:
 - w even: f is constant and only c_0 may be nonzero;
 - w odd: f(X)=c_1 X and only c_1 may be nonzero.
If P(U) is the unknot, these specialize to f=1 and f=X, respectively.
In particular, no winding-zero pattern has a universal nonconstant
rational dependence on ordinary Jones.

PROOF OF FINITE COLORED EXPANSION AND PARITY
The Kauffman skein module of the solid torus is F[z]. The standard
Jones--Wenzl/Chebyshev basis S_j(z) has degree j and parity j. Inserting
a zero-framed companion evaluates S_j at its unreduced color j+1;
normalizing by quantum dimensions only changes the coefficients c_j.
The fixed pattern and its framing correction give coefficients
independent of the companion. The homology class modulo2 of each state
is preserved by smoothing. Hence the annular bracket of a knot pattern
of winding w contains only powers z^(w mod2), and therefore only
labels j congruent to w modulo2. All sums are finite.

TORUS FORMULA AND THE UNIVERSAL COEFFICIENT RATIO
Put [n]=(q^(n/2)-q^(-n/2))/(q^(1/2)-q^(-1/2)). In one chirality
convention the normalized, zero-framed torus formula is
 H_(j+1)(T(2,2m+1);q)
   =1/[j+1] sum_(a=0)^j (-1)^(j-a)[2a+1]
      q^((2m+1)(a(a+1)-j(j+2))/2).
Mirroring replaces q by q^-1 and does not affect the argument.
Set x=q^-m and define the polynomial
 G_j(x)=1/[j+1] sum_(a=0)^j (-1)^(j-a)[2a+1]
             q^((a(a+1)-j(j+2))/2) x^(j(j+2)-a(a+1)).
Its exponents are nonnegative, and H_(j+1)(T(2,2m+1))=G_j(q^-m).
For j>=1 its highest two powers of x have degrees
 j(j+2), j(j+2)-2,
and their coefficient ratio (second divided by first) is
 R=-q[3],
independent of j. Both coefficients are nonzero in F.
In particular G_1(x)=b x^3+a x with a/b=R.
For same-parity labels j and j-2 the leading-degree gap is4j.
Even without using parity, the gap to j-1 is2j+1>=3 for j>=1.
Thus lower colors do not alter the two highest coefficients of a
nonconstant finite linear combination whose largest label is j.

PASSAGE FROM KNOTS TO A POLYNOMIAL IDENTITY
The asserted satellite dependence on the torus family implies
 sum_j c_j G_j(x)=f(G_1(x))
as rational functions in F(x). Indeed, after clearing denominators the
difference is a polynomial in x that vanishes at infinitely many
distinct elements x=q^-m of F. A nonzero polynomial over a field has
only finitely many roots.

RATIONAL f MUST BE POLYNOMIAL
Write f=P/Q with P,Q coprime in F[X]. Bezout gives U P+W Q=1.
Substituting G_1 shows P(G_1) and Q(G_1) are coprime in F[x]. Since
the left side of the preceding identity is polynomial, Q(G_1) divides
P(G_1); hence Q(G_1) is constant. As deg G_1=3, Q is constant.
This also shows that excluding the finitely many torus inputs at which
a rational expression is undefined does not affect the conclusion.

LEADING-COEFFICIENT COMPARISON
Suppose f has positive degree d and let J>=1 be the largest index
with c_J nonzero. The identity first gives
 3d=J(J+2).
On the left, the ratio of the coefficients at degrees3d-2 and3d is R.
On the right, the top monomial of f contributes ratio dR, by choosing
one factor a x and d-1 factors b x^3 in (b x^3+a x)^d.
Every lower power of G_1 has degree at most3d-3, and therefore does
not affect this ratio. Thus R=dR. Since R is nonzero, d=1.
The degree equation then forces J=1. Subtracting c_1G_1 leaves the
constant c_0, so f(X)=c_0+c_1X. If f is constant, distinct highest
degrees of the G_j similarly force all c_j with j>0 to vanish.
Pattern parity yields the two asserted cases.

CONSEQUENCES AND LIMITS
This forbids universal Jones-preserving ribbonization by a fixed
winding-zero satellite pattern. It also forbids purported universal
rules V_(P(K))=V_K^d, or any other nonconstant polynomial/rational
rule, for such patterns. The obstruction already occurs on two-strand
torus companions and does not require a hypothetical Jones-one knot.

It does NOT rule out the much weaker implication
 V_K=1 implies V_(P(K))=1.
An implication on that single fiber is not a universal functional
identity. Nor does the theorem prove that a pattern with constant
annular skein is geometrically trivial: skein equality does not imply
isotopy. Patterns depending on the input K, or operations other than
one fixed satellite, are outside its hypotheses. A fixed odd-winding
pattern with the linear rule cV is allowed; this theorem classifies
its skein action, not its geometry.

SINGLE-COLOR GENERALIZATION
The same proof works with any one fixed color H_N, N>=2, in place of
H_2. Put s=N-1. If a finite colored linear combination is universally
f(H_N) with f rational, then f is affine and the only nonzero colors
in the combination are H_1 and H_N. Indeed G_s has degree s(s+2)>=3
and the same highest-coefficient ratio R. The comparison gives d=1
and J(J+2)=s(s+2), hence J=s. After subtracting the affine expression,
the distinct degrees of the G_j force every remaining coefficient to
vanish. This explains why a winding-zero converter affine in H_3 is
compatible with the theorem, whereas one nonconstant in H_2 is not.
