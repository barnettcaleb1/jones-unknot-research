ONE SPECIAL CURVE: A BAR-NATAN REALIZABILITY OBSTRUCTION
2026-10-31. Derived in this investigation; no novelty claim.

STATUS AND SCOPE
The proof below excludes a particular formal Euler cancellation from being the
Khovanov homology of an actual classical link. It does not prove generic Jones
unknot detection, and does not assert that every special curve has length
divisible by four. The latter is not a theorem in the cited 2022 source.

THEOREM (restricted tangle statement)
Let T be a classical four-ended tangle with infinity filling the unknot. Use
the preferred parametrization in which the zero filling L=T(0) has determinant
zero (the double cover is zero surgery on the strongly invertible covering
knot). Work with reduced Khovanov homology over F_2. Suppose its multicurve has
one rational component r_1(s) and exactly one special component s_(4n)(infinity),
where n>=1 and length is in the PUBLISHED KWZ2022 convention. Then L cannot have
the Jones polynomial of the two-component unlink.
Both components occur with multiplicity one and carry the standard rank-one
local system (+1 over F_2). This is the Kh geography convention, not a claim
about arbitrary BN curves with potentially higher-rank local systems.

No absolute grading calibration of the rational curve is needed. All possible
overall quantum and homological shifts of both components are allowed in the
argument. Replacing the whole tangle by its mirror is permitted.

STEP 1: THE TWO SECTORS AND THEIR SHAPES
The special pairing is delta-thin and has dimension 4n. The rational pairing
has delta-Euler magnitude |s| if s!=0, or zero for s=0. Since det L=0, their
delta-Euler characteristics sum to zero, so |s|=4n. Put k=4n.
The rational pairing is reduced Kh of a two-strand torus link with k crossings,
up to overall bigrading shift and orientation change. Orientation changes only
give another overall shift. Mirror the whole link if necessary to select the
chirality below. Its Euler polynomial, after a convenient quantum shift, is

 R(Q)=sum_{j=0}^{k-2} (-1)^j Q^(2j+1) + Q^(2k+1).

It is delta-thin and has one generator at each displayed exponent. Equivalently,
before this shift its polynomial is
 [Q^(-3k)+(Q^2+1+Q^-2)Q^(-k)]/(Q+Q^-1).
This is the standard alternating two-strand torus-link calculation; reduced
F_2 Kh is thin with rank k. Important shape features of R:
  minimum quantum exponent 1, next exponent 3;
  maximum quantum exponent 2k+1, preceding exponent 2k-3.
Thus its unique homological top has a gap of two in homological degree below it.

Lemma 2.5 of KWZ gives the special Euler polynomial, up to sign and Q monomial,

 E_n(Q)=Q^(-k-1)(1+Q^4)(1-Q^6) sum_{i=0}^{n-1}Q^(8i).

Define E(Q)=Q^k E_n(Q). This sector is also delta-thin, with one generator at
each nonzero Euler monomial. Its min exponent is -1 and next exponent is 3;
its max exponent is 2k+1 and preceding exponent is 2k-3. The min coefficient
is +1 and max coefficient -1. In particular BOTH ends have homological gap2,
so mirroring preserves the required special-sector shape.

The exact identity is
 R(Q)+E(Q)=Q+Q^-1.
It follows by summing the geometric series, or multiplying by Q+Q^-1 and using
 (1+Q^2)(1+Q^4)(1-Q^6)/(1-Q^8)=1+Q^2+Q^4.

STEP 2: UNIQUENESS OF EULER CANCELLATION, ALLOWING ALL SHIFTS
Assume the actual reduced Kh Euler polynomial is Q+Q^-1. For arbitrary shifts
a,b and signs epsilon,eta in {+1,-1}, it must therefore satisfy
 epsilon Q^a R(Q)+eta Q^b E(Q)=Q+Q^-1.                 (1)
The only solution is a=b=0 and epsilon=eta=+1.

Proof. If the two maximum exponents differ, the larger maximum cannot cancel,
so it must equal 1. Both supports have width greater than 2. Consequently their
minimum exponents must cancel, hence a+1=b-1, or a=b-2. Then the E maximum
dominates and equals 1, giving b=-2k. The canceled minimum is -2k-1. At exponent
-2k+1, only R contributes: its bottom gap is 2 while E's bottom gap is 4. This
exponent is below -1 because k>=4, contradicting (1).
Thus the maxima agree, so a=b. Their coefficients at the common maximum are
epsilon and -eta. If they did not cancel, the coefficient would have absolute
value 2, impossible on the right. Thus epsilon=eta. The displayed identity now
reduces (1) to epsilon Q^a(Q+Q^-1)=Q+Q^-1, forcing a=0, epsilon=+1. QED.

The actual reduced quantum grading of a two-component link is odd integral, so
the shifts above are integers. The extremal argument in fact does not need
integrality. Crucially, the homological shifts remain unrestricted apart from
the Euler signs: the rational top has EVEN homological parity, and the special
top has ODD homological parity. Both occur at the SAME maximum quantum grade.

STEP 3: A SHIFT-INDEPENDENT SPECTRAL SEQUENCE OBSTRUCTION
Here is the abstract lemma actually used.

Suppose a bigraded vector space is a direct sum of two delta-thin sectors,
each having a unique top homological class and no class one homological degree
below its own top. Suppose the two top classes have the same quantum grading
and opposite homological parities. Then it cannot be the E1 page of a spectral
sequence with differential degrees (1,positive quantum degree) converging to a
rank-two vector space supported entirely in homological degree zero.

Proof. Their top homological degrees differ because their parities differ.
Let H be the larger one. There is exactly one class in homological degree H,
and no classes above H. It has no outgoing differential. In its own sector there
is no incoming source in degree H-1. In the other sector, a potential source in
degree H-1 can exist only when that sector's top degree is H-1; that top has the
SAME quantum degree as the target, so no differential of positive quantum degree
can connect them. If the other top is lower, there is no source at all.
These support exclusions persist to every spectral-sequence page, since pages
are subquotients of E1 in their bigradings. Thus the unique class at H survives.
If H!=0 this contradicts support of the limit in degree zero. If H=0, E1 has
only one class in degree zero and no higher classes, so its limit cannot have
rank two in degree zero. QED.

This lemma applies to the forced pair R,E by Step 2 and the top gaps in Step 1.

STEP 4: WHY AN ACTUAL JONES-UNLINK LINK HAS THAT BN LIMIT
The normalized Jones polynomial's first derivative at 1 determines the linking
number of a two-component link (its nonzero proportionality factor is irrelevant
here). Equality with the unlink polynomial therefore gives lk(L)=0.
Turner's characteristic-two BN spectral sequence has E1=Kh over F_2 and
d_r of degree (1,2r), r>=1. For the reduced theory at a basepoint, the filtered
homology has two canonical orientation classes, of homological degrees 0 and
2lk(L), hence rank2 at homological degree0 here.

For completeness, the reduced link assertion follows from Turner's proof:
at u=1 the Frobenius algebra splits into idempotents a=x+1 and b=x. The unreduced
canonical basis is indexed by orientations of the components, with degree
2 times the total linking between the reversed subset and its complement.
Reduction at the basepoint with x=0 keeps a and kills b there, selecting exactly
one member of each globally reversed orientation pair. For two components these
two surviving degrees are precisely 0 and 2lk. This also shows there is no
uncontrolled overall homological shift in the target theory.

Steps 2--4 contradict one another, proving the restricted tangle statement.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT DO
The formal all-n Euler identity is valid but is NOT classically realizable with
these exact two sectors and unlink polynomial: BN detects an unavoidable class.
This is a real realization obstruction, stronger than per-component Euler
nonvanishing and independent of absolute anchor shifts. Multiple special curves
can supply additional incoming sources, so the proof does not cover them.
It also says nothing by itself about whether an arbitrary Jones-one knot admits
an unknot-anchored presentation with this one-special property.
