WHITEHEAD QUOTIENT SKEIN MAP: INDEPENDENT AUDIT
31 October 2026. This audits the root agent's geometric argument.

VERDICT

The argument is sound for the standard equivariant Whitehead pattern and its
inherited strong inversion, with the peripheral markings below. It proves
injectivity of a linear map on the two-dimensional Conway-tangle skein space.
It does not assert that ordinary Jones of a companion determines its quotient
tangle, and does not address all possible strong inversions of a satellite.

1. THE LINEAR MAP AND ITS GEOMETRIC MEANING

Let W be the exterior of the Whitehead link P union A, with A the unknotted
axis and P the Whitehead pattern. Choose the standard strong inversion of this
pattern piece compatible with the companion strong inversion. Its quotient is
a fixed tangle in a ball with a smaller four-marked-point ball removed.

Insertion in this inner ball gives a Q(A)-linear map

                  F : S(B3,4 points) -> S(B3,4 points).

Both spaces have dimension two; linearity is the local Kauffman skein relation.
The input is the actual quotient tangle of the companion exterior, not the
Jones polynomial of the companion knot. There is one input ball, so this is
a linear map, rather than a cabling operation requiring multiple copies.

Use preferred meridian-longitude markings. In satellite gluing, up to coherent
orientation signs, the peripheral identification is

                     mu_K -> lambda_A,
                     lambda_K -> mu_A.

The outer meridian is the fixed meridian of P. Its filling is an actual unknot
branch set for each satellite, since the filled cover is S3. The outer preferred
longitude is independent of K: lk(P,A)=0, and a Seifert surface for P lies in
the solid torus S3 minus nu(A). Its boundary push-off remains the preferred
zero-linking longitude after every companion insertion. Equivalently, in the
link exterior [lambda_P]=lk(P,A)[mu_A]=0 already before the gluing.

2. THE TEST FAMILY IS ACTUALLY TWIST KNOTS

The rational-zero input Q0 is the quotient of the unknot exterior with its
preferred marking. More generally choose the rational input Qn whose meridian
disk, expressed on the marked boundary of that exterior, is

                         lambda_U + n mu_U.

Under the satellite identification this becomes mu_A+n lambda_A. Thus the
inserted solid torus performs 1/n surgery on the Whitehead-link component A.
For n=0 this is the original meridional filling. Since A is unknotted, the
ambient manifold after 1/n surgery is S3. The remaining component is a twist
knot. The output boundary's preferred longitude is unchanged because lk(P,A)=0.

Dubois--Huynh--Yamaguchi, arXiv:0706.2213v3, Remark 2(4)--(5), state together
that W(1/n)=E(J(2,-2n)) and

                    Delta_(J(2,2n))(t)=n t^2+(1-2n)t+n.

Symmetrizing the polynomial for W(1/n) therefore gives

                    Delta(t)=-n(t+t^-1)+(1+2n),
                    a_2=-n.

Mirroring or the opposite clasp convention changes the relevant sign; the
nonvanishing for n nonzero is what the argument uses. The n=0 remaining knot
is the actual unknot, not merely an Alexander-one knot.

3. RANK ONE WOULD VIOLATE THE LONGITUDE OBSTRUCTION

F(Q0) is the quotient of the actual unknot, in its preferred marking. Hence it
is the standard rational-zero skein vector, up to a framing unit, and is nonzero.
Suppose rank F=1. Then for any n, F(Qn)=c_n(A) F(Q0) for a scalar in Q(A).

It is essential not to silently call c_n a framing monomial. Evaluate both
sides using the OUTER meridional closure: both are actual unknot diagrams.
Their framed bracket evaluations differ by a framing unit. Thus c_n really
is a signed Laurent monomial. In particular it is neither zero nor an arbitrary
rational function.

The outer longitude closure of F(Qn) would consequently have the Jones
polynomial of the two-component unlink up to a signed Laurent monomial.
The two-filling obstruction proved in
work/fanout16/realizability_zero_filling.txt then forces the corresponding
companion (the twist knot W(1/n)) to have a_2=0. This contradicts a_2=-n for
any n nonzero. Therefore rank F=2 and F is injective over Q(A).

4. CONSEQUENCES THAT DO NOT ADD UNCOLORED-TO-COLORED ASSUMPTIONS

Suppose an actual companion K has an inherited Whitehead quotient whose
skein vector is proportional to the preferred rational-zero vector. Since
F(Q0) lies on this same line, write F(T_K)=c F(Q0). Injectivity gives

                              T_K=c Q0.

The meridional unknot closure again makes c a framing unit. Thus the companion
quotient itself is Jones-flat relative to the preferred rational-zero tangle.
The longitude obstruction forces a_2(K)=0.

In fact the following projective condition is preserved in BOTH directions:

  T_K lies on the preferred rational-zero skein line
       iff F(T_K) lies on the preferred rational-zero skein line.

Forwards is linearity, backwards is injectivity. This gives more than simply
reapplying the Casson test to the Whitehead double, whose Alexander polynomial
is already one. Finite compositions of either clasp version of the untwisted
Whitehead pattern remain injective and preserve this preferred line. Therefore
no inherited quotient of any iterated untwisted Whitehead double of a core
with a_2(K) nonzero can be the Jones-flat formal tangle under investigation.

SCOPE WARNINGS

* This is a statement about the entire marked quotient-tangle bracket, not
  about the ordinary Jones polynomial of Wh(K) or K.
* The rational-zero line and preferred markings are fixed throughout.
  It is not enough to say vaguely that an output resembles some rational
  tangle after an unspecified boundary reparametrization.
* The proof applies to the specified compatible/inherited inversion. Another
  strong inversion requires its own pattern-quotient identification.
* A core with a_2=0 is not excluded by this argument. The result transfers
  invisibility back to that core; it does not prove such invisibility impossible.
* No novelty claim is made. This is a deduction from the local skein relation,
  the standard Whitehead surgery description and the audited Lescop obstruction.
