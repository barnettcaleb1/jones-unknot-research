WATSON COUPLED TWISTS: AN EXACT OBSTRUCTION AND A SHARPER REMAINING GATE
1 November 2026. Bounded research deduction; no novelty claim.

RESULT

For Watson's mirrored-three-braid template, every actual knot with Jones
polynomial one must have an UNKNOT as the template's underlying two-bridge
knot. In that degenerate case its full bracket factors into the brackets
of the two input denominator closures. Consequently BOTH denominator
closures must themselves be Jones-one knots.

This applies to arbitrary two-arc input tangles, including nonsimple
tangles, and all powers of the coupled-twist operation. Thus the standard
nontrivial-base Kanenobu and Watson templates cannot meet the Jones-one
fiber at all. Dropping the simplicity hypothesis does not evade this
obstruction. The proof does not use Khovanov homology or a finite search.

The remaining unknot-base case is also settled for this marked template:
its output is the actual connected sum of the two denominator closures.
The proof uses the three-braid normal form, a cap trace, and cancellation
of opposite twists about complementary triples on a six-punctured sphere.
It does NOT infer topology merely from rank-one bracket factorization.

Consequently every such coupled-twist orbit that contains an actual
unknot consists entirely of unknots, even for nonsimple input tangles.
A Jones-one u=1 output already has a Jones-one u=1 denominator summand.
This closes this particular generic-bridge candidate; it does not prove
Jones unknot detection outside this family.

1. ACTUAL TEMPLATE AND ITS TWO-BRIDGE BASE

Let alpha be a three-strand braid. In a six-strand rectangle take

   beta=j(alpha)s(alpha),
   j(sigma_i)=sigma_i,
   s(sigma_i)=sigma_(6-i)^(-1),     i=1,2.

The two images have disjoint supports. At both ends use a four-ended
input tangle on the middle four endpoints and a cap joining the two
outer endpoints. Denote the resulting closed diagram by L_alpha(T,U).
This is Watson's Figure 7 construction. The same template, after a
rotation of the picture, appears in his later Khovanov paper.

With T=U=0, each middle endcap consists of the two adjacent cups. The
left three-strand half, capped at its two inner endpoints at both ends
and closed by the remaining outer arc, is a four-plat J_alpha. A sphere
between the two braid halves exhibits

             L_alpha(0,0)=J_alpha # mirror(J_alpha).

Here J_alpha has bridge number at most two, as seen directly from its
four-plat: there are two upper caps and two lower caps. Whenever the
template produces a knot, J_alpha is a knot. Indeed, if alpha's endpoint
permutation fixes its outer strand, the paired outer strands close up
to a component disjoint from both input tangles, so the template is not
a knot. Otherwise the capped four-plat is connected.

This connectivity qualification is necessary. We consider actual knot
outputs and input tangles without closed components; closed input
components would themselves survive as extra components.

2. EXACT EVALUATION MATRIX

Put R=Z[A,A^-1], delta=-A^2-A^-2. Write the bracket vectors in Watson's
0,infinity basis as
             [T]=(a,b),       [U]=(c,d).
The denominator closure has bracket
             D(T)=a+delta b,  D(U)=c+delta d.
Here D in a polynomial expression means its bracket; the corresponding
geometric closed links will also be denoted D(T),D(U).

The six-strand cap C pairs (1,6),(2,5),(3,4). Sliding a crossing around
the cap gives C sigma_i=C sigma_(6-i), for i=1,2, and hence

                    C j(alpha)s(alpha)=C.

This is a framed Reidemeister-II/cap isotopy, not an invariant-only
cancellation. Consequently the four evaluations are

             X_alpha = [[X,delta],[delta,delta^2]],
             X = <J_alpha><mirror(J_alpha)>
               = V_J(A^-4)V_J(A^4).

The framing exponents in the mirrored product cancel. Thus

 <L_alpha(T,U)> = X ac + delta(ad+bc)+delta^2 bd
                = (X-1)ac + <D(T)><D(U)>.                (1)

Watson proves the same matrix form in Proposition 5.1 and the discussion
after Figure 7 of his original paper. His fixed-point calculation then
gives the opposite-twist Jones invariance, subject to compatible writhes.
Equation (1) itself is a bracket identity and does not need that
compatibility assumption.

3. THE DETERMINANT FACTOR IS A WHOLE-FAMILY OBSTRUCTION

Set zeta=exp(pi i/4), so delta(zeta)=0 and t=A^-4=-1. Let
p=det(J_alpha)>0. For the knot J_alpha,
                    X(zeta)=p^2.
Since a(zeta)=<D(T)>(zeta) and c(zeta)=<D(U)>(zeta), equation (1) gives

 det(L_alpha(T,U))=p^2 det(D(T)) det(D(U)),                (2)

where determinants are absolute values of Jones evaluation at -1; this
definition agrees with the usual integral link determinant, including
zero for a split link. Writhe factors at zeta have absolute value one.

There is also a proof needing no conventions for link determinants. If
V_L=1, then <L>(zeta) is a root of unity. But (1) expresses it as
p^2 a(zeta)c(zeta), where a(zeta),c(zeta) are algebraic integers in
Z[zeta]. A nonunit integer p cannot divide a unit of that ring.
Therefore p=1.

A two-bridge knot has determinant equal to its positive Schubert
numerator. Numerator one gives the unknot. Hence

             V_L=1  ==>  J_alpha=U.                      (3)

For the standard Kanenobu template J_alpha=4_1 and p=5, so 25 divides
every output determinant. NO choice of nonsimple T,U, and no number of
coupled twists, gives Jones one in that template. More generally every
nontrivial two-bridge base, not only an alternating or simple input
subclass, is excluded. This does not say such families cannot test
Khovanov-rank bounds at other fixed Jones polynomials.

4. THE ENTIRE UNIT-FIBER REDUCES TO THE INPUT DENOMINATORS

If J_alpha=U, then X=1 as a full Laurent polynomial. Equation (1) becomes

              <L_alpha(T,U)>=<D(T)><D(U)>.                (4)

If L has Jones polynomial one, its bracket is a signed A-monomial, hence
a unit of R. Each factor in (4) is therefore a Laurent unit. After writhe
normalization each denominator link has signed-monomial Jones polynomial.
For a c-component link, |V(1)|=2^(c-1), so each denominator has exactly
one component. For a knot, V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0 force a signed monomial
Jones polynomial to be exactly 1. This proves

 V_(L_alpha(T,U))=1 ==> J_alpha=U and V_(D(T))=V_(D(U))=1.  (5)

Conversely, if J_alpha=U, both denominator closures are Jones-one knots,
and L_alpha(T,U) is a knot, (4) makes its Jones polynomial a monomial;
the same normalization makes it 1. This is an exact characterization
of the unit polynomial in this template, conditional only on connectivity.

Integer twists at an input do not change its denominator knot: the
twists slide into the closing cap as Reidemeister-I curls. Thus the
denominator knot types in (5) are constant along the coupled-twist orbit.
If either denominator fails the Jones-one condition, the entire orbit
misses the unit fiber. Neither 'contains any prescribed tangle' nor
'has the same Jones polynomial as another extension' means the output
has the Jones polynomial of a prescribed closure of that tangle.

5. WHAT IS AND IS NOT A NEW GENERIC BRIDGE

For an arbitrary hypothetical counterexample K, cut it into a tangle
T with D(T)=K and take U with denominator an unknot. Formula (4) then
allows Jones-one outputs without colored Jones information. This is a
real ordinary-polynomial route at the algebraic level; it differs from
the Whitehead/color-three gate. But no theorem here makes an output
nontrivial AND unknotting-number one. A local-knot placement simply
retains K and cannot supply that missing topology.

For the canonical braid alpha=sigma_1 or sigma_1^(-1), a direct endpoint
trace and cap isotopy give

             L_alpha(T,U) = D(T) # D(U).                 (6)

The two outer braid crossings cap off one pair of endpoints separately
at T and U; only the two middle strands connect those capped tangles.
The separating sphere meets them twice. The crossing decorations can
be removed outside the input boxes. This is a geometric statement for
this elementary marked template, not an inference from (4).

Therefore this canonical degenerate template does not reduce unknotting
number to one: a nontrivial u=1 knot is prime (Scharlemann), so a u=1
output in (6) must already have a u=1 nontrivial denominator summand.
Likewise, if its output is U, both denominator summands are actual U.

Adding a paired sigma_2 power immediately at either end can be absorbed
into that input tangle. Its denominator closure is unchanged by the
cap-cancellation isotopy C sigma_2 sigma_4^(-1)=C. Thus the same argument
applies to the displayed noncentral word family

            alpha=sigma_2^r sigma_1^(+/-1) sigma_2^s.

The remaining central-power issue has a complete geometric resolution.
Here are the markings and the algebra, so that the argument can be
checked separately from the bracket computation.

Use the standard representation phi:B3 -> SL(2,Z),
 sigma_1 -> U=[[1,1],[0,1]], sigma_2 -> L=[[1,0],[-1,1]].
The capped inner pair has meridian e2=(0,1), fixed by L. The double
cover of the four-plat is the gluing of two solid tori whose meridians
have intersection |det(e2,phi(alpha)e2)|. Thus det J is the absolute
value of the upper-right entry of phi(alpha).

When det J=1 write phi(alpha)=[[a,epsilon],[c,d]], epsilon=+/-1.
Direct integer matrix multiplication gives

 phi(alpha)=L^r U^epsilon L^s,
 r=epsilon(1-d), s=epsilon(1-a).

Indeed the upper-left and lower-right entries are a,d, and determinant
one gives c=epsilon(ad-1), exactly the lower-left product entry. The
kernel of phi is generated by z^2, where z=(sigma_1 sigma_2)^3 is the
central full twist. Hence, as an actual B3 word,

          alpha=z^(2k) sigma_2^r sigma_1^epsilon sigma_2^s.   (7)

This is an exact group normal form in the required det-one subset, not
an inference from equality of two Jones matrices.

For beta=j(alpha)s(alpha), each central factor becomes a positive full
twist on the left triple and the opposite full twist on the right triple:

       j(z)^m s(z)^m = Delta_left^(2m) Delta_right^(-2m).

Its signs follow directly from s(sigma_1)=sigma_5^-1 and
s(sigma_2)=sigma_4^-1. The left and right factors commute with their
respective three-braid blocks, so they may be put adjacent in the
six-strand shell.

Now view that shell as S2 x I between the TWO ENDCAP BALLS. Each endcap
ball contains all six endpoints: its arbitrary four-ended tangle plus
the outer cap. On the six-punctured end sphere, the curves surrounding
the left triple and the right triple are parallel complementary curves.
Their positive Dehn twists represent the SAME mapping class. Therefore
the opposite powers above cancel in Mod(S2,6).

The possible residual kernel from the spherical braid group is the
2pi rotation class. It is absorbed on closure by rotating an entire
endcap ball. This moves the whole contained tangle and any local knots
with the ball, so no extra endpoint or local-knot marking is violated.
Equivalently, the point-pushing isotopy gives a diffeomorphism of S2 x I
with trivial boundary maps; extending it by the identity over the two
endcap balls identifies the resulting closed link in S3. This is why
all six endpoints must lie inside the two endcap balls. A central twist
about an arbitrary intervening annulus would not justify the argument.

Thus the paired central power in (7) is closure-trivial for arbitrary
T,U. The paired sigma_2 end powers are absorbed into the two endcaps
without changing their denominator closures, as already proved by
cap slides. The elementary sigma_1^epsilon template is (6). Combining
these three actual geometric steps proves

 det J_alpha=1  ==>  L_alpha(T,U) is D(T)#D(U)              (8)

whenever these are knot closures, with the closure orientations/mirror
conventions inherited from the actual top and bottom endcaps.

The braid track independently audited the complementary-sphere argument,
including the residual spherical-braid rotation and arbitrary knotted
input tangles. It specifically checked the condition that the two
central powers be adjacent in the same S2 x I shell with opposite signs;
centrality and the displayed definition of s ensure those conditions.

Eight controlled diagram checks, using the nonsimple/nonrational input
T=rotate(1/2+1/3) and its mirror, reduced to two five-crossing connected
summands for elementary, end-twisted, and centrally twisted examples.
They are convention checks, not a proof by extrapolation. Reproducible
script and PDs: watson_degenerate_check.py and .json in this directory.

6. IMPLICATION FOR THE CURRENT PROOF ARCHITECTURE

THEOREM A (UNKNOT ORBIT RIGIDITY). If a knot in a Watson mirrored-three-
braid coupled-twist orbit is an actual unknot, every knot in that orbit
is an unknot, with no simplicity assumption on T or U.

Proof. Its determinant is one, so (8) gives D(T)#D(U)=U. Each summand
is therefore U. Coupled integer twists leave both denominator knots
unchanged. Equation (8) applies to every member, giving U#U. QED.

This is a topological conclusion, not an invocation of Jones detection
for the denominator closures. It strengthens the earlier simple-tangle
exclusion based on Lobb and Khovanov theory for this particular template.

THEOREM B (NO FIRST u=1 COUNTEREXAMPLE IN THIS TEMPLATE). If an output
has V=1 and u=1, one of its two denominator knots is itself nontrivial,
has V=1, and has u=1; the other denominator knot is U.

Proof. Equations (3)--(5) give a det-one base and Jones-one denominator
knots. Equation (8) gives their actual connected sum. A nontrivial u=1
knot is prime, so exactly one summand is nontrivial and is the output
knot itself. QED.

Thus Watson's construction cannot be the generic compiler proposed in
research20: it cannot turn denominator knots outside the u=1 sector
into a first Jones-one u=1 output. It also cannot create a counterexample
by starting from an actual unknot member of the twist orbit.

One can still use the algebraic reduction (5) as a structural descent for
a counterexample ALREADY presented in this template. In the det-one case
it is the ordinary prime-factor reduction, now certified geometrically.
It is not automatically a strict complexity descent: one denominator
may be U and the other may be the entire original knot. If both are
nontrivial, genus additivity gives strictly smaller-genus Jones-one
summands. No theorem says that an arbitrary counterexample has a Watson
presentation with two nontrivial capped inputs. Therefore no global
minimal-counterexample proof follows by declaring the template universal.

This excludes one concrete exact-Jones, non-mutation operation class.
It does not exclude all paired-crossing moves of research20, arbitrary
higher-arity tangles, or a different two-cut construction. The next
candidate must escape the mirrored-three-braid/rank-one degeneration
rather than merely use more complicated tangles inside this template.
