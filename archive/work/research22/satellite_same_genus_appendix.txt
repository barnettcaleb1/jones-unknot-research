SATELLITES AND SAME-GENUS PRIME RIBBON CONCORDANCES: QUICK AUDIT

Conclusion: arbitrary satellite patterns preserve ribbon concordance,
including winding-zero Whitehead patterns. Distinct prime same-genus
ribbon-concordant knots therefore already arise by a standard construction.
Equal genus is not a general obstruction to the remaining Jones/Kh
dominance pairs being ribbon concordant.

PRIMARY EXPLICIT STATEMENT
Kenneth L. Baker, A note on the concordance of fibered knots,
J. Topology 9 (2016), 1--4, DOI10.1112/jtopol/jtv024.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.7646
In v3 (17 June 2015), Remark6 on p2 states that satellite operations
preserve ribbon concordance, citing Kawauchi1980 Lemmas3.1--3.2. It gives
nonisotopic twisted Whitehead doubles of ribbon-concordant companions;
the accompanying distinction uses JSJ theory and Kouno--Motegi1993.
The corresponding discussion was Remark5 in an earlier version.

GEOMETRIC CHECK
Choose a time-monotone arc joining the ends of a ribbon annulus C.
Write a pattern as m parallel longitudinal strands outside a meridional
tangle box. Off a neighborhood of that arc, take m normal parallel copies
of C; on the neighborhood insert the fixed pattern tangle times I.
The critical points are parallel copies of the old minima and saddles;
the product tangle introduces no maxima. Zero framings extend across a
concordance, so the endpoint satellites have their prescribed framings.
This uses the geometric strand count m, not the algebraic winding number;
winding zero causes no exception.

CONCRETE SAME-GENUS PRIME PAIR
Take J to be the trefoil and R to be the stevedore ribbon knot 6_1.
There is a ribbon concordance J -> J#R. Applying the untwisted Whitehead
pattern W yields
  W(J) -> W(J#R).
Both knots have standard genus-one surfaces and are nontrivial satellites,
so both have genus exactly one. Every genus-one knot is prime, by genus
additivity. They are distinct: their companion exteriors, hence the rooted
JSJ structures of their Whitehead-double exteriors, differ. Alternatively
use the Whitehead-double distinction explicitly discussed by Baker.
This example makes no assertion that the two Jones polynomials agree.

SCOPE OF THE DUNFIELD--GONG QUESTION
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.21825
Printed p52, Section7 item(4), asks whether prime K1>K0 can have the same
Seifert genus, without a hyperbolic qualifier. Their finite graph has
strict genus decrease along each recorded edge, but the unrestricted
question as written does not impose the restriction needed to avoid the
satellite examples above. Do not elevate that empirical observation to a
general same-genus obstruction. In particular, the current filtered
equal-Jones candidate pairs remain unverified ribbon pairs; their equal
genus alone neither certifies nor excludes ribbon concordance.
