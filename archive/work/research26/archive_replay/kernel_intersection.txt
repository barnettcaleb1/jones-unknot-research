PASS 26 — PARTIAL KERNEL INTERSECTION: COMPLETE SCAFFOLD AND COVERAGE OBSTRUCTION
1 November 2026 UTC. No braid enumeration and no Jones-one knot constructed.

Outcome
=======
The normal-subgroup intersection route is mathematically valid, but the
published partial kernels inspected here do not supply all its hypotheses.
The Piwocki–Traczyk construction has a simple quantitative obstruction:
from the known seeds its certified block density never exceeds 1/4,
whereas full Temperley–Lieb coverage requires at least 1/2. Fixed-strand
commutators do not repair this automatically: the relevant generic
algebra ideals are idempotent, not nilpotent.

2. Uniform notation and the complete counterexample scaffold
===========================================================
Work over F=Q(A). Write rho_j for the generic TL_n simple block with
partition [n-j,j], 0 <= j <= floor(n/2). Put
  K(n,r) = intersection_{j=0}^r ker(rho_j),
with the harmless scalar normalization fixed consistently.
The PT quotient by diagrams with MORE THAN r cups retains these blocks.

For framing safety, define at a FIXED strand count n:
  N_j = ker(rho_j) intersect ker(w),
where w:B_n -> Z is exponent sum.
Suppose EVERY N_j is nontrivial. Each is a normal subgroup. Since no
nontrivial central full twist has exponent sum zero, every N_j is
noncentral. Long's normal-subgroup intersection lemma implies their
finite intersection is nontrivial. At each induction step the
intersection remains inside ker(w), so it remains noncentral.
This intersection is the full generic TL kernel with zero writhe.

Ito's Theorem 1 then turns it into hyperbolic Jones-one knots of
arbitrarily large genus by multiplying an unknot-closing braid by a
suitable kernel braid. His proof supplies a pure kernel element, so the
closure stays a knot. This is a complete conditional counterexample
chain, not a claim that the missing N_j are known to be nontrivial.
Primary: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2028, Theorem 1 and the Long Lemma 2.1
discussion immediately preceding Corollary 2.

Nonfaithfulness of an individual block WITHOUT the ker(w) condition is
insufficient as stated: a central power can be invisible to that block.
Likewise an arbitrarily chosen commutator of two nontrivial normal-kernel
elements need not be a nontrivial braid. The normal-intersection theorem
gives existence; an explicit candidate still needs nontriviality proof.

3. What the one-overlap construction actually guarantees
======================================================
Let alpha0 in K(k,r), beta0 in K(m,s). Add m-1 trivial strands to the
right of alpha0, and k-1 to the left of beta0. In B_(k+m-1), their
supports share exactly one strand. The commutator belongs to
  K(k+m-1,r+s).
This is the unambiguous construction in PT immediately after Theorem 1
and Figure 5; one line of the displayed hypothesis has an endpoint-index
discrepancy, so the explicit embedding, not that isolated index, is used.

Algebraically, mixed terms contain a diagram from each support region.
Their cup counts are at least r+1 and s+1. Sharing one strand permits
at most one loss, giving at least r+s+1 cups. This argument depends on
the geometry of the two embeddings; it is not a generic ideal-product
identity at fixed n.

The right/left split variant of Theorem 2 has the same (n,r) bookkeeping.
It permits weaker input hypotheses but does not change that arithmetic.
The published right/left split example supplies (11,2).

4. Quantitative no-go for iterating the verified seeds
====================================================
Define
  e(n,r)=n-1-2r,       theta(n,r)=r/(n-1).
Under the one-overlap operation,
  e(k+m-1,r+s)=e(k,r)+e(m,s),
and theta_out is the weighted average of theta_in, with weights k-1,m-1.

The presently certified seed parameters are:
  (5,1)  : e=2, theta=1/4  (Burau kernel);
  (9,2)  : e=4, theta=1/4  (PT);
  (11,2) : e=6, theta=1/5  (PT split construction).
Therefore EVERY finite tree of these operations satisfies theta<=1/4.
Adding unused strands only decreases theta and increases e.

Full TL coverage requires r>=floor(n/2). Its required theta is 1/2
for odd n and exceeds 1/2 for even n. Equivalently, its e is <=0.
Thus this procedure cannot certify a full kernel from these seeds.

For balanced self-iteration:
  n_l=(n_0-1)2^l+1,     r_l=r_0 2^l,     e_l=e_0 2^l.
The number of killed blocks increases, but so does the coverage deficit.

This excludes the GUARANTEED coverage of the recipe. It does not prove
that a particular commutator cannot accidentally kill extra blocks.
Such an accidental identity would be new mathematical input, requiring
exact verification and independent braid nontriviality.

The same e obstruction applies to any collection of split seeds whose
certified depths are strictly below full depth. A seed at full split
depth would be a genuinely stronger input, not supplied by iteration
of the cited partial examples.

5. Why fixed-n multiplication/intersection is not a shortcut
==========================================================
At fixed n the known prefix kernels are nested:
  K(n,r) intersect K(n,s) = K(n,max(r,s)).
Taking the intersection of whole such kernels does not add a previously
uncontrolled complementary block. Normal conjugation preserves every
block kernel, so conjugating a prefix-certified word gives the same
guarantee, not a new block range.

Likewise generic TL_n is a direct sum of full matrix algebras.
Every two-sided ideal I is a sum of blocks and satisfies I^2=I.
In particular the cup ideal I_(n,r) is idempotent after passing to F.
There is no nilpotent filtration argument by which commutators of
elements in 1+I must enter a deeper cup ideal.

Explicit algebraic countertest: in any surviving matrix block of size
at least two, put
  X=[[1,1],[0,1]],  Y=[[1,0],[1,1]].
Their commutator is [[3,-1],[1,0]], not identity. Extend by identity in
all other blocks. X and Y agree with identity on every already-killed
block, yet their commutator remains nonidentity in the same first
surviving block. These are algebra elements, not claimed braid images;
they refute the proposed purely ideal-theoretic inference.

Ordinary strand padding also cannot erase a full TL defect:
TL_n -> TL_(n+s) adds vertical strands to each diagram and is injective.
It preserves a known prefix guarantee, but does not create a full kernel.

6. Complementary rectangular kernel does not finish the known examples
=====================================================================
The spherical-boundary construction audited in pass25 controls the
top rectangular block at even strand count N. Intersecting this with a
PT prefix still leaves all intermediate labels. For example, padding
the (9,2) example to N=10 controls j=0,1,2; the rectangular mechanism
controls j=5; j=3,4 are still missing.

A prefix reaching j=N/2-1 plus a noncentral rectangular-block kernel
WOULD finish by the fixed-n intersection lemma. But the known density
bound cannot reach that prefix. This identifies a clean sufficient
new theorem without pretending it has been established.

7. Decision and exact next gate
===============================
Do not generate larger examples merely by repeated one-overlap
commutators: the stated guarantee cannot reach the endpoint.

A worthwhile new construction must do at least one of:
(a) give a noncentral, exponent-zero kernel for each missing block at
    one common strand count;
(b) prove an additional exact cancellation that improves the e
    bookkeeping beyond the one-overlap estimate;
(c) establish a genuine braid/representation centralizer mismatch,
    hence a nontrivial full kernel, as in the earlier scaffold.

Known partial kernels plus normality alone do none of these. No easier
complete gate has emerged in this pass. The source correction and the
coverage obstruction are the concrete outputs, not a claimed solution.
