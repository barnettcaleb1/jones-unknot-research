SCALAR ANNULAR PATTERNS: A COMPLETE SATELLITE TEST AND CONSTRUCTION GATES
1 November 2026. Bounded research pass; no priority claim.

STATUS

No actual essential winding-zero knot pattern with scalar full annular
Kauffman bracket was found in the focused primary-source search. This is
a search outcome, not a theorem that none exists. The new useful result
is a finite, exact ordinary-Jones certificate for that condition, together
with a dichotomy ruling out hidden constant subsequences in the proposed
companion-power construction. Two tempting geometric construction classes
are excluded below. Nothing here proves Jones unknot detection.

1. WHY A NONUNIT SCALAR PATTERN WOULD MATTER

Let P be a knot pattern in a solid torus, with algebraic winding zero and
geometric wrapping w>0. Work with zero-framed companions. If its full
annular bracket belongs to Q(A) times the empty diagram, then satellite
evaluation makes V_{P(J)} independent of J. The framing correction is
independent of J in this convention. Its value is V_{P(U)}, which need
not be 1.

Take J_r to be the connected sum of r copies of a trefoil. Schubert gives
b(J_r)=r+1 and b(P(J_r))>=w b(J_r). Hence an essential scalar pattern
would give an unbounded-bridge family in ONE ordinary Jones fiber.
This refutes broad whole-Jones bridge boundedness without first finding
a Jones-one knot. It does not by itself refute unknot detection when
V_{P(U)} is nonunit. This implication was already recorded in pass25.

Source for the bridge inequalities: Schultens, Additivity of Bridge
Numbers of Knots, https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~jcs/pubs/addbri15.pdf .

This is the full ANNULAR skein of the pattern, not the 2-by-2 map on
quotient tangles of a strongly invertible pattern exterior. The earlier
zero shell factor d=0 and flat-quotient equivalences do not identify these
two conditions. Nor does equality of one ordinary closure imply either.

2. FINITE CERTIFICATE USING ONLY ORDINARY JONES POLYNOMIALS

Proposition. Suppose a meridional disk meeting P in at most 2d points is
given, and P has winding zero. Fix a trefoil T of the chirality below.
The following are equivalent:

 (a) P has scalar full annular bracket.
 (b) The d+1 polynomials V_{P(U)}, V_{P(T)}, V_{P(T#T)}, ...,
     V_{P(#^d T)} are exactly equal.
 (c) V_{P(J)} is independent of the companion J.

In particular, a pattern with a verified four-point meridional disk
requires only THREE ordinary satellite Jones calculations for a positive
certificate. Each equality must be of complete Laurent polynomials;
root evaluations or finitely many Taylor coefficients are insufficient.
The geometric disk bound is essential to the stopping criterion.

Proof. Put H_N(U)=1, H_1=1, H_2=V, for reduced zero-framed colored Jones.
The skein degree bound and parity give an expansion over Q(q^(1/4))

                    V_{P(J)} = sum_{i=0}^d c_i H_{2i+1}(J).

Quantum dimensions and the pattern normalization are absorbed into c_i.
They are independent of J. The usual colored connected-sum formula gives
H_N(J_r)=H_N(T)^r. Write lambda_i=H_{2i+1}(T), so lambda_0=1.

These lambda_i are distinct and lambda_i!=1 for i>0. For completeness,
the standard two-strand torus-knot formula, in the convention
V_T=q^-1+q^-3-q^-4, is

 H_{j+1}(T)=1/[j+1] sum_{a=0}^j (-1)^(j-a)[2a+1]
                     q^(3(a(a+1)-j(j+2))/2),
 [n]=(q^(n/2)-q^(-n/2))/(q^(1/2)-q^(-1/2)).

The smallest numerator exponent comes uniquely from a=0. Subtracting
the denominator valuation gives

        val_q H_{j+1}(T)=-(3j^2+5j)/2,
        val_q lambda_i= -6i^2-5i.

Under (b), subtracting the r=0 equation gives

             sum_{i=1}^d c_i(lambda_i^r-1)=0,  1<=r<=d.

Factor lambda_i-1 from column i. The remaining row-r entries are the
monic degree-(r-1) polynomials 1+lambda_i+...+lambda_i^(r-1).
Consequently the determinant is, up to sign,

             product_i(lambda_i-1) product_{i<j}(lambda_j-lambda_i),

which is nonzero. Thus every positive-color coefficient vanishes. The
Chebyshev/Jones-Wenzl basis is a basis of the solid-torus skein, so (a)
follows. Skein evaluation proves (a)=>(c), and (c)=>(b) is immediate.

This is an elementary deduction from the standard satellite and colored
connected-sum formulas, not a claim that an actual passing P is known.
It reuses the connected-power/Vandermonde method already recorded in
outputs/jones-cabling-and-realizability.txt; the present conclusion is a
finite scalar-pattern certificate. The same torus-knot formula was
independently checked in outputs/jones-satellite-rigidity.txt.

Primary formula sources: Morton, The coloured Jones function and
Alexander polynomial for torus knots, Sections 2--3,
https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~su14/papers/colouredjones.pdf ; and
Garoufalidis, The Jones slopes of a knot, Section 4.8, equation (26),
https://guests.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/stavros/publications/printed/the_jones_slopes_of_a_knot.pdf .
The standalone verify_satellite_certificate.py checks the determinant
symbolically through d=4, the signed recurrence through |r|=8, and the
trefoil valuations through color 13. It uses only Python's standard
library. These finite arithmetic checks supplement the all-parameter
proofs rather than establish their scope.

3. THE EXACT DICHOTOMY FOR COMPANION CONNECTED POWERS

For ANY fixed winding-zero P, either its annular class is scalar, or the
Jones polynomials V_{P(J_r)} are eventually pairwise distinct and their
q-valuations tend linearly to minus infinity.

Indeed, let k>0 be the largest i with c_i!=0. The quantities

                    val_q(c_i)+r val_q(lambda_i)

have pairwise distinct slopes. For all sufficiently large r, the i=k
term is the unique one of smallest valuation. Hence

       val_q V_{P(J_r)}=val_q(c_k)-r(6k^2+5k).

There is no cancellation at that lowest exponent. In particular, even
an infinite sparse constant-Jones subsequence of this companion-power
family is impossible unless P is scalar. This reduces this entire
satellite strategy to a precise geometric realization problem; it is
not a low-order necessary test. Reflection of the trefoil gives the
analogous statement at the opposite degree end.

This bridge-growth conclusion does NOT automatically imply growth of
Khovanov rank. A rank conclusion requires a separate actual theorem;
there is no general bridge-to-Khovanov-rank inequality being used here.

4. SERIAL CLASPS DO NOT MAKE A SCALAR PATTERN

Jimenez Pascual's lassos L(r1,...,rm) are consecutive twisted sums of
m+1 nested core circles. His Proposition 3 gives an exact signed
recurrence. If every ri is nonzero, its top-degree coefficient is

             [z^(m+1)] <L(r1,...,rm)> = product_i f_{ri}(A),

where, for s>0,

       f_s=A^(s-2) sum_{j=0}^{s-1}(-A^-4)^j,
       f_{-s}=A^(2-s) sum_{j=0}^{s-1}(-A^4)^j.

Each factor is nonzero. To see the product formula, iterate the first
twist-box recurrence. Its z times tail term gives f_r times the top
tail coefficient, while the zero-box term removes two nested circles
and has degree at most m-1. Induct on m, starting with the core z.
Thus the annular degree is exactly m+1 for all choices of nonzero
positive AND negative twist integers. Zero boxes have the explicit
geometric reductions listed after Definition 2 in that paper. Repeat
those reductions first; the only scalar terminal possibility is local.
Tying an arbitrary local knot into a strand multiplies the bracket by a
nonzero ordinary Jones factor and cannot kill the top coefficient.

This excludes a whole signed serial-clasp mechanism, not just positive
or alternating diagrams. It does not cover arbitrary arborescent or
multi-input patterns. The paper uses a scaled scalar basis element;
that convention does not change the positive-degree coefficient above.
Source: https://arxiv.org/html/1501.01734 , Proposition 3 and Definition 2.

Martin gives further published obstructions: Theorems 5.1 and 5.2 prove
the wrapping conjecture for his specified adequately wrapped annular
families, their iterated Whitehead doubles, and braid cables. These
families cannot supply an essential scalar example.
https://arxiv.org/html/2103.01269 .

5. MUTATING A LOCAL SCALAR SEED DOES NOT SOLVE THE GEOMETRY

Ordinary Conway mutation preserves splitness of a link. Thus mutating
a split pattern-plus-unknotted-axis link, while retaining that axis,
does not make the pattern essential. Lickorish--Thistlethwaite explicitly
record splitness invariance in their 1988 paper, p536:
https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=com-001%3A1988%3A63%3A%3A32 .

The analogous no-go for CLOSED genus-two mutation follows from the
valid Proposition 2.1 of Dunfield--Garoufalidis--Shumakovitch--Thistlethwaite
and the following relative-boundary sphere argument.

Let M be the exterior of a split two-component link, with splitting
sphere S separating its two boundary tori. For a closed incompressible
mutation surface F, choose S transverse to F. An innermost intersection
circle on S bounds a disk on F by incompressibility. Inside this F-disk,
choose an intersection circle innermost on F; its disk has interior
disjoint from S. Surgery of S along that disk gives two spheres, each
with fewer F-intersections. Their mod-2 classes sum to [S]. A proper arc
between the boundary tori has odd intersection with S, so at least one
new sphere still separates those tori. Iterate. The resulting splitting
sphere is disjoint from F, and it survives mutation because the connected
surface F lies on one side of it.

If the genus-two F is compressible, DGST Proposition 2.1 replaces the
mutation by mutations along one or two incompressible tori, or by a
homeomorphism. Apply the preceding sphere argument to those tori. A
homeomorphism also preserves the existence of a sphere separating the
two boundary components. No special meridian-preservation claim for that
homeomorphism is needed: after the actual mutation, a sphere separating
the two exterior boundary tori is a splitting sphere for any subsequent
two-component S3 link interpretation. In particular, whenever the output
axis is again an unknot, the output annular pattern is still local.

This is a deduction, independently checked for the sphere-surgery step
by the braid agent. It does NOT use DGST Proposition 2.7, which the
authors explicitly withdrew in their erratum. Their Proposition 2.1
remains the input here.
https://arxiv.org/html/math/0607258 , Proposition 2.1 and Remark 2.2.

Therefore the attractive recipe 'put a local knot in a knotted genus-two
handlebody and hyperelliptically mutate it into an essential annular
pattern' fails this splitting-sphere test. Its skein invariance is real,
but the needed change of geometric locality cannot occur in this class.

6. WHAT THE COLORED BING-DOUBLE CONNECTION DOES AND DOES NOT GIVE

Ren, Remark 1.3, notes a genuine implication: if vanishing annular
degrees in ALL colors forced locality, colored Jones would detect the
unknot. One component of the untwisted Bing double of a hypothetical
all-colored-Jones-invisible knot is the test pattern in the complement
of the other component. This is a conditional construction, not an
actual essential scalar pattern available from known knots. Ordinary
V_K=1 cannot be silently promoted to the required colored identities.
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11084 .

7. STRATEGIC CONCLUSION

The nonunit scalar target is a legitimate way to falsify a broad global
bridge-bound scaffold without solving unknot detection first. It is now
testable using finitely many complete ORDINARY satellite polynomials
once a geometric wrapping upper bound is certified. Serial clasp
chains, the stated adequate Whitehead/cable classes, and mutation of a
local seed are bad places to seek it. No actual candidate survived or
was produced in this bounded pass. The missing work is an actual
essential pattern with all positive annular coefficients zero; there
is no remaining formal linear-algebra uncertainty in the certificate.
