PASS 27 — POINT-PUSHING DOES NOT COLLAPSE THE MISSING JONES BLOCKS
1 November 2026 UTC. Bounded structural pass; no word scan.
No nontrivial full Jones kernel or Jones-one knot was constructed.

Complete endpoint
=================
At a fixed n, a nontrivial exponent-zero kernel for EVERY generic TL_n
block gives a nontrivial common kernel by Long's normal-intersection
lemma. Ito then supplies nontrivial hyperbolic Jones-one knots.
For B5 it suffices to construct a nontrivial kernel in the residual
five-dimensional [3,2] block: its exponent-zero normal kernel intersects
the already known Burau kernel nontrivially. The framing qualification
is essential in general; an invisible central full twist in one block
alone does not suffice.
Endpoint source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2028 .

Outcome
=======
There is a precise obstruction to the proposed Birman-sequence shortcut.
Two different point-pushing groups must be distinguished:

* Deleting an ordinary strand from the PURE DISK braid group gives a
  free group F_(n-1). Its projective image is Zariski dense in EVERY
  nontrivial generic TL block, including rectangular blocks.

* Forgetting the distinguished capped-boundary puncture on a SPHERE
  gives a different point-pushing group. It is projectively invisible
  on rectangular blocks, but its projective image is Zariski dense in
  every nonrectangular nontrivial TL block.

Thus neither operation makes the missing nonrectangular blocks factor
through a smaller puncture group or preserve a hidden proper linear
sector. This does NOT establish faithfulness: dense matrix images may
have nontrivial kernels. It identifies exactly why a dimension-drop or
vacuum-label intuition does not provide the missing kernel.

1. Normalization and primary inputs
==================================
Use raw bracket generators
 rho(sigma_i)=A I+A^-1 e_i,  delta=-A^2-A^-2,
over Q(A), and write V_(n,h) for the block with h through strands:
  0<=h<=n, h=n mod2, shape[(n+h)/2,(n-h)/2].
The scalar block h=n is excluded from the assertions about nontrivial
matrix blocks. Work at n>=4. Projective statements ignore every scalar
framing convention.

Kuperberg Theorem1.1 gives projective complex Zariski closure PGL(V)
for each such Jones block at a non-root-of-unity parameter. The theorem
is stated after scalar normalization in SL. Taking a transcendental
specialization of A yields the same generic conclusion, or one may
state everything below at that specialization.
https://msp.org/gt/2011/15-1/gt-v15-n1-p02-s.pdf
Theorem1.1, printed p12; the block notation is X(n*1;h;t).

The finite-index pure braid subgroup has the same projective closure:
a connected irreducible algebraic group cannot be a finite union of
cosets of a proper closed subgroup.

Kasahara Proposition2.4 is the generic branching rule; Proposition2.6
states that the center-normalized representation factors through the
mapping class group of the sphere with n punctures precisely for
rectangular Young diagrams.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.0304 , pp3–4.

2. Ordinary disk point-pushing retains the full matrix closure
============================================================
Let F=ker(P_n -> P_(n-1)), deleting the last strand. This is a free
group of rank n-1, generated by the standard A_(i,n). It is normal in
P_n and contains A_(n-1,n)=sigma_(n-1)^2.

On every nontrivial TL block, both eigenvalues of sigma_(n-1) occur,
namely A and -A^-3. One justification is irreducibility: if this
generator were scalar, every conjugate generator would be the same
scalar and the whole representation would be one-dimensional.
Hence sigma_(n-1)^2 has distinct generic eigenvalues A^2,A^-6.
Its projective image is nonidentity.

Let H be the projective Zariski closure of rho(F). Normality of F
implies H is normalized by rho(P_n), hence by its dense closure PGL(V).
Since PGL(V) is a connected simple adjoint algebraic group, a nontrivial
closed normal subgroup is all of PGL(V). Therefore
  closure_projective(rho(F)) = PGL(V).
The same conclusion holds for [F,F]: the commutator map on the dense
set rho(F) has closure generating the derived group of PGL(V), which
is PGL(V) itself.

Consequences: restricting to the exponent-zero point-pushing sector
does not force invariant subspaces, simultaneous triangularization,
a common nonscalar commutant, or confinement to a proper algebraic
matrix subgroup. A scalar-matrix commutator strategy involving ALL of F
cannot work. A specific pair with accidentally commuting images remains
possible; no claim here excludes that exact faithfulness problem.

Freeness/semidirect-product source:
D.D.Long, Constructing representations of braid groups, p223,
discussion of the pure braid local system and Lemma2.3.
https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~long/pubpdf/ConstructiongRepresentations1994.pdf
Also Cohen–Prassidis, Introduction:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0404278 .

3. Capped-boundary point-pushing: rectangular is the only escape
==============================================================
Cap the disk boundary by a once-marked disk. Projectively the center
already acts trivially, giving a representation of
 Mod(S^2,n+1;distinguished puncture).
Now forget that distinguished puncture. Let Q be the preimage in B_n
of its point-pushing kernel. It is normal in B_n.

Write D_m=Delta_m^2 and consider
  P=D_n D_(n-1)^(-1).
P belongs to Q: after the distinguished puncture is forgotten, the
curve enclosing the other n-1 punctures bounds a once-punctured disk
on its opposite side, so its twist becomes trivial; the disk boundary
twist also becomes trivial. This is the geometric spherical relation.

The raw full twist on V_(m,k) is
  A^(k(k+2)-3m) I.
For 0<h<n, the restriction V_(n,h) to B_(n-1) has BOTH channels h-1
and h+1. Branching therefore gives the eigenvalues of P:
  A^(2h-2)  on the h-1 channel,
  A^(-2h-6) on the h+1 channel.
Their ratio A^(4h+4) is nonconstant and never1 generically.

Thus Q has nontrivial projective image on every nonrectangular
nontrivial TL block. Applying the same closed-normal-subgroup argument
as in section2 shows that its projective closure is ALL PGL(V).
This is stronger than merely saying that the representation fails to
factor through the forgetful map.

When h=0, only the h+1=1 channel exists and P acts by A^-6 I.
Kasahara Proposition2.6 supplies the global statement: the entire
point-pushing subgroup is projectively invisible in precisely this
rectangular case. The scalar h=n representation is another trivial
exception and was excluded from the outset.

Checks against the previous explicit cases:
  B5 residual h1: eigenvalues1,A^-8;
  B6 middle h2: eigenvaluesA^2,A^-10;
  B6 rectangle h0: scalarA^-6.
This explains the earlier partial mechanism uniformly; it does not
create a complementary kernel for its missing block.

4. Free-group restriction is an exact reduction, not an easier theorem
=====================================================================
There is a useful elementary centralizer fact:
  C_(P_n)(F)=Z(P_n)=<D_n>,  n>=3.

Proof. Split P_n=F semidirect P_(n-1), where the action on F is Artin's
faithful action. If (u,b) centralizes F, Artin(b) is conjugation by u^-1.
Artin's action fixes the boundary word omega=x1...x_(n-1). Therefore
u centralizes omega. In a free group this centralizer is <omega>.
The only braid whose Artin action is conjugation by omega^k is the
corresponding power of D_(n-1), by Artin faithfulness and the standard
full-twist action. The central full twist D_n projects to D_(n-1).
After dividing by the matching D_n power, our element lies in F and
centralizes F; the center of the nonabelian free group F is trivial.
This proves the displayed equality, independently of any Jones claim.

For ANY representation rho:B_n->GL(V), put
 N=ker(rho) intersect ker(w).
Then
 N != {1}  iff  ker(rho restricted to F) != {1}.

Forward: take a nontrivial pure power k of an element of N.
If [k,F] were trivial, the centralizer fact would make k central,
contradicting w(k)=0. Some [k,f] is therefore nontrivial and lies in
N intersect F.
Reverse: a nontrivial x in F cannot be central in P_n, since
F intersect Z(P_n)={1}. Choose g in P_n with [x,g]!=1.
Then [x,g] lies in ker(rho) and has exponent sum0.

Hence finding a free point-pushing relation is a fully sufficient
kernel certificate, but existence of such a relation is EQUIVALENT
to the required noncentral block nonfaithfulness. Merely switching
from braids to free words has not reduced the unresolved hypothesis.

Cohen–Prassidis discuss a related associated-graded criterion, with
explicit caution about applying it to the open representation problems.
Their graded criterion should not be confused with a finite-order
Taylor test or an automatic injectivity/noninjectivity conclusion.

5. Small geometric subgroups and character-variety intuition
===========================================================
Every nontrivial two-row TL block contains the B3 [2,1] block on
restriction to a standard three-puncture disk: remove boxes down to
[2,1]. This generic B3 block is faithful (reduced Burau, with a
nontrivial full-twist scalar in the raw convention). Thus a kernel
cannot be supported in a geometrically three-strand subgroup or any
of its braid conjugates. This does not exclude two free generators
whose words collectively involve more punctures.

At a special parameter, or on classical characters after actually
forgetting a puncture, point-pushing may disappear. Neither property
means the GENERIC Jones block factors through that classical quotient.
The two eigenvalues above give an explicit test against such a
factorization. The categorical vacuum-label explanation works exactly
for h0; it does not apply to the nonzero boundary colors of the
missing blocks. Matrix dimension versus free-group rank is irrelevant:
free groups admit faithful low-dimensional linear representations.

6. Decision
===========
The tested mechanism does not supply a complementary generic kernel.
It yields a uniform obstruction: the missing blocks retain full
projective algebraic complexity on either relevant point-pushing group.
No new word scan or larger commutator iteration is justified by this
analysis.

The next meaningful gate remains an EXACT nontrivial free-word relation
in one missing block, with braid nontriviality independently certified,
or a genuinely predictive single-pair centralizer mismatch. Once found,
Long+Ito closes the counterexample chain. No density theorem, lower
dimension, finite Taylor identity, or forgotten-puncture analogy
provides that relation. No novelty or proximity-to-solution claim.
