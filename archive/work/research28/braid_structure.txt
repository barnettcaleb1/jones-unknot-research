1 November 2026 UTC. No braid-word scan; no kernel or Jones-one knot found.

Complete counterexample endpoint
--------------------------------
Let rho be the generic five-dimensional [3,2] Jones block of B5. If a
nontrivial exponent-zero braid lies in ker(rho), its normal kernel has
nontrivial intersection with the already known generic Burau kernel by
Long's normal-intersection lemma. The scalar block is trivial on exponent
zero. This produces a nontrivial full generic TL5 kernel, and Ito supplies
nontrivial hyperbolic knots with Jones polynomial one.

This pass tests a concrete way of producing the first missing relation:
commutators, or differences, of full twists about three-puncture curves.
The outcome is a new restriction on that construction, not evidence that
the missing relation exists. The endpoint does not use all-colored unknot
detection.

Primary endpoint: Ito, A kernel of a braid group representation yields a
knot with trivial knot polynomial, arXiv:1402.2028.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.2028

1. Exact rank-one formula
-------------------------
Work over Q(A), with rho(sigma_i)=A I+A^-1 e_i and
delta=-A^2-A^-2. Let a be an essential curve enclosing exactly three of
the five punctures, and D_a its positive Dehn twist. There is a rank-one
idempotent P_a satisfying

  rho(D_a)=A^-6 I+(A^6-A^-6)P_a.                 (1)

For the standard curve a0 around punctures1,2,3, D_a0=(sigma1 sigma2)^3.
Restricting [3,2] from B5 to B3 gives [3] once and [2,1] twice. The full
twist has scalar A^6 on [3] and A^-6 on [2,1]. The projector is the
three-strand Jones--Wenzl projector

 P_a0 = I - delta/(delta^2-1)(e1+e2)
             + 1/(delta^2-1)(e1e2+e2e1).

It has no pole at A=1, since delta^2-1=3 there. For a=g(a0), set
P_a=rho(g)P_a0 rho(g)^-1. Both rho(g) and its inverse have Laurent
entries, so every P_a is regular at A=1. This definition is independent
of g by (1).

The branching and symmetric-group specialization are standard Hecke
facts, recorded explicitly in Kasahara, Remarks on the faithfulness of
the Jones representations, Propositions2.4 and2.5, printed p3.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.0304
Kasahara's earlier genus-two paper, equation(2.2), also exhibits a
separating twist as a scalar plus a rank-one matrix, after the different
genus-two normalization. We do NOT need to identify those parameters:
(1) is independently checked in the existing raw TL5 module.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0012216

2. Every classical overlap is nonzero
-------------------------------------
At A=1 the module is the ordinary S5 representation [3,2]. A concrete
model is the five-dimensional subspace of rational functions on the ten
edges of K5 satisfying zero sum at every vertex. S5 permutes vertices,
and the standard inner product is the sum over edges.

For a three-element subset S, define the edge function w_S by

  -1  on edges wholly inside S,
  +1  on edges with one endpoint in S,
  -3  on the single edge in its complement.

Its vertex sums vanish and ||w_S||^2=18. The S3 subgroup permuting S has
a one-dimensional invariant subspace in [3,2], spanned by w_S. Therefore
P_a(1), where a encloses S, is the orthogonal projector onto Q w_S.
Any pure-braid difference between choices of a disappears at A=1.

For two triples S,T, elementary edge counting gives

  <w_S,w_T> = 18 if S=T,
              -6 if |S intersect T|=2,
              +6 if |S intersect T|=1.

Consequently, for arbitrary geometric three-puncture curves a,b,

  tr(P_a(1) P_b(1)) = 1    if their enclosed subsets agree,
                      1/9  otherwise.                 (2)

This is not an assumption that a curve is determined by its enclosed
punctures. Different curves with the same subset remain a genuine and
unresolved case. The statement concerns only their specialized matrices.

3. The exclusion theorem
------------------------
THEOREM. For any a,b as above, the matrices rho(D_a),rho(D_b) commute
if and only if P_a=P_b. In particular, commuting matrices require a and b
to enclose the SAME subset of punctures. The conclusion remains true for
any two nonzero integer powers D_a^r,D_b^s.

Proof. Equation(1) and the nonzero generic eigenvalue difference reduce
commutation to P_a P_b=P_b P_a. Two commuting rank-one idempotents over
a field are either equal or have zero product: their product is an
idempotent whose image is contained in both one-dimensional images; if
nonzero, the images coincide, and commutation then gives equality.
The zero-product alternative is excluded by (2). Equality is impossible
for different subsets, again by (2), since its trace product would be1.
For a nonzero integer r,

 rho(D_a^r)=A^-6r I+(A^6r-A^-6r)P_a,

and the coefficient remains nonzero in Q(A). This proves the last claim.

Thus all pairs of three-puncture twists with different enclosed subsets,
with arbitrary geometric winding and arbitrary nonzero powers, are
excluded from this source of commuting-image/noncommuting-braid examples.
No unitarity assumption or root-of-unity inference is used.

The same-subset alternative is stronger than a commutator condition:
if distinct curves a,b have P_a=P_b, the actual braid D_a D_b^-1 is
already a nontrivial exponent-zero element of ker(rho). Distinct positive
Dehn twists are distinct mapping classes. Long/Ito then finish the full
counterexample route. But neither (1) nor (2) predicts such equality.

4. A second limitation: high powers do not produce a universal relation
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
For a fixed pair with different subsets there is an integer N0 such that
rho(D_a^N),rho(D_b^N) generate a free group for every N>=N0. This is a
statement about their matrices, not only about their braid elements.

Here is a proof without a word search. Choose a positive real A0 near1,
but different from1. All matrices and projectors are real and regular.
By (2), the scalar s=tr(P_a P_b) lies strictly between0 and1 for A0
sufficiently close to1. The two-dimensional space

  W=im(P_a)+im(P_b)

is invariant under both projectors. Its dimension is2, and neither
projector's image or kernel in W coincides with the other's: a coincidence
would force s=0 or1. On P(W), each D has exactly two distinct fixed points
and eigenvalue ratio A0^12. It is a real hyperbolic projective map. The
four fixed points are distinct. Choose disjoint small neighborhoods of
them. Sufficiently large positive and negative powers send complements
of repelling neighborhoods into attracting neighborhoods. The ordinary
ping-pong argument gives a free group. One such faithful specialization
excludes any generic word relation among these powered generators.

This is only eventual freeness for each fixed pair. It supplies neither
a universal N0 independent of the curves nor faithfulness of the original
unpowered pair. It does show why replacing a categorical polynomial
identity by a universal group-word identity cannot be automatic: the
actual low-rank matrix family already contains free powered pairs.
