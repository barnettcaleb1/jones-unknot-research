PASS 30: EXACT JONES FIBERS AND A PROJECTIVE-GRAPH SCAFFOLD AUDIT
Status: no fixed-Jones knot family with unbounded rational reduced Kh was certified. A new primary-source conditional scaffold has a valid zero-complexity endpoint, but its inequality is conjectural. One graph-induction step in the source fails; a contraction-based replacement is valid and has a clear topological limitation.

1. Bounded literature check

I searched specifically for exact Jones-preserving single crossing changes, Jones-invisible bands, and fixed-Jones families with unbounded Khovanov rank or quantum spread. The searches did not produce a certified knot example. This is a bounded search result, NOT a claim that no such example is known.

The precise published question at the band stage remains visible in Wang's own 2021 presentation: whether the Jones polynomial detects the trivial band in a band sum of a split two-component link. His paper proves homological band detection and shifting of its nonzero complement; it does not prove the complement's graded Euler characteristic is nonzero. The later split-link theorem proves that bounded ranks under band twisting force the marked smoothing components to be split. These are different statements; neither supplies the missing Euler nonvanishing.

Primary sources:
  https://people.math.harvard.edu/~jxwang/GaugeTheoryVirtualSlides.pdf , slide16
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01070 , introduction and Theorems1.7–1.8
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.09110 , Proposition1.7 and Remark1.8

Thus the exact concrete unresolved statement is: for every nontrivial band b joining a geometrically split two-component link, is V(K_b)-V(K_#) nonzero? A negative answer produces a whole Jones-constant knot twist family with unbounded quantum spread by Wang; it need not give unbounded rank. A Jones-invisible crossing whose oriented smoothing is nonsplit instead produces unbounded rank. These implications are already proved in the main report and were not recomputed here.

2. Why the explicit published link construction does not settle the knot test

Kauffman's Knot Diagrammatics §9.3 and his shorter Detecting Links With The Jones Polynomial construct two-component links with unlink Jones polynomial related by a single crossing change. They derive a complementary tangle S with scalar bracket [S]=kappa[infinity]. Source: https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~kauffman/MLinks.pdf , final three pages; equivalent discussion in https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=5c89143d6ad74d2ae5eb7157474ef2541357f512&repid=rep1&type=pdf , §9.3.

There is an exact component-count obstruction to treating this as an arc-only knot construction. Use the bracket normalization <unknot>=1, delta=-A²-A^-2. Since the changed links have two-component unlink Jones polynomial, |kappa(1)|=2. The numerator and denominator closures of S have brackets kappa and delta*kappa, respectively. Their absolute evaluations at A=1 are therefore 2 and4. The elementary identity |V_L(1)|=2^(number_of_components-1) implies that these closures have respectively two and three components.

Any four-ended tangle consists of two arcs and c closed components. Its two standard closures have component counts either (c+1,c+2), (c+2,c+1), or (c+1,c+1), according to endpoint connectivity. The pair (2,3) forces c=1 and infinity connectivity. Thus the scalar tangle in this particular link construction necessarily has a closed component. Deleting it or joining it to an arc changes the skein problem and is not licensed by the scalar-bracket identity. This explains the exact remaining geometric step without relying on visual interpretation of the source figure.

3. New primary-source scaffold: Boyle–Spyropoulos

Boyle–Spyropoulos, Myopic Tutte polynomials and Khovanov homology in RP³, arXiv2609.40122v1 (30 September2026), defines beta_epsilon(L)=min_D(c(D)-alpha_epsilon(D)); alpha counts the minimum checkerboard edges deleted to make the graph local. Conjecture1.1(2) asserts beta_epsilon(L)≤span J_epsilon, with the local-link case restricted to knots and epsilon=Re. Section1.1 explicitly gives the intended implication to ordinary Jones unknot detection. Theorem5.3 states a small-beta classification with a prime hypothesis. Its proof begins with a direct beta0 graph argument.

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40122v1 , §1.1, Definition1.6, Conjecture1.1(2), Theorem5.3 and its proof.

Independent endpoint audit: there is no essential prime/nonlocal gap at beta0. A connected checkerboard graph with two distinct vertices has a nonloop edge; that edge alone is local, so its maximum local edge set has size≥1. A local loop likewise gives a local edge set of size1. Consequently beta0 forces a one-vertex graph containing only projective loops. Their crossing-sign choices simplify to the Upsilon_n family. This argument does not use primeness or nonlocality. The source's zero-complexity family has Jones1 only for the ordinary unknot. Therefore the conjectural inequality really does give a complete conditional detection proof for local classical knots. It remains an unproved inequality, not a new proof of detection.

4. A precise induction issue and an audited repair

The source proof says that beta(G)=n implies the existence of an edge e with beta(G-e)=n-1. That assertion is false. Embed a triangle as an essential simple closed curve in RP². It has e=3, alpha=1, beta=2. Deleting any edge leaves a local path with two edges, alpha=0, beta=2. No edge deletion lowers beta. This identifies a gap in that stated induction, not a counterexample to its small-beta classifications or its direct beta0 endpoint.

The parent's replacement works by signed-graph contraction. Give every edge its projective holonomy eta_e in {+1,-1}; these are NOT the crossing signs. A subgraph is local exactly when all its cycles have trivial holonomy, equivalently it is balanced. Hence

  beta(G)=max_s #{uv in E(G): eta_uv s_u s_v=+1},

where s_v are vertex signs. A balanced subgraph extends to a switching on all vertices, so both directions of this equality hold.

For a maximizing switching, its satisfied spanning subgraph H must be connected when G is connected. If H had more than one component, flip all spins on one component. Internal statuses stay fixed and every cut edge becomes satisfied, contradicting maximality. Choose a nonloop edge e of H and gauge it positive. Spin assignments on G/e are exactly assignments on G with its endpoints equal; at least one global optimum has this property. Removing the always-satisfied e then gives

  beta(G/e)=beta(G)-1.

If only one vertex remains, deleting a positive/local loop also decreases beta by1. Iteration therefore reaches a one-vertex bouquet of negative/projective loops in exactly beta graph reductions. These are checkerboard smoothings. They are not isotopies of the original knot, so the reduction does not itself bound bridge number or homology.

5. What bounded beta does and does not currently control

Random vertex spins show

  beta(G)≥ number_of_local_loops + (number_of_nonloop_edges)/2.

Also beta(G)≥|V(G)|-1 since a spanning tree is local. Thus bounded beta bounds vertices and all edges except projective loops. Projective loop edges at distinct vertices cannot coexist disjointly in RP²: one-sided closed curves have mod-two intersection1. All unbounded projective loops therefore occur at one vertex. Their endpoint pairs interleave in the cyclic order; the loop-only cyclic word has the form 1,...,n,1,...,n. The bounded remaining edge darts cut this into boundedly many candidate parallel blocks.

This suggests a finite-twist-template lemma: simplify opposite crossing signs within each block and prove that each block lifts to an ordinary fixed-strand twist site in the double cover S³→RP³. If that lemma is proved with the necessary marked-embedding details, it may bound bridge number for local knots with bounded beta. I have NOT established that last geometric identification. No beta-to-bridge or beta-to-Kh theorem is claimed here. In particular, graph contraction/smoothing must not be substituted for knot-preserving simplification.

The firm new endpoint is the beta0 classification and its use in the paper's conditional proof. The genuinely missing theorem is the stated beta-versus-Jones inequality (or a constructive local-knot version sufficient for it); graph optimization alone does not compare beta with the cancellations of a signed Jones state sum.
