INDEPENDENT GEOMETRIC AUDIT: BOUNDED PROJECTIVE LOCAL COMPLEXITY
1 November 2026. Research deduction, not a novelty claim.

Scope. The parent supplies the signed-holonomy identity
 beta(G)=e(G)-alpha(G)=maximum number of balanced edges under vertex spins.
Here G is a connected graph embedded in RP2, and alpha is the minimum
number of edges to delete to make G local. This note audits the actual
medial-diagram geometry needed after that identity. No graph deletion
or contraction is mistaken for an isotopy of the link.

1. The bounded skeleton.

First separate the local-graph case. If the realized checkerboard graph
G itself is local, then alpha(G)=0 and e(G)=beta(G)<=b, so its diagram
is already bounded and belongs to a finite list. Its complementary
region may be a Mobius band: no cellular duality of the two checkerboard
graphs is asserted or used. In the remaining case G is connected and
nonlocal, hence cellular in RP2. The argument below applies to this
realized embedded graph alone and never requires its geometric dual.

Every spanning tree is balanced after a spin assignment, so v(G)<=b+1
when beta(G)<=b. Every local loop is balanced for every spin assignment;
every non-loop is balanced with probability 1/2 for independent uniform
spins. Projective loops are never balanced. Therefore the total number
of edges OTHER THAN projective loops is at most 2b. Their total number
of darts is at most 4b.

Any two projective loops based at different vertices would be disjoint
essential simple closed curves in RP2, impossible because their mod-2
intersection number is one. Consequently all projective loops, if any,
are based at a single vertex v. At that vertex, any pair has interleaving
darts: otherwise a small perturbation makes the curves disjoint.
Pairwise interleaving forces their cyclic dart subword to have the form
  1,2,...,n,1,2,...,n
up to relabeling, cyclic choice and reversal. Other darts are inserted
into this word. At most 4b inserted darts break it into at most 4b+1
linear blocks of projective loops consecutive in BOTH halves.
This bound is intentionally nonoptimal.

2. A consecutive block is an ACTUAL twist region.

Remove the interior of a small vertex disk about v. The remaining
surface RP2 minus that disk is a Mobius band. Each projective loop
becomes a properly embedded essential arc in this Mobius band; cutting
along any such arc produces a disk. Pairwise disjoint essential proper
arcs in a Mobius band are parallel spanning arcs. Consecutive arcs
cobound a rectangle, with the other two sides on the vertex-disk boundary.

For successive loops in one block, both of these boundary intervals
contain no other graph darts. The rectangle therefore contains no
other graph edges: any graph inside it would have to attach across
its boundary, which has neither an available vertex sector nor a
crossing of graph edges. A component wholly inside would violate
connectedness of G. Thus the corresponding two medial crossings have
an empty bigon between them.

A block of r loops gives a chain of r crossings with r-1 successive
empty bigons. A regular neighborhood of this open chain is an embedded
disk in RP2. The link projection inside it is a two-strand braid with
r crossings. Assigning either over/under choice at a crossing assigns
one of the two braid generators. Adjacent inverse generators cancel
by a genuine Reidemeister-II isotopy in this disk. The entire block
is consequently an integer-twist box. The integer is the signed
exponent in this local braid convention; no identification with a
particular global oriented crossing-sign convention is required.

The disk thickens to a 3-ball in the twisted interval bundle over RP2:
local orientability is enough. All cancellations and twist replacements
occur in that ball and preserve the represented RP3 link.

Cyclic exception. If there are no other darts, the projective loops
can form a cyclic chain. Choose one adjacency at which to open the
chain and retain the other r-1 bigons. Their neighborhood is still a
disk twist box, with a fixed exterior closure. For r=1, use a small
crossing disk; for r=0, there is no box. Thus the beta=0 family creates
no unbounded number of boxes.

3. Finitely many templates, then a classical bridge bound.

Record each block by one twist-box placeholder, leaving the actual
integer exponent free. The remaining graph has at most 2b ordinary
edges and at most 4b+1 block representatives, hence at most 6b+1 edges.
The vertex number, cyclic orders, edge-twist data of its surface ribbon
neighborhood, crossing choices outside boxes, and box markings all
have finitely many possibilities. For connected graphs in RP2 these
ribbon data plus the complementary-surface data determine finitely
many ambient embedding types: the nonlocal case is cellular; the local
case has one projective complementary component and disks. There are
no arbitrary mapping-class parameters left on RP2.

Therefore beta(G)<=b gives finitely many ACTUAL RP3 twist templates.
This records a bigon-chain isotopy; it is not a deletion-induction claim.
In particular it does not use the contested sentence in the source
asserting beta(G-e)=beta(G)-1 for some edge.

Under the unbranched double cover S3 -> RP3, each twist 3-ball has two
disjoint ball lifts. Thus every RP3 template lifts to a fixed S3 template
with twice as many variable two-strand braid boxes. Each such fixed
S3 template has a bridge presentation with a number of maxima bounded
independently of its integer twist exponents: put every braid box
monotonically in a local height coordinate and keep the fixed exterior
arcs fixed. Take the maximum over the finite list of templates.

For a LOCAL classical knot K embedded in a ball of RP3, the lifted link
is a split union of two copies of K. Restricting any bounded-bridge
presentation of this lifted link to either component bounds b(K).
No split-link bridge additivity formula is needed. Hence there is a
finite function B(b) with
  beta_Re(K)<=b  =>  bridge(K)<=B(b).

4. Full-proof relevance and remaining gate.

A uniform beta bound on ONE specified nonempty ordinary-Jones fiber
would now give a uniform bridge bound on that fiber. If V_H=1 for a
nontrivial H, taking J#H^{#r} for an anchor J in that fiber contradicts
Schubert's connected-sum bridge formula as r grows. Thus such a beta
bound would imply Jones unknot detection. The geometry above supplies
the beta-to-bridge implication; it does not supply any Jones-to-beta
bound. The latter remains the exact unproved global premise.
