PROJECTIVE SPANNING-TREE CANCELLATION: MECHANISM AND OBSTRUCTIONS
1 November 2026. Bounded research note; no Jones-unknot theorem claimed.

Primary source: Boyle--Spyropoulos, Myopic Tutte polynomials and Khovanov
homology in RP3, arXiv:2609.40122v1, Sections 1.1, 4, 5.2 and 6.
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40122v1

1. What would complete the proposed route.

The proposed inequality beta_Re(K) <= span V_K, with beta minimized over
all RP2 diagrams of the classical local knot K, would imply detection:
V_K=1 forces beta_Re(K)=0, and the zero-complexity bouquet classification
has only the ordinary unknot with this Jones polynomial. Prior work also
gives a bridge bound from bounded beta, so even a uniform beta bound on
one complete Jones fiber would suffice by connected-sum amplification.
Neither chain turns a skein smoothing into a knot-preserving isotopy.

IMPORTANT UPDATE DURING THIS PASS: the independent lift/shading audit in
projective_local_crossing_audit.txt proves beta_Re(K)=c(K) for classical
local knots. Consequently the proposed span inequality fails on T(3,4).
The discussion below remains useful to distinguish the algebraic and
geometric mechanisms, but should NOT be presented as a still-supported
route through that specific span inequality. This does not settle the
Jones unknot-detection problem, or refute a generic whole-fiber crossing
bound merely by refuting the particular numerical span bound.

2. Exact tree cancellation is not a diagram simplification.

The source's signed spanning-tree weights change under swapping the
ordering of two oppositely signed edges. In some cases paired weights
cancel; in others both weights remain unchanged (Lemma 4.3). This proves
order independence of the polynomial. It supplies neither an isotopy nor
a disk supporting a Reidemeister move. Similarly, Gaussian elimination
in the tree chain complex need not come from a geometric simplification.

Care with normalization: for a local diagram the two separate graph
sums each equal the bracket, so their sum is twice the bracket. For a
nonlocal diagram their sum equals the bracket (Proposition 4.4). Mixing
these conventions can manufacture a false cancellation argument.

The two kinds of edge signs have different jobs. Tait crossing signs
record over/under data and enter tree weights. Holonomy signs record the
orientation double cover of RP2 and enter the balanced-subgraph formula
for beta. They must not be identified.

3. A precise positivity normal form is too restrictive.

Let G be the selected Tait graph. Give each edge weight 1 or 0 according
to its Tait crossing sign. Then the positive-edge count p(T) is constant
over all spanning trees exactly when this edge weight is constant on
every connected component of the cycle matroid of G.

Proof. If a matroid component is monochromatic, every basis takes its
fixed rank worth of edges, so its contribution is constant. Conversely,
equal basis weights imply that every allowed one-element basis exchange
swaps equally weighted elements. Any two elements of one circuit can
be exchanged in a suitable basis: extend the circuit minus one element
to a basis. Circuit connectivity then forces constant weights in each
matroid component. Loops and coloops are singleton components and impose
no condition. This proves both directions.

The source's tree grading v is the number of one chosen crossing sign
in the tree. Its differential changes v by one, and constant v places
the sector on one reduced-Khovanov diagonal. Therefore the normal form
"all nonloop edges have one crossing sign; arbitrary signs only on
projective loops" forces that sector to be thin. More generally the
matroid condition above has the same consequence. For a classical local
knot the surviving sector is its ordinary reduced homology.

This cannot be universal: nonalternating torus knots, including T(3,4),
are Khovanov-thick. Stošić proves the thickness statement in Khovanov
homology of links and graphs, arXiv:math/0605579, and the thesis also
discusses the torus-knot stability mechanism.
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0605579

Thus a valid global mechanism must accommodate genuine variation of
tree gradings and genuine cancellation; moving all difficult crossing
signs into projective loops is not a general solution.

4. A monotone classical cancellation-to-move rule is also false.

Every classical shadow admits a descending crossing assignment giving
the unknot. Its local Tait graph has alpha=0 and beta=c(D), regardless
of those crossing choices. Hence fixed-diagram holonomy optimization
cannot by itself bound beta(D) by Jones span.

More strongly, a rule that always simplified an unknot through classical
diagrams without increasing beta would be a crossing-monotone unknotting
rule, because beta=c on those diagrams. Certified hard-unknot diagrams
need at least three extra crossings in any such Reidemeister sequence:
Burton et al., Hard Diagrams of the Unknot, arXiv:2104.14076.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14076

This statement is deliberately limited to classical diagram sequences.
It does not itself rule out excursions through nonlocal RP2 diagrams.
The separate lift/shading theorem now gives the stronger invariant-level
obstruction for the specific proposed numerical beta bound.

5. Strategic conclusion.

The tree expansion supplies exact cancellations and a clean thinness
criterion, but no general cancellation-to-isotopy theorem. The new
local-knot identity beta_Re=c removes the hoped-for escape of lowering
this particular complexity below classical crossing number. A weaker
whole-Jones-fiber complexity theorem remains logically sufficient, but
no new geometric bridge to it was obtained in this bounded pass.
