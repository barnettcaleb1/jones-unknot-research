1 November 2026. Scope: mathematical audit, not a priority claim.

Verdict: I found no mathematical gap in the proposed proof, with the
local-diagram convention stated as below. The argument establishes
beta_Re(K) >= c_S3(K_tilde) for a nullhomologous projective KNOT and

1. Integral index and deck action.

For an oriented immersed circle Gamma_0 on the oriented sphere, the
integer region index I exists because its integral 1-cycle bounds.
The additive constant cancels in J=I-I composed with tau. Since the
antipodal map reverses the sphere orientation and carries the lifted
orientation of Gamma_0 to that of Gamma_1, -I composed with tau has
exactly the correct jumps across Gamma_1. Thus J is the index of the
TOTAL oriented lifted curve and is antisymmetric. No choice of a
preferred outside region, and no splitness of the lifted link, is
needed. Changing the orientation or exchanging lift components changes
J by sign and leaves its parity shading unchanged.

An odd face cannot have a connected, deck-invariant lift: J would be
constant on such a face and equal to its negative, forcing J=0. Thus
f=I mod2 genuinely chooses one of the TWO lifts of each odd face. It
is not a merely local or inconsistently chosen sheet assignment.

At a self crossing a small path between opposite black sectors meets
Gamma_0 either twice or zero times, giving even index change. At a
mixed crossing it meets Gamma_0 once. This proves the claimed edge
holonomies simultaneously for the whole graph. Deleting the mixed
edges therefore leaves a balanced graph and hence a local graph in
RP2. This supplies the needed lower bound beta(G_odd)>=s(D); it does
not require equality with the maximum balanced-edge count.

Deleting Gamma_1 from the genuine lifted S2 diagram leaves a diagram
of Gamma_0 with exactly s(D) crossings. Hence s(D)>=c(Gamma_0).
Deck reversal of both surface orientation and interval height preserves
ambient orientation and crossing signs; it does not convert a self
crossing into a mixed crossing or change this counting argument.

2. The vulnerable Seifert-state step survives the audit.

The oriented crossing arcs and their oriented smoothing have the same
boundary in a crossing disk, so their difference bounds an integral
2-chain supported in that disk. Choose the correction in one lifted
disk and its negative pullback in the other. This produces an index
J' which agrees with J outside the crossing disks and remains exactly
antisymmetric, not just antisymmetric modulo2.

The local index test can also be seen without a diagram convention:
for one oriented transverse crossing the four sector indices, cyclically,
are k,k+1,k+2,k+1 (or the reverse). Seifert smoothing joins the two
equal-index opposite sectors, while the sectors differing by2 remain
separate. Therefore the continued odd region is precisely the black
Seifert-state neighborhood N used in the Euler-characteristic argument.

Every smoothed circle in RP2 is contractible. Two essential simple
circles cannot be disjoint, and one essential circle would contradict
the preserved zero mod2 class of the whole knot. Their complement has
one and only one component containing the crosscap. Its lift is connected
and invariant under tau, and J' is constant on it. Antisymmetry therefore
forces J'=0 there. Consequently N has no crosscap. This argument does
not assume that a Seifert surface for the original knot is local, and
it does not use smoothing as an isotopy of the knot.

For a connected NONLOCAL shadow, the embedded shadow and its realized
Tait graphs are cellular. Thus the black faces give vertex disks and
N has the stated disk-and-band Euler characteristic. There is no hidden
use of disk faces for a local shadow with a Mobius outside region.

3. Independent grading arithmetic.

Table 2 gives the following A exponents modulo4:
  positive internal edge: +1; positive external edge: -1;
  negative internal edge: -1; negative external edge: +1.
For a spanning tree with v-1 internal edges, their sum is
  2 sum_internal epsilon_e - sum_all epsilon_e
    = 2(v-1)-sum_all epsilon_e mod4.
Writhe normalization subtracts 3w. Thus
  r=2(v-1)-sum epsilon_e-3w mod4.
This is even. If m edges have epsilon_e different from oriented
crossing sign s_e, the contribution (epsilon_e+3s_e)/2 is even
for an agreeing edge and odd for a disagreeing edge. Therefore
  r/2 = v-1+m mod2.

In these conventions the black band is present in the Seifert state
exactly when epsilon_e=s_e, giving chi(N)=v-c+m. This is also the
standard contraction/A-smoothing convention encoded by Table 2;
changing the shading changes BOTH the edge signs and the neighborhood.
Oriented smoothing toggles the parity of the component count at each
crossing, so for a knot the number q of Seifert circles satisfies
  q=c+1 mod2.
Consequently
  r/2 = chi(N)+q mod2.
A planar component contributes chi+b=2 modulo2, and the one possible
nonorientable component contributes chi+b=1 modulo2. Since N has no
crosscap, r=0 mod4. With t=A^(-4), this is exactly the integral sector.
This check proves G_odd=G_Re and is not a choice of whichever shading
happens to give a favorable bound. At a crossing change both epsilon
and the oriented crossing sign reverse, leaving this sector unchanged.

4. Local diagrams and scope of the minimum.

For a disk diagram use the canonical odd shading, whose graph is local:
the index is zero in the outside crosscap region and odd regions are
inside the local disk. Thus alpha=0 and beta=c(D). Proposition4.4
identifies the local graph expansion with the ordinary bracket. The
proof need not assert cellular duality or a disk vertex neighborhood
for the Mobius outside face of the other shading. This formulation
avoids the only convention-sensitive shorthand in the earlier note.

Taking a minimal classical disk diagram gives the upper bound. Every
nonlocal diagram gives the lower bound by sections1--3. Small values
of min(beta_Re,beta_Im) in source projective families do not refute this:
the theorem singles out Re, and it is possible for only Im to be small.

5. Explicit contradiction and independent polynomial scope.

The standard torus formulas for T(3,4) give
  V(t)=t^3+t^5-t^8, span(V)=5,
  Delta(t)=t^3-t^2+1-t^(-2)+t^(-3), span(Delta)=6.
These formulas also follow by simplifying the torus Jones and Alexander
rational expressions; no table identification is needed. The elementary
inequality span(Delta)<=2g(K)<=c(K) already gives c(K)>=6. Hence
  span(V)=5 < beta_Re(K)=c(K).
I did not independently rerun the parent's separate 256-state braid
certificate; the parent reports it also verifies these polynomials with
unknot and trefoil convention tests. Mirroring changes neither span.

This audit supports the stated theorem and the failure of the cited
span conjecture. It does not turn the unit Jones fiber into a bounded
crossing family; that remains exactly the missing global premise.
