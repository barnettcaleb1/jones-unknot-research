PROJECTIVE LOCAL CROSSING NUMBER OF A LOCAL KNOT
1 November 2026. Research deduction with proof; no priority claim.

THEOREM.
Let K be a nullhomologous knot in RP3, and let K_tilde be either
component of its inverse image under S3 -> RP3. In the conventions of
Boyle--Spyropoulos, arXiv:2609.40122v1,
                       beta_Re(K) >= c_S3(K_tilde).
In particular, if K is a classical knot placed in a ball in RP3, then
                       beta_Re(K) = c_S3(K).

The general inequality does not assume that the lifted two-component
link is split. Locality is needed only to identify K_tilde with the
given classical K and to obtain the reverse inequality from a disk
diagram. No conclusion about beta_Im is claimed.

INPUTS AND NOTATION.
The source defines alpha(G) to be the minimum number of edges whose
deletion makes the embedded checkerboard graph G local in RP2, and
beta(G)=e(G)-alpha(G). beta_Re(K) minimizes beta(G_Re(D)) over diagrams.
For a connected nonlocal diagram, the two realized checkerboard graphs
are cellular. Their spanning-tree bracket expansions have distinct
normalized A-exponent residues0 and2 modulo4; under t=A^(-4), these
are respectively the integral and half-integral Jones sectors.

We use the previously audited elementary holonomy fact: choosing one
lift of each vertex of G to S2 switches its Z2 edge-holonomy signs;
the edges of positive sign form a local subgraph. Thus beta(G) is at
least the number of positive edges in any such assignment. Equivalently,
beta(G) is the maximum such number. Only this lower-bound direction
is needed here.

Here is the elementary topological justification. Any cycle consisting
of positive edges lifts closed, so its image in pi_1(RP2)=Z/2 is trivial.
The inclusion of each connected component of that subgraph therefore
has trivial fundamental-group image. Its regular neighborhood has no
crosscap and is planar. The disjoint neighborhoods leave the unique
crosscap in a complementary component; choose an essential simple
closed curve there. A sufficiently small Mobius neighborhood of that
curve is disjoint from the subgraph. Its complement is a disk containing
the entire subgraph. This proves the lower-bound direction directly,
also when the subgraph is disconnected.

PROOF.

1. Lift the diagram and count self crossings.

Let D be any generic RP2 diagram of K. Pulling its twisted interval
bundle back to S2 gives S2 x I, which embeds in S3 as the complement
of two open balls. Thus the lifted diagram is a genuine S3 diagram
of two components A and tau A; tau is the antipodal map on S2 and
reverses the interval coordinate. Orient both components by lifting
an orientation of K.

Each crossing of D lifts either to one self crossing on each component,
or to two mixed crossings between the components. Write s(D) for the
number of crossings of the first kind. Deleting tau A from the lifted
diagram produces an ordinary diagram of K_tilde with s(D) crossings.
Therefore
                         s(D) >= c_S3(K_tilde).              (1)

2. Construct the canonical shading and its sheet choices.

An oriented immersed closed curve A on S2 has an integer region-index
function I, unique up to a constant, whose jumps across A are the
oriented intersection signs. Since tau reverses the orientation of
S2 and carries the oriented A to the oriented tau A, the function
-I composed with tau is an index function for tau A. Set
                         J = I - I composed with tau.
Then J is an index function for A union tau A and J(tau x)=-J(x).
It is independent of the additive constant chosen for I.

Consequently J mod2 descends to a checkerboard coloring of D. Choose
the odd color as black, and call its checkerboard graph G_odd. With
f=I mod2, the two lifts of every black face satisfy
                         f(x)+f(tau x)=1.
The value of f therefore selects one lift of every black vertex.

At a self crossing, a small path between the opposite black sectors
crosses either two arcs of A or no arcs of A, so f has the same value
on the two sectors. At a mixed crossing it crosses one arc of A,
and f changes value. Thus the above sheet choices make every self
edge positive and every mixed edge negative. The holonomy fact gives
                         beta(G_odd) >= s(D).                (2)

3. Identify G_odd as the integral Jones sector.

This is essential: choosing whichever shading is convenient would not
justify a statement about beta_Re, whose minimum uses a fixed sector.

Perform the oriented Seifert smoothing of D, and perform both lifted
copies of each local smoothing simultaneously. Integral index functions
are preserved outside the smoothing disks: the original oriented arcs
and their smoothed replacement have the same boundary, and their
difference is the boundary of an integral 2-chain in the disk. Modify
J by that chain inside each disk, using the negative pullback change
in the antipodal disk. The resulting smoothed index J' still satisfies
J' composed with tau=-J', agrees with J away from the disks, and its
parity extends the original black/white shading across the smoothing.
Equivalently, at an oriented crossing the two sectors joined by the
Seifert smoothing have equal integer indices; sectors differing by2
are not joined. This local index rule identifies the continued odd
region with the black state neighborhood used in the grading check.

Every Seifert circle downstairs is contractible. Indeed, disjoint
essential simple closed curves in RP2 cannot occur twice, whereas
one essential circle would contradict the zero total mod2 homology
class preserved by the smoothing. The complementary regions of these
contractible circles have exactly one nonlocal component. Its lift is
connected and tau-invariant. Since J' is constant on that lifted
region and antisymmetric, its value there is zero. In particular the
nonlocal component is white. The black side N of the Seifert state
is therefore local, with no crosscap.

For completeness, the precise grading calculation follows. Let v be
the number of vertices of G_odd, c the crossing number of D, epsilon_e
the Tait sign of an edge, and w the oriented writhe. A spanning-tree
term has normalized A exponent modulo4
                    r_G = 2(v-1)-sum_e epsilon_e-3w.         (3)
An internal positive edge contributes exponent1 mod4, an external
positive edge contributes -1, and a negative edge reverses these
contributions. This proves (3) directly from the source tree weights.

Let m count the crossings where epsilon_e differs from the oriented
crossing sign. Equation (3) gives
                         r_G/2 = v-1+m mod2.                (4)
The black-state neighborhood for the oriented Seifert smoothing has
the v vertex disks and the c-m bands where these two signs agree.
Thus chi(N)=v-c+m. If q is the number of Seifert circles, each oriented
smoothing toggles component-count parity, so q=c+1 mod2, since K has
one component. Hence (4) becomes
                         r_G/2 = chi(N)+q mod2.             (5)

For a subsurface N of RP2, chi(N)+number_of_boundary_components is
even precisely when N is local: each orientable component is planar,
and a nonlocal component contributes the unique crosscap. Since N is
local, (5) gives r_G=0 mod4. Therefore the G_odd spanning-tree sum has
integral exponents after t=A^(-4), and G_odd is exactly G_Re.

4. Complete the inequality and the local equality.

For every connected nonlocal diagram, (1)--(2) and the sector
identification yield beta_Re(D)>=c_S3(K_tilde). If D itself is local,
use its canonical odd checkerboard graph. It lies in the disk and has
alpha=0, so its beta is its crossing count and the same inequality is
immediate. This agrees with the source's ordinary planar graph convention
and its local-graph expansion in Proposition4.4(i). The local
case may have a Mobius complementary region; no cellular duality of
its two graphs is assumed or needed. Minimizing proves the general
inequality. For a local classical K, a crossing-minimal disk diagram
also gives beta_Re(K)<=c_S3(K), proving equality. QED.

CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PROPOSED GLOBAL ROUTE.
The local-knot specialization of Boyle--Spyropoulos Conjecture1.1(2)
would say span(V_K)>=beta_Re(K)=c_S3(K). This is false. A countertest
does not require a crossing-number table: for the positive T(3,4),
independent exact calculations on the braid (sigma_1 sigma_2)^4 give
                 V(t)=t^3+t^5-t^8,       span(V)=5,
                 span(Delta)=6.
The elementary Seifert bound span(Delta)<=2g<=c gives c>=6, already
enough to contradict span(V)>=c. Mirroring reverses exponents but
does not change either span.

Thus bounded beta_Re on local knots is equivalent to bounded classical
crossing number. It implies finitely many local knot types. In particular
the earlier weaker beta-to-bridge theorem is valid but is not the sharp
description of the local case. A bound on beta_Re in a specified Jones
fiber is precisely a crossing-complexity bound on that fiber; the broad
all-fiber formulation cannot hold in the presence of known infinite
fixed-Jones knot families. None of these conclusions solves Jones
unknot detection or supplies a bound on the unit fiber.
