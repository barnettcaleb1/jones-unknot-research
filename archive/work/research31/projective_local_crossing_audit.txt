INDEPENDENT SECTOR AND LOCAL-CROSSING AUDIT
1 November 2026. Joint construction by parent and tangle agent;
independent algebra/topology audit recorded by homology agent.

Claim under the definitions of Boyle--Spyropoulos 2609.40122v1:
for every classical knot K embedded locally in RP3,
                     beta_Re(K) = c(K).

This note supplies the sector-identification step and checks the entire
inequality. It uses the prior balanced-subgraph identity
 beta(G)=max over vertex lift choices of the number of edges preserving
         those choices in the orientation double cover S2 -> RP2.

1. Canonical coloring and balanced self-crossing edges.

Let D be any generic RP2 diagram of K. Its lifted diagram in S2 consists
of two immersed circles A and B=tau(A), where tau is antipodal. These
are projections of the two lifted copies of K. Orient B by the deck
pushforward of the orientation of A. Choose an integer winding function
I on S2 minus A. Since tau reverses the orientation of S2,
                  J = I - I composed with tau
is an integer index function for the oriented union A+B and satisfies
J composed with tau = -J. Its parity h is tau-invariant and gives a
canonical checkerboard coloring downstairs. Changing I by a constant
does not change J. Call the h=1 regions black.

Write f=I mod2. On a black face, f(tau x)=1-f(x), so selecting the lift
where f=1 chooses one lift of every black vertex. Across a crossing
whose two strands belong to A (or both to B), opposite black sectors
have the same f value. Such a Tait edge therefore preserves the chosen
lifts. Across an A--B crossing the f value changes. Consequently every
self-crossing of the lifted component A supplies a balanced edge of
the black Tait graph G. Hence
                  beta(G) >= c_self(D).

After deleting B, A is a genuine spherical diagram of the lifted knot K,
with exactly c_self(D) crossings. The cover is the usual S3 cover of
RP3; its complement of the two projection poles is S2 times an interval.
Thus
                  c_self(D) >= c(K).

2. Exact parity of the tree sector.

Let epsilon_e=+/-1 be the Tait CROSSING sign in G (not a holonomy sign),
and let w be the oriented diagram writhe. Table 2 of the source shows
that an internal tree edge contributes exponent epsilon_e modulo4 and
an external edge contributes -epsilon_e modulo4. Since every tree has
v(G)-1 edges, every normalized tree monomial has A-exponent residue
       r_G = 2(v(G)-1) - sum_e epsilon_e - 3w        (mod4).
The ordinary integral Jones sector is r_G=0; the half-integral sector
is r_G=2. This also follows from Proposition6.8. Switching a crossing
does not change r_G, since the resulting change is +/-2 +/-6, a multiple
of4. Thus the sector labeling is a shadow property.

Here is a direct identification for the canonical black shading above.
Perform the oriented Seifert smoothing at every crossing. The resulting
circles in RP2 are all contractible: their total mod2 class is zero,
and pairwise disjoint essential circles in RP2 can number at most one.
Let s be their number and c the number of original crossings. Smoothing
one crossing changes component parity, so
                         s = c+1 (mod2).

Let N be the neighborhood of the black state subgraph. A local crossing
check shows that this subgraph includes precisely the edges for which
epsilon_e equals the oriented crossing sign. This convention can also
be checked on the two Reidemeister-I configurations: a positive Tait
bridge is a negative oriented curl, whereas a positive local Tait loop
is a positive oriented curl. If m counts unequal signs, the state graph
has c-m edges, and therefore
                         chi(N)=v(G)-c+m.
Meanwhile the exponent formula above gives
 r_G/2 = v(G)-1+m = chi(N)+c-1 = chi(N)+s             (mod2).

The boundary of N is exactly the s state circles. For a subsurface of
RP2, chi(N)+s modulo2 records whether N contains the unique crosscap:
every orientable component contributes an even number; a nonorientable
component contributes one.

Finally, the oriented smoothing can be performed equivariantly in the
lift, changing the index function J only in the crossing disks. The
smoothed J remains antisymmetric and agrees outside those disks with
the old index. The unique nonlocal complementary region downstairs has
a connected, tau-invariant lift. J is constant on that lift and equals
its own negative, hence J=0 there. Therefore the canonical odd (black)
side of the state contains no crosscap. N is orientable and r_G=0.

This proves that G is the Re graph, closing the crucial labeling gap.
For an already local diagram the usual local graph convention gives
the same conclusion directly; neither cellular-dual arguments nor an
ambiguous assignment of the zero Jones sector are needed in that case.

3. The minimized identity and an explicit numerical contradiction.

For every RP2 diagram of the local knot,
 beta_Re(D) >= c_self(D) >= c(K).
Taking the minimum proves beta_Re(K)>=c(K). Conversely, a classical
crossing-minimal disk diagram has alpha_Re=0 and beta_Re=c(K). Equality
follows.

The torus knot T(3,4) has
 V(t)=t^3+t^5-t^8,                 span V=5,
 Delta(t)=t^3-t^2+1-t^(-2)+t^(-3), span Delta=6.
The usual torus formulas give these expressions directly. The general
Seifert/Alexander inequality span Delta <= 2g(K) <= c(D) already yields
c(T(3,4))>=6, so no crossing-number table is required to contradict
beta_Re <= span V. The known exact crossing number8 is stronger but
unnecessary.

Scope. This refutes that proposed numerical span inequality for local
knots, subject to the stated source definitions. It does not refute
Jones unknot detection. It also does not refute an unspecified bound
on crossing number in ONE suitably chosen complete exact-Jones fiber.
It does rule out such a bound for EVERY fiber: Watson's Theorem5.1
constructs an infinite family of distinct knots with identical Khovanov
homology, hence identical Jones polynomial. Only finitely many knots
have bounded ordinary crossing number, so their beta_Re values are
unbounded. This is compatible with bounded bridge number or homology
in that family; the implication beta-bounded => bridge-bounded cannot
be reversed. Primary source:
https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~liam/files/identical.pdf

Question5.4(1)
specialized to a local summand would predict the equality above; it is
posed as a question, not as a theorem jointly asserted with the span
conjecture.
