LOCALITY AND PROJECTIVE CROSSING COMPLEXITY
1 November 2026. Research deduction; no priority claim.

Main result, with independently audited parity step:
For a classical knot K regarded as a local knot in RP3,
                    beta_Re(K) = c_S3(K).
Consequently bounded beta_Re gives bounded classical crossing number
and finitely many classical knot types, not merely bounded bridge number.
This is a statement about local knots. It does not identify beta_Im,
and it is not proved here for links with several components.

Definitions and source conventions are those of Boyle--Spyropoulos,
Myopic Tutte polynomials and Khovanov homology in RP3,
arXiv:2609.40122v1, Definition1.6 and Proposition4.4.
https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40122v1
For an embedded checkerboard graph G, alpha(G) is the minimum number
of edges whose deletion makes G local, and beta(G)=e(G)-alpha(G).
The prior signed-holonomy lemma identifies beta(G) with the maximum
number of edges made positive by choosing lifts of its vertices to S2.

1. The lift and the self-crossing lower bound.

Take any generic RP2 diagram D of local K. Its lift to S2 is a diagram
of K0 disjoint union K1, two copies of K exchanged by the antipodal
deck map tau. It is a genuine S3 link diagram: the lifted interval
bundle is S2 x I, embedded between two balls in S3. Locality implies
the two copies are split, although splitness is not needed for the
following crossing-count argument.

Classify a crossing of D as self if its two lifts are self-crossings
of K0 and K1, respectively, and mixed if both lifts are crossings
between K0 and K1. If s(D) is the number of self crossings, deleting
K1 from the lifted diagram leaves an S3 diagram of K0 with exactly
s(D) crossings. Thus s(D) >= c_S3(K).

2. A canonical shading makes all self edges positive.

Orient K, and orient its two lifts compatibly with the covering.
Let I be an integer winding-index function of the oriented immersed
curve K0 on S2. Such a function exists and is unique up to a constant.
Because tau reverses the orientation of S2 while preserving the
orientations of the lifted knot curves, -I composed with tau is an
index function for K1. Therefore
                       J = I - I composed with tau
is an integer index function for K0 union K1 and satisfies
                       J composed with tau = -J.
Its parity descends to a checkerboard coloring of D in RP2.
Call the color J odd black, and let G_odd be its checkerboard graph.

Write f=I mod2. On the black faces,
                       f + f composed with tau = 1.
Thus f chooses one lift of every black face/vertex. Across opposite
black sectors at a self crossing, f changes by zero: a small path
crosses either two arcs of K0 or no arcs of K0. At a mixed crossing
it changes by one, since it crosses exactly one arc of K0.
Consequently the holonomy switching associated to these vertex-lift
choices makes every self edge positive and every mixed edge negative.
The positive self edges therefore form a local subgraph. In particular
                       beta(G_odd) >= s(D).

The possibility that one black face meets both lift components does
not obstruct the argument: f is constant on the entire lifted face,
and its opposite value on the other lift is precisely the needed
single vertex-sheet choice.

3. Crucial sector identification: G_odd is G_Re.

This step is needed; an unspecified favorable shading would not imply
a bound for beta_Re, since the minima for the two sectors are separate.

Perform oriented Seifert smoothing at every crossing of D, and do so
equivariantly in its S2 lift. The local replacement of two oriented
crossing arcs by the oriented smoothing preserves their integral
boundary outside the small crossing disk. Their difference bounds
an integral 2-chain in that disk. Thus the winding-index function
extends across each smoothing, agrees outside the disks, and can be
chosen equivariantly to remain antisymmetric under tau. Its parity
extends the black/white shading to the smoothed diagram.

All Seifert circles downstairs are contractible in RP2: there cannot
be two disjoint essential simple curves, and exactly one essential
circle would contradict the zero mod2 homology of the original knot.
The complement of these circles has exactly one nonlocal region.
Its lift is connected and tau-invariant. The smoothed integer index
is constant on that lifted region and antisymmetric, hence is zero
there. Therefore the odd-colored side N of the Seifert state is local:
it does not contain the unique nonlocal complementary region.

Here is the exact grading check translating that fact to the Re label.
Let v be the number of vertices of G_odd, c=c(D), epsilon_e its Tait
signs, and w the oriented writhe. A spanning-tree term has normalized
A exponent, modulo4,
                r_G = 2(v-1) - sum_e epsilon_e - 3w.
This follows directly from the tree weights in the source: an internal
positive edge contributes exponent1 mod4, an external positive edge
contributes -1, and negative edges reverse these signs. For
                m = #{e : epsilon_e differs from crossing sign},
one obtains
                        r_G/2 = v-1+m mod2.
The Seifert-state black neighborhood N contains exactly the edges
whose Tait and oriented signs agree, so chi(N)=v-c+m. If its boundary
has q Seifert circles, oriented smoothing toggles component parity
at every crossing, giving q=c+1 mod2. Hence
                       r_G/2 = chi(N)+q mod2.
A subsurface N of RP2 has chi(N)+number_of_boundary_components odd
exactly when N contains the unique crosscap; otherwise this number
is even. Since the black Seifert-state side N is local, r_G=0 mod4.
Under t=A^(-4), its normalized tree expansion therefore has integral
exponents: it is precisely the Re sector. The other graph, for a
nonlocal connected diagram, has the opposite half-integral parity.

This also explains why crossing signs do not affect this label:
switching a crossing reverses both its Tait and writhe signs, leaving
r_G unchanged mod4.

For a geometrically local diagram, use the usual local Tait expansion;
both source graph expansions compute the ordinary bracket, and its
crossing count is already at least c_S3(K). No cellular duality of a
local diagram with a Mobius complementary region is assumed.

4. Minimize over diagrams.

For every nonlocal projective diagram of local K, Sections1--3 give
                     beta_Re(D) >= s(D) >= c_S3(K).
For local diagrams the same bound follows from alpha=0. Conversely
a crossing-minimal classical diagram in a disk has alpha=0 and
beta_Re=c_S3(K). Taking the minimum proves the stated equality.

5. Consequences and their exact scope.

(a) Source Question5.4(1), with its first link the local unknot, has
an affirmative answer for local K and epsilon=Re. No claim is made
about the general connected-sum question or epsilon=Im.

(b) Source Conjecture1.1(2), specialized to local knots, would imply
span(V_K) >= c_S3(K). This is false for standard nonalternating knots.
For example the conventional positive T(3,4) has crossing number8
and Jones polynomial t^3+t^5-t^8, up to mirroring t -> t^(-1), whose
span is5. The crossing number and polynomial can be verified by the
standard torus-knot formulas; the parent is independently checking
this explicit example before using it in the report.

(c) A bounded beta_Re on one specified nonempty Jones fiber would now
give a finite fiber of classical knot types. This remains an unproved
global premise, and broad all-fiber finiteness is already contradicted
by known infinite fixed-Jones families. The weaker one-fiber statement
still implies unknot detection by connected-sum amplification. Thus
the projective invariant does not presently provide a new route around
the ordinary fixed-Jones crossing-complexity obstruction.

(d) Independently of the sharper equality, the previously audited
template proof yields b(K)<=12 beta_Re(K)+2 for nontrivial local K:
at most6b+1 RP2 twist blocks lift to at most12b+2 S2 twist regions;
Blair--Kjuchukova--Velazquez--Villanueva Cor1.4 gives b(L)<=2t(L),
and b(K disjoint union K)=2b(K). Crossing-free trivial components
must be handled separately in applying the twist bound. Therefore
Question5.4(4)'s universal beta bound is false already on repeated
connected sums of trefoils. This does not answer its separate alpha
question.

6. Discarded weaker filling approach, retained as an audit warning.

For a hyperbolic augmented RP3 twist-template exterior, sufficiently
long simultaneous filling slopes produce irreducible hyperbolic
exteriors, so they cannot give local knots, whose exteriors have an
RP3 connected-summand. Thus local parameters lie in finitely many
coordinate slices with at least one bounded slope. This observation
alone does NOT prove finiteness: an exceptional partial filling can
create a persistent reducing sphere, after which surviving twist
circles on the classical side can vary indefinitely. Nor can one
silently apply a reducible-filling estimate with a no-lens-summand
hypothesis: the local exterior already has an RP3 lens summand.
The direct index/shading proof above avoids these difficulties.
