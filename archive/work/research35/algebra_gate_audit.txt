COMMON-PHASE AND RANK-ONE OBSTRUCTION FOR TWO ORIENTED INVERSE BANDS
Independent audit, pass 35. Verdict: the proposed gate is valid with the
orientation and single-disk hypotheses stated below. Determinant magnitudes
alone would not prove it; the branched-surface signature supplies the sign.

1. EXACT GEOMETRIC HYPOTHESES
Start with an actual oriented unknot U and two disjoint orientation-compatible
inverse bands. Each individual band splits U into an oriented two-component
link, and applying both gives a three-component link L. Cap U with its standard
properly embedded disk in B4, below the band moves. The resulting surface F is
an oriented disk with two 1-handles, and its one-band subsurfaces F1,F2 are
oriented annuli. No claim that these annuli lie embedded in S3 is needed.

The double cover W of B4 branched over F is B4 with two 2-handles. In their
natural basis its intersection/surgery matrix is a symmetric integer 2x2
matrix B. The one-band covers Wi have matrices [B_ii], and H1(boundary W)
is coker B. Therefore H1(Sigma2 L)=Z plus finite torsion implies rank B=1.
The additional condition that the torsion is nonzero is not needed for this
rank conclusion or the obstruction below.

Let Si be the Jones polynomial of the boundary of Fi, with its induced
orientation. If Si is instead indexed by the smoothing SITE removed from
the final fused knot, its label is swapped; sums are unaffected.

2. WHY THE PHASE IS COMMON
Use x=sqrt(t), V(U)=1, and delta=V(U2)=-x-x^-1.
For any nonzero B_ii, the one-band branched cover gives
  |det(partial link i)|=|B_ii|.
More strongly,
  sigma(partial link i)=sign(B_ii),
with the common convention identifying link signature with the signature
of the oriented double branched cover.

Here is a way to see the latter assertion without assuming a formula for an
immersed S3 annulus. Glue Fi to the negative of a pushed-in Seifert surface
for its oriented boundary, inside two 4-balls forming S4. The closed branch
surface is oriented, so its normal Euler number is zero. The G-signature
theorem says its double cover has signature zero. Novikov additivity then
identifies the signature of Wi with the usual link signature. This also
shows exactly why orientation-compatible bands are essential.

For explicit primary-source support, Owens--Strle, Disoriented homology and
double branched covers, Lemma 10.1 and its proof give this gluing and normal-
Euler calculation; Section 7 gives the lifted handle construction:
  https://arxiv.org/html/2207.09358#S10
  https://arxiv.org/html/2207.09358#S7

Eisermann's The Jones polynomial of ribbon links, introduction, Sections
2.1--2.3 and Section 7.6 equation (13), gives
  V_E(L;q=i)=i^(-sigma(L))*|det(L)|.
His square-root variable is q=-sqrt(t), and his two-component unlink has
polynomial q+q^-1. Thus our two-component polynomial satisfies
  Si(x=i)=-V_E(partial link i;q=i).
For sigma=sign(B_ii)=+/-1 this becomes precisely

                          Si(i)=i*B_ii.               (1)

If B_ii=0, both sides are zero by the determinant identity. Reversing the
GLOBAL branched-cover/signature convention changes the common factor i to
-i; it cannot change the two phases independently.
Source:
  https://pnp.mathematik.uni-stuttgart.de/igt/eiserm/publications/ribbonlinks.pdf

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION: this uses the ordinary signed determinant V_E(i),
not the generalized determinant obtained after dividing out unlink factors.
Eisermann explicitly warns that the analogous phase rule for the latter is
false. We do not use that false extension.

3. THE TWO-BAND DIAGONAL CANNOT CANCEL
A nonzero real symmetric rank-one matrix is positive or negative semidefinite.
Hence B_11 and B_22 have the same weak sign and
  tr B = B_11+B_22 != 0.
Equation (1) implies S1(i)+S2(i)=i tr B != 0. But delta(i)=0. Consequently

                         S1+S2 != 2 delta.             (2)

If the double smoothing also has Jones polynomial delta^2, the exact TL2
calculation in work/research34/twist_formula.txt says that the whole diagonal
V(K_k,k)=1 is equivalent to S1+S2=2 delta. Thus (2) excludes it for ANY actual
oriented two-band presentation satisfying the stated branched-cover rank.
This is not restricted to the one particular LLL(1) band pair tested earlier.
The diagonal is calibrated so both sites have normalized eigenvalue
z=A^8=t^-2; reversing a local twist-coordinate convention reverses that
coordinate in the geometric statement.

The same two-site classification also shows that a linear trajectory
(m,n)=(ak,bk) with a,b both nonzero and a!=b would require S1=S2=delta.
Then (1) forces B_11=B_22=0, incompatible with nonzero rank one. Therefore
NO nonconstant linear Jones-one trajectory twisting BOTH bands exists under
these hypotheses and the double-smoothing condition. Coordinate trajectories
are not all excluded: if B_ii=0, their determinant test is silent, and their
full Jones condition still has to be checked.

This does not rule out isolated nonzero Jones-one pairs (m,n), nonlinear
parameter sets, different branch-cover homology, or unoriented bands.

4. PRECISE HIGHER-BAND OBSERVATION
The phase relation (1) applies individually to r disjoint oriented inverse
bands based on the same disk: the r-handle matrix has principal one-handle
forms [B_ii]. Hence a nonzero semidefinite B always has sum_i Si(i)!=0.
For the SAME normalized twist exponent k at all r sites, multilinearity
expresses V as the polynomial in s=z^k
  sum_(J subset {1,...,r}) (S_J/delta^|J|)*s^(r-|J|)*(1-s)^|J|,
where S_empty=1. If this equals 1 for all integer k, independence of the
Bernstein basis forces sum_i Si=r*delta (and analogous higher-subset sums).
Thus a nonzero semidefinite B excludes this uniform diagonal for any r.
Mixed-sign higher-rank matrices can have trace zero, so this particular
first-level obstruction then vanishes; it does not prove that cancellation
actually occurs. No general mixed-rate higher-band theorem is claimed.
