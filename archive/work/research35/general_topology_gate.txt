GENERAL TWO-BAND COMMON-PHASE OBSTRUCTION
Independent audit, 2026-11-01. Established under the explicit hypotheses below.
This is an obstruction to a proposed construction, not a proof of Jones detection.

STATEMENT
Let U be an actual unknot. Attach two disjoint, coherently oriented fission bands
b1,b2, so doing both produces a three-component link L. Let S1,S2 be the two-component
links obtained by doing just b1 or just b2, oriented by the resulting annuli.
Suppose b1(Sigma2(L))=1. In the standard convention

  delta=V_(two-component unlink)=-(x+x^-1),  x=t^(1/2),

one has S1(i)+S2(i) != 0. Consequently the Laurent-polynomial equality

  V_S1(x)+V_S2(x)=2 delta(x)

is impossible. Here S_i(i) means V_Si evaluated at x=i.
No assumption on the Jones polynomial of L is required for this obstruction.
In particular it applies to every such two-band unknot fusion of LLL(1), whose
branched cover has H1=Z direct-sum Z/144. The nonzero torsion assumption is stronger
than needed: free rank one alone suffices.

PROOF
1. Choose a standard disk D for U pushed into B4 and put the two bands in successive
radial levels near its boundary. Their orientations extend across D. The resulting
properly embedded ribbon surface F=D+b1+b2 is connected and orientable, with one
0-handle and two1-handles. Since its boundary has three components, F is planar.
The original disk is an actual disk because U is an actual unknot; Jones1 alone
would NOT justify this step.

The double cover W of B4 branched along F has a handle description consisting of
one0-handle and two2-handles. This is the usual branched-cover handle correspondence:
a surface saddle lifts to a2-handle; the first disk gives the standard covered B4.
The two handles have a common symmetric integral linking/intersection matrix

                  B = [[a,c],[c,b]].

Keeping only band bi keeps only its corresponding handle, so the partial branched
cover Wi has matrix [a] or [b], with the very same framing integer.
No diagonal change or independent boundary-framing choice is being made here.

2. The surgery presentation gives H1(Sigma2(L);Z)=coker B. Therefore the assumed
free rank one gives rank_Q B=1. In particular B is nonzero and has exactly one
nonzero eigenvalue. It follows that

               tr B=a+b !=0.

Equivalently B is positive or negative semidefinite, so its nonzero diagonal entries
have the same sign. Either diagonal may be zero; that case causes no problem.

3. For an oriented properly embedded surface F_i in B4 with its boundary-induced
orientation, the signature of the branched cover Wi is the ordinary link signature
of its boundary. A useful framing-safe proof is to cap F_i by an oppositely pushed
Seifert surface in the other B4. The closed branch surface in S4 is oriented, hence
has normal Euler number zero, since its integral homology class is zero. The
G-signature formula and Novikov additivity then identify the two signatures.
This proof also covers degenerate intersection forms; no rational-homology-sphere
assumption is required for the signature identity.

Thus sigma(S1)=sign(a), sigma(S2)=sign(b), in the common symmetrized-Seifert convention.
Also H1(Sigma2(S1))=coker[a], and similarly for b. Hence their absolute determinants
are |a|,|b| when nonzero, and zero when the corresponding integer is zero.

4. Use the ORDINARY signed determinant phase formula. Eisermann uses q=-sqrt(t),
positive two-component unlink polynomial, and

  V_E(L;q=i)=i^(-sigma(L)) |det(L)|.

For a two-component link the powers of x are odd, so our evaluation at x=i is the
negative of his evaluation at q=i. Therefore, when a!=0,

  V_S1(x=i)=-i^(-sign(a)) |a| = i a.

For a=0 both sides vanish, and likewise V_S2(i)=i b. A global reversal of all
signature/cover conventions changes the same overall factor for BOTH entries,
which does not affect the conclusion. In the stated conventions the sum is

                  V_S1(i)+V_S2(i)=i(a+b) !=0.

Since delta(i)=0, the proposed polynomial identity cannot hold. QED.

OPTIONAL QUANTITATIVE FORM
Every nonzero symmetric integral rank-one2x2 matrix can be written B=d v v^T,
where v=(v1,v2) is a primitive integral vector and d is a nonzero integer.
For example, take v as a primitive generator of its rational column line; integral
columns give B=v w^T, symmetry gives w=dv, and primitivity forces d integral.
The gcd of the entries of B is |d|, so its Smith form is diag(|d|,0). Consequently,
with N=|Tor H1(Sigma2(L))| (N=1 allowed),

  |V_S1(i)+V_S2(i)| = N(v1^2+v2^2) >= N,
  |V_S1(i)|=N v1^2,   |V_S2(i)|=N v2^2.

This concerns the bands' surgery basis, not an arbitrary diagonalized matrix.
It agrees with the audited LLL(1) example, where both partial values are144i.

APPLICATION TO THE TWO-TWIST FORMULA
If the double-cut link has V_L=delta^2 and the uncut anchor is U, the two-antiparallel-
site recurrence gives, using effective twist monomials X,Y,

 V-1 = (S1/delta-1)Y(1-X)+(S2/delta-1)X(1-Y).

On the equal-effective-exponent diagonal X=Y, it is identically zero precisely when
S1+S2=2delta. The theorem excludes that diagonal whenever b1(Sigma2(L))=1.
It does not identify geometric handedness with effective exponent sign, exclude all
individual off-diagonal Jones1 points, or cover bands that do not form the stated
oriented disk-plus-two-band surface. It also does not say arbitrary twist-constant
families are trivial. The opposite-effective-exponent diagonal has a different
criterion, namely S1=S2=delta, and is separately excluded by the same evaluation.
