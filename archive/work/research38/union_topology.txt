PASS38: SYMMETRIC-UNION / ORBIFOLD SOURCE-SCOPE AUDIT
2026-11-01. Bounded check of a purported new route; no candidate or new theorem.

DECISION
The recently surfaced orbifold paper does NOT prove that every one-region partial
knot of Kinoshita--Terasaka is trivial, and does NOT provide a known nontrivial partial.
Its answer to Lamm--Tanaka Question (2) concerns a different issue. The known KT
presentation has partial unknot. Finding a different, nontrivial partial remains
a hard inverse geometric construction, already investigated in our earlier work.
There is no new ready-made axis to test here.

WHAT THE NEWER PAPER ACTUALLY SAYS
Boileau--Kitano--Nozaki, arXiv2510.12372v3, 19 April 2026, now titled
A preorder on the set of links with applications to symmetric unions:
https://arxiv.org/html/2510.12372v3

Section 7.2 explicitly identifies Proposition 7.6 as answering Lamm--Tanaka
Question (2). It says that a nontrivial symmetric union and its partial knot have
nonisomorphic pi-orbifold groups; in particular the partial cannot be the whole
knot itself. It does NOT force the partial to be the unknot. The proof uses
Hopficity, determinant squaring, and nontriviality of the preferred longitude's
orbifold image for a nontrivial determinant-one knot.

The other applicable restriction is Corollary 7.4: an arborescent symmetric union
cannot have a partial whose prime factor has a hyperbolic double branched cover.
This does not exclude every hyperbolic partial knot: knot-exterior hyperbolicity
and branched-cover hyperbolicity are different properties. Theorem 1.11's
homological obstruction to admitting a symmetric union does not apply to KT,
whose branched-cover H1 is zero. The small-source finiteness theorem also cannot
be invoked without smallness; our existing Tillmann source shows KT is not small.
These distinctions were already recorded in proof12 and the living report.

PUBLISHED JONES STATEMENT, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT SUPPLY
Lamm--Tanaka, On partial knots for symmetric unions, Topology Appl.375 (2025),109593:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.topol.2025.109593
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166864125003918
The publisher abstract states that any partial knot of a one-twist-region KT
presentation has Jones polynomial 1. It does not assert a nontrivial partial exists.
The full article remains unavailable in this audit; the abstract and visible
section snippets were checked. No audit of its entire proof is claimed. Our
independent Tanaka-formula computation in proof12 already recovered that Jones
conclusion and restricted the twist to one even value, up to mirror convention.

PRECISE EXISTING TOPOLOGICAL FILTER, NOT A NEW CLAIM
For completeness, combine that previously audited even-twist computation with:
Boileau--Kitano--Nozaki, On the genera of symmetric unions of knots,
https://arxiv.org/html/2407.09881
Proposition 1.5 gives the meridian-preserving, longitude-killing epimorphism for
an even symmetric union; Corollary 1.8 gives g(K)>=2g(partial). The ordinary
Alexander polynomial is the square of the partial's Alexander polynomial.
Since g(KT)=2, Delta(KT)=1, and KT has a three-bridge diagram, a nontrivial
one-region partial J must satisfy
  g(J)=1, Delta_J=1, V_J=1, mr(J)<=3.
Our earlier audited genus-one/three-bridge classification and pretzel Jones-moment
calculation exclude b(J)<=3. Thus any such J would have
  mr(J)=3 < b(J), with b(J)>=4.
This would contradict BOTH Jones unknot detection and the meridional-rank
conjecture. This is a reason to downgrade this particular inverse-design target,
not a proof that no such J exists. In particular, mr<=3 must not be silently
replaced by bridge number<=3.

The prior complete derivation and its source checks are in
work/proof12/tangle/findings.txt and Sections 4--7 of
outputs/jones-finite-tests-and-cancellation.txt. The classification source is
Fukuhama--Ozawa--Teragaito, Genus one, three-bridge knots are pretzel (1999):
https://www.komazawa-u.ac.jp/~w3c/research/pdf/pretzel.pdf
No new classification of genus-one meridional-rank-three knots was located in
this bounded primary-source check. This is not an exhaustive literature claim.

ONE ADDITIONAL PRIMARY CHECK THAT DOES NOT HELP HERE
Colorings of symmetric unions and partial knots, arXiv2504.08589, Theorem 1.3:
https://arxiv.org/html/2504.08589
The theorem bounds Fox p-colorings of the union between the partial's coloring
count and its square divided by p. At determinant one both branched-cover H1
groups vanish, so every Fox p-coloring is constant and all three quantities are
p. Thus this particular recent obstruction is automatically saturated here;
it supplies no new KT partial candidate or exclusion.

STOP CONDITION / PRACTICAL RESULT
Do not label this lead a new construction or reopen an undirected axis search.
It becomes concrete only upon producing an actual new one-region symmetric
KT diagram together with a geometrically certified nontrivial partial, or a
new classification theorem for the narrow quotient-knot class above. The checked
orbifold paper supplies neither. The question-answer headline was the wrong scope.
