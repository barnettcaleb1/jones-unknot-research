HEEGAARD EVALUATION OF A VIRTUAL JONES-ONE TORUS DIAGRAM
Independent algebra audit, pass 38

Scope. This is an exact criterion for an actual knot diagram on a torus.
It does not certify a particular diagram reconstructed from a published
picture, and virtual nontriviality does not imply nontriviality in S^3.

1. Fix a consistent unreduced convention.

Let R=Z[A,A^{-1}], delta=-A^2-A^{-2}. The empty link has bracket 1,
a disk-bounding zero-framed circle has bracket delta, and a positive
framing twist multiplies the bracket by -A^3. For an actual oriented
knot diagram D in S^3,

  bracket_unred(D)=delta*(-A^3)^{w(D)} V_D(A^{-4}).

For a general framed knot the exponent is its framing relative to the
preferred zero framing. This convention avoids the factor-of-delta
ambiguity caused by using the reduced planar bracket with a surface
skein module.

The Kauffman bracket skein module of T^2 x I has its usual multicurve
basis. Thus a finite surface diagram expands as

  F=f_empty + sum_{s,n>=1} f_{s,n} c_s^{(n)},

where s=(p,q) is a primitive unoriented slope, c_s^{(n)} is the disjoint
union of n parallel curves with torus surface framing, and contractible
circles have already been absorbed into the Laurent coefficients.
Distinct s are identified up to simultaneous sign reversal only.

The virtual evaluator is the linear map

  epsilon_v(1)=1,  epsilon_v(c_s^{(n)})=delta^n.

It is the usual virtual state sum in the unreduced convention. In
particular a virtual knot with V=1 has epsilon_v(F)=delta times a
framing monomial.

2. The actual Heegaard functional.

Fix the standard unknotted Heegaard torus in S^3 and a marking
M in SL(2,Z). The embedding of its collar induces an R-linear map H_M
from the surface skein module to R. It sends c_s^{(n)} to the bracket
of n parallel copies of the transformed torus slope M s, retaining
surface framing. This is the surface-framed torus link T(np,nq)
when M s=(p,q).

The map is not an algebra homomorphism for stacking in T^2 x I.
In particular, one must NOT replace the bracket of n parallel curves
by the nth power of the bracket of one curve: the parallel curves can
link. One also cannot evaluate a Laurent polynomial in different
slope elements by a scalar substitution compatible with all products.

For completeness, with coherent component orientations and the usual
positive torus-braid convention, p>0 gives the explicit formula

  H_M(c_s^{(n)})
    =delta*(-A^3)^{n^2 p q} V_{T(np,nq)}(A^{-4}).              (1)

Here each component's surface self-framing is pq and every pair has
linking number pq. The total writhe used in Jones normalization is
therefore n*pq + n*(n-1)*pq = n^2*pq, not merely n*pq.
Equivalently, the standard braid (sigma_1...sigma_{np-1})^{nq}
has writhe nq(np-1); changing its n component framings to the torus
framings adds nq. Their sum is n^2 pq. For either coordinate-axis
slope the evaluation is delta^n, directly. Reversing the global
crossing convention mirrors all formulas; defining H by framed
embedding avoids that convention issue altogether.

Consequently exact actual evaluation is the finite sum

  H_M(F)=f_empty+sum f_{s,n} H_M(c_s^{(n)}).                  (2)

For an actual knot, the NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT Jones-one criterion is

  H_M(F)/delta is a unit of Z[A,A^{-1}].                    (3)

Necessity is ordinary framing normalization. For sufficiency the
normalized ordinary Jones is a Laurent monomial. A knot has V(1)=1
and V'(1)=0, so that monomial must equal 1. Thus (3) does not require
guessing the difference between a torus-diagram writhe and the framing
of its S^3 embedding. If that framing f has been independently tracked,
the equivalent more specific equation is H_M(F)=delta*(-A^3)^f.

3. A simple, completely finite sufficient slope test.

If ONE marking M sends every essential slope in the support of F to
a meridian or longitude, then

  H_M(F)=epsilon_v(F).                                    (4)

Proof: any n parallel meridians or any n parallel longitudes are an
n-component unlink with each surface framing zero. This proves (4)
on every basis term, hence on their linear combination. If the virtual
diagram has Jones 1, equation (3) then proves its S^3 embedding has
Jones 1, with no further framing inference.

This slope test is decidable just from the finite support:
* zero slopes: automatic;
* one primitive slope: always possible;
* two distinct slopes s,t: possible exactly when |det(s,t)|=1;
* three or more distinct slopes: impossible for this particular test.

The last two assertions use invertibility of M and the fact that the
two unoriented coordinate axes have determinant absolute value one.
Conversely a pair with determinant absolute value one is an integral
basis, up to orientation/sign choices.

Failure of this sufficient test does NOT prove failure of (3).
Weighted cancellation between torus-link evaluations in (2) may still
occur. Nor does passing the test imply a counterexample: the resulting
embedded knot may be geometrically unknotted. Filling the two sides of
a supporting torus destroys information that virtual equivalence
retains.

4. Source audit and current concrete test.

Primary source: H.A. Dye and L.H. Kauffman, Minimal surface
representations of virtual knots and links, AGT 5 (2005), 509-535,
https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0401035
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0401035

The surface-state definition is on printed pp.512-513. Section 3,
Figures 2-7 and equations (4)-(6), gives the one-virtualized-crossing
construction. Theorem 3.4, printed p.521, states the standard Jones
divisibility implying that a knot with monomial Jones has Jones 1.
Corollary 3.5 supplies nonclassical genus-one virtual Jones-one knots
by virtualizing an unknotting crossing. This is not a statement about
their classical images in a filled Heegaard torus.

There is a convention warning when extracting a concrete F from those
figures: if Figure 7's two essential states are read as two meridians
and two longitudes, with precisely the alpha,beta basis of equation
(4), forgetting curves in the printed equation (6) gives

  (A alpha+A^{-1} beta)+(A^{-1}alpha+A beta)*delta
     =-A^{-3}alpha-A^3 beta,

whereas the printed switched/virtual bracket in equation (4) is
-A^3 alpha-A^{-3} beta. Exchanging the outer smoothing weights repairs
this algebraically, but does not by itself identify which diagram or
basis convention needs changing. This is a reconstruction warning,
not a settled claim of a publication error. An explicit virtual PD
and its ribbon-surface state expansion are authoritative; the parent
is independently computing those states. No concrete S^3 Jones-one
candidate is certified in this algebra note.

Verdict. Equations (2)-(3) are the exact finite unit test; equation (4)
is a useful sufficient condition. The missing geometric test is
whether a correctly reconstructed Jones-one torus diagram that passes
them becomes a nontrivial knot after the Heegaard filling. Virtual
nontriviality cannot substitute for that test.
