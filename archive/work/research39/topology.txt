PASS 39: A PRECISE SKEIN-TO-TOPOLOGY ENDPOINT, AND ITS ARITHMETIC OBSTRUCTION
Bounded research audit. No generic Jones theorem or counterexample is claimed.

Outcome. There is an established-input endpoint: an integer homology sphere
with tame universal Kauffman skein module and generic skein dimension one
is S^3. However, generic finite-dimensionality alone can lose an entire
family of actual group representations. The explicit +1 surgery below
exhibits this failure inside the exact class of manifolds a surgery-based
Jones proof would use. It does not refute rank-one detection without
tameness; its generic rank is not computed here.

Duplicate check: earlier handlebody-vacuum (proof9), ribbon-disk group
(research22), AJ/color promotion (research23), and positivity/cluster
(research29) routes were checked. The closed-manifold specialization and
torsion obstruction below is separate. No finite Jones probe is proposed.

1. What really follows from a simple entire skein module.

Write S_A(Y) for the Kauffman bracket skein module and S_gen(Y) for its
extension to Q(A). Farajzadeh-Tehrani--Frohman--Kania-Bartoszynska, Cor7.6
(indeed Thm7.7), show for a homology sphere with tame universal module that

  dim_Q(A) S_gen(Y) >= dim_C S_{-1}(Y).

Here S_{-1} is the unreduced SL2(C) character algebra. Tameness means the
Q[A,A^-1] module is a direct sum of cyclic modules and has no torsion
submodule for at least one cyclotomic polynomial Phi_(2m), m odd. The
primary source is https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17037, Section7.2.

Zentner proves every integer homology sphere other than S^3 has an
irreducible SL2(C) representation: https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.08530,
Duke Math J167 (2018), 1643--1712. Such a character differs from the trivial
one. Evaluation at those two points gives character-algebra dimension at
least two. Consequently tame + generic rank one implies Y=S^3.

Thus the complete proposed global chain would be

  V_K=1
    -> S_A(S^3_(+1)(K)) is tame and has generic rank one [UNPROVED]
    -> S^3_(+1)(K)=S^3
    -> K=U [no nonmeridional S^3 surgery on a nontrivial knot].

Only the final two arrows are established. Calling the first arrow
"vacuum rigidity" or "simple scalar gives simple complement" does not
prove it. On the Jones-one fiber that combined missing assertion already
has the force of the original problem. A Jones polynomial is one closure
functional, whereas the module involves arbitrary links inside a filled
manifold and all handle-slide relations. No justified construction here
promotes that scalar to the module or supplies tameness.

2. An actual geometric stress test, with exact matrices.

Let T be a right-handed trefoil and let Y=S^3_(+1)(T#T). This is an integer
homology sphere. The trefoil group has presentation <x,y | x^2=y^3>.
Take mu=xy^-1 and lambda=h mu^-6, where h=x^2=y^3. The latter peripheral
identity is the torus-knot fiber push-off: h has linking number 2*3=6,
so subtracting six meridians gives the preferred longitude. For the
connected sum, identify the meridians and multiply the longitudes.

Let z be a primitive 11th root, and put

  M=diag(z,z^-1), a=(z^-1-z)^-1, c=-1-a^2,
  X=[[a,1],[c,-a]], Y0=M^-1 X.

Directly det X=det Y0=1, tr X=0, tr Y0=1. Hence

  X^2=Y0^3=-I, XY0^-1=M, rho(lambda)=-M^-6.

For the second summand conjugate X,Y0 by D_t=diag(t,t^-1), t in C*.
This preserves M, so the two representations glue on the connected-sum
group. The +1 filling relation is satisfied identically:

  rho(mu lambda_1 lambda_2)=M*M^-12=M^-11=I.

Every resulting representation is irreducible: M has distinct eigenlines
and both off-diagonal entries of X are nonzero. In particular c=0 would
force z^4-z^2+1=0, hence z^12=1, impossible at order11. Their characters
vary, since the trace of the word x_1 x_2 is

  2a^2 + c(t^2+t^-2).

This is a nonconstant regular function of t. Therefore X(Y) contains a
positive-dimensional component, not merely infinitely many matrices
representing a single conjugacy class. This bending argument is elementary
and is not asserted to be a novel character-variety construction.

verify_bending.py checks the identities over the exact cyclotomic field
Q[z]/(1+z+...+z^10), including three distinct character values. It uses
only Python's standard library. The group/peripheral identifications are
the topological input; the program does not pretend to prove them.

3. What the stress test decisively rules out.

Gunningham--Jordan--Safronov prove finite-dimensionality of S_gen for
every closed oriented three-manifold. Belletti--Detcherry state this as
Theorem1.6 in https://arxiv.org/html/2406.17454. Their Cor2.2 says a
positive-dimensional character variety forces (A-zeta)-torsion for every
root of unity of odd order or order2 modulo4 (with complex coefficients
when zeta is not rational). Thus this specific Y has finite generic skein
dimension, but infinite-dimensional S_{-1}, and fails tameness.

The source theorem controls this loss of information; this example does
not compute an explicit torsion element. It kills any argument that
automatically specializes a finite generic basis to recover all classical
representations, or assumes tame arithmetic for all +1 knot surgeries.
It does NOT show that its generic module has rank one, and does NOT
disprove the narrower hypothetical assertion that Jones-one surgeries
have tame rank-one modules. The test knot T#T itself has nonunit Jones.

4. Research decision.

The useful new constraint is to preserve the integral parameter module
and its cyclotomic torsion throughout a surgery/gluing argument. Merely
solving a generic transfer matrix or recognizing a scalar closure will
not do this. A concrete future claim can be tested against the matrices
above immediately: if it would make all +1 surgeries tame from generic
finite-dimensionality, it is false. No new computation of a Jones-one
candidate or proof of the missing first arrow emerged from this bounded
pass. Shelve this as a full-solution route until an explicit construction
relates the whole Jones polynomial to integral handle-slide relations;
the established endpoint alone is not progress on that connection.
