PASS 40: LEE–YANG / POTTS ZERO-THEOREM SCOPE AUDIT
2026-11-01. Bounded check; no unknot-detection proof or new candidate.

Verdict
-------
The natural Lee–Yang route has a precise mismatch of variables and domains.
The exact Jones specialization of the planar random-cluster polynomial never
lies in the nondegenerate real ferromagnetic region Q>0, v_e>=0. Lee–Yang's
circle theorem also concerns field fugacities at fixed interactions, whereas
the Jones specialization varies Q and interactions at zero field. Direct
circle-rootedness and real-rootedness of the Jones polynomial already fail
for the trefoil. No new global analytic mechanism survives this check.

This is separate from the activity-polynomial strong-Rayleigh counterexamples
in creative6/statmech_findings.txt, and from the finite positive local-model
obstruction in fanout16/physics.txt. It does not reprove or extend those results.

1. Exact all-polynomial identity
-------------------------------
Take a connected planar Tait graph G with n vertices. Give edge e sign eps_e
in {+1,-1} so the smoothing that includes that edge has coefficient A^eps_e;
the other smoothing has coefficient A^(-eps_e). Put s=sum eps_e and

  delta=-A^2-A^(-2),
  Z_G(Q,{v_e})=sum_{S subset E} Q^{k(S)} product_{e in S} v_e.

The boundary of the planar spanning ribbon subgraph S has

  b(S)=2k(S)-n+|S|

components. Therefore, with the bracket of one circle normalized to 1,

  <D>=A^(-s) delta^(-n-1)
      Z_G(delta^2,{delta A^(2 eps_e)}).

This is an identity of rational functions whose right side simplifies to a
Laurent polynomial; it is not a prescription for numerical division at
delta=0. With t=A^(-4), V_K(t)=(-A^3)^(-w(D))<D>. Hence V_K=1 is exactly

  Z_G(delta^2,{delta A^(2 eps_e)})
      =(-A^3)^{w(D)} A^s delta^{n+1}.                 (1)

The graph-to-bracket construction is primary-source standard (Kauffman,
1989, Sections 3–4). The explicit normalization in (1) is derived above,
so no change of convention is hidden in the proposed analytic bridge.

2. The real ferromagnetic domain is absent
-----------------------------------------
Write x=A^4. Along the Jones curve,

  Q=x+2+x^(-1),        v_e=-1-x^(eps_e).             (2)

If even one v_e is real and nonnegative, y=x^(eps_e) is real and y<=-1.
Consequently

  Q=2+y+y^(-1)<=0,

with equality only at y=-1. Thus no diagram containing an edge has a point
on (2) with Q>0 and every v_e>=0. If both edge-sign types occur, demanding
both weights nonnegative forces x=-1 and Q=delta=0. For t>0, every weight
is even farther outside the physical range: v_e=-1-t^(-eps_e)<-1.

The usual Potts interaction is v_e=exp(J_e/T)-1. Positive-temperature
ferromagnetism gives v_e>=0, and real antiferromagnetism gives -1<=v_e<=0.
Neither supplies a positivity theorem on the positive-real Jones curve.
The obstruction is exact for this representation; it is not a claim that
every possible change of variables or different statistical model fails.

Independently, the Lee–Yang circle theorem concerns diagonal field fugacity
z in a multiaffine field polynomial, keeping the admissible interactions
fixed. Equation (2) instead varies the number-of-colors parameter and
interactions simultaneously, with no magnetic-field variable. One cannot
identify its t-variable with z merely because both arise in a partition
function. Ruelle's primary formulation makes this distinction explicit.

3. Actual-knot test with complete normalization
----------------------------------------------
For a trefoil use G=C_3, n=3, s=w=3, all eps=+1. Then

  Z_{C_3}(Q,v)=Q^3+3Q^2v+3Qv^2+Qv^3.

Substituting (2) into the bracket formula gives

  <D>=-A^5-A^(-3)+A^(-7),
  V(t)=t+t^3-t^4=t(1+t^2-t^3).

Thus V(1)=1 and V(2)=-6: a nonzero root lies in (1,2), so it is not on
the unit circle. The cubic t^3-t^2-1 has discriminant -31, so it also has
two nonreal roots. Removing the monomial does not make Jones polynomials
universally real-rooted or circle-rooted. Mirroring inverts these roots
and changes neither failure.

4. What would an analytic endpoint actually require?
----------------------------------------------------
A useful theorem would have to use additional, verified geometry of the
signed planar graph and prove that identity (1) forces a disk-bounding
medial knot. No such theorem was obtained here. A statement merely saying
that every nontrivial knot has a nonzero complex Jones root is equivalent
to the original conjecture: a Laurent polynomial with no nonzero root is
a monomial; the knot identities V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0 force that monomial to
be 1. Renaming detection as a zero-existence assertion adds no mechanism.

Zero-free regions for a partition polynomial are not by themselves the
needed rigidity statement either. They can hold uniformly for many
nontrivial graphs. One would still need to connect the special identity
(1), including its framing prefactor, to a topology-changing reduction.
The bounded check stops here; no new budget or positivity conjecture is
proposed as progress toward the generic theorem.
