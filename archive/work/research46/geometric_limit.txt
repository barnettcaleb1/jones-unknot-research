PASS 46 — FINITE-ORDER JONES CORRECTIONS CANNOT FIRST CLOSE IN A TAME LIMIT
2026-11-01

Verdict
=======
There is a complete compactness implication, but it does not give a new
construction: sufficiently controlled geometric convergence makes the knot
type eventually constant. An exact Jones-one limit would therefore already
be an exact Jones-one knot at finite stages. Conversely, if every stage has
V != 1, arbitrarily high Taylor agreement forces unbounded geometric
complexity quantitatively. This rules out a proposed escape by shrinking
successive corrections while retaining uniformly thick, bounded-length rope.

1. An exact order bound, including the Laurent-support subtlety
==============================================================
For V_K != 1 define

  nu(K) = ord_{t=1}(V_K(t)-1),   s(K)=maxdeg V_K-mindeg V_K.

Then

                   nu(K) <= s(K) <= c(K).                 (1)

Here c is the minimal crossing number. No claim of novelty is made:
Kauffman--Saito--Sawin, On Finiteness of Certain Vassiliev Invariants,
q-alg/9507035, Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 (PDF pp.2--4), already give the
crossing-level conclusion. We supply an independent elementary argument
that also treats the potentially troublesome one-sided exponent window.

https://arxiv.org/pdf/q-alg/9507035

Do NOT simply write span(V-1)<=span(V): it is false, for example for the
trefoil V=t+t^3-t^4. The needed extra input is the universal Jones identity
V(omega)=1 at a primitive cube root omega. Together with V(1)=1 and
V'(1)=0, these are recorded as V=1 mod (t-1)(t^3-1) in Stoimenow,
Polynomial values, the linking form and unknotting numbers, Proposition
3.2, citing Jones's original result:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405076

Proof of the first inequality in (1).
If the exponent interval of V contains zero, adjoining/subtracting its
constant term cannot increase its span. A nonzero Laurent polynomial of
span s has at most s zeros at 1, counted with multiplicity.

Otherwise mirror if necessary and write V=t^M P(t), M>=1, deg(P)=s.
If s=0, V(1)=1 makes V=t^M, contrary to V'(1)=0. Thus s>=1.
A Laurent polynomial with r distinct exponents has order at most r-1
at 1, by the Vandermonde matrix for Euler derivatives (t d/dt)^j.
Since V-1 has at most s+2 terms, the only exceptional possibility is
nu=s+1. In that case the unique polynomial supported on M,...,M+s
whose Taylor jet through degree s equals 1 is

  V(t)=1-(1-t)^(s+1) Q(t),
  Q(t)=sum_{j=0}^{M-1} binom(s+j,s) t^j.                 (2)

Existence follows by truncating (1-t)^(-s-1): the displayed V is divisible
by t^M, has degree M+s, and differs from 1 to order s+1 at t=1.
Uniqueness follows from the same Vandermonde matrix on M,...,M+s.

The coefficients a_j=binom(s+j,s) are strictly positive and increasing.
Q has no unit-circle zero. For M=1 this is immediate. For M>=2, if
Q(z)=0 and |z|=1, then

  a_(M-1) z^M = a_0 + sum_{j=1}^{M-1}(a_j-a_(j-1)) z^j.

The weights on the right are all positive and sum to a_(M-1).
Equality in the triangle inequality forces z^0 and z^1 to have the same
phase, hence z=1, where Q is positive. Therefore (2) contradicts
V(omega)=1. This excludes nu=s+1 and proves nu<=s.

The second inequality in (1) is the usual bracket state bound: for a
connected c-crossing diagram the all-A/all-B state circle counts satisfy
s_A+s_B<=c+2, so the bracket span is at most 4c. Writhe normalization
does not change span, and t=A^-4 divides the span by four.

2. Quantitative geometric deterioration
=======================================
Let length be L and thickness tau be the RADIUS of the largest embedded
normal tube (equivalently reach), so Rop=L/tau. The classical bound is

             Rop(K) >= ((4*pi/11)c(K))^(3/4).             (3)

This convention and estimate appear in Cantarella--Kusner--Sullivan,
On the Minimum Ropelength of Knots and Links, Invent. Math.150 (2002),
257--286: definition in the introduction and Section1; Section9, printed
article p.279 (preprint p.23), gives the crossing estimate. Their Lemma6
and Theorem7 also give the positive-thickness compactness/isotopy result.
https://www.jasoncantarella.com/downloads/papers/ropelength/ropelen.pdf
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-002-0234-y

Consequently every actual knot satisfying V!=1 and
V=1 mod (t-1)^n obeys

  c(K)>=n,
  Rop(K)>=((4*pi/11)n)^(3/4),
  tau <= L * (11/(4*pi*n))^(3/4).                         (4)

Thus bounded physical length forces thickness to zero at least at this
rate; fixed positive thickness forces length to diverge. This applies to
every realization of the knot type, not just a poor diagram produced by
the construction. Arbitrary rescaling cannot help because ropelength is
scale invariant.

The already certified finite-order inverse knots R_N in
work/research28/quantum_promotion.txt have V_R_N!=1 and
V_R_N=1+O((t-1)^(N+1)). Applying (4) yields c(R_N)>=N+1 and
Rop(R_N)>=((4*pi/11)(N+1))^(3/4). This is a consequence of their
established properties, not a new realization claim.

3. Why convergence cannot postpone the exact example to infinity
================================================================
Suppose smooth parametrized embeddings gamma_n:S^1->R^3 converge in C^1
to a smooth embedded gamma. A normal tube around gamma exists. For large
n, gamma_n lies in it and is transverse to its normal fibers; the closest
point projection has degree one and is a diffeomorphism on gamma_n.
Sliding each point along its normal fiber gives an isotopy, extended to
an ambient isotopy. Hence K_n=K for all sufficiently large n.

If ord_1(V_Kn-1)>=n, this fixed polynomial has arbitrarily high vanishing
order and is exactly 1. If all K_n are nontrivial, K is nontrivial. But
the same conclusion holds for EVERY sufficiently late finite stage:
the counterexample has not been created only by a limiting process.
In particular, a sequence all of whose Jones polynomials are nonunit
cannot have such a C^1 embedded limit.

For the compactness version, bounds L<=L0 and tau>=tau0>0 yield bounded
curvature and equicontinuous tangents in constant-speed parametrizations.
After translations and a subsequence one obtains a C^1 embedded limit;
positive thickness prevents new self-contact. This is the situation of
the cited Lemma6 and Theorem7. Alternatively (3) gives a crossing bound,
so (1) directly proves that n beyond the bound already forces V_Kn=1.

Uniform C^0 convergence alone is insufficient. Tie a fixed nontrivial
local knot into a shrinking ball along a round circle. The resulting
nontrivial knots converge uniformly to that unknotted circle, with lost
thickness and no C^1 convergence to its smooth parametrization. Infinite
accumulating modifications may instead produce wild or singular limits;
the ordinary Jones polynomial and the question here concern tame knots.
Bounded length alone is meaningless as a topological compactness input,
since any knot can be rescaled to prescribed length.

4. Exact scope of the conditional endpoint
==========================================
The following are equivalent as existence statements:

 (a) There is a nontrivial tame knot with V=1.
 (b) There are nontrivial K_n with V_Kn=1 mod(t-1)^n and uniformly
     bounded ropelength.

(a) implies (b) by a constant sequence. Conversely (1)--(3) force an
exact Jones-one finite stage once n exceeds the common crossing bound.
Thus a bare uniform-geometry hypothesis is not an independently easier
bridge. For the known all-finite-order constructions with V!=1 it is
actually impossible, quantitatively. This pass produces an obstruction
to the proposed limiting mechanism, not a solution of unknot detection.
