PASS 48: GEOMETRIC AUDIT OF THE LOW-DEGREE SKEIN TORSION ROUTE
2026-11-01. Bounded deduction and route identification, not a solution.

No actual knot-minus-unknot torsion pair, marked relative lift, or Jones-one
nontrivial knot was constructed. The useful outcome is a structural boundary
on the proposal and recognition of a previously investigated bottleneck.

Setup
-----
M is the exterior of a two-component unlink, equivalently ST#ST. Its
separating sphere is S. Let x1,x2 be the two core knots and y their standard
connected sum through the neck. Put R=Z[A,A^-1], delta=-A^2-A^-2.
The explicit source relation is
  (A^2-A^-2)([x1 disjoint-union x2]-delta[y])=0.
Its two actual link representatives are x1 disjoint-union x2 and y
 disjoint-union U, each with two components.

Primary source checked this pass: Bakshi--Le--Przytycki,
https://arxiv.org/html/2604.09971v1 , Sections 2.2, 3.1, 3.5.
The source develops handle-slide relations by using marked relative arcs;
it does not supply a band-compatible knot-minus-unknot version of J1.

1. The banded J1 proposal re-enters the old one-band problem
----------------------------------------------------------
In every reciprocal-integer filling of the two axes giving S^3, both
unbanded links above are unframed U_2. Any single band connecting the two
components therefore produces a fusion-one ribbon knot. If one endpoint
is U and the other is nontrivial, the latter is U#_b U for a nontrivial band b.

Miyazaki's theorem says that a band sum of a split pair J,K equals J#K if
and only if the band is trivial (a splitting sphere meets it in one cocore).
Consequently band triviality, not the closed torsion class, is exactly what
separates the unknot from a nontrivial knot for these filled U_2 inputs.
Primary source:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.11266 , Theorem 1, definition preceding it.
This theorem and its relevant application were already used in passes21/23.
They must not be counted as new mathematics here.

The exact remaining Jones condition is also old: Wang's band-twist formula
for a split input is V(K_{b+n})=V(J#K)+q^(4n)P_b (consistent normalization).
For J=K=U, a nontrivial Jones-one band would require P_b=0. The absence of a
proof that nontrivial b forces P_b nonzero was explicitly isolated in
work/research21/ribbon_euler_audit.txt. That note independently audited
Wang's primary source https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01070 .
Thus the current closed torsion witness does not give a new family of
candidate bands; a concrete marked lift would be the new ingredient.

The lack of extra geometry is substantial: start with ANY one-band ribbon
presentation U_2 -> K. Around small arcs of the two unlink components,
away from band attachment arcs and band interior, choose disjoint standard
meridian circles C1,C2 in disjoint small balls. They form an unlink and are
disjoint from the band. Each pair (Ui,Ci) is a standard Hopf pair in its
unlink-component ball. The U_i represent x_i in the resulting M. Hence the
same unbanded J1 relation is available before applying this arbitrary band.
It imposes no Jones condition on the banded result. This is an explicit
geometric universality statement for the unbanded setup, not a realization
of its missing relative lift.

2. A two-intersection sphere gate reduces to one-axis patterns
-------------------------------------------------------------
Suppose an ACTUAL framed knot P in M intersects S transversely in exactly
two points. Cap its two pieces by the standard boundary arcs in the
punctured solid tori, obtaining framed solid-torus knots P1,P2. Then P is
P1#P2 through the neck. Let pi(xi) be their integral annular skein classes.
Over F=Q(A), the connected-sum skein is F[x1,x2]. In this module,

  delta[P] = p1(x1)p2(x2).                                      (1)

One justification of (1) retains all geometry. Each punctured solid torus
contains a regular neighborhood of its spherical boundary, a thin arc
leading to P_i, and a solid-torus neighborhood of P_i. This is a punctured
solid torus with the inner spherical boundary fixed. Gluing the two such
neighborhoods along S gives an embedded ST#ST in M whose core curves map
to P1,P2 and whose connected-sum core maps to P. Apply the actual n=1
source relation by this embedding, and invert A^2-A^-2. The construction
uses an embedding, which induces a skein map; it does not attach a band to
an unmarked class. Framings of the capped pieces are chosen to sum to the
framing of P.

The generic connected-sum identification itself is established in
Przytycki, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9911120 , Theorem1.2/Corollary1.3.
No use is made here of that paper's later integral Theorem7.1, whose general
claim was subsequently corrected/disproved; only the generic theorem and
the explicit ST#ST low-degree relation are needed.

Assume now [P]-[y] is R-torsion. Since delta[y]=x1x2 generically, (1) gives

  p1(x1)p2(x2)=x1x2.

Independence of the two polynomial variables forces p1=c1*x1 and
p2=c2*x2 with c1*c2=1. Since both p_i are integral Laurent polynomials,
c1 and c2 are units of R, hence c1=+/- A^r and c2=c1^-1.

Consequently a putative torsion counterexample with only two neck
intersections already contains one-axis patterns whose annular brackets
are monomial multiples of the core. In any S^3 filling P becomes the
ordinary connected sum of the filled P1 and P2. Its normalized Jones
polynomial is their product, so if V(P)=1 each factor is a Laurent unit;
V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0 force each factor to equal1. If P is nontrivial, at
least one factor is a nontrivial Jones-one knot.

This proves no detection theorem. It says that a genuinely new interaction
from the two-axis torsion setup must avoid this simple two-intersection
neck situation; otherwise it reduces to the earlier one-axis Jones-trivial
pattern problem. Since S separates, transverse intersection counts are
even. A P disjoint from S lies entirely in one summand and cannot have the
generic class of y, which depends on both variables. Thus any torsion
candidate not reducible as above must have geometric intersection at least
four with the chosen reducing sphere.

3. A whole family of geometric operations is automatically harmless
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Let phi:M->M be orientation preserving and preserve the filling slopes
on the boundary tori (allowing simultaneous permutation of tori/slopes).
It extends over the filling solid tori. If that filling is S^3, the extended
map carries P to phi(P), so these are the same knot type in S^3. For the
unknot conclusion no mapping-class theorem is needed: the extended map
carries an embedded spanning disk to an embedded spanning disk.

In particular, sphere twists or sphere-slide homeomorphisms supported in
the interior of M cannot turn an unknot into a nontrivial knot in any fixed
S^3 filling. The same is true for a band carried along by the homeomorphism.
A relation created solely by these genuine geometric moves cannot give the
needed knot pair. One needs a skein-linear relation not realized by a
slope-preserving homeomorphism of the marked objects.

This statement does NOT assert that all torsion relations come from such
homeomorphisms. The source's closed J1 relation changes connectivity and
is obtained by taking a linear combination of resolved diagrams. It is
exactly that linear step whose endpoint data cannot be discarded.

Conclusion / next-action consequence
------------------------------------
Do not search arbitrary one-band diagrams merely because J1 is available:
that reopens the previous fusion-one P_b problem without adding a constraint.
Do not use sphere-supported homeomorphism or a two-point neck factorization
as a source of new Jones-one topology. A viable genuinely new torsion
construction would need an explicit marked relation with interacting neck
strands (at least four for a knot candidate), together with a band that
survives in that relation and an independent nontriviality certificate.
No such construction was found in this bounded pass.
