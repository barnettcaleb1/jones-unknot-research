PASS 50: FRAMED RATIONAL TANGLE UNIQUENESS FROM FILLING THEORY
1 November 2026. Research deduction from established results; independent
audit requested. No claim of literature novelty or a Jones detection proof.
The main proof below covers two nonsplit products. The appended Ni-Vafaee
argument closes the remaining split-product case, giving all distinct-
slope systems in Sikora's Conjecture 2, subject to the stated proof audit.

THEOREM (nonzero-determinant case)
Let X and R be tangles in the same oriented marked ball, with R rational.
Let P and Q be rational closing tangles with distinct underlying slopes.
Suppose the corresponding closed links agree by balanced framed isotopy:

             C_P(X)=C_P(R),       C_Q(X)=C_Q(R).

Suppose both common products are nonsplit rational links, equivalently
their determinants are nonzero. Then X is rational. Consequently Sikora's
uniqueness of framed rational solutions makes X equal to R as a balanced
framed tangle.

In particular, the proposed construction using two nontrivial rational
products cannot be repaired by searching other A'/B' parameters. Their
branch involutions and three lens fillings do not evade this theorem.
The proof is uniform and requires neither census enumeration nor a claimed
extension of the S^3 lens-surgery characterization to homology spheres.

PROOF

1. Two probes give the full bracket, hence all closure determinants.

Sikora, Theorem 21, says the bracket vectors of two solutions to this
framed system are equal. Therefore [X]=[R]. Every other rational closure
also has equal brackets and equal determinants. With the convention
that the unreduced unknot bracket is delta, divide the polynomial bracket
of a nonempty closed link by delta before evaluating at A=exp(pi*i/4).
Directly evaluating the unreduced bracket would instead vanish identically.
Framing changes at this root have absolute value one, so determinants are
independent of the chosen representative framings.

2. Recover a common meridian and preferred longitude without assuming S^3.

Let M be the oriented double branched cover of X and V the solid-torus
cover of R. Their boundary tori carry the same marking. Choose slopes
mu and lambda at distance one such that V(mu)=S^3 and
V(lambda)=S^1 x S^2. These correspond to rational closures of R that
are respectively the unknot and two-component unlink.

Step 1 gives |H_1(M(mu);Z)|=1 and infinite H_1(M(lambda);Z). Write
Y=M(mu). It is an integral homology sphere; it is not yet known to be
S^3. The core of its filling solid torus is a knot K with exterior M.
Thus H_1(M;Z)=Z, and the unique primitive slope giving infinite first
homology after filling is the preferred longitude of K. It is lambda.
The oriented common boundary marking therefore identifies mu and lambda
with the meridian and longitude of the unknot exterior V.

Write the original two slopes as p_i mu+q_i lambda, with coprime p_i,q_i.
Nonzero determinants mean p_i != 0. Distinct slopes imply
q_1/p_1 != q_2/p_2. The balanced framed equivalences in the hypothesis
are orientation-preserving ambient equivalences of the underlying branch
links. Lifting them gives orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms

              Y_(p_i/q_i)(K) = S^3_(p_i/q_i)(U),   i=1,2.

These are the SAME signed surgery coefficients on both sides. This is
stronger than equality of lens-space orders, and stronger than an
unoriented homeomorphism of lens spaces. The common peripheral marking
and the auxiliary determinant-zero closure are essential here.

3. Casson-Walker forces the Alexander second derivative to vanish.

Let Delta_K(t) be the symmetric Alexander polynomial normalized by
Delta_K(1)=1. Ni-Wu, Proposition 3.1, gives for a null-homologous knot in
a rational homology sphere, in their Casson normalization,

 lambda_CW(Y_(p/q)(K)) - lambda_CW(S^3_(p/q)(U))
       = lambda_CW(Y) + (q/(2p))*Delta_K''(1).

Our K lies in an integral homology sphere, so this applies. The left
side is zero for both i. Subtracting the two equations yields

             Delta_K''(1)=0,       lambda_CW(Y)=0.

The meridional q=0 case can be read directly as lambda_CW(Y)=0; in the
intended case where both products are nontrivial, neither probe is mu.
No inference that lambda_CW(Y)=0 implies Y=S^3 is made.

4. Floer simplicity turns this vanishing into Delta_K=1.

Each of the two fillings is a lens space and hence an L-space. Since the
slopes are distinct, Rasmussen-Rasmussen Proposition 1.3 implies M is
Floer simple. Their Proposition 1.4 says its normalized Turaev torsion
has only coefficients zero and one. Since H_1(M)=Z, it has the form

      tau(M)=D(t)/(1-t)=sum_(n>=0) a_n t^n,
      a_0=1, a_n in {0,1}, and a_n=1 for all sufficiently large n.

Here D(t) is the polynomial normalization of the Alexander polynomial.
Its coefficient at n is a_n-a_(n-1), with a_(-1)=0. Thus, after zero
coefficients are suppressed, its coefficients alternate +1,-1,+1,...,+1.
The first coefficient is +1. The final nonzero coefficient is also +1,
since the sequence a_n eventually returns to and remains at one.

For a knot in an integral homology sphere, symmetry gives
D(t)=t^g Delta_K(t), where 2g is the degree of D (this g is an exponent,
not an assumed topological genus). Consequently there are strictly
increasing positive integers n_1<...<n_k such that

 Delta_K(t)=(-1)^k + sum_(i=1)^k (-1)^(k-i)*(t^(n_i)+t^(-n_i)).

To justify the nonzero central term: all noncentral coefficients occur
in symmetric pairs, whereas D(1)=1 and all its nonzero coefficients are
plus or minus one. Its number of nonzero coefficients is odd, and hence
the central coefficient is nonzero. Alternation then gives the displayed
signs. The symmetric normalization has Delta_K(1)=1 because D(1)=1.

Differentiating the displayed polynomial at one gives

          Delta_K''(1)=2*sum_(i=1)^k (-1)^(k-i)*n_i^2.

If k is positive, group from the largest exponent downward: every pair
n_j^2-n_(j-1)^2 is strictly positive, with an additional positive n_1^2
when k is odd. Therefore Delta_K''(1)>0 for every nonconstant possibility.
Step 3 forces k=0 and Delta_K(t)=1. This is the crucial use of the TWO
L-space fillings; Delta''=0 alone would not suffice for a general knot.

5. The Thurston norm forces a boundary compression.

Rasmussen-Rasmussen Corollary 2.3 says that, for boundary-incompressible
Floer-simple M, the Thurston norm of the relative generator equals
deg(D)-1. Here deg(D)=0, which would give norm -1, impossible. Thus
the boundary of M is compressible.

A connected orientable torus-boundary manifold with compressible boundary
is a solid torus connected summed with a closed manifold Z. Since
H_1(M)=Z, Z is an integral homology sphere. Filling a slope with nonzero
determinant produces L(p,q)#Z. But one of our actual fillings is a lens
space, and lens spaces are prime; by prime decomposition Z is S^3.
This also covers a filling yielding S^3. Therefore M is a solid torus.
The classical branched-cover criterion then makes X a rational tangle.
End proof.

WHAT HAS BEEN EXCLUDED, AND WHAT HAS NOT

All actual two-probe systems with a rational comparator and TWO NONSPLIT
rational products are excluded as a source of a rational/nonrational
framed ambiguity. This includes every attempted member of the explicit
Baker-Doleshal-Hoffman A'/B' branch-tangle families if it satisfies those
two marked product conditions. A parameter scan is unnecessary: successful
unframed matches must retain a varying framing defect.

The argument through two L-space fillings does not itself cover a system
with one product the two-component unlink. The separate argument below,
identified by the relative_torsion agent and independently source-checked
here and by the parent, closes that case. It does not incorrectly treat
S^1 x S^2 as an L-space.

This is a proof for a tangle-equation subclass, not a proof that the Jones
polynomial detects the unknot. Its topological hypotheses require actual
link equivalences, whereas a putative Jones-one knot supplies only equal
polynomials. Sikora's Theorem 3 is a one-way implication from Jones
detection to framed uniqueness; the converse must not be inferred.
Literature novelty has not been established.

APPENDIX: THE ZERO-DETERMINANT EDGE AND THE FULL TANGLE STATEMENT

Suppose one common product is the two-component unlink. Its slope is
lambda, and M(lambda)=S^1 x S^2 geometrically. As above, bracket equality
still supplies an auxiliary determinant-one closure, so H_1(M)=Z and
lambda is the primitive homological longitude. The other probe differs
from lambda. Its rational product is therefore nonsplit, so its cover is
a lens space and is an L-space.

Let L be the core of the lambda-filling solid torus in S^1 x S^2. Its
exterior is M, and it has an L-space surgery. Ni-Vafaee, Null surgery on
knots in L-spaces, Theorem 1.1, says that M fibers over S^1. Choose a
connected fiber F, corresponding to a primitive class of H^1(M;Z)=Z.

The boundary map H_2(M,partial M;Z) -> H_1(partial M;Z) identifies the
relative generator with the primitive lambda. Indeed M has the homology
of a solid torus, and its peripheral inclusion sends the auxiliary
meridian to a generator of H_1(M). The oriented boundary circles of a
fiber are all parallel with coherent orientations on the boundary torus.
If there were b such circles, [partial F] would equal +/-b*lambda. But
the fiber class is primitive and the boundary map has primitive image,
so b=1.

Fill at lambda and cap the single boundary circle of F with a disk. The
monodromy extends over the disk, yielding a closed surface bundle over
S^1 whose total space is M(lambda)=S^1 x S^2. Its connected-fiber bundle
map induces an epimorphism pi_1(S^1 x S^2)=Z -> Z, hence an isomorphism.
The fundamental group of the closed fiber, which injects as its kernel,
is trivial. Thus the closed fiber is a sphere, F is a disk, and M is a
solid torus. This proves X rational also in the remaining case.

There cannot be two split rational products at distinct slopes: the
solid-torus comparator has only one homological-longitude slope.
Together with the main proof, all possible rational products are covered.
The result therefore proves the distinct-slope framed uniqueness
statement formulated as Sikora's Conjecture 2, as a deduction from the
cited theorems, pending independent mathematical verification.

Allowing closed circle components in a general 2-tangle does not leave
another case. The auxiliary closure with determinant one has double
branched cover with trivial H_1, and so its branch link has exactly one
component: for a link with c components, the mod-2 first Betti number of
its double branched cover is c-1. A closed circle component of X would
survive alongside at least one component made from the two open strands
in every closure, contradicting this. Therefore any possible solution
already has exactly two strings and no closed components.

The explicit hypotheses still require actual balanced framed link
equivalences, common markings, and distinct underlying slopes. Equality
of Jones polynomials alone cannot replace these geometric hypotheses.
Consequently this does not solve generic Jones unknot detection.

OPTIONAL HIDDEN-S^3 EXTENSION OF PASS49

Before the stronger two-nonsplit-products argument, there is a useful
separate observation: if [X]=[R] and the branched-cover exterior M admits
ANY S^3 filling, even one not among the observed probes, then this is an
unknot closure of X. The double-cover involution of S^3 has nonempty
fixed set; Smith theory gives a single fixed circle, and the Smith
conjecture implies the quotient branch link is an unknot. The comparator
closure has determinant one by bracket equality and is rational, so it
is also the unknot. Use this auxiliary unknot probe and either distinct
actual rational product in the pass49 same-slope KMOS proof. This excludes
every hidden-S^3 member without treating arbitrary integral homology
spheres as S^3. The result is subsumed by the main theorem when both
observed products have nonzero determinant.
