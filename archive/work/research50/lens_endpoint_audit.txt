VERDICT
No remaining substantive gap found in the combined proof, with the explicit
normalization and orientation precautions below. The two nonzero filling
case and the one zero filling case exhaust the system in Sikora's Conjecture
2, because the two probe tangles have DISTINCT UNDERLYING RATIONAL SLOPES.
The paper explicitly imposes distinctness as unframed tangles in §4.1.
The argument also excludes closed-loop components of an unknown tangle.
Consequently its logical scope is the full stated Conjecture 2, rather than
only a two-string subclass, if the elementary reductions below are included.
This verdict is an internal mathematical audit, not external expert review.

1. THE FRAMED INPUT IS STRONG ENOUGH, AND IS USED CORRECTLY.
Sikora Theorem 21 provides equality of full two-entry bracket vectors for
any two solutions of the same system. Balanced framed isotopy preserves the
bracket and implies ambient isotopy of the underlying links in oriented S3.
Sikora's bracket convention normalizes the unknot to 1. If one instead uses
the unreduced skein convention with unknot delta, divide the bracket of a
nonempty closed link by delta BEFORE evaluating at A=exp(pi*i/4).
That division is a Laurent-polynomial identity, not division by the complex
number zero. The absolute value of the resulting root evaluation is the
link determinant. Framing units have absolute value one there.

Equality of determinants alone would not provide the two oriented lens-space
identifications needed in the main argument. The input really does provide
them, by lifting the actual branch-link isotopies. Likewise, an orientation-
reversing equivalence or a comparison of lens-space orders is insufficient.
The theorem must preserve its common peripheral marking throughout.

2. CLOSED LOOPS DO NOT REQUIRE AN EXTRA HYPOTHESIS.
Choose a rational auxiliary closure C for which C(R) is an unknot. Bracket
vector equality gives equality of the brackets of C(X) and C(R).
There is an elementary component-count check: for an r-component nonempty
link L, the absolute value of its reduced bracket at A=1 is 2^(r-1).
Proof: at A=1 the skein expansions of a crossing and its switch coincide.
Crossing switches turn L into the r-component unlink; Reidemeister-I changes
only the sign at A=1, and the unlink evaluates to (-2)^(r-1).
Thus the auxiliary closure C(X) has one component. A closed loop in X remains
a separate closed component under every rational closing tangle, while the
two arcs contribute at least one component. Therefore X contains no loops.
This proof is self-contained; the determinant-odd-if-and-only-if-knot fact
would provide an alternative. The supplied Stoimenow PDF URL returned 403 to
this auditor, so its proposition was not independently read here.

3. COMMON INTEGRAL PERIPHERAL COORDINATES ARE RECOVERED, NOT ASSUMED.
Let M and V denote the double covers of X and the rational comparator R.
Choose a V-unknot filling mu and V-unlink filling lambda with distance one.
Auxiliary closure determinants give H1(M(mu))=0 and b1(M(lambda))>0.
The first condition implies H1(M)=Z: it is a group generated by the image of
mu, is infinite since a connected oriented torus-boundary manifold has first
Betti number at least one, and is therefore infinite cyclic. The second
condition then identifies lambda with the primitive kernel slope of
H1(boundary M)->H1(M). Equivalently, M is the exterior of the filling core
K in the integral homology sphere Y=M(mu), with preferred longitude lambda.
No claim that Y is S3 occurs at this stage.

There are infinitely many auxiliary V-unknot slopes mu+n lambda. Hence choose
mu different from either original slope if desired. This removes q=0 from
the two Casson-Walker applications, although it is not mathematically needed.
The original slope coordinates p_i mu+q_i lambda are the SAME signed surgery
coordinates for M and V; that is the crucial force of the auxiliary longitude
identification. Distinct slopes with p_i nonzero have different q_i/p_i.

4. REDUCIBILITY CAN BE REMOVED BEFORE THE TORSION STEP.
At least one of the two rational products is not the two-component unlink,
because V has a unique slope giving infinite first homology. Hence at least
one filling of M is a lens space, allowing S3 as the order-one case.
Prime-decompose M. There is one piece with the torus boundary. Every closed
summand has H1=0, since the boundary piece already has positive first Betti
number and H1(M)=Z. These closed summands survive every boundary filling.
A lens space has no nontrivial integral-homology-sphere connected summand,
by prime decomposition. Thus all closed summands are S3. M is irreducible.
This also excludes nonseparating-sphere summands, which would add an extra Z.

This early reduction is recommended for exposition. In particular it avoids
any distracting ambiguity from Rasmussen--Rasmussen §2.1's discussion of
Delta versus barDelta in connected-sum examples. For the integral knot
exterior here, the normalized torsion is the usual Alexander polynomial
divided by 1-t, up to the stated monomial normalization.

5. THE TWO NONZERO FILLING CASE PASSES.
The actual oriented lens identifications say
Y_(p_i/q_i)(K) is orientation-preservingly homeomorphic to L(p_i,q_i).
Ni--Wu Proposition 3.1 applies to null-homologous knots in arbitrary rational
homology spheres, not merely knots in S3 despite the title of that paper.
With symmetric Delta_K and Delta_K(1)=1 its formula is
lambda(Y_p/q(K))=lambda(Y)+lambda(L(p,q))+(q/(2p))*Delta_K''(1).
Subtracting the two resulting linear equations gives Delta_K''(1)=0 and
lambda(Y)=0. The latter alone is never used to identify Y with S3.

Rasmussen--Rasmussen Proposition 1.3 gives Floer simplicity from the two
DISTINCT L-space filling slopes. Proposition 1.4 gives normalized torsion
coefficients a_n in {0,1}, with a_0=1 and eventual constant tail 1. Therefore
D=(1-t)tau has nonzero coefficients alternating +1,-1,...,+1. Symmetry of
the integral knot Alexander polynomial gives D=t^g Delta_K with degree 2g.
The symmetric polynomial has a nonzero central coefficient: its noncentral
coefficients occur in pairs, and its value at 1 is odd. Write its positive
exponents 0<n_1<...<n_k. Then
Delta_K=(-1)^k+sum_i (-1)^(k-i)(t^n_i+t^-n_i), and
Delta_K''(1)=2*sum_i (-1)^(k-i)*n_i^2.
For k>0, pair terms downwards from the largest exponent. Each paired square
difference is positive, with a positive n_1^2 left over when k is odd.
Thus Delta_K''(1)=0 forces k=0, Delta_K=1, and D=1.
It is essential to differentiate the SYMMETRIC polynomial, not D.

Rasmussen--Rasmussen Corollary 2.3 says a boundary-incompressible Floer-simple
manifold has Thurston norm deg(barDelta)-1. Here H1=Z and barDelta=D=1;
this would give norm -1. Hence boundary is compressible. Together with the
early irreducibility reduction, this immediately gives M a solid torus.
Alternatively the draft's later connected-sum removal also gives that result.

6. THE ZERO-SLOPE EDGE PASSES.
If one rational product is the unlink, M(lambda)=S1 x S2. The other product
is nonsplit rational and gives a lens space, hence an L-space filling.
View M as the exterior of the core L of the lambda filling in S1 x S2.
Ni--Vafaee Theorem 1.1 exactly states that a knot in S1 x S2 with some
L-space surgery has fibered complement. No longitudinal-surgery or prior
irreducibility hypothesis is missing from its statement.

Choose the connected-fiber version of the fibration M->S1. Its map on H1 is
an isomorphism since H1(M)=Z. The boundary inclusion surjects on H1: the
relative group H1(M,boundary M)=H^2(M)=0 for an integral homology solid
torus. Thus the fibration restricted to the boundary torus surjects on H1.
If the surface fiber met that boundary in k circles, its boundary fibration
would have image kZ; hence k=1. The fiber boundary has slope lambda.
Capping this one boundary circle by the lambda filling extends the bundle
to a closed-surface bundle over S1 with total space S1 x S2. Its fiber group
injects into Z. A closed oriented positive-genus surface group cannot do so.
The capped fiber is a sphere, the original fiber is a disk, and M is a solid
torus. This is a complete endpoint, not just a genus bound.

7. EXHAUSTION AND FINAL IDENTIFICATION.
For a solid-torus comparator, every rational closing slope produces a lens
space or S1 x S2. The latter occurs at precisely the longitude. Two distinct
slopes therefore lie either in case 5 or in case 6. No two-zero case exists.
Once the cover of the two-string X is a solid torus, the classical rational-
tangle criterion makes X rational. Sikora Corollary 20 or Proposition 1 then
identifies its balanced framing and rational slope with R, giving uniqueness.

This covers the full stated framed rational-solution uniqueness conjecture.
It does not imply Jones unknot detection: Sikora Theorem 3 asserts the forward
implication Jones detection => framed uniqueness, and the converse has not
been established. The proof uses actual link isotopies in addition to bracket
equality. Merely knowing V_K=1 cannot supply those isotopies.
