Pass 50: the S1 x S2 / lens-space edge case is closed
2026-11-01

PROPOSITION. Let M be a compact connected orientable 3-manifold with boundary
a torus and the integral homology of a solid torus. Let lambda be its rational
longitude. If M(lambda) is S1 x S2 and some other Dehn filling M(alpha) is an
L-space, then M is a solid torus.

In particular this applies when M(alpha) is a nonsplit-rational-link double
branched cover, hence a lens space with finite first homology. It needs no
matching of the oriented lens-space parameter q with a reference solid torus.

SOURCE. Yi Ni and Faramarz Vafaee, 'Null surgery on knots in L-spaces',
Transactions AMS 372 (2019), 8279--8306, Theorem 1.1, printed page 8280
(PDF page 2): a knot in S1 x S2 that admits an L-space surgery has fibered
complement. The published primary PDF was read and the exact statement checked:
https://www.its.caltech.edu/~yini/Published/NullSurgery.pdf
DOI: https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/7510
The statement has no irreducibility or longitudinal-surgery hypothesis.

PROOF. Regard M as the exterior of the core L of the lambda filling in
M(lambda)=S1 x S2. The alpha filling is a surgery on L producing an L-space.
Ni--Vafaee Theorem 1.1 makes M a bundle f:M -> S1 with connected compact
oriented surface fiber F (choose the primitive fibration class).

The inclusion H1(boundary M;Z) -> H1(M;Z) is surjective: its cokernel maps
injectively into H1(M,boundary M;Z), and this relative group equals
H^2(M;Z)=0 by Poincare--Lefschetz duality and the homology-solid-torus
hypothesis. Also f_*:H1(M;Z) -> Z is an isomorphism because it is surjective
and H1(M;Z)=Z. Therefore the composite H1(boundary M;Z) -> Z is onto.

The boundary restriction of f is a bundle of the torus over the circle. If its
fiber had k circle components, its image on fundamental groups, hence on H1,
would be kZ (monodromy cyclically permutes the k components). Surjectivity
therefore gives k=1. Thus F has exactly one boundary component. This boundary
lies in the kernel of H1(boundary M) -> H1(M), so its slope is lambda.

Capping F with a disk and extending its monodromy over that disk extends f to
a bundle of M(lambda)=S1 x S2 over S1 with closed connected orientable fiber
F_hat. In any surface bundle, pi1(F_hat) injects into pi1(total space).
Here the total group is Z. A closed connected orientable surface of positive
genus has a fundamental group that cannot inject into Z. Consequently F_hat
is S2, and F is a disk. An orientable disk bundle over S1 is a solid torus.
QED.

APPLICATION TO THE FRAMED TWO-PROBE AUDIT. If the earlier bracket/homology
argument really supplies H1(M)=Z, identifies the reference longitude lambda,
and a shared rational product is the two-component unlink, then its filling is
S1 x S2. A second shared nonsplit rational product supplies the required lens
space L-space filling. This proposition resolves precisely that edge case.
It does not audit the separate argument intended to establish the integral
homology hypothesis, nor the two-nonzero-filling Casson--Walker/Rasmussen chain.
