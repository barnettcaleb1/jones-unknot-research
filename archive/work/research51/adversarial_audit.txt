2. THE DECISIVE TEST: LOCAL KNOT INSERTION
Let K be any knot, choose the trivial 2-tangle R, and tie K locally into one
of R's arcs. Call the resulting tangle T_K. After correcting the framing,
its full bracket vector is

                 [T_K]=V_K(A^-4)[R].

The local knotting ball has a sphere boundary meeting the branch set twice.
Its double cover is a separating sphere. Consequently

       M_K = Sigma_2(T_K) = (S1 x D2) # Sigma_2(K).

For any nonlongitudinal filling slope p/q,

       M_K(p/q) = L(p,q) # Sigma_2(K).

These statements concern actual geometry, not merely formal skein vectors.
If K were a Jones-one counterexample, T_K would have exactly the rational
bracket [R]. Also det K=1, so Z=Sigma_2(K) would be an integral homology sphere.

Now suppose the proposed bridge were granted. It would say M_K is Floer
simple. But the connected-sum formula for hat Heegaard Floer homology gives

       rank HFhat(L(p,q)#Z) = |p| rank HFhat(Z).

Therefore, for this entire local-knot family,

       M_K is Floer simple  <=>  Z is an L-space.

The reverse implication follows because every p!=0 filling is then an
L-space; the forward implication follows from any L-space filling. The
longitudinal filling has positive first Betti number and cannot be an L-space.
RR Proposition 1.3 identifies the multiple-filling criterion with Floer
simplicity. Their Lemma 2.8 and the surrounding discussion record the relevant
connected-sum tensor-product behavior.

An integral homology-sphere L-space need not be S3. Thus this bridge would
at best force the putative counterexample's double cover to be an L-space;
it would not prove its fundamental group trivial. The tangle cover is already
boundary compressible regardless of whether Z is S3. Applying a Thurston-norm
argument cannot discard Z. There is no contradiction in a manifold of the
form (S1 x D2)#Z being Floer simple but not a solid torus.

This locates the precise failed inference in an attempted generic reuse of
pass 50: replacing 'actual lens-space fillings' by 'L-space fillings' loses
the prime-decomposition argument that removes Z. Those two topological inputs
are not interchangeable.

The obstruction is robust to simple Casson-invariant repairs. Replace K by
K#mirror(K). It still has Jones polynomial one if K does, while its double
cover is Z#(-Z). If Z is an L-space then this is also an L-space, and its
Casson invariant vanishes by additivity and orientation reversal. These
facts do not imply Z#(-Z)=S3. Thus even adding zero Casson invariant to the
proposed Floer-simple bridge leaves this same local-summand gap.

This diagnostic is NOT a constructed Jones-one knot or a counterexample to
the bridge. It proves that the proposed bridge-plus-current-endpoint is an
incomplete proof scaffold. The actual generic hypothesis V_K=1 has not been
converted into topology excluding the summand Z. Claiming otherwise would
hide the original problem inside a new assumption.

3. DECISION
Keep the internally checked two-actual-closure theorem as a partial result.
Do not describe the Floer-simple bridge as known, as supported by RR, or as
by itself sufficient to finish the generic theorem. No proof of that bridge,
and no mechanism removing the residual Z, has emerged. The honest generic

new search through lens-filling or tangle parameter families is justified.
