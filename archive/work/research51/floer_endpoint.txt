PASS 51: WHAT THE FLOER ENDPOINT PROVES, AND THE TWO MISSING BRIDGES
1 November 2026. Bounded audit; no new Jones theorem or counterexample.

1. The proposed conditional endpoint is correct.

Let M be a compact connected oriented manifold with one torus boundary
component and H_1(M;Z)=Z, realized as the exterior of a knot in an integral
homology sphere. Suppose M is Floer simple and its symmetrically normalized
Alexander polynomial satisfies Delta(1)=1 and Delta''(1)=0.

Then M is a solid torus connected summed with an integral homology sphere Z.
Moreover Z is an L-space. If M is irreducible, Z=S^3 and M is a solid torus.
If M is a double branched tangle cover, the latter conclusion implies that
the tangle is rational.

Proof. Rasmussen-Rasmussen Proposition 1.4 gives coefficients 0 or 1 for
the normalized torsion D(t)/(1-t). Consecutive differences imply the
nonzero coefficients of D alternate +1,-1,+1,...,+1. Symmetry gives

 Delta(t)=(-1)^k + sum_(i=1)^k (-1)^(k-i)*(t^n_i+t^-n_i),
                  0<n_1<...<n_k.

Thus Delta''(1)=2 sum_i (-1)^(k-i)n_i^2 is strictly positive unless k=0:
pair terms from the largest exponent downward. Our hypothesis implies
Delta=1. If M had incompressible boundary, Rasmussen-Rasmussen Corollary
2.3 would give Thurston norm deg(D)-1=-1. Therefore the boundary compresses,
and elementary prime decomposition gives M=(S^1 x D^2)#Z. Homology makes
Z an integral homology sphere.

For each nonlongitudinal slope p/q,

                    M(p/q)=L(p,q)#Z.

The connected-sum formula for hat Heegaard Floer homology, over a field,
gives rank HFhat(M(p/q))=|p|*rank HFhat(Z). Consequently one such filling
is an L-space iff Z is an L-space. Floer simplicity supplies an L-space
filling, so Z is an L-space. Conversely, if Z is an L-space, every
nonlongitudinal filling is one, and Rasmussen-Rasmussen Proposition 1.3
makes M Floer simple. Finally an irreducible M cannot contain the nontrivial
connected-sum sphere separating Z, so Z=S^3. End proof.

This argument is the correct extension of pass50. In pass50 an ACTUAL
lens filling eliminated Z by prime decomposition. Bracket equality by
itself does not supply such a filling.

2. Even strengthened numerical endpoint data do not eliminate Z.

Let P be the Poincare homology sphere and set Z=P#(-P). Both orientations
of P are L-spaces; their connected sum is an L-space. It is a nontrivial
integral homology sphere, but Casson additivity and orientation reversal
give lambda(Z)=0. Its correction term likewise vanishes by additivity
and orientation reversal. Take

                         M=(S^1 x D^2)#Z.

Then H_1(M)=Z, Delta_M=1, Delta_M''(1)=0, M is Floer simple, and its
meridional filling is an L-space homology sphere with Casson invariant
zero. Yet M is not a solid torus. Its nonlongitudinal fillings are
L(p,q)#P#(-P), not lens spaces.

More strongly, for EVERY p != 0 and each Spin^c structure s,

 lambda_CW(M(p/q)) = lambda_CW(L(p,q)),
 d(M(p/q),s) = d(L(p,q),s),
 rank HFhat(M(p/q)) = |p|.

Thus all these fillings are L-spaces with exactly the lens-space
Casson-Walker invariants and correction terms. The boundary longitude
and the Alexander polynomial also agree with the solid-torus model.
This remains insufficient to identify the filled manifolds topologically.
Ozsvath-Szabo explicitly discuss this very Z as an 'invisible' homology
sphere in Absolutely graded Floer homologies, Section 6 (printed page 34).
This example and its Floer phenomenon are established literature, not
a new construction from this pass.

This is an actual counterexample to the ABSTRACT endpoint inference
from those homological/Floer/Casson data. It is not claimed to satisfy
the original rational-bracket equality, and it is not a Jones
counterexample. It shows exactly why the closed summand cannot be erased
by extending the numerical part of the argument.
It is deliberately a REDUCIBLE model; it does not refute a stronger
statement restricted to irreducible homology spheres or irreducible M.

3. Local knot insertion locates the unresolved generic case precisely.

Start with a rational tangle R. In a small ball meeting one arc once,
replace the trivial subarc by a long version of a knot J. Denote the
resulting tangle X_J. The one-strand ball skein module has rank one,
so, after the usual framing normalization, this insertion multiplies
the bracket of every closure by V_J(A^-4). In particular,

              V_J=1  implies  [X_J]=[R].

The double branched cover of the local ball is the double branched cover
Sigma_2(J) with a ball removed. The local insertion therefore changes
the exterior by a connected sum:

              M_J=(S^1 x D^2)#Sigma_2(J).

If J were a nontrivial Jones-one knot, determinant one would make
Sigma_2(J) an integral homology sphere. Smith's theorem implies it is
not S^3. Hence M_J is reducible and the closed-summand obstacle is present
for EVERY counterexample inserted by this elementary construction.

The Alexander polynomial of this exterior as a knot exterior in the
auxiliary homology sphere is already 1: its meridional core is an unknot
contained in a ball of Sigma_2(J). Thus Delta''=0 is automatic in this
family and supplies no extra restriction on J. Also,

 M_J is Floer simple  iff  Sigma_2(J) is an L-space.

The equivalence follows from the filling/connected-sum calculation in
Section 1, and is independent of any conjectural Jones detection claim.

Therefore a proposed universal implication

          [X]=[rational R]  implies  cover(X) is Floer simple

would already entail the substantial unproved assertion

          V_J=1  implies  Sigma_2(J) is an L-space.

Even granting that assertion leaves the nontrivial L-space summand
Sigma_2(J) to eliminate. Adding irreducibility of M silently excludes
the local insertion construction and is not a generic reduction.

4. Existing categorification does not provide the missing implication.

Ozsvath-Szabo construct a spectral sequence from reduced Khovanov homology
(with the appropriate mirror convention) to hat Heegaard Floer homology
of the double branched cover. It gives

       rank HFhat(Sigma_2(J)) <= rank reduced Kh(mirror J).

The Jones polynomial is the graded Euler characteristic of Khovanov
homology. V_J=1 allows cancelling contributions in different homological
degrees; it does not bound the total rank above by one. Consequently
the spectral sequence cannot turn V_J=1 into an L-space conclusion
without a further theorem controlling that cancellation. Claiming that
it does would reverse the available inequalities.

For alternating/quasi-alternating branch links, established special
theorems give L-space covers. But no argument here converts an arbitrary
rational-bracket tangle or Jones-one knot to that geometric class.

5. What a genuinely independent input would have to do.

The independent topological endpoint is now complete: Floer simplicity
and Delta''=0 classify the exterior as a solid torus plus an L-space
homology-sphere summand. The missing input is not another surgery
calculation. It must either establish an actual L-space filling from
the bracket data AND exclude the closed summand, or construct a different
tangle from a general Jones-one knot with irreducible cover while retaining
enough justified Floer information. Neither operation has been supplied.

The Ozsvath-Szabo conjecture classifying irreducible integral-homology-
sphere L-spaces as S^3 or either orientation of the Poincare sphere is
an independently meaningful existing conjecture. It could constrain
irreducible prime summands after an L-space conclusion, but it is not a
proved input and does not by itself manufacture Floer simplicity or
cancel Poincare summands of opposite orientations. Invoking it would
add a major open problem rather than finish this proof.

Thus there is no newly established independent bridge to a generic
Jones proof in this pass. Calling the missing rank-control assertion
a new conjecture would merely rename the obstacle. The actual result
is the precise endpoint classification and the local-insertion stress
test showing why it does not already settle the generic question.
