# Quantum-control obstruction at width six

Status: candidate theorem with a complete algebraic proof below and an independent exact span certificate. Parent review pending. No novelty claim. This note serves U5-W6/U5-KL6 and does not prove either Key Lemma or Jones unknot detection.

The initially considered positivity/tomography direction is already represented in the archive (EARLY-08, EARLY-30, MID-15, LATE-26). It is not new progress. The useful additional result is an exact obstruction to a particular **extra operation** suggested by quantum control: applying a selective phase to the two-cap vacuum while fixing its eight-dimensional complement. This operation exists as a skein linear combination but cannot be a non-scalar generic braid operator.

## Theorem: no generic selective phase on a two-cap line

Put s=A^-2. Let rho:B_6 -> GL_9(Z[s,s^-1]) be the normalized Temperley-Lieb cell representation W(6,2), with generators

    T_i = I + s e_i,   delta=-(s+s^-1),
    (T_i-I)(T_i+s^2 I)=0.

Let

    P(s)=e_1 e_3 / delta^2.

This is the rank-one idempotent of the cap system (12)(34), with the remaining two points free. If b is any braid and alpha,gamma are rational functions in s satisfying

    rho(b)=alpha(s) I + gamma(s) P(s),

then gamma=0. Thus rho(b) is scalar. The same statement holds for every braid-conjugate of this projector.

### Proof step 1: the two eigenvalues are Laurent units

The eigenvalues are alpha (multiplicity eight) and lambda=alpha+gamma (multiplicity one). Both lie in Q(s). Since rho(b) and rho(b)^-1 have matrices over R=Z[s,s^-1], alpha,lambda and their inverses are integral over R. The ring R is integrally closed, so these four rational functions belong to R. Therefore alpha and lambda are units:

    alpha=epsilon s^a, lambda=eta s^c,
    epsilon,eta in {+1,-1}, a,c in Z.

This step uses the actual integral braid lattice, not only a representation over Q(s). The usual cell/link-state lattice supplies it; T_i^-1 is again Laurent-integral by the displayed quadratic relation.

### Proof step 2: the permutation of b is trivial

At s=1 this is the S_6 representation W=S^(4,2). Realize W as the kernel of the vertex-incidence map from the permutation module on the fifteen two-element subsets of {1,...,6} to the six-dimensional permutation module on vertices. Its character is

    chi(pi) = binom(f(pi),2) + c_2(pi) - f(pi),

where f counts fixed vertices and c_2 counts 2-cycles. The first two terms count fixed two-element subsets; subtracting the vertex character gives the formula.

Our assumed spectrum at s=1 has trace 8 epsilon+eta, in {9,7,-7,-9}. The character formula takes value 9 only at the identity and never takes 7,-7,-9. Indeed, a nonidentity permutation has f<=4 and c_2<=(6-f)/2, yielding values between -1 and 3. Thus epsilon=eta=1 and the permutation is identity: b is pure.

### Proof step 3: the infinitesimal image of a pure braid is only transposition span

Let tau_ij denote the transposition operator on W. The pure braid group is generated by conjugates of sigma_i^2. Differentiating

    T_i(s)^2=(1-s^2)T_i(s)+s^2 I

at s=1 gives

    (T_i^2)'(1)=2(I-tau_i).

A conjugate of sigma_i^2 consequently has derivative 2(I-tau_ij), up to the appropriate permutation of indices. Since its value at s=1 is I, derivatives add under products and negate under inverses. Therefore

    rho(b)'(1) in S := span_Q{I,tau_ij : 1<=i<j<=6}.

On the other hand, differentiating the assumed selective-phase form gives

    rho(b)'(1)=a I + (c-a) P(1),

because lambda(1)-alpha(1)=0 eliminates the derivative of P. It remains to show P(1) not in S.

### Proof step 4: P(1) has squared distance 11/30 from S

This calculation is entirely in the ordinary real representation W, with the invariant inner product inherited from the fifteen orthonormal pair coordinates. Write

    w = e_13 - e_14 - e_23 + e_24.

It lies in W, has norm squared four, and spans the simultaneous -1 eigenspace of tau_12,tau_34. That eigenspace has dimension one, so

    P(1)=w w^T/4.

The class sum of the fifteen transpositions is 5I on W, so S is already spanned by the transpositions. Their Hilbert-Schmidt Gram matrix G is

    G_ab = Tr(tau_a tau_b)
         = 9 if a=b,
           0 if the two pairs share exactly one vertex,
           1 if the pairs are disjoint.

The trace values follow from the character formula (identity, 3-cycle, double transposition). For the vector v_a=Tr(P tau_a), direct application to w gives

    v_12=v_34=-1, v_56=1, all twelve other v_a=1/2.

The fifteen-pair space splits orthogonally into constant functions (dimension 1), vertex sums x_i+x_j with sum_i x_i=0 (dimension 5), and the incidence kernel (dimension 9). On these summands G has eigenvalues 15,6,10 respectively. These can be verified by summing over pairs disjoint from a given pair; no spectral theorem beyond this elementary decomposition is needed.

The corresponding squared component norms of v are 5/3,4/3,3. Explicitly, its constant component is 1/3, and the vertex component has x_1=...=x_4=-1/6, x_5=x_6=1/3. Thus the orthogonal projection of P onto S has squared norm

    v^T G^-1 v = (5/3)/15 + (4/3)/6 + 3/10 = 19/30.

Since ||P||_HS^2=Tr(P)=1, the squared distance is 11/30>0. Hence P not in S.

Step 3 now forces c=a. The Laurent-unit eigenvalues are identical at every s, so gamma=lambda-alpha=0. Conjugating the whole alleged braid identity proves the statement for braid-conjugate cap lines. QED, subject to checking the stated integral cell normalization.

## Exact certificate and reproducibility

`phase_span_check.py` independently works in the ambient fifteen-pair module using only rational arithmetic. If B is its vertex-incidence matrix, the orthogonal projection onto W is

    Q_{S,T}=1_{S=T} - |S intersection T|/4 + 1/10.

The script finds rank 15 for the span of Q and the fifteen Q tau_ij, and a rational linear functional annihilating all sixteen operators but not P. Its nine-entry certificate is saved in `phase_span_check.json`. The script does not enumerate braid words. The 11/30 argument above is a shorter conceptual replacement for that matrix certificate.

## Meaning for the attempted physics route

The controlled-phase gate

    U_theta=I+(exp(i theta)-1)P

is a perfectly valid rank-one quantum operation at a unitary evaluation. Quantum algorithms often assume such a gate, or construct it using measurement and auxiliary control. At width four a single braid generator already has scalar-plus-rank-one form; this is what makes the zero-pairing-to-commuting-half-twists argument possible. At width six the theorem proves that **no fixed ordinary braid word supplies the analogous generic selective phase**, even if one allows arbitrary rational scalar functions in the alleged form.

This makes the extra-resource requirement precise: a repair through quantum control must use a geometric operation beyond a fixed braid word on these six fundamental strands (for example an actual handle attachment or a colored surgery projector), and then prove its classical topological effect. Merely taking a linear combination of braid diagrams realizes a skein operator, not an ambient isotopy or a diffeomorphism. The actual unknot closure provides an embedded disk, but no construction converting that disk into the required generic controlled-phase operator has been obtained.

Limits: the result does not prove rho faithful, does not classify scalar braid images, does not exclude exact selective phases at isolated roots of unity, does not exclude approximation, and does not exclude level-dependent braid words. It also does not prove any lifting of Jones equality to a splitting disk. It is an obstruction to a specific elegant quantum-control repair.

## The normalization issue, recorded only as an archive cross-check

For a 2b-plat, the normalized cup vector Omega_b has

    |<Omega_b,rho(beta)Omega_b>| = |V_plat(beta)(root)| / d^(b-1),
    d=2 cos(pi/r)>1.

One proof is to use the categorical evaluation in which a circle has value d: the unnormalized b-cup vector has squared norm d^b, while a knot evaluates to phase*d*V_K. Hence V=1 gives amplitude d^(1-b), strictly below the Cauchy-Schwarz maximum for b>1. Actual stabilized unknot plats already exhibit this failure of saturation. This is the standard unitary plat framework of Aharonov-Jones-Landau, especially Claim 3.8 and Theorem 3.2: https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0511096 . It agrees with the previously archived unitary-interior obstruction and is not counted as a new result.
