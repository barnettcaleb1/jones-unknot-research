STRATEGY14: ROTORS, GEOMETRIC COMPLETION, AND COOPERATIVE CANCELLATION
Scope: conceptual research design, no enumeration, no new candidate or novelty claim.
Recommendation after structural audit: reject the inverse-braid subclass. Any
four-rotor construction must use a NON-boundary-parallel tangle with an explicit
unknot completion. Keep cup-trivial six-rotors as a more demanding second stage.

1. WHY THIS IS DIFFERENT FROM OUR EARLIER TWIST FAMILIES

A rotor is an actual tangle diagram with cyclic symmetry; its half-turn through
three-dimensional space changes the gluing to the outside tangle. The polynomial
identity holds before choosing that outside tangle. We can therefore spend the
construction effort on topology rather than solve another Laurent equation.

Known input (Anstee--Przytycki--Rolfsen, 1989): Theorem3.1 preserves the bracket
for n-rotors with n<=5. Their Question3.5 explicitly asks for a nontrivial rotant
of the unknot. Theorem4.1 extends bracket preservation to n<=7 when the rotor is
cup-trivial: capping a specified adjacent endpoint pair makes it a trivial tangle
relative to the remaining boundary (extra trivial circles are allowed).
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405382
DOI10.1016/0166-8641(89)90031-X

MacColl's Theorem1.5 exhibits four-rotants with different Khovanov homology, so
ordinary mutation's Khovanov obstruction cannot simply be reused for all rotors.
This does not imply a rotant of the unknot exists. His actual family is not an
unknot-seed construction.
https://arxiv.org/html/1501.00851
DOI10.4153/CMB-2015-034-6 (2016)

A normalization observation specific to our target: if a link diagram has the
same bracket as an actual unknot diagram, its normalized Jones is a signed
monomial. Its value at1 has absolute value 2^(components-1), forcing one component.
For a knot, V(1)=1 and V'(1)=0 then force that monomial to be1. Thus a rotor of
an actual unknot satisfying the bracket theorem needs no additional unproved
writhe-equality assumption. This observation is not a general statement that
arbitrary rotants have equal normalized Jones without orientation conditions.

2. FIRST RECIPE TESTED AND RULED OUT: INVERSE COMPLETION

The initially proposed recipe was a four-fold rotor R admitting a genuine
four-strand braid presentation beta
for a chosen division of its eight boundary endpoints. Preserve the actual
symmetric rotor diagram as part of the data; an arbitrary braid is NOT a rotor.
Put beta^(-1) in the exterior stator, followed by delta4=sigma1 sigma2 sigma3,
and use ordinary braid closure. The original diagram is certified U because
 beta beta^(-1) delta4 = delta4,
and closure(delta4) destabilizes to the unknot. No unknot recognition or Jones
inference is needed for the seed.

After the half-turn, determine the actual braid beta^tau in exactly the same
boundary convention. The candidate is
 closure(beta^tau beta^(-1) delta4).
The following structural audit answers the early stopping question: every
boundary-parallel four-rotor of the stated standard type is flip-equivalent.
Consequently this inverse-braid recipe CANNOT produce a counterexample.

INVERSE COMPLETION IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY A DEAD END. A small conceptual control
shows why. In B3 write a=sigma1,b=sigma2, beta=a^2, delta3=ab. A vertical spatial
half-turn interchanges a,b. The original closure beta beta^(-1) delta3 is U; the
flipped closure is
 closure(b^2 a^(-2) ab) = closure(b^2 a^(-1)b)
 = closure(b^3 a^(-1)),
which destabilizes to the positive trefoil. This beta has NO three-fold rotor
symmetry and hence gives NO Jones identity. It only refutes the proposed shortcut
that inverse completion must cancel geometrically after every flip. Achieving
actual cyclic symmetry is precisely the additional hard condition.

EARLY KILL TESTS, before any large construction:
(a) If R is isotopic rel boundary to its flipped image, discard.
(b) More generally, cut the double branched cover of the original U along the
lifted rotor sphere. If the lifted half-turn extends over either piece, the new
cover is still S3 and Smith's theorem forces the rotant to be U. Extension over
one piece is a sufficient kill test, not a necessary condition or a complete
classification. For rational pieces, the full condition involves a product of
two handlebody groups; the braid agent is handling that formulation.
(c) Explicit Markov/Reidemeister cancellation of the candidate is conclusive;
a failed simplifier proves nothing. A nonabelian finite quotient of its knot
group or reduced Khovanov rank>1 would certify nontriviality.
(d) Nontrivial Fox colorings cannot succeed here: exact Jones1 already forces
determinant1. Use nonabelian conjugation quandles if using color constraints.

3. SECOND MECHANISM: CUP-TRIVIAL SIX-ROTORS AS COOPERATIVE DEVICES

The biology-inspired design principle is collective dependence: removing one
interaction disables the whole arrangement. Here the exact operation is CAPPING
an adjacent pair of endpoints, not deleting a strand. They are not equivalent.
A Brunnian pure braid becomes trivial on strand deletion, but capping joins arcs
and can preserve or create knotting. Likewise vanishing finitely many Vassiliev
invariants is not the full bracket identity required here.

A possible geometric implementation uses claspers, with an explicit condition
stronger than Brunnian deletion. Start with a cup-trivial symmetric trivial
six-tangle R0. Consider a cyclically invariant family of tree claspers G. Demand:
 for each allowed cup c_i, EVERY connected clasper has a leaf that can be made
 a trivial disk-leaf by an isotopy of c_i(R0), disjoint from all other leaves
 and edges, and fixed at the remaining boundary.
Call this the cap-slide condition. It is an exact geometric certificate to
construct, not an asserted consequence of drawing a commutator or wheel.

Conditional construction lemma: if this condition holds, surgery along G yields
a cup-trivial six-rotor R. After capping, every clasper has a trivial disk-leaf,
so its surgery is isotopically ineffective. Equivariance of the embedded surgery
construction must also be checked to obtain actual six-fold diagram symmetry.
The local trivial-leaf principle is standard clasper calculus; see Habiro,
Claspers and finite type invariants of links, Geometry & Topology4(2000),1--83,
Section2 and Proposition2.7.
https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/GT/ftp/main/2000/2000-1.pdf
Brunnian deletion itself is characterized using claspers in Habiro--Meilhan,
Finite type invariants and Milnor invariants for Brunnian links, Theorem5.1:
https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/preprint/file/RIMS1520.pdf
Neither source supplies the cap-slide construction or the needed unknot completion.

The second missing certificate is a stator S for which R union S is the actual U,
but the flip destroys the cancellation. An exterior inverse construction should
use an explicit cancelling surgery-link pair or an explicit isotopy, not a formal
'inverse clasper' in an associated graded group. If the unknot proof only uses one
of the designated cups, it is likely useless: cup-triviality survives the flip
and the same local simplification remains available. The sought cancellation
must depend on the global way several boundary arcs are connected.

A cyclic orbit of claspers can supply symmetry, and trivial-leaf cap slides could
supply the cup condition. Neither automatically supplies the compatible global
unknot completion. Therefore this is a construction recipe with TWO concrete
geometric obligations, not a claimed counterexample generator.

4. STRUCTURAL AUDIT: WHY THE TRIVIAL-TANGLE SUBCLASS FAILS

This is an internally checked deduction from a primary equivariance lemma and
standard four-punctured-sphere topology, not a novelty claim.

Label the eight endpoints cyclically0,...,7, and let r send i to i+2. For the
braid partition top={0,1,2,3}, bottom={4,5,6,7}, the invariant perfect matchings
crossing this partition are exactly the antipodal matching and
 {(0,5),(2,7),(4,1),(6,3)}.
Without the braid partition, the invariant matchings consist of the antipodal
matching and the four orbits {(2k,2k+j): k=0,1,2,3}, j odd (labels mod8).
Each of the latter is one free orbit of four strands. Thus the originally suggested
'antipodal matching only' obstruction is FALSE.
The antipodal matching is nevertheless impossible for disjoint actual spatial
arcs with this C4 symmetry: an antipodal arc is fixed setwise by r^2 and therefore
has a point on the rotation axis; r fixes that point while sending the arc to a
distinct arc, forcing an intersection. Thus the relevant braid rotor has a free
four-strand orbit. Extra internal closed components are impossible in a rotor
with an actual unknot completion, since they remain separate components.

Now suppose R is boundary-parallel. Meier--Scott, Equivariant trisections for
group actions on four-manifolds, Lemma3.9 and Definition3.8, gives an invariant
almost-disjoint system of bridge disks. Select the orbit of one disk. The four
strands are distinct, so these four disks are genuinely disjoint, not just
almost-disjoint. They avoid the rotation axis, because an axis point in one disk
would belong to its distinct translates as well.
https://arxiv.org/html/2501.17999#S3.SS2

In the quotient boundary sphere, let N,S be the axis ends and p,q the two endpoint
orbits. The projected disk has one shadow arc alpha joining p,q and avoiding N,S.
The allowed half-turn descends to an involution exchanging p,q and exchanging
N,S. It is the standard Conway double-transposition on the four-punctured sphere.
Its pillowcase lift is a half-period translation, hence it fixes every essential
simple closed curve slope. (The permutation alone would not suffice; the actual
order-two Conway map is essential.) The boundary of a small neighborhood of alpha
is such a curve. The disk it bounds containing p,q determines the unoriented arc
class, so alpha and its flipped image are isotopic fixing all four marked points.

Lift this isotopy starting at the identity through the cyclic branched cover.
All eight endpoint lifts stay fixed individually. Considering the entire shadow
preimage, rather than one chosen lift, eliminates deck-transformation ambiguity.
Push both shadow systems slightly into a boundary collar and use isotopy extension
relative to the boundary. This yields R isotopic to its flip rel the whole ball
boundary. Thus every exterior completion has the same knot before and after.
A braid tangle is boundary-parallel, so the proposed subclass is eliminated.

The small B3 unknot-to-trefoil control above remains correct: its tangle lacks
cyclic rotor symmetry, exactly the hypothesis needed for this obstruction.

5. REVISED PRIORITY AND STOPPING RULE

Do not launch an inverse-braid rotor search. A meaningful four-rotor must be a
non-boundary-parallel tangle with an explicit unknot completion. The first design
obligation is therefore an actual spanning-disk or Reidemeister certificate for
such a completion; rotational symmetry alone is insufficient. This is a narrower
and harder target than the initial proposal, and this pass supplies no such object.

The cup-trivial six-rotor clasper scheme remains a conditional alternative: cup
triviality does not assert that the unfilled rotor is boundary-parallel. It needs
cap-slide certificates plus a global, nonlocal unknot completion. Prioritize it
only if an explicit small cap-slide gadget is available. The present structural
lemma is proved only in the four-rotor setting stated above; no generalized
six-rotor exclusion is being asserted.

The DNA connection supplies controlled reconnection operations and the insistence
on distinguishing individual from collective effects; it does not supply a
mathematical implication from biological simplification to Jones rigidity.
Energy minimization can prioritize drawings but cannot certify nontriviality:
hard unknots can have large simplification barriers. Maintain actual isotopy or
spanning-disk certificates for the seed, and a separate invariant for the output.

These are counterexample routes to the generic problem, not reductions proving
that every possible Jones-one knot must be a rotant. No such completeness theorem
is known or claimed here.
