BOUNDED AUDIT: A CYCLIC ORBIT OF GLOBALLY NUGATORY SURGERY CIRCLES

Proposed data: an actual C4-symmetric trivial rotor R0 in B, an asymmetric stator
S with R0 union S=U, and a C4 orbit of disjoint circles eta_i in int(B) disjoint
from R0. Require the WHOLE eta link to bound disjoint embedded disks in S3 minus
U, with the surgery framings specified by those disks. Equal 1/k surgeries then
keep the global knot U: the surgery link is a split unlink disjoint from U.
The fact that the circles lie in B does not require their globally nugatory disks
to lie in B.

No immediate contradiction was found. Global splitness alone does not justify
local harmlessness: the map pi1(B minus R0) -> pi1(S3 minus U)=Z need not be
injective. Zero linking, null-homotopy, and the existence of separate (possibly
intersecting) disks are each weaker than the required simultaneous embedded-disk
certificate. These weaker conditions cannot be substituted in the construction.

A definite killed subclass: if the framed eta link bounds disjoint disks already
in B minus R0, the surgery can be performed in local balls disjoint from R0.
It is then harmless relative to the boundary and cannot create a useful rotor.
The desired disks must essentially leave B in their position relative to R0.

For crossing circles (disks meeting U twice with opposite signs), the theorem
that every cosmetic crossing change of U is nugatory provides no extra local
conclusion. It only confirms the global disk property already required. For
surgery disks meeting many strands, that crossing-change theorem does not apply.
Primary reference for the distinction: E. Kalfagianni, Crossing Changes of
Fibered Knots, Definition6.1 and Theorem6.3.
https://users.math.msu.edu/users/kalfagia/mynugatory15.pdf

Three geometric obligations remain before this becomes a counterexample recipe:
1. Draw an actual orbit with the simultaneous global disk certificate, and show
   that it does not possess the local disk certificate above.
2. Track the rotor-ball identification after surgery. The equivariant surgery
   description must yield an actual standard C4 rotor diagram, and its spatial
   flip must be the proposed flipped surgery configuration. Abstract cyclic
   invariance of a surgery presentation is not itself the finished diagram.
3. Certify that the flipped augmented link has a geometric property that makes
   the filled knot nontrivial. Nonsplitness alone is not enough. If using
   hyperbolic Dehn filling, one needs a verified hyperbolic augmented-link
   complement and a sequence of surgery slopes with lengths tending to infinity.

The original augmented link is split and hence nonhyperbolic. Any hyperbolicity
argument must concern the FLIPPED augmented link. No such link was constructed
or tested here. No general theorem was located that upgrades global nugatoriness
to local nugatoriness for a C4 orbit and an asymmetric stator.
