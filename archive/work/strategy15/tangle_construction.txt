STRATEGY15: ACTUAL GEOMETRY FOR A ROTOR OF THE UNKNOT
Status: bounded construction audit. No non-boundary-parallel C4 rotor with a
certified unknot completion was constructed. No Jones counterexample is claimed.

1. A GEOMETRIC EARLY-KILL TEST FOR THE STATOR

Write a proposed closed link as R union S, with R the rotor and S its exterior
stator, and let tau be the spatial half-turn used in the rotant operation. If
there is an isotopy tau(S) to S relative to the boundary endpoints, then
 tau(R) union S is isotopic to tau(R union S).
This follows simply by applying the global rotation and then the stator isotopy.
Hence an unknot seed remains an unknot, independently of how complicated R is.
A successful completion must break this flip symmetry. In particular, four
independent, symmetrically arranged cap simplifications cannot provide the
needed collective cancellation. This is a sufficient kill test, not a complete
classification of stators.

2. A CONCRETE CERTIFIED BUILDING BLOCK FROM KINOSHITA'S THETA GRAPH

Ozawa--Taylor's Figure4 supplies an actual non-boundary-parallel 2-tangle T:
remove a regular neighborhood of the indicated edge, including both vertices,
from the Kinoshita theta graph. The remaining two edges are the two strands of T.
Their explicitly depicted trivial-tangle filling produces trefoil # figure-eight.
If T were boundary-parallel, this would be a two-bridge presentation of that
composite knot, contradicting Schubert's bridge-number theorem.

There is also an actual unknot filling: reconnect the two remaining edges locally
at the two former vertices. This gives their constituent cycle, which is an
unknot by the Brunnian property. Thus T is a certified nonrational, unknot-fillable
2-tangle, with a second filling whose nontriviality is independently known.
This is an existing geometric resource, not a new example.

Source: Makoto Ozawa and Scott A. Taylor, Two More Proofs that the Kinoshita Graph
is Knotted, Figure4 and the preceding proof; arXiv1803.05474v2 (2018), American
Mathematical Monthly126(4) (2019).
https://arxiv.org/html/1803.05474

The missing construction is substantial: putting four copies of T in a cyclic
arrangement does not prove that a single exterior stator closes their union to U.
It must supply one connected knot using every rotor strand, and an actual
spanning disk or isotopy for that knot. A proof using four separately available
caps is vulnerable to the flip-symmetric-stator test above. This pass does not
assert that the local building block extends to the desired rotor.

3. THE NATURAL BRANCHED-DOUBLING RECIPE IS RULED OUT

Let theta be a spatial theta graph and let kappa be an unknotted constituent
cycle. Double-cover S3 branched over kappa and lift the third edge to a knot K.
Calcut--Metcalf-Burton, Lemma2.3, proves the exact equivalence
                  K is the unknot  <=>  theta is unknotted.
Consequently, lifting the third edge of a NONTRIVIAL Brunnian theta graph can
never provide an unknot anchor. The proof uses the equivariant Dehn lemma, not
Jones detection. In their Example3.1, Kinoshita's graph lifts to T(3,5), despite
all three constituent cycles being unknots.

This is an all-examples exclusion for this particular construction, not merely
a failed small example. It does not exclude other ribbon-boundary, unzipping,
or stator-gluing operations on spatial graphs.

Source: Jack S. Calcut and Jules R. Metcalf-Burton, Double branched covers of
theta-curves, J. Knot Theory Ramifications25(8) (2016),1650046, Lemma2.3 and
Example3.1. DOI10.1142/S0218216516500462.
https://isis2.cc.oberlin.edu/faculty/jcalcut/prime_theta_curve.pdf

4. WHY RIBBON THICKENING NEEDS MORE THAN CONSTITUENT UNKNOTS

There is a basic control example before any knotted spatial graph is used.
Take a planar theta graph as the core of two disks joined by three positively
half-twisted bands. This is the standard genus-one Seifert surface of a trefoil.
The three core cycles are unknotted, but the surface boundary is not. Therefore
all-constituents-U data do not determine a thickened graph's knot boundary.
The band framing and the attachment order are essential data.

A C3-symmetric single classical knot traversing every edge equally must use each
edge an even number of times: at a trivalent vertex, 3m incident strand ends
must be paired. The minimum is two traversals per edge. Doubling is therefore
natural, but it is precisely where ribbon framing and collective interactions
become unavoidable. Strand deletion is not the same as capping these doubled
strands, and neither is the same as a band fusion.

There is nonetheless a controlled finite-template target. Fix an ACTUAL
orientable surface made from two disks and three bands whose core is a chosen
Brunnian theta graph. Record its attachment order and integer framings. Cutting
two bands leaves an embedded disk and hence an actual unknot boundary. Cutting
one band leaves an annulus whose core is a constituent unknot; its two boundary
components are controlled by the recorded annular framing. Only the full
three-band boundary retains the collective graph information.

Full twists in the bands then admit an exact finite skein expansion from these
cut surfaces. This could isolate the one joint coefficient invisible to all
proper cuts. It supplies a tractable computation once an explicit surface diagram
is fixed, but it does not itself force that coefficient to be the unknot value.
No such band family was computed in this pass. A cyclic ribbon boundary also
needs a separate check that the operation is an APR rotor, with the right number
of boundary endpoints; a doubled C3 graph is not automatically a standard C4
rotor.

5. ACTUAL FOUR-ROTOR DIAGRAM INSPECTED

MacColl's Figure1 (from Jin--Rolfsen's example) is a genuine four-rotor whose
rotants can be distinguished by Khovanov homology. Its drawing has four repeated
sectors, but each is a 3-tangle: two external endpoints and four connections to
neighboring sectors. It is not a necklace of four 2-tangles. Thus replacing a
sector by the Kinoshita block above requires a new wiring construction. The known
completion in that figure is a two-component link, not an unknot certificate.

Joseph MacColl, Rotors in Khovanov Homology, Canad. Math. Bull.59(1) (2016),159--169,
Figure1 and Theorem1.5; DOI10.4153/CMB-2015-034-6.
https://arxiv.org/html/1501.00851

RECOMMENDED NEXT CONCRETE STEP

Require one drawn rotor/stator pair, together with a topological unknot
certificate, before any word or framing enumeration. The Kinoshita edge-complement
is an actual nontrivial unknot-fillable building block; its cyclic assembly is
the missing problem. Reject ordinary inverse-braid completions (previous pass),
flip-symmetric stators (Section1), and third-edge branched lifts of nontrivial
theta graphs (Section3) at construction time. The remaining opening is an
asymmetric global reconnection that uses all strands and has a visible spanning
disk before the flip. This is a concrete geometric obligation, not a result
that such a construction exists.
