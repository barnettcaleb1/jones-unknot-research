INDEPENDENT AUDIT: TRIVIAL COMPLETIONS OF UNKNOT-COMPLETABLE TANGLES
Status: internally checked deduction; no novelty claim. Audit of braid agent's
inductive argument in rotor_topology.txt. No appeal to Jones detection.

Statement. Let R be an n-string tangle in a marked ball B, without closed
components. If some complementary n-string tangle S makes R union S an unknot,
then there exists a boundary-parallel complementary tangle S' making R union S'
an unknot. R, its ball, its marked endpoints, and its embedding remain fixed.
No equivariance is required of S'.

Base n=1. The sphere gives a connected-sum decomposition of the unknot into the
closures of the two ball-arcs. Uniqueness of prime decomposition implies both
arcs are unknotted. Thus a boundary-parallel completion exists.

Compression. For n>=2 the punctured splitting sphere F has nonabelian fundamental
group, whereas the unknot exterior has fundamental group Z. Hence F is not
pi1-injective. The Loop Theorem supplies a compression on one side of F. One may
also see the side issue from the amalgamated-product normal form: injection on
both sides would imply injection into the glued exterior. The compressing disk
cuts the corresponding ball into two balls. Each contains a positive even number
2n_i of punctures, with n_i<n. Positivity follows from essentiality on F; parity
follows because the associated sphere intersects the closed knot an even number
of times. Its disk is disjoint from the knot, so the tangle strands do not cross
between the two resulting balls.

Case 1: the compression is in S. Write S=S1 union_D S2. Apply induction to the
COMPLEMENTARY n1-string tangle of the ball containing S1, replacing S1 by a
boundary-parallel completion while keeping the whole closure U. Recompute the
complementary n2-string tangle after this replacement and apply induction again.
The two new stator pieces are boundary-parallel and glue along an endpoint-free
disk. Their boundary shadows can be pushed off that disk, so their union is
boundary-parallel. Sequential replacement is essential to this formulation.

Case 2: the compression is in R. Write R=R1 union_D R2. Each R_i already embeds
in U, so induction gives a boundary-parallel unknot completion S_i'. Realize
S_i' as a small push-off of disjoint shadow arcs on the boundary of its rotor
ball, with all shadows avoiding the endpoint-free disk used for the disk-sum.
Choose one shadow on each side. The complement of each shadow forest in its
boundary sphere is connected. Therefore an embedded boundary rectangle can
join the interiors of the chosen shadows, crossing the neck once and avoiding
all other shadows. Use its untwisted small push-off as the connecting band.

Boundary-parallelness: perform the band surgery on the shadow arcs and their
push-offs simultaneously. The two resulting arcs are again disjoint boundary
shadows. The entire stator is their small push-off together with the unchanged
shadows. Thus it is boundary-parallel directly, without assuming that a general
band sum of trivial tangles is trivial.

Unknot certificate: before the connection, the two closed unknots U_i are in
separate slightly enlarged balls. The chosen untwisted band runs through their
single connecting neck, outside the interiors of those balls. This is the
standard connected-sum band, so the closure is U1#U2=U. An arbitrary ribbon band
would NOT suffice; it could tie a nontrivial ribbon knot. The explicit collar
and single-neck choice is the required additional detail.

Marking: the disk-sum reconstructs the given rotor ball and its original R_i.
All adjustment occurs in the complementary collar/stator, so the fixed marked
R is unchanged.

LIMIT OF THE DEDUCTION
This establishes a new choice of stator preserving the ORIGINAL unknot closure.
If the old stator made the FLIPPED rotor nontrivial, the argument does not prove
that this remains true for the replacement stator. Thus it is not by itself a
reduction of all hypothetical rotant counterexamples to trivial-stator ones.
It is also not a claim that every rotor which admits such a completion is trivial.
