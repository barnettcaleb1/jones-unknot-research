## 1. What the answer does and does not supply

There is a useful inductive invariant: the ideal generated by the integral two-coordinate state. Twists preserve it, and star multiplication cannot repair a proper ideal. It obstructs every later horizontal or vertical unit closure in the banked grammar. Its divisorial part has an exact nonnegative gain formula when opposite finite-place zeros and poles of children's channel slopes meet. The full ideal is strictly stronger: it excludes every positive member of N-INF's displayed family by one reduction modulo (7,t-2), even though those members have no common polynomial factor.

For the primitive remainder, the full finite divisor of the eigen-coordinate gives an exact span formula. In particular a fixed body with both eigenchannels present has at most two possible horizontal unit twists, irrespective of its nesting depth. The two-ended cancellation problem is an exact exhaustion of a finite pole budget, not an unspecified number of end jets.

Neither statement proves a strict inequality on all realizable primitive bodies. A formal integral state can satisfy the unit equation and all the stated finite scalar gates. The missing input is geometric realizability of the primitive unit fibre. No osp positivity assertion is made.

## 2. Integral eigen-coordinates: the apparent Phi_3 denominator cancels

Set

\[
 \Lambda=\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}],\quad d=1-t^3,
 \quad C=(1-t)(1+t^2),\quad e=-\frac{t}{1+t^2},
 \quad \Phi_3=1+t+t^2.
\]

Use the banked integral state \(v=(a,c)\), whose channel amplitudes are \(a,b=a+dc\). In this note A,B ALWAYS denote the following eigen-amplitudes, not Theorem N's channel products \(\prod a_i,\prod b_i\). Define

\[
 A=a+Cc,\qquad B=Cc,\qquad z=B/A.
\]

Then

\[
 a=A-B,\qquad b=A-eB,\qquad
 A=\frac{ta+(1+t^2)b}{\Phi_3}.
\]

Thus the last quotient is integral on the actual lattice. The earlier homogeneous pair \((ta+(1+t^2)b,(1+t^2)(b-a))\) has a mandatory common factor \(\Phi_3\); remove it before discussing unit equations.

In this common raw framing the three operations are

\[
 F_m:(A,B)\mapsto(A,(-t)^{-m}B),
\]

\[
 \begin{split}
 A_{12}&=A_1A_2-eB_1B_2,\\
 B_{12}&=A_1B_2+B_1A_2-(1+e)B_1B_2,
 \end{split}
\]

and, for \(r=t^{-3N}\), \(k=(r-1)/\Phi_3\in\Lambda\),

\[
 Y^N:\binom A B\mapsto
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1+(1+t^2)k&tk\\(1+t^2)k&1+tk
 \end{pmatrix}\binom A B.
\]

The last determinant is \(r\). All formulas follow by substituting \(a,b\); integrality of the star formula uses \(B_i\in C\Lambda\). In particular

\[
 z_{12}=\frac{z_1+z_2-(1+e)z_1z_2}{1-ez_1z_2}.
\]

The preferred framing adds one common Laurent unit. It affects none of content, slopes, spans or unit status. The raw horizontal closure of \(F_m(v)\) is

\[
 f_m=A-(-t)^{-m}B. \tag{1}
\]

## 3. Irreversible ideals, divisorial content and its exact gain

First keep the full ideal

\[
 I(v)=(a,c)\subseteq\Lambda.
\]

Unimodularity of X,Y proves I(F_m v)=I(Y^N v)=I(v). The bilinear star rule below proves

\[
 \boxed{I(v*w)\subseteq I(v)I(w)\subseteq I(v)\cap I(w).} \tag{2a}
\]

Consequently every node in a unit-producing construction tree must have I(v)=Lambda, not merely gcd(a,c)=1. A witness is a finite-field evaluation where both coordinates vanish and t is nonzero. Such a zero state survives every subsequent star product and the invertible X,Y operations. Integrality of the two final closure functionals is proved below. This is an arithmetic obstruction; the slope ratio at a common zero has discarded the relevant information.

For an irreducible \(p\) of \(\Lambda\), let

\[
 \gamma_p(v)=\min(\nu_p(a),\nu_p(c)).
\]

Both \(X,Y\) are in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(\Lambda)\). Therefore \(\gamma_p\) is invariant under \(F_m,Y^N\), including negative exponents. The integral star law is

\[
 (a_1,c_1)*(a_2,c_2)=
 (a_1a_2,a_1c_2+c_1a_2+dc_1c_2).
\]

It proves, at every prime including those dividing \(d\),

\[
 \gamma_p(v_1*\cdots*v_s)\geq\sum_i\gamma_p(v_i). \tag{2}
\]

For \(p\nmid d\) the channel-coordinate change is locally invertible. Assuming the channel amplitudes are nonzero, put \(r_i=\nu_p(b_i/a_i)\). Then the stronger exact formula is

\[
 \boxed{\gamma_p(v_1*\cdots*v_s)=\sum_i\gamma_p(v_i)
 +\frac{\sum_i|r_i|-|\sum_i r_i|}{2}.} \tag{3}
\]

Indeed, the left side is \(\min(\sum_i\nu_p(a_i),\sum_i\nu_p(b_i))\), and \(2\min(x,y)=x+y-|x-y|\). Zero channels can instead be treated directly with the valuation minimum; they are not divided by in (3).

Consequently a zero of one child slope and a pole of another at the same finite place create permanent scalar content. In eigen-coordinates these are the two boundary values \(z=1/e\) and \(z=1\). Any tree producing a unit must be unimodular at every node and must avoid these opposite signs at every star node, for every \(p\nmid d\). This is an inductive necessary condition, not a claim that every nontrivial tree violates it.

There is no cancellation of this obstruction by the vertical formula's displayed denominator. With vertical parameter \(\ell\), write

\[
 w_1=t+(1+t^2)(-t)^\ell,\qquad
 w_V=t(-t)^\ell+t^{3\ell-1}\Phi_5.
\]

Then the raw vertical functional is

\[
 \frac{w_1a+w_Vb}{\Phi_3}
 =\frac{w_1+w_V}{\Phi_3}a+(1-t)w_Vc. \tag{4}
\]

The first coefficient is integral: \(w_1+w_V=t+\Phi_3(-t)^\ell+t^{3\ell-1}\Phi_5\), and at a primitive third root \(\omega\), \(\Phi_5(\omega)=-\omega^2\), so the numerator vanishes. Division by the monic \(\Phi_3\) stays integral, also for Laurent exponents. Horizontal closure is plainly integral. Thus any nonunit content divides every final closure in this grammar, including the odd vertical closures that represent the actual knots.

For actual preferred bodies, ROOTS gives \(a(1)=a(i)=a(-i)=1\). Hence \(\gcd(a,C)=1\), and

\[
 \gcd(A,B)=\gcd(a,Cc)=\gcd(a,c)
\]

up to Laurent units. State content and eigenpair content agree. This observation is optional for (2)–(4), but useful for the next reduction.

## 4. An exact global budget for cancellation at both ends

Assume \(A,B\ne0\), \(\gcd(A,B)=1\), and \(f_m\ne0\). Work over \(\mathbb C\) for divisor multiplicities. Let

\[
 H=\sum_{p\in\mathbb C^*}(-\nu_p z)_+,
 \quad r_0=\nu_0z-m,\quad r_\infty=\nu_\infty z+m,
\]

\[
 w_m=(-t)^{-m}z,\qquad
 \tau_0=(\nu_0(1-w_m))_+,\quad
 \tau_\infty=(\nu_\infty(1-w_m))_+.
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{span} f_m
 =H+(-r_0)_++(-r_\infty)_+-\tau_0-\tau_\infty.} \tag{5}
\]

Proof. The finite nonzero poles of \(1-w_m\) are precisely those of \(z\). Its end poles have orders \((-r_0)_+\), \((-r_\infty)_+\). The degree of its zero divisor equals the degree of its pole divisor. Subtract the two end zero orders. The remaining finite zeros are exactly those of \(f_m=A(1-w_m)\): primitivity makes every pole cancel against a zero of \(A\), leaving a nonzero value proportional to \(B\). Finally the number of roots in \(\mathbb C^*\), with multiplicity, of a nonzero Laurent polynomial equals its span. This proves (5).

For a nonprimitive pair write \(g=\gcd(A,B)\), \(A=gA_0\), \(B=gB_0\). The ratio z and all the displayed end data are unchanged, but the exact formula is

\[
 \operatorname{span}f_m=\operatorname{span}g+
 H+(-r_0)_++(-r_\infty)_+-\tau_0-\tau_\infty. \tag{5'}
\]

This follows from additivity of span under multiplication. A nonunit constant gcd also obstructs a unit, although its span is zero. The uncorrected identity (5) must not be used on nonprimitive pairs.

Under \(F_m\), the full finite nonzero divisor of \(z\), and

\[
 \kappa(z)=\nu_0z+\nu_\infty z,
\]

are unchanged. Both ends can have order-zero resonance simultaneously only if \(\kappa=0\); then the twist is uniquely \(m=\nu_0z=-\nu_\infty z\). Order-zero resonance does not automatically mean cancellation: the relevant leading constant must be 1. At simultaneous order-zero resonance, unit status is the exact saturation

\[
 H=\tau_0+\tau_\infty. \tag{6}
\]

One contact can be zero. For genuinely two-ended cancellation both contacts are positive. No residual \(\Phi_3\) zeros are allowed: they disappeared with the compulsory common factor in Section 2.

For actual preferred bodies \(f_m(-1)=a(-1)=1\), because \((-t)^{-m}=1\) at \(-1\). Hence span zero means a Laurent unit, not merely an arbitrary constant times a monomial. The identically-zero case cannot occur there.

### At most two outer twists, without a scan

For any fixed nonzero pair \(A,B\), a unit difference (1) can occur only when a top or bottom exponent of the two summands aligns:

\[
 \boxed{m\in\{\nu_0(B/A),-\nu_\infty(B/A)\}.} \tag{7}
\]

For primitive pairs with \(H>0\), this follows immediately from (5): some \(\tau\) must be positive, which requires the corresponding \(r\) to be zero. If \(H=0\), \(z\) is a Laurent polynomial, so \(\nu_0z+\nu_\infty z\leq0\). Away from both values in (7), neither contact is positive, and at least one end pole is strictly positive; (5) again excludes a unit. Equivalently, this is the elementary support observation that two disjoint extreme exponents cannot both disappear in a monomial difference. Thus (7) needs no primitivity or genericity hypothesis, only the stated two nonzero channels; nonunit common content excludes all values first. This is the banked Lemma W end-alignment argument in the Phi3-free normalization, not a separate new non-cancellation principle.

If one eigenchannel is identically zero, this is a separate one-channel family: all closures differ by units, and the surviving amplitude decides unit status. For actual bodies \(A\) cannot vanish identically, since \(A(1)=a(1)=1\); \(B=0\) means \(c=0\), a scalar state. Scalar state is not a geometric triviality certificate.

This sharpens a fixed-body finite-window test to two exact candidate parameters. It does not bound the number of different bodies or settle their geometry, and it concerns horizontal two-channel closures, not the three-channel vertical family.

The bound is sharp on an actual rational box. For the raw state B(xy)=X^-1Y^-1(1,0), the eigenpair is

\[
 A=t^3-t^2+t,\qquad B=t^{-1}-1+t-t^2.
\]

Direct subtraction gives \(A-t^2B=t^4\) and \(A+tB=1\), so the two distinct twists m=-2,-1 both give units. This explicit identity supplies sharpness independently of the referee's larger sample. It is not a nontrivial unit-knot example.

## 5. What still has to be proved

No nonconstant ordered potential can be nondecreasing under every \(F_m\) and every \(Y^N\): applying their allowed inverses forces equality. A useful potential must be twist-invariant, use a chosen reduction direction, or carry additional data. Equations (2)–(3) satisfy that requirement; unrestricted end-distance monotonicity does not.

The full ideal makes a decisive distinction on N-INF's own family. For \(u=B(XY)\), direct multiplication gives

\[
 a=1+t^{-3}-t,\qquad b=1-t^{-2}+t^{-3},\qquad c=-t^{-2}.
\]

The two channel polynomials are coprime: their difference is a Laurent unit times \(1-t^3\), and at \(t^3=1\) their common candidate value is \(2-t\ne0\). Thus every repeated star product \(u^k\) is primitive, as are its \(Y\)- and \(F\)-transforms. One can also see this from c_k=c sum_{j=0}^{k-1} a^{k-1-j}b^j: at a prime dividing a, it reduces to c b^{k-1}, which is nonzero.

However, reduce the integral state modulo the maximal ideal (7,t-2). Then t^-3=1, t^-2=2, and

\[
 (a,c,d)=(0,5,0)\quad\text{over }\mathbb F_7.
\]

The star square is therefore (0,0), as is every higher star power. Since the X,Y determinants are units at t=2, all the bodies

\[
 P_k=Y^{-k/3}u^k,\qquad F_m(P_k),\qquad k>0,\ k\equiv0\pmod6,
\]

and all their subsequent descendants in the grammar have proper state ideal and cannot have a unit closure. No twist parameter or k is enumerated. Algebraically the box ring modulo this ideal is the dual-number algebra F_7[s]/(s^2), and u becomes 5s. The new point is the distinction between having no common irreducible factor and generating the unit ideal over Z[t,t^-1]. This family was already nonflat through the rational-body theorems; the calculation is a clean all-parameter calibration of the proposed inductive invariant, not a new class claim.

There is also a useful formal negative control for any algebra-only strict version of (6). Choose \(s=(-t)^\mu\) and any \(E\in\Lambda\), and put

\[
 (a,c)=(1+(s-1)CE,E),\qquad
 (A,B)=(1+sCE,CE).
\]

Then \(A-sB=1\), and a-(s-1)Cc=1 proves that the full state ideal is Lambda. Choosing \(E\) divisible by sufficiently high powers of \(t+1\) and by \(\Phi_6\Phi_{10}\) preserves any prescribed finite trivial jet at \(-1\) and the stated scalar root congruences. The values at \(1,\pm i\) are automatically 1. If the support of \(sCE\) straddles exponent zero, both ends cancel and (6) is saturated. This is only a formal lattice state, not an actual body or a counterexample knot. It does not supply an associated covering knot satisfying every topological constraint.

The next substantive theorem would have to distinguish reachable unimodular states from this formal unit fibre, beyond the lattice and finite scalar gates. A proposed strict contact-budget inequality must first be restricted away from actual unit closures (which include rational trivial examples); an equality-classification theorem with a geometric triviality conclusion would be the useful target. The present note does not establish it.

## 6. Round-E scope recorded with the new reductions

N-INF proves infinitely many exact signatures, even eligible order-2 signatures. It does not rule out finite control with unbounded registers, symbolic recurrences, or a coarser finite quotient sufficient for an obstruction. Its displayed family proves unbounded coefficient values; a separate claim of unbounded simultaneous contact depth needs its own argument.

ARB-2 retains all of Section 4.1's hypotheses: eligible nonrational factors with signatures in S**, at least two total factors, and span of each nonrational a-amplitude at least 3 (used with no rational partners). Horizontal special twist is nonzero; vertical parameter is odd. The family is not asserted closed under arbitrary nesting. M300/D36 retain their recorded amplitude-span conditions. No certificate replay or novelty verification was performed in this theory turn.

The integral-body vertical proof in arb_REPORT Section 5a is correct: its negative monomial at exponent \(\ell+1-3N\) cannot lie in the five-term positive block for odd \(|\ell|\geq3\), forcing \(-3N=-\ell\); then the negative exponent \(\ell\) (positive \(\ell\)) or \(\ell+2\) (negative \(\ell\)) lies outside the block and away from the remaining singleton. Thus the previous n=0 hole closes for odd \(|\ell|\geq3\). The \(\ell=\pm1\) cases remain separately scoped.
