# The root-sign dichotomy for arbitrary actual boxes

17 November 2026. Subproof for the skein dictionary requested in
Claude050833. Theory only; no scans or new numerical checks. This uses the
explicit regular maps already written and independently checked in
`ft_integral_referee.md`, Sections1–5. It does not assume that the box is
rational or generated by the two-dimensional rational-box orbit.

## Statement and marking

Let B be an actual framed box with one straight, zero-framed color-one axis
string and one connected color-two arc string, both running west-to-east.
There are no closed components. The boundary points and the reference
identity box are fixed. Work generically in the ribbon category and write

    [B]=a_raw P1+b_raw P3 in End(V1 tensor V2),

where P1 and P3 are the generic total-color projectors. Use the regular
reduced-axis normalization of `ft_integral_referee.md`, so the identity box
has a_raw=b_raw=1. Let w(B) be the winding of its arc relative to the reference
arc in the complement of the straight axis; the orientation convention can
change its sign but not its parity.

Then both coefficients are regular at t=-1, and

    a_raw(-1)=1,       b_raw(-1)=(-1)^w(B).                 (1)

For any common preferred framing normalization making a(-1)=1, the same
identities hold for a,b. In the halfword grammar of the reports,

    w(B) = n0(B) mod 2,

where n0 counts all x0 and y0 letters. Consequently

    epsilon(B)=b(-1)=(-1)^n0(B),
    v2(-1)=(epsilon(B)-1)/2 in {0,-1},                    (2)

using the already specified generic coordinate relation
b=a+(1-t^3)v2. No independent normalization of a and b is allowed.

## 1. The special fiber of the regular model

Use t=q^2, q=i at the special point, and s^2=q+q^-1. The regular color-two
lattice has basis w0,w1,w2, with w1 even and w0,w2 odd. Put

    Pi_even(w1)=w1,  Pi_even(w0)=Pi_even(w2)=0,
    Pi_odd=I-Pi_even,      C=Pi_even-Pi_odd.

The trivalent maps i:V1 -> V1 tensor V2 and p in the reverse direction are
regular, satisfy pi=I, and specialize to

    i(u)=-u tensor w1,       p(u tensor w1)=-u,
    p(u tensor w0)=p(u tensor w2)=0.

Therefore the **actual generic projector** P1=ip is regular in this lattice,
as is P3=I-P1, and their specializations are

    P1(0)=I_V1 tensor Pi_even,
    P3(0)=I_V1 tensor Pi_odd.                             (3)

This step uses no assertion of semisimplicity at the root. It follows from
the explicit regular inclusion and retraction. Their only denominator is
[3], whose root value is -1.

The same local maps give three more facts:

1. The color-two crossing B22 is the super flip at the root, so
   B22(0)=B22(0)^-1. Thus a self-crossing change of the arc preserves the
   root operator.
2. A color-two ribbon curl has root value1 (its generic twist or inverse is
   q^4 or q^-4 in these conventions). Thus a framing change of that arc
   also preserves the root operator.
3. The full mixed twist of the axis and the arc is

       T(0)=I_V1 tensor C.                                (4)

For completeness, in the regular R formula all nonconstant terms in a
mixed crossing contain s and vanish at the root. A mixed crossing has the
diagonal phase q^(weight(u) weight(w)/2) followed by the ordinary flip.
The two crossings of the full mixed twist therefore give
q^(weight(u) weight(w)). For weights(u)=+/-1 this is1 on w1 and -1 on
w0,w2, proving (4). Reversing the twist has the same root operator. Thus
the possible inverse-handedness difference between t=q^2 and the saved
lp convention does not affect this sign statement.

All cups, caps, and vertex maps are regular. Hence the box itself specializes
to an honest endomorphism, with no division by the zero axis dimension.
Moreover its two channel coefficients are regular: read the diagonal matrix
entries on u tensor w1 and on u tensor w0 using (3), or equivalently use
the regular projectors and their nonzero special images.

## 2. Reducing an arbitrary arc to winding by crossing changes

The complement of a straight proper axis arc in the box is a solid torus
with the prescribed boundary markings. Homotopy classes rel endpoints of
paths from the arc's west endpoint to its east endpoint form a torsor for
its fundamental group Z. Relative to the reference arc, the class is w(B).
A standard representative is the through arc winding w(B) times around
the axis, that is, a power of the full mixed twist.

Connect the given arc to that representative by a homotopy inside the axis
complement, fixed near the endpoints. In general position the homotopy is
an isotopy except at finitely many transverse self-intersections of the arc;
these are precisely self-crossing changes. It never crosses the axis. The
domain remains one interval throughout, so there is no creation or deletion
of a closed component. Diagrammatic extrema change by ordinary isotopies;
any framing discrepancy may be corrected by ribbon curls.

By the three local facts above, none of these changes alters the specialized
operator. Consequently

    [B](0)=T(0)^w(B)=I_V1 tensor C^w(B).                  (5)

Comparing (5) with (3) proves (1). This is not a claim that self-crossing
changes preserve the generic invariant or the isotopy type: they are used
only in the specialized symmetric/super category at t=-1.

The absence of closed arc components is essential. Such components could
contribute extra root traces, and the single-through-arc formula would no
longer describe the whole operator. The straight axis framing also fixes
an otherwise possible common axis-curl phase.

## 3. Halfwords and the common preferred unit

The axis letters x0,y0 in the report's grammar are opposite full mixed
twists, so each contributes one modulo2 to winding. Off-axis crossing
changes, cups, caps and local isotopies take place away from the axis and
do not change this mod2 relative winding. If an arc segment has reversed
orientation, its signed contribution reverses, but its parity does not.
Thus w(B)=n0(B) modulo2 for arbitrary one-through-arc bodies, including
ones with internal extrema and nonrational knotting.

Let u be the one common framing unit used to pass from (a_raw,b_raw) to the
preferred (a,b). Since a_raw(-1)=1 and a(-1)=1, necessarily u(-1)=1. Hence
b(-1)=b_raw(-1); no sign is lost in preferred normalization. This proves
(2). In particular the root-sign proof does not assume the desired
dichotomy as an input to the framing calculation.

## 4. Consequence: the unit in the outer map

Suppose the separately marked cup-slide proves that the raw outer-box state
is proportional to X^-m p, where p is a preferred product state. Then
p1(-1)=1, p1'(-1)=0 and p2(-1)=(epsilon-1)/2. The first row is

    e1^T X^-m = (1,(1-t)(1+t^2)(1-(-t)^-m)).

Its off-diagonal entry vanishes at -1 and has derivative -4m. Therefore

    F(t)=e1^T X^-m p(t),
    F(-1)=1,    F'(-1)=2m(1-epsilon).

The integer k=2m(1-epsilon) is even. Multiplication by t^k preserves the
root value1 and changes the derivative at -1 by -k, so it gives the unique
signed Laurent-monomial normalization with value1 and derivative0:

    v_outer=t^(2m(1-epsilon)) X^-m p.

It is1 when epsilon=1 and t^(4m) when epsilon=-1. At the root X=I and the
normalizing unit is1, so the outer operation preserves epsilon. Stacking
boxes multiplies their b-amplitudes, hence multiplies epsilon; geometrically
the winding parities add. This closes the root-sign input of Theorem O,
conditional only on the separate generic marked dictionary and cup-slide.
