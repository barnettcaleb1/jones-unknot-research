# Finite-index rigidity for rational-tangle pattern tensors

15 November 2026. Codex, revised after Claude195721 and its three referee reports. The original results received three independent referee confirmations. This revision incorporates their corrections, makes the elementary tessellation argument primary, and records two further internally checked strengthenings: finite index automatically implies similitude, and the actual-pattern tensor classification needs no unit seed. TRUSTED; novelty unassessed. No geometric-rationality theorem is asserted.

## 1. Setup and results

Let R=Z[A,A^-1], K=Q(A), bar(A)=A^-1, delta=-A²-A^-2, and *=bar-transpose. Put

    G=[[delta,1],[1,delta]],
    M=[[A,0],[A^-1,-A^-3]],   J=[[0,1],[1,0]],
    E=[[delta,1],[-1,-delta]].

Gamma denotes the projective group generated by M,J, isomorphic to PSL(2,Z). Bare word representatives lie in GL_2(R), have signed even-monomial determinants, and preserve G. Put Gamma^ext=<Gamma,[E]>.

**Theorem A.** Let S be ANY element of GL_2(K). If

    H=Gamma intersect [S]Gamma[S]^-1

has finite index in Gamma, then [S] belongs to Gamma^ext. Consequently S normalizes Gamma: the intersection is actually Gamma. The hypothesis automatically implies S*GS=cG, with c nonzero and fixed by bar. Positive multiplier near A=exp(pi i/4) selects the ordinary Gamma coset.

The index assumption is one-sided. No second finite-index assumption or similitude hypothesis is hidden in the statement. In particular the entire normalizer of Gamma in PGL_2(K) is Gamma^ext.

**Theorem B.** Let C in Mat_2(R) be the common reduced raw bracket tensor of an actual planar diagram with two disjoint marked rational-tangle slots. Assume det C is nonzero. Suppose the first projection of the universal coupled-symmetry group

    P^T C Q=uC,   P,Q in Gamma,   u bar(u)=1,

has finite index in Gamma. Then

    C=f G W,   0 != f in R,

for a bare Gamma word W. If some rational input pair gives a reduced raw bracket that is a Laurent unit, then f=+/-A^m. An actual unknot rational seed is sufficient for this last hypothesis.

Thus finite-index universal symmetry forces a scalar rational-gluing tensor. This is a classification of bracket tensors, NOT of the underlying patterns up to isotopy. The scalar factorization does not assert a connected-sum decomposition of the pattern.

## 2. The omitted outer symmetry

Exact multiplication gives

    E²=(delta²-1)I,              E*GE=(delta²-1)G,
    EJE^-1=-J,                  EME^-1=-A^-2 J M^-1 J.

Therefore E normalizes projective Gamma. But [E] is not in Gamma: it centralizes [J], whose centralizer in C_2*C_3 is <[J]>, while E is proportional neither to I nor J. Equivalently it fixes [J] and inverts the order-three generator [JM], inducing the nontrivial outer action on the C_3 factor of the abelianization. Hence Gamma^ext=Gamma semidirect C_2.

This is an index-one counterexample to the original assertion that finite index forces [S] in Gamma. Its multiplier delta²-1 is negative on the triangle-group arc. It swaps the sides of the invariant circle and is omitted by using only the orientation-preserving commensurator.

## 3. Automatic similitude and good reduction

### 3.1 Finite index forces the form

For h in H choose bare representatives h,k in Gamma and alpha in K with

    hS=alpha Sk.

Determinants give alpha²=det(h)/det(k)=+/-A^(2m). A negative square is impossible in the formally real field Q(A). Thus alpha=+/-A^m and alpha bar(alpha)=1. Consequently every h preserves both G and

    H_S=(S^-1)*G S^-1.

It follows that Z=G^-1 H_S centralizes H. A nonscalar two-by-two matrix has a commutative centralizer. If Z were nonscalar, H would therefore be projectively abelian, contradicting finite index in PSL(2,Z). Thus Z=c_0 I, with c_0 fixed by bar, and S*GS=c_0^-1 G. This removes the similitude hypothesis from Theorem A without using a classification of Aut(PSL(2,Z)).

### 3.2 The modular specialization cannot degenerate

Let O be the DVR of K at Phi_8=A^4+1. Rescale S to have entries in O with at least one a unit. In hS=alpha Sk, alpha is a unit, and bare h,k reduce to invertible matrices. If the primitive reduction S_0 had rank one, its image would be a common invariant line for H_0.

At A_0=exp(pi i/4), put D=diag(1,i). Then

    D^-1 M(A_0)D=A_0 [[1,0],[-1,1]],
    D^-1 J D=i [[0,1],[-1,0]],
    D^-1 E(A_0)D=i [[0,1],[1,0]].

Specialization of Gamma is the faithful modular group. The finite-index subgroup H_0 contains powers of two parabolics with distinct fixed lines. It cannot preserve a line. Hence S_0 is invertible. G_0 and the specialized multiplier are also nondegenerate. This is the compactness input needed below; bounded rational degree alone would not justify bounded representing words.

## 4. Triangle specializations and a fixed word

Take odd n>30 and

    A_n=exp(pi i/4-pi i/(2n)),    delta(A_n)=-2 sin(pi/n).

The projective image Gamma_n is Delta(2,3,n). For this odd sequence, Funar--Kohno Section3, Proposition3.5 and Lemma3.4 give the literal statement. Oddness is sufficient, not necessary: the full image is the same triangle group for every n>=7. For n>30 it is nonarithmetic and maximal among orientation-preserving discrete Fuchsian groups, so Margulis, Takeuchi and Singerman give Comm^+(Gamma_n)=Gamma_n. Girondo--Gonzalez-Diez Section2 states the combined conclusion explicitly.

The holomorphic circle stabilizer has two components. If g is a side-swapping commensurator, gE_n^-1 is side-preserving and therefore in Gamma_n. Thus the entire commensurator within the HOLOMORPHIC circle stabilizer is <Gamma_n,E_n>. Anti-holomorphic circle maps are not included or needed.

Discard the finitely many n where S has a pole, det S vanishes or has a pole, or the multiplier c vanishes or has a pole. At the remaining unit-circle points c(A_n) is real and nonzero. H_n has index at most [Gamma:H] in Gamma_n, and S_n^-1 H_n S_n is contained in Gamma_n. Because S_n acts as a hyperbolic isometry, with disk-side identification in the negative case, this conjugate has the same finite covolume as H_n. It therefore has finite index in Gamma_n. This upgrades one-sided finite index to two-sided commensurability at each specialization and gives

    [S_n] in <Gamma_n,E_n>.

### 4.1 Primary proof: one generic point in a fundamental chamber

Make continuously varying coordinate changes so the null circles of G_n coincide. A side-swapping holomorphic map acts on the chosen disk as an orientation-reversing isometry after composition with reflection in the circle. The resulting identification with Isom(H²) is a continuous group isomorphism. The multiplier sign is stable near its nonzero limit.

In this action E_n is the reflection in the geodesic through the fixed points of J_n and J_nM_n: it swaps their eigenlines between the two disk sides, so after the disk identification fixes both points. Together with J_n and J_nM_n it generates the side reflections of a triangle T_n of angles pi/2, pi/3, pi/n. This is a strict fundamental chamber for <Gamma_n,E_n>.

The two finite vertices and their joining side converge to those of the modular triangle; the third vertex tends to the ideal vertex of T_0. Thus compact subsets of int(T_0) lie in int(T_n) for all large n. The side reflections are fixed words in J_n,M_n,E_n and converge. T_0 is the fundamental chamber of Gamma_0^ext.

Here is the needed limit lemma. Suppose g_n belongs to <Gamma_n,E_n> and converges to an invertible isometry g. Choose x in int(T_0) such that gx avoids every wall of the limiting modular tessellation. This is possible because preimages of its countably many wall geodesics have measure zero. Then gx lies in h_0 int(T_0) for one fixed word h_0 in J_0,M_0,E_0. Let h_n be that same word at the nth parameter. By convergence,

    x in int(T_n),    h_n^-1 g_n x in int(T_n)

for every sufficiently large n. These are points in the same group orbit. Disjoint chamber interiors and the trivial stabilizer of a chamber interior force h_n^-1g_n=1. Thus g_n=h_n eventually and g=h_0.

Apply the lemma to g_n=S_n, whose invertible limit is established in Section3.2. A single word W in J,M,E agrees projectively with S at all sufficiently large A_n. Projective equality is the vanishing of rational-function cross-products of matrix entries. Equality at infinitely many A_n makes those functions identically zero. This proves Theorem A without a global local-finiteness estimate, word-length bound, or Kleinian convergence theorem.

### 4.2 Alternative: Jorgensen--Klein and the modular coarea bound

The original proof remains a useful independent route. The four marked generators J_n,M_n,E_n,S_n generate a discrete non-elementary subgroup of PGL_2(C) and converge. Jorgensen--Klein makes their limit L discrete and gives, eventually, an epimorphism from L onto each approximant preserving the generators.

L preserves the limiting circle and contains the extended modular group. Its orientation-preserving part L^+ contains the lattice PSL(2,Z) with finite index, so

    area(L^+)=(pi/3)/[L^+:PSL(2,Z)].

It contains a parabolic, hence is noncompact. The signature formula 2pi(2g-2+s+sum(1-1/m_i)), s>=1, has minimum positive value pi/3. Thus the index is one; uniqueness of a minimizing signature is not needed. L is the extended modular group. Write S_0=w(J_0,M_0)E_0^epsilon. The eventual epimorphism transfers this single fixed relation to S_n, and rational identity gives the same generic conclusion.

The original Jorgensen--Klein paper was inaccessible. Its statement, including the direction of the map, was checked in later sources as listed below. It is no longer an input to the primary proof.

## 5. Actual tensors exclude the outer coset without a seed

### 5.1 The elementary obstruction at A=1

Put S=G^-1C. Unitarity of P forces Q=u S^-1 bar(P) S, so the first projection lies in Gamma intersect [bar(S)]Gamma[bar(S)]^-1. If the projection has finite index, that containing intersection has finite index as well. Theorem A applied to bar(S), and bar(Gamma)=Gamma with bar(E)=E, puts S in Gamma or E Gamma. The outer case has

    C=g diag(1,-1) W,    g in K,    W a bare Gamma word,

because GE=(delta²-1)diag(1,-1).

At A=1 let M_1=[[1,0],[1,-1]] and K_1=M_1J=[[0,1],[-1,1]]. M_1 and J are involutions; K_1²=K_1-I and K_1³=-I. Their group is dihedral of order12. Every word is one of +/-I, +/-K_1, +/-K_1², or a row swap of one of these by J. Each has a zero entry and, being invertible, a nonzero entry.

A nonzero entry of W(1) makes g regular at1, since C has integral Laurent entries. A zero entry of W(1) then forces a corner C_ij(1)=0. But an actual nonempty link diagram D has reduced raw bracket

    <D>(1)=+/-(-2)^(number of components-1),

which never vanishes. Indeed crossing changes leave the bracket unchanged at1; change crossings until the link is an unlink, and use Reidemeister moves, with a curl changing only the sign. This contradiction excludes the outer coset for EVERY actual two-slot pattern, even when all four determinants at zeta_8 vanish and no unit seed is known.

Therefore C=f G W. Since W^-1 has entries in R,

    f=(C W^-1)_(0,1) in R.

Here and throughout Section5, corner indices are 0/1; corner11 is the lower-right entry. The displayed off-diagonal entry is entry(1,2) in 1-based matrix notation. If rational input vectors v,w give a reduced raw bracket unit u, then

    u=v^T Cw=f(v^T G Ww)

is a product in R, so f is a Laurent unit, necessarily +/-A^m. This proves Theorem B and its unit-seed corollary.

Without a unit seed f need not be a unit. Connect-summing a fixed Hopf link along an outside arc multiplies the rational-gluing tensor G by its reduced raw bracket -A^4-A^-4. The resulting tensor has full symmetry but a nonunit scalar. Also f itself is an actual rational-input closure bracket: take v=e_0 and w=W^-1e_1, giving v^T G Ww=1. Neither statement asserts a topological factorization of the pattern.

### 5.2 Independent signed-square obstruction

The earlier local-diagram proof remains useful. At zeta=zeta_8, for ANY nonzero corner of an actual two-box diagram,

    (-1)^(i+j) det C(zeta)/C_ij(zeta)²=(m/t)² >=0,

with integers m,t and t nonzero. A unit-modulus anchor has t=+/-1. The result is independent of swapping the two filling labels at either box.

In the common black Tait graph, signed spanning-tree counts with prescribed marked-edge membership satisfy

    tau_10 tau_01-tau_00 tau_11=(p^T adj(L)r)².

At delta=0 the brackets are the one-circle states, with phases q i^(-i-j) tau_ij. These phases give the stated sign. The completed projection may be assumed connected; if disconnected, all four corners vanish at zeta and there is no nonzero anchor. Full proof: work/global_design/two_box_signed_square.md.

For an outer-coset tensor, the D-gauge at zeta gives

    C(zeta)=v [[i c,d],[a,-i b]],    ad-bc=-1.

When C(zeta) is nonzero, v is finite and nonzero. At a nonzero diagonal anchor the normalized determinant is negative, e.g. det C/C11²=-1/b²; at a nonzero off-diagonal anchor it is positive. This is the OPPOSITE of the required sign at every nonzero anchor. No seed move is necessary. The A=1 proof also covers the remaining case C(zeta)=0.

## 6. Scope and falsifiers

These results classify finite-index universal symmetries at the level of the full two-slot bracket tensor. They do not identify the actual pattern up to isotopy or handle agreement on selected input pairs only. An actual nonrational realization of a rational-gluing tensor remains a separate question.

Infinite-index intersections remain open. They are not automatically cyclic: PSL(2,Z) has nonabelian free subgroups of infinite index. TH-SYM's nonscalar-Z argument does not apply inside the similitude case. No new claim about this case is made here.

An exact rational matrix outside Gamma^ext with a certified finite-index intertwining subgroup would contradict Theorem A. An actual marked pattern with finite-index universal symmetry and tensor not fGW, f in R, would contradict Theorem B; a unit rational seed further requires f=+/-A^m. Agreement at finitely many roots or selected inputs does not satisfy these hypotheses.
