## Good reduction at Phi_8

Let O=Q[A] localized at Phi_8, with valuation v. Scale S so its entries lie in O and at least one is a unit. For every h in the generic finite-index subgroup H=Gamma intersect S Gamma S^-1, choose bare Laurent representatives h,k in Gamma and write

    h S=c_h S k.

Their determinants are O-units. Taking determinants and cancelling det S gives 2v(c_h)=0, hence c_h is an O-unit. Reduction therefore gives h_0 S_0=(c_h)_0 S_0 k_0. If S_0 had rank one, its image would be an H_0-invariant line. But specialization identifies projective Gamma faithfully with the standard modular group, so H_0 has finite index in PSL(2,Z). It contains powers of two parabolics with distinct fixed lines and cannot preserve one complex line. Thus S_0 is invertible.

The similitude factor also has nonzero finite specialization: det(S* G S)=c^2 det G, and both det S and det G are units at Phi_8. In particular the projective matrices used in the algebraic-limit argument converge to genuine invertible matrices.

## Specializing one-sided index

H is finitely generated. Choose its finite generating set and the finitely many corresponding conjugate Gamma words. Apart from finitely many poles/degeneracies, their identities specialize. The image H_n has index at most [Gamma:H] in Gamma_n and lies in S_n Gamma_n S_n^-1. Thus it is a finite-covolume subgroup of both triangle lattices. Containment of a lattice in another discrete lattice forces finite index; the two conjugate lattices have the same covolume, including when the conjugating isometry reverses orientation. Therefore S_n belongs to the full commensurator, not merely a one-sided intersection set.

For a negative real similitude factor, S_n is not an element of PU(1,1): as a holomorphic Möbius map it swaps the two sides of the invariant circle. It is correctly viewed in the extended real-circle stabilizer, embedded in PGL(2,C), or as an orientation-reversing isometry of the hyperbolic plane. This is a discrete Kleinian extension of the orientation-preserving Fuchsian lattice. R_n supplies the negative component. Since R_n normalizes Gamma_n, any negative commensurator element multiplied by R_n is positive; the positive commensurator theorem then identifies the full one.

## Algebraic limits eliminate the bounded-word gap

[Inoue, Remarks on algebraic convergence of discrete Möbius groups, Theorem1](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kyodo/kokyuroku/contents/pdf/1022-15.pdf) records the Jorgensen–Klein theorem in precisely the needed direction: a non-elementary fixed-generator Kleinian algebraic limit is discrete, and the correspondence from limit generators to approximating generators eventually extends to an epimorphism. The cited original is Jorgensen–Klein, Quart.J.Math.Oxford(2)33(1982),325–332, DOI10.1093/qmath/33.3.325.

Apply this to the four named generators J_n,M_n,R_n,S_n. The limit group K_0 is still contained in the closed extended circle stabilizer for G_0, hence is extended Fuchsian. It contains the extended modular group. Its positive subgroup is a discrete overgroup of PSL(2,Z), so has finite area. It contains a parabolic and therefore has a cusp. The least possible area of an orientable finite-area noncompact hyperbolic orbifold is pi/3, attained by signature(0;2,3;1 cusp). Since PSL(2,Z) already has area pi/3, the positive overgroup is equal to it. Including R_0 gives the full extended modular group.

Consequently S_0=w(J_0,M_0)R_0^epsilon for one fixed finite word w. The eventual epimorphism carries THIS RELATION to

    S_n=w(J_n,M_n)R_n^epsilon.

It is not necessary to bound word lengths representing S_n beforehand. This is exactly the missing rigidity in the original letter's bounded-degree argument. The infinitely many specializations then force the projective rational-function identity S=w(J,M)R^epsilon.

## Integral scalar upgrade for actual tensors

Suppose a further geometric argument excludes the R coset and gives C=f G W, where W is a bare Gamma matrix in GL_2(Z[A,A^-1]) and C has integral Laurent entries. Then

    f=(C W^-1)_(1,2)

because G_(1,2)=1. Thus f is itself integral Laurent. If integral rational input vectors v,w give an actual unknot closure in the **reduced** normalization, its bracket u is a Laurent unit and

    u=f(v^T G W w).

Both factors lie in the integral Laurent ring, so f is a unit: f=+-A^m. The second factor is a unit too. This argument must use reduced bracket u, not the absolute bracket delta u. It proves equality with a rational tensor up to framing unit, not topological rationality.
