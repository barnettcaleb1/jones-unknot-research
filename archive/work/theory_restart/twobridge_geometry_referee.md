# Two-bridge quotient geometry and the matrix coefficient

16 November 2026. Bounded referee of Sections 1–3 of
`/Users/calebbarnett/jones-lanes/theory-restart/mech2/twobridge/REPORT.md`.
This checks the geometric input to Theorem B; its leading-exponent argument
is audited separately in `twobridge_automaton_referee.md`.

## Verdict

The side-preserving class and the two-dimensional colored calculation are
sound. They give an all-word route to Theorem A, not merely a fit to the
computed examples. The statement must use the **mixed braid group**, and
the projector argument should be replaced by the single-edge argument
below. The only direction of the topological double-coset assertion needed
for Theorem B is elementary and can be proved by its cap moves. The stronger
converse need not be asserted.

Combining these repairs with the separately repaired automaton proves
nonflatness for the standard, side-preserving strong inversions of every
nontrivial two-bridge knot. This includes all inversion classes when
q² is not 1 modulo p, and the unique inversion of a two-strand torus knot.
It does not cover the additional exceptional inversion in the remaining
hyperbolic cases. It does not prove END or NP for the unsigned homology.

## 1. Which group acts

The three moving objects have labels (1,2,2): one axis strand and two
legs of the doubled knot arc. A single crossing exchanging labels 1 and 2
does not act on a fixed ordered tensor product. Thus the phrase
“representation of B_3 modulo its centre” is inaccurate.

The relevant subgroup is the subgroup of B_3 preserving the distinguished
axis strand. It is generated by a full axis–band twist and the exchange
of the two bands. With the handedness used in the report these are

    Y = sigma_1²,   X^-1 = sigma_2.

This is the mixed three-strand braid group, equivalently the two-strand
annular braid group or Artin group of type B_2. The relation is

    Y X^-1 Y X^-1 = X^-1 Y X^-1 Y,

and its common value is the central full twist of the three strands.
After dividing by this centre the group is C_2 * Z. There is no need for
an action of the full ordinary B_3 on this fixed colored space.

The displayed local cablings agree literally with the implementation:
`Tangles.cpp`, `Tangle::quotient`, lines 967–1040, sends an axis `y0` to
`y0.y1.y1.y0`, and an off-axis `x1` to `x2.x1.x3.x2`. Cups and caps are
doubled and the final full-twist correction records the preferred framing.
This is a useful marking check, not the proof of the colored formula.

## 2. An all-word proof of Theorem A

Work at generic A in the Temperley–Lieb ribbon category over Q(A), with
delta = -A²-A^-2. Write V_j for the Jones–Wenzl color j. The projector on
two parallel strands is f_2 = 1-delta^-1 e.

### 2.1 Extract the color on one edge, then move it

For the quotient theta graph Theta=A union k, color both axis half-edges
by 1 and the arc k by 2. The normalized evaluation is the spin-2
eigenvalue J of the quotient tangle, up to the fixed ribbon-framing unit.
Equivalently, close the spin-2 projector around the relevant pair of
boundary strands and divide its quantum trace by Delta_2. The spin-0
term is killed by f_2; the identity term contributes Delta_2 times its
coefficient. The trivial theta gives the same nonzero normalization.

Insert f_2 on the **single edge k**, and move it along that edge. Naturality
of colored braiding permits it to pass each crossing, and idempotence
permits duplicate placements. In a two-band presentation this puts f_2 on
both band legs. This proves the passage to the colored braid calculation
for every word.

This is preferable to the report's sentence that every correction
turnback “passes through every crossing and closes to c.” An arbitrary
internal turnback need not be assigned a fixed output connectivity before
the rest of a diagram is specified. The single colored edge and its
projector already give the required identity, without that assertion.

### 2.2 The two vectors and their local eigenvalue properties

The multiplicity space is

    H = Hom(V_1, V_1 tensor V_2 tensor V_2).

The fusion of the two V_2 objects has colors 0,2,4, of which just 0 and 2
can couple with V_1 to total color 1. Hence dim H=2. In the six-endpoint
planar model the two surviving projected matchings are exactly the two
listed in Theorem A; the other three contain a cap killed by f_2.

Let E_1 be the band cup, i.e. the color-0 fusion of the two V_2 legs.
Let phi be the cap functional that fuses the axis V_1 with the adjacent
V_2 leg to color 1 and closes the remaining vertex. These are the cup
and cap in the marked quotient construction. Normalize the trivial-theta
evaluation so that phi(E_1)=1.

The band exchange X preserves the color-0 cup line, so E_1 is an
X-eigenvector. The full mixed twist Y preserves the color-1 fusion at
the other cap, so phi is a left Y-eigenvector. These statements are local
fusion identities, independent of the intervening word.

Choose F spanning ker(phi), use (E_1,F) as basis, and divide X and Y by
their eigenvalues on this cup and cap respectively. Both divisions are
ribbon-framing units, not rational functions with nonunit denominators.
The matrices have the forms

    X = [[1,xi],[0,lambda]],   Y = [[1,0],[eta,mu]].

For a crossing of colors a,b, the eigenvalue in channel c is

    (-1)^((a+b-c)/2) A^((a(a+2)+b(b+2)-c(c+2))/2),

or its inverse for the other handedness. Consequently the ratio of the
two (2,2) half-twist eigenvalues is -A^-4=-t. The ratio of the two
(1,2) full-twist eigenvalues is A^12=t^-3 with the opposite crossing
handedness in this marked half-diagram. Thus lambda=-t, mu=t^-3.
Reversing the handedness everywhere replaces t by t^-1. One trefoil
calibration fixes which of these conventionally equivalent formulas is
being used; it is not an induction hypothesis or an empirical extension
to long words.

The mixed central relation implies (YX^-1)² is scalar: a full twist is
scalar on a fixed total color. YX^-1 cannot itself be scalar, since the
two normalized eigenvalue ratios -t and t^-3 are distinct generically.
A nonscalar 2-by-2 matrix with scalar square has trace zero. Taking that
trace gives

    xi eta = t^-3(1-t^4).

This is nonzero. Rescale F so xi=1-t^4; then eta=t^-3. The result is
exactly the pair of matrices in Theorem A.

Every local normalization was a power of A with sign. The fixed
trivial-theta normalization cancels between numerator and denominator.
Therefore the discrepancy with the preferred-framing J is a monomial
unit, not an unspecified rational scalar. For chronological word
w=L_1...L_m (L_1 nearest the cup),

    J(T_w) = unit * phi rho(L_m)...rho(L_1) E_1.

This proves the all-word statement by composition. It covers negative
letters as well, by invertibility of the same colored crossings.

## 3. The cap moves sufficient for the nonunit conclusion

Do not require the unsupported full biconditional “T_w is trivial iff
w belongs to the double coset.” The implication needed after the
automaton is only this:

> A word that reduces, modulo the mixed centre, to powers of X at the
> band-cup end and powers of Y at the axis-band-cap end has the trivial
> underlying theta graph; its quotient is rational.

There are three local moves. A power of X at the band cup twists a
ribbon whose core is the cup; it changes its framing and not its core
arc. A power of Y at the other vertex rotates the end of k about the
axis at that vertex; it likewise changes only the ribbon framing. The
mixed full twist rotates the whole band cup once about the distinguished
axis and can be moved into these closing neighborhoods. Its core is
unchanged; the residual ribbon twist is precisely the framing change.
These moves take the word to the empty theta diagram. In particular the
underlying covering knot is the unknot. The twist corrections made to
choose the Seifert framing do not alter this implication.

The repaired algebraic referee identifies exactly the fully degenerate
normal forms. In matrix-composition order they are generated by

    (E Y^-1)^r = central * X^-r,
    (E Y)^(r-1) E = Y^-1 X^r,

where E=Y^-1 X. Including the end factors discarded in forming normal
form gives the cap double coset <Y><X>. Reversing the order to chronological
words gives the report's <X><Y>. Thus every zero-span case reached by the
automaton is removed by the explicit moves above. No new classification
of unknot plat diagrams or strong inversions is hidden in this step.

The contrapositive, together with Theorem A and the repaired automaton,
is the desired statement: a nontrivial covering knot in the standard
two-bridge class has a nonunit J.

## 4. Scope from a primary classification source

Use Hirasawa–Hiura–Sakuma, *The equivariant genera of marked strongly
invertible knots associated with 2-bridge knots*, arXiv:2312.06156v2,
Section 2, Proposition 2.2, and Figures 2.1–2.3:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.06156.

In their notation K(q/p), p is odd and q is chosen even. The standard
symmetric four-plat constructions give the inversions h_(q/p) and
h_(q'/p), with qq'=1 modulo p. For a hyperbolic knot these are distinct
when q² is not 1 modulo p. When q²=1 they are equivalent and there is an
additional exceptional inversion. A two-strand torus knot has a unique
inversion class. The report's two-band model is the standard construction,
not the exceptional one.

Their continued fraction is the negative even expansion and they write
q/p where the report writes p/q. Translate signs and reciprocals before
assigning numerical labels. In particular, no numerical continued-fraction
shortcut that divides an odd entry by two certifies a fraction label.
This does not affect a theorem stated directly for the marked words.

The source classifies up to equivariant equivalence, not its stronger
isotopy refinement; its Remark 2.3 explicitly distinguishes those notions.
The quotient invariant and flatness are preserved under the equivalence
used here, so the stated scope is sufficient. One can cite this primary
source instead of relying on a code comment quoting the unread original
Sakuma Proposition 3.6.

## 5. What is and is not obtained

This is a class theorem for the quotient tangles of standard strong
inversions of two-bridge covering knots. Their rational closures need
not be two-bridge links; that would be the wrong reason for nonflatness.
No prior-art claim has been checked in this referee pass.

The surviving leading and trailing coefficients of J are an Euler
polynomial result. They do not imply that the actual top and bottom
quantum degrees of N have nonzero Euler characteristic: invisible
cancelling groups could lie outside the polynomial's support. The
categorified CAT/junction statement, END(N), NP, the exceptional
side-exchanging inversions, and any mixed braid group with more band
legs remain separate questions.

Nor does a Reidemeister-II model of the formal bare object B classify
all formal ladders. The local RII cancellation explains that particular
presentation. A theorem that every ladder can occur only as an unreduced
word would be an additional realizability theorem, not a consequence of
the two-dimensional matrix calculation.
