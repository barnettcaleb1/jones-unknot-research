WHITEHEAD FACTOR AND CLASP-CIRCLE AUDIT
31 October 2026; bounded internal research audit, not a novelty claim.

1. Exact shell factor up to Laurent unit and mirror

Use delta=-A^2-A^-2 and t=A^-4. The preferred trefoil quotient constructed
from R=<-1>+<1/2>+<1/3>, then T=rotate(R)+<-6>, has
  f_infinity(T)=A^-18,
  f_zero(T)/f_infinity(T)=-A^2+A^6-A^22-A^30.
The parent calculation is stored in trefoil_longitude_bracket.json.
The difference from delta is A^-2(1+A^8)(1-A^24), and therefore
  x(T)=(R(T)-delta)/(1-delta^2)
      =A^2(A^4-1)(1+A^8)(1+A^12).

The numerator closure of the original Montesinos tangle is an actual
unknot, and its cover is the Seifert manifold D^2(2,3). Thus the cover is
the trefoil exterior, up to chirality. Rotation makes the actual unknot
closure the meridian closure. The other closure at the displayed -6
shift has determinant zero, so its filling slope is the unique preferred
longitude. This fixes the peripheral marking, not just the underlying
unmarked exterior.

A nontrivial torus knot has a unique strong inversion up to conjugacy.
Primary convenient statement: Hirasawa--Hiura--Sakuma, Proposition 2.1(2),
https://www.sci.osaka-cu.ac.jp/OCAMI/publication/preprint/pdf2022/22_04.pdf
(arXiv:2206.02097), citing Sakuma's original Proposition 3.1.
An orientation-preserving equivalence preserves meridian and preferred
longitude slopes, so its matrix on the peripheral torus is +/-I. No
boundary Dehn twist remains. Downstairs residual marking ambiguity is
one of the pillowcase half-translations (Conway flips), trivial on the
rank-two rational-tangle skein module. More conservatively, any residual
framed map fixes every rational slope: fixing Q0,Q_infinity,Q1 forces
one common scalar unit, so it cannot introduce a nonmonomial channel
change.

For the Whitehead shell, one of the input probes Q_1,Q_-1 gives a trefoil;
which one depends on clasp and surgery sign. Since x(Q_1)=-A^2 and
x(Q_-1)=-A^-2, the normalized transmission factor is, up to a Laurent
unit and A -> A^-1,
  d_Wh(A)=(1-A^4)(1+A^8)(1+A^12).
The uniqueness argument covers the two standard inherited clasp
placements. This proves the same zero multiplicities and nonvanishing
for both, without claiming a chosen sign/framing convention is identical.

For an r-fold tower over the trefoil, x is thus a Laurent unit times
  [(1-A^4)(1+A^8)(1+A^12)]^(r+1).
The slope-1/2 closure factor is independently checked below. The
complete difference is a signed t-monomial times
  (t-1)^(r+2)(t^2+t+1)(t^2+1)^(r+1)(t^3+1)^(r+1).
A normalization point is essential: infinity and 1/2 fillings have the
same endpoint pairing, so orientations can agree on both tangle arcs.
Their writhe difference comes solely from the fixed cap. Consequently
normalizing the reference Q0 closure gives exactly 1, and the difference
V-1 is linear in x with no unknown additive monomial. For an independent exact cap check, rotate(Q_-2) has coefficients
(1-A^4,A^-2). Its Q0 closure is A^6 and Q_infinity closure is
-A^4-A^-4. Dividing the reference unit and subtracting delta gives
A^2-A^-10=A^-10(A^12-1). The standard-library file
whitehead_factor_check.py checks this and the factorization exactly.

2. Proposed converse via the Whitehead clasp: NOT CERTIFIED

The proposal was to start with a nontrivial Jones-flat strongly invertible
J, form Wh(J), unknot it by surgery on its clasp crossing circle C, and
use the resulting U union C' as an essential zero-factor shell.
The algebra and surgery markings are sound IF Wh(J) union C is
componentwise strongly inverted by the SAME inherited inversion used
in transmission. The standard clasp circle fails that condition.

Primary geometry inspected directly:
Dai--Mallick--Taniguchi, Exotic disks and singular instanton Floer homology,
arXiv:2606.05819v1, Example 2.22, Figure 4 on printed page14, Figures5--6
on page15. URL https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05819
Local downloaded PDF exotic.pdf and rendered page exotic14.png preserve
the inspection evidence.

In Figure4, for both inherited placements the rotation axis runs along
the clasp twist direction. The two fixed points of Wh(J) are on the
doubled cap at the opposite fixed point of the companion. The clasp
strands are exchanged. A standard disk transverse to this twist region
has its boundary crossing circle rotated freely. It is NOT a strongly
inverted component, and its quotient boundary is a torus rather than the
four-punctured sphere needed for the rank-two shell construction.

The alternate local model h(x,y,z)=(x,-y,-z), with helices
  gamma_+/- (z)=(+/-cos(pi z), +/-sin(pi z), z), -1<=z<=1,
and disk z=0 is valid locally: its crossing circle is strongly inverted,
and both knot fixed points lie in this disk. But this is not the
inherited Whitehead placement in the primary diagram. Extending that
local action over the doubled companion, while retaining the inherited
quotient's flatness, is an additional unproved geometric requirement.
Thus no converse or equivalence with Jones-one unknotting-number-one
knots follows from the standard clasp argument as currently stated.

3. General local gate for an invariant crossing disk

Let tau preserve ambient orientation and strongly invert K and C=boundary D.
If D is tau-invariant, then tau restricts to a reflection of D. Because
it reverses BOTH the orientation of D and the orientation of K, it
preserves each oriented intersection sign in D intersect K. If the two
punctures are interchanged by tau, their signs agree, giving lk(C,K)=+/-2.
Hence an invariant TWO-puncture crossing disk with lk(C,K)=0 must have
both punctures fixed individually; these are the two fixed points of K.
This is the exact local distinction between the valid perpendicular
helix model and the free clasp circle in the inherited Whitehead model.
It does not rule out a more elaborate nonlocal circle or a different
strong inversion; those require an actual construction.
