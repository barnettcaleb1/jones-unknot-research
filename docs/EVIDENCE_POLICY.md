# Evidence, status, and precedence

The archive used `PROVED` to mean either a cited published result or a written argument checked by the other collaborating AI system. Those are different kinds of evidence. This public edition preserves that historical label but **does not translate it into external mathematical validation**.

| Historical label | Public reading |
|---|---|
| PROVED / REFEREED | Internally checked argument or attributed published theorem; inspect which evidence applies |
| TRUSTED | Provisional argument believed by its author; a promised referee may never have reported |
| OPEN / UNDECIDED | Unresolved step or construction |
| DATA / CHECKED | A stated finite computation, with its domain and implementation dependencies |
| SUSPECT | A credible objection remains unresolved |
| REFUTED / WITHDRAWN | A claim or inference failed; its original record is retained for provenance |

The early ledger also uses codes such as `IP`, `FC`, and combinations. Their original explanations are preserved in the handoff documents; do not silently equate them with later labels.

## Resolve conflicting records

1. Read the precise statement and its hypotheses, not just the node name.
2. Look for a later correction, counterexample, scope restriction, or withdrawal in the correspondence and current scaffold.
3. Read the full proof note and cited source at the stated version and theorem number.
4. Separate an algebraic identity from geometric realization, an actual diagram from a formal object, and a bounded search from a general theorem.
5. Separate a successful fixed certificate from a claim that a whole pipeline is reproducible.

The claim index is an index of records, not an automatic reconciliation engine. A ledger entry may survive only in a narrowed form. Historical sections sometimes reuse numbers; file path, heading text, and correspondence identifier are the stable provenance.

At the final pause, the new JT₂ gadget restrictions were still TRUSTED. Their independent referee workflows had stopped before returning verdicts. The EF Taylor-depth calculation had received an arithmetic read and a direct comparison with the printed seed and framing. Neither fact implies a general Jones detection theorem.

## Reporting a correction

Identify the smallest affected claim, give an exact witness or source location, and explain whether the conclusion is false, the proof has a gap, or the scope is too broad. State which dependent claims are affected. Do not erase the failed argument: append a correction and link both directions.

A statement about another author's work must distinguish the published theorem from our interpretation of its proof. A failed argument for a conjecture does not refute the conjecture. No novelty or priority claim is established merely because a literature search found nothing.
